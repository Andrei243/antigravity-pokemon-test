# Game data files

The game's species, moves, abilities, items and maps live as JSON in `PokemonPlatinumEngine/Data/` and are copied next to the executable when it builds. `GameDataFiles` reads them: property names are camelCase, enum values are written by name (`"Grass"`, `"PokeBalls"`), comments and trailing commas are allowed. A missing or malformed file stops start-up with an error naming the file.

**The files a person writes are read strictly** (plan 24 · X4, `GameDataFiles.Strict`): each region's `world.json` and `overlays/`, the map files of `maps/`, `audio/sound-map.json`, `facility-trainers.json` and `facility-prizes.json`. A key no field has (`hidenBy`; and case counts, so `BgmTrack` is refused, not read), a value of the wrong type or a required field left out (a map file's `name`, `width`, `height` and `ground`, a `world.json`'s `region`) stops the load with the file, the JSON path and the key: `world/sinnoh/overlays/route_204_south.json: $.people.lass_sarah.hidenBy: no such field`. The small records written on one line (a door, a trigger, a height plate) refuse an unknown key too. The generated files (the five tables, the trainers of our own and the masters', the later sets, the worlds' chunks, areas, matrices, habitats, calendar and encounters) and the player's own (saves, `settings.json`) are read leniently, so an older save still opens; `DataFileStrictTests` reads the generated ones strictly, so a field renamed without running the importer fails there and never in a game, and `tools/ContentCheck -- --generated` does the same. A model's options file (`<model>.json` beside a model file) with a key no setting has is reported with the file and the key, and none of its settings are used. Scripts are read by `ScriptParser`, which names a mistake by its file and line.

**Schemas**: `docs/schemas/` holds a JSON schema of each format a person writes (`world`, `overlay`, `map`, `sound-map`, `model-options`, and the importer's patches `species-patch`, `items-patch`, `moves-patch`), written from the records the game reads them into by `SchemaTests` (`GOLDEN=accept dotnet test PokemonPlatinumTests --filter Golden` rewrites them); `.vscode/settings.json` maps each folder's files to its schema, so an editor completes and checks the keys. The strict load is the check; a schema is a convenience.

`species.json`, `moves.json`, `abilities.json`, `items.json` and `trainers.json` are generated by `tools/DataImporter` from the Platinum decompilation (Generations 1–4) and PokeAPI (everything later); see its README. Don't edit them by hand: put corrections in `tools/DataImporter/Overrides/` and run the importer. Maps come two ways: rooms and Kanto's stand-in are hand-made files in `maps/`; the outdoors of Sinnoh and its caves are made from the imported world in `world/` ("World files" below).

The ground layer and the solid grid are separate because they don't always agree: doors sit in solid walls but are open, signposts are solid, and furniture props make the floor under them solid.

A **room** (any `interior` but `None`) is drawn as a doll's house round its floor: the side wall west of the first column that has anything but wall (`#`) on it, the back wall north of the first such row, the other side wall in the last column and the front wall, with its door, in the last row. The rooms made by hand begin at (1, 2). A room rebuilt to the original's plan keeps the original's tiles, so the people, warps and triggers of the original's events hold: its floor begins where the original's does (`OreburghNorthHouse1F` at (3, 3)), and its door is the tile below the original's exit mat, which is where the overlay's `door` puts whoever comes in. Wall tiles inside the floor are the room's own inner walls (the Valley Windworks'), drawn cut away down to the wainscot.

**A Gym** (plan 01 · M9) is such a room with `interior` `Gym`: its floor is baked from its tiles as the field's ground is (lawn, flowers, paths) and its plain floor (`_`) is the hall's own tiles, in the colours of its `arenaType`, as its walls are (style guide, "Gyms"). A door in its back wall (the Hearthome Gym's) is a warp on the row above the floor, open in `solid` and wall (`#`) in `ground`: it is drawn on the wall, and the player walks into it. `puzzle` names the rules the room plays by (`GymPuzzle.Create`): `EternaClock` (the flower clock, which closes the tiles under its face its time doesn't open and makes its hour hand's tip a hop), `VeilstoneBags` (the people of `npcType` `PunchingBag` and `TireStack` are the bags and the stacks of tyres, kicked and knocked down by the original's rules), `HearthomeRoom1` and `HearthomeRoom2` (a dark room whose doors are chosen as the player comes in: the warps on its back wall are its doors, and the one of the wrong signs leads back to the entrance). The people of a Gym's puzzle stand where the original's code makes them, written into the file as people with ids (`punching_bag_1`, `tire_stack_1`), and `Bollard` is a post that stands in the way like them. A person of `npcType` `Snowball` (the Snowpoint Gym's `snowball_1` to `snowball_19`, plan 01 · M9 2b) needs no puzzle: it is a snowball on the ice, which a slide with speed breaks by the field's own rule (`IceSlide`) and which is back the next time the player comes in. `SunyshoreGears1` to `SunyshoreGears3` (plan 01 · M9 2c) are the Sunyshore Gym's three rooms of gears: the tiles of a gear (its hub, the tiles its arms can reach, a gear on edge's column) are open void (`V`, `.` in `solid`) at the height of its walkway, the dark between them is void too but solid, and the puzzle closes the walkways its state doesn't have; its buttons are the room's triggers, whose scripts run `gearbutton`.

**A room of the League** (plan 01 · M9, part 2d) is a hall like a Gym's (`interior` `Gym`), with `battleArena` `League` where a battle is fought in it. The rooms of the Elite Four name their master's type (`arenaType`: `Bug`, `Ground`, `Fire`, `Psychic`), which gives the stage and the room's look; a room with no type (a passage, the Champion's room, the hallway, the Hall of Fame) names its look with `hall`: `League`, `Champion` or `HallOfFame` (style guide, "The League's rooms"). A door that shuts is a person of the map of `npcType` `LeagueDoor` with the `hiddenBy` flag that takes it away (`entrance_door`, `exit_door`): in the back wall's row it stands on the warp it shuts and is drawn on the wall, shut, and the warp's doorway is drawn open once it is gone; in the front wall's row it is the gate across the kerb. The `puzzle` of a room with a platform lift is its shaft's name (`LeagueLiftAaron`, `LeagueLiftBertha`, `LeagueLiftFlint`, `LeagueLiftLucian`, `LeagueLiftChampion`, `LeagueLiftChampionRoom`; `LeagueLift.Shafts` has each one's tiles and floors), the room's `heights` give the two floors, and a trigger on the platform's middle tile runs `platformlift`. Tiles of `behaviour` `ShinyFloor` (the Hall of Fame's and its hallway's) mirror whoever stands on them. Void tiles (`V`) in a room of fire are its lava.

**A room the Bicycle is ridden through** (plan 01 · M11, part d: the Cycling Road's two gate houses) says `"bike": true`: a rider who comes in stays on the Bicycle, where in any other room they get off at the door. **A room's way that is one of several on a tile** (plan 01 · M11, part c and d): a warp of a map file may carry `until` or `from`, a flag it is there until or from, as an overlay's `switches` do. The car of the Ribbon Syndicate's lift has two on its door's tile, and the car of the Department Store's lift six, one to a floor, each `from` a flag the floor's own arrival script sets.

**A house on the original's plan** (plan 01 · M11, part a: the houses of the south-west and the centre, the flower shop, the Old Chateau) is a room of `interior` `House` and can say five things more. **A way on through its back wall** is, as a Gym's, a warp on the row above the floor, open in `solid` and wall (`#`) in `ground`: it is drawn as a doorway framed in wood (the Old Chateau's corridor), or as a lift's steel doors where `behaviours` gives the tile the behaviour `Door` (the Hearthome houses, whose lift leads straight to the tile before the doors of the other floor). **Stairs at the side** (`SideStairsUp`, `SideStairsDown` and their mirrors) are of wood there, and a block of wall that reaches the back wall with nothing behind it (the flank of a staircase) stands as tall as the back wall, where every other inner wall is cut to the wainscot (`MapScene.BackWallJog`). **Two levels**: with `heights` the room is walked and drawn with relief, its upper floor standing on a panelled face (the Old Chateau's hall and its gallery, three tiles up, with two flights between; the tiles beside a flight, which the original blocks for its balustrade, are plain floor (`_`) that `solid` shuts). **Wild Pokémon** live on the tiles `behaviours` gives the behaviour `OldChateauFloor`, by the file's `wildEncounters` and `encounterRate`, as the Lost Tower's do. Part b's rooms (the east and the sea) can say three things more. **A way through a wall that has come forward** into the room (the Pokémon Mansion's corridors, whose back wall stands three tiles before the hall's) is a warp on a tile of that wall, wall (`#`) in `ground` and open in `solid`: the doorway is drawn on the wall's face, and whoever takes it walks into it. **Nothing** (`V` in `ground`, shut in `solid`) is what lies beyond the one tile of wall round a floor that is no rectangle: it is drawn as the dark outside the room, the wall before it is cut to the front wall's kerb, and the side walls end where it begins. It never lies behind the floor, within a tile of it, or west of its first column. And **someone behind a table** (a café's bar, a reception desk) is spoken to across it where a record of `behaviours` (`{ "x": 7, "y": 5, "width": 3, "depth": 1, "behaviour": "Counter" }`) gives the table's tiles the behaviour `Counter`, as the original's tiles have it. **A lift** (plan 02 · S13: the Vista Lighthouse) is two rooms. A tile's type is what it looks like. What it *does* is its **tile behaviour** (`TileBehavior`, Platinum's own values; `docs/tile-behaviours.md`), which on a hand-made map follows from the type: tall grass has wild Pokémon, water needs Surf, a ledge is hopped the way it faces, ice is slid on, snow and marsh slow a walker (`Map.BehaviourAt`). Code can say more for a tile with `Map.SetBehaviour`, and give the map heights with `SetHeight` and `SetDeck`, as the harness's terrain lab does. A map file says the same with `behaviours` (rectangles of tiles given a behaviour: `{ "x": 3, "y": 3, "depth": 3, "behaviour": "BlockWest" }`, `width` and `depth` 1 when left out) and `heights` (the original's height plates, as the world files hold them, laid by `MapFile.LayHeights`: each tile stands on the plate under its middle, a plate at least three quarters of a tile above another over the same tile is a deck, and a tile no plate covers, a wall or a block of rock, takes the highest height met looking along each of the four ways past other such tiles). A person's `level` is the height they stand at when it isn't the ground's (someone on a deck). A person's `stance` (`Sit`, `SitGround`, `Lie` or `Stand`, plan 11 · C7) is how they are set where the furniture under them doesn't decide it: someone on a bench, a chair or a sofa sits, on a cushion sits on the ground and on a bed lies without being told (`Map.StanceOf`); an overlay's person takes the same field. The Oreburgh Gym is the first room with all three (plan 01 · M9 1b).

**Battle arenas** (plan 04 · G8): outdoors, the ground under the player picks the stage a battle is fought on: water (surfing), sand, snow and cave floor tiles give the `Water`, `Sand`, `Snow` and `Cave` stages, paving where the area's header names a `City` background the `City` stage (a street, plan 22, wave 48), and anything else the map's own `battleArena`, or `Grass` (the meadow, with the map's `trees` behind it and a lake if the map has one). Rooms are `Indoors` unless they name another. `Forest` is for maps deep in the trees; `Gym` and `League` are halls themed on `arenaType` (a `League` room without one is the Champion's); a gym or League map keeps its hall whatever the floor.

**Buildings** of a hand-made map are not listed: a block of roof (`r`, `b`, `g`), wall and door tiles is one building, its bottom row the front wall (doors, and signposts set into it as name plates). What kind it is follows from where its door leads: a map whose name ends in `PokemonCenter` or `PokeMart`, or `RowanLab`; anything else is a house, built in the map's `architecture` (one of the fifteen towns' ways of building in the style guide: `Timber`, `Plaster`, `City`, `Clapboard`, `Brick`, `Cottage`, `HalfTimber`, `Townhouse`, `Farm`, `Stone`, `Marsh`, `Harbour`, `Snow`, `Seaside`, `Resort`). Where that isn't enough (a school, an office, a building with no door yet), `buildings` names the kind for the building that covers the given tile: `School`, `Office`, `TvStation`, `Terminal`, `Apartments`, or any other `BuildingKind`. The roof tile's colour is the roof's colour for houses. (On a map of the imported world a building is placed by its model instead: see "From models to buildings" below.)

Who the player is isn't known when a map is written: the name and the look are chosen in the introduction. So a line of `dialog`, a person's `name` and a map's `displayName` say `{player}` for the player's name and `{assistant}` for the name of the character the player didn't choose (Dawn, or Lucas), and the game fills them in when the text is shown (`PlayerIdentity.Fill`); never write either name out. The `npcType` `Assistant` is drawn as that other character and `Player` as the player's own. A test reads every map for names written out and for placeholders the game doesn't know.

An NPC has `name`, `npcType` (picks the character model), `x`, `y`, `facing` and optional `dialog` lines, plus whichever of `isHealingNurse`, `isPokeMartClerk` (with `mart`, a counter of `marts.json`'s `specialties`, for a clerk who sells a town's own goods), `isPCTerminal`, `isStarterBriefcase` and `isTransportAttendant` (takes the player to the next region, see `RegionDatabase`) apply. Give it an `id` only when something else refers to it (trainers, the starter briefcase, a script that names the person); the others get a fresh one each load. `movement` (the original's movement type without its prefix, `wander_around`, `look_around`, `look_north_and_east`) with `rangeX` and `rangeZ` (left out when nought) moves a person about as the world's people move (`PersonMovement.Parse`, plan 02 · S6): the Hearthome Gym's trainers wander, Veilstone's black belts look about.

What a person does when spoken to is a script (`docs/scripts.md`). Left to themselves they run the common one for what they are: the nurse's for `isHealingNurse`, the clerk's, the PC's, the briefcase's, the attendant's, a trainer's, or plain talk for someone with `dialog`. `script` names one of their own instead, looked for in the map's script file first (`scripts/<map name>.txt`) and among the common ones second: Mom heals with `"script": "Healer"`, which says her `dialog` rather than the nurse's lines. `hiddenBy` names a story flag that takes the person off the map while it is set, and `shownBy` one they wait for; whoever is off the map is neither drawn nor in the way. A signboard may carry a `script` too (its `text` is then what the script's `sayown` says), and `triggers` are rectangles of tiles that start a script when the player steps into them: every time, or only while the story `variable` has the `value`.

**Pokémon in the field.** A Pokémon standing on the map (plan 10 · F1: the mine's Machop) is a person of the map whose `npcType` is `Pokemon`, with the `species` it is (a species of `species.json`, or a form by its name: `Giratina-Origin`). It is drawn as its species' field sprite (style guide, "Pokémon in the field"), stands in the way like a person, and runs its `script` when spoken to, or the common `Pokemon`: its cry, then its `dialog`. A file whose Pokémon names no species, or whose `species` is on anyone else, doesn't load.

**Items on the ground.** An item to pick up is a person of the map whose `npcType` is `ItemBall`, with the `item` it holds (a name from `items.json`), a `count` when it is more than one, and a `hiddenBy` flag of its own: picking it up sets that flag, which is what takes the ball off the map and keeps it off in every game saved afterwards. A file whose ball has no flag, or an item that isn't one, doesn't load. `hiddenItems` are items nobody can see, found by looking at their tile: the tile, the `item`, a `count` when more than one, and the `flag` set once it has been found. In the imported world nothing of this is written by hand: the balls and the hidden items of an open area are there by themselves, from its area file.

**Furniture and tile scripts** (plan 08 · P8). A room's `Television`, `Bookshelf` and `TrashCan` props say something when looked at with nothing written for them (`common.Television`, `Bookshelf`, `TrashCan`; `FieldScripts.ForFurniture`), and so do the tiles of the imported games' rooms whose behaviour is a television, a bookshelf, a bin or a map on a wall. A piece that should say something of its own takes a signboard with a `script`, or a `tileScripts` entry: the tile (inside the room) and the script it runs, as an overlay's `read` gives a tile of the world one. Either goes before the furniture's common script.

A trainer carries a `trainer` block. On a hand-made map it says everything; in an overlay of the imported world it is the lines alone (plan 08 · P7, below):

```json
"trainer": {
  "id": "youngster_tristan",
  "name": "Tristan",
  "trainerClass": "Youngster",
  "party": [ { "species": "Starly", "level": 5 }, { "species": "Burmy", "level": 5, "moves": [ "Tackle" ] } ],
  "prizeMoney": 80,
  "dialogueBefore": "…",
  "dialogueAfter": "…",
  "sightRange": 3,
  "doubleBattle": true
}
```

A trainer whose `id` is one of Platinum's in `trainers.json` (`youngster_darius`) and whose `party` is empty takes its team, its mind, its items and its prize money from there, so a room's Gym trainers write only their id and their lines (plan 02 · S5). The trainer's `id` is what the save file records once they are beaten, so don't change it for a trainer who is already in the game. Party Pokémon are rolled fresh (gender, nature, moves for their level) every time the maps load; `moves` is for a Pokémon whose trainer chose them (Platinum's own trainer data does for some) and is left out otherwise. `prizeMoney` is Platinum's: the level of the last Pokémon, four times over, by the rate of the trainer's class (`include/data/trainer_class_prize_mul.h` in the decompilation), and twice that for a double battle. `doubleBattle` (left out when false) sends two Pokémon out at a time, when the player has two that can fight; two people may carry the same trainer with the same `id` (a pair of twins), and beating one beats both. 

In an overlay of the imported world (plan 08 · P7) the block is the trainer's lines and nothing else: `{ "dialogueBefore": "…", "dialogueAfter": "…" }`. Who the trainer is (the `id`, which is the table's: `youngster_tristan`; the name, the class, the team, the items, the prize money and whether they battle two at a time) is `trainers.json`'s alone, found by the trainer the area file's object names as its script (`TRAINER_YOUNGSTER_TRISTAN`), and how far they see is the object's `sight` (none: they battle only when spoken to or by a script). An `id` is given only for someone a script battles whose object names no trainer (Jubilife City's grunts: `galactic_grunt_jubilife_city_1`). `OverlayTrainerTests` holds that no overlay writes anything else and that every trainer of the world is the table's.

`MapFile.FromMap` turns a `Map` back into a file, and `DataFileTests` checks that every map file loads and writes back out unchanged, so a generated file must use the same layout `GameDataFiles.Serialize` writes.

## World files

The imported Sinnoh (plan 01) is stored differently from the hand-made maps above: as **chunks** of 32 by 32 tiles, **matrices** that lay chunks out in a grid, and **areas** that say what a part of a grid is and what stands on it. `tools/MapImporter` writes all three from the Platinum decompilation; the records are in `Data/WorldFiles.cs`. A full run writes every one of them to `tools/MapImporter/out/world/` (8.7 MB, not checked in), to look at.

What the game loads is in `PokemonPlatinumEngine/Data/world/sinnoh/`:

| File | Written by | |
| --- | --- | --- |

| `matrices/NNN.json`, `chunks/NNN.json`, `areas/<key>.json` | `dotnet run --project tools/MapImporter -- --data` | Only what `world.json` asks for: the matrices of its maps, the chunks of its open areas and of every chunk next to one (in view from its edge), and the open areas. A re-run reproduces the files exactly and removes those no longer needed; it never touches the two below. |
| `overlays/<key>.json` | hand | What the import can't or mustn't bring for an open area: its music, where its doors lead, who its people are and what they say |

A GBA region's folder (plan 18 · W7; `Data/world/kanto/`, `Data/world/hoenn/` once plans 19 and 21 make them) is the same, written by `dotnet run --project tools/MapImporter -- --game firered --data` (or `emerald`) by the region's own `world.json`, and holds two files more: `trainers.json`, the region's trainers in the shape of `Data/trainers.json` (decision 8; the GBA's prize money and personalities are left at nought, from tables the region's plan adds), and `spawns.json`, its heal locations in the shape of a region file's `spawns`, to copy there. The run writes nothing until a region has its `world.json`.

Johto's folder (plan 20 · J1; `Data/world/johto/`) is written by `dotnet run --project tools/MapImporter -- --game heartgold --data` from HeartGold's maps, by its own `world.json` (which opens nothing yet), and holds beside it `trainers.json` (HeartGold's 737 trainers in the shape of `Data/trainers.json`, each id after the prefix `johto_` as Kanto's take `kanto_`: ids are unique across every region's file; plan 18's decision 8; prize money and personalities at nought, the trainers' lines never read), `phone.json` (the Pokégear's 75 contacts: `id`, `type`, `class`, `trainer` by the region's trainer ids, `area` by key, `gift`, `rematchDay` and `rematchTime`, each left out where there is none) and `habitats.json` (every Johto area with a table, its grass by Morning, Day and Night, in HeartGold's version). Johto's area keys are HeartGold's map constants without `MAP_` (`new_bark`, `route_29`). The import writes HeartGold's behaviours as this game's by name (`tools/MapImporter/Hg/HgBehaviours.cs`): Headbutt stays at `0x06`, Whirlpool at `0x11`, Stop Sliding at `0x4D`, and magma and the three ladders, which clash with Platinum's values, are `0xE6` to `0xE9`, none of them named in `TileBehavior` until plan 20 · J2 gives them their rules. A trainer of the phone book who battles again has the teams HeartGold numbers after their first as `rematches` (plan 20 · J3: `johto_youngster_joey_2` to `_4`), and an area with grass has `hoennSounds` and `sinnohSounds`, the two species each of the radio's sounds puts in the grass's slots 2 and 3, and 4 and 5. Hoenn's folder (plan 21) has the same `phone.json` written by hand for the PokéNav's Match Call (plan 21 · H2, part b: `id`, `type`, `class`, `area`) and a `townmap.json` made from its overworld's matrix, a town on its own cell where a route took its chunk (the PokéNav's map page). Written by hand beside Johto's: `townmap.json` (the Pokégear's map card, plan 20 · J3: a block for each of Johto's areas on the cells of HeartGold's overworld its chunks take, Kanto's half of the grid out of view) and `radio.json` (what each of the radio's stations says, our own lines by `RadioStation`'s name; `{species}` and `{place}` fill Professor Oak's talk).

So opening an area is: add its key to `world.json`, run the importer with `--data`, write its overlay. `WorldTests` then checks that every chunk in view has its file, that every entry of the overlay points at something that exists, and that the area can be walked into and out of.

An overlay and a room are held to what this document says by `tools/ContentCheck` (plan 16 · T8; its README lists the checks): `dotnet run --project tools/ContentCheck` names, a line each, whatever an overlay refers to that its area hasn't, a door to no map or onto a blocked tile, a field these formats don't have (the reader skips one without a word, so `"dialogue"` for `"dialog"` leaves someone silent), a script, a look or a flag that isn't there, and a line that runs out of the text box's three rows or holds a character the interface can't draw. `--plan <out dir> <Room or area key>` draws the room or the area from above with everything the file places tagged and named, to hold the file against the picture.

Lengths are in tiles and heights too (one tile is 16 of the original's units). x runs east and z south; a chunk's own coordinates start at its north-west corner.

### `world.json`

```jsonc
{
  "region": "Sinnoh",
  "maps": [
    { "name": "Sinnoh", "matrix": 0, "trees": "Pine" },
    { "name": "LakeVerity", "matrix": 102, "area": "lake_verity", "trees": "Pine" },
    { "name": "OreburghGate1F", "matrix": 3, "area": "oreburgh_gate_1f", "setting": "Cave" }
  ],
  "areas": [ "twinleaf_town", "route_201", "verity_lakefront", "lake_verity", "sandgem_town", "route_202", "route_219", "jubilife_city", "route_203", "oreburgh_gate_1f" ],
  "hidden": [ { "var": "VAR_HIDDEN_LOCATION_SPRING_PATH", "value": 786, "chunkX": 23, "chunkY": 21, "chunksWide": 2, "chunksHigh": 2 } ],
  "swaps": [ { "var": "VAR_HIDDEN_LOCATION_SEABREAK_PATH", "value": 4136, "cells": [ { "chunkX": 28, "chunkY": 15, "chunk": 119 } ] } ]
}
```

Each entry of `maps` becomes one `Map` named `name`, as large as its matrix (matrix 0, the overworld, is 30 by 30 chunks: 960 by 960 tiles), so a position on it is a tile of the whole matrix and never changes when more of the region opens. `area` names the area of a matrix that has none per chunk (a lake, a cave); `trees` is the kind of tree where an area's overlay names none. `setting` is `Outdoors` when left out, `Cave` ("Caves" below) or `Void` ("The Distortion World" below). A map of one area takes that area's camera from its header (`Cave`, `ZoomedIn`; `FieldCamera`) and is dark if its header's weather says so. `areas` lists the areas that are **open**: their people, signs, doors and wild Pokémon are in the game and their ground can be walked on. Every other area is scenery: its chunks, where the game has them, are drawn and solid; where it has none there is forest. `hidden` lists places of the overworld the story reveals (plan 01 · M8, the original's `MapMatrix_RevealSpringPath`): a block of chunks of matrix 0 that is forest, as if it weren't there, until the variable `var` holds `value`, after which its chunks are in the map and drawn again (`Map.AddHiddenPlace`, `MapDatabase.ApplyHiddenPlaces`). `swaps` lists chunks the story swaps for others (plan 01 · M10, part b, the original's `MapMatrix_RevealSeabreakPath`): while `var` holds `value`, each of the `cells` of a map's matrix (`map`, the overworld's when left out) shows the chunk it names instead of the matrix's own. The map is built a second time round those cells with the other chunks (`WorldMapBuilder.BuildSwapped`: their ground, heights and models, and what the area's events do to tiles) and keeps both grounds (`Map.AddSwappedPlace`); `MapDatabase.ApplySwappedPlaces` lays down the one the story is at. The areas and their people, items and signs are the same either way, so whoever stands in a swapped cell must stand on ground that is open in both; a swap of chunks a building of the world's models stands on is refused. The importer's `--data` ships the chunks swapped in. A swap may name a `flag` in place of `var` and `value` (plan 19 · K6, part b): its chunks lie in their cells while the flag is set (the Seafoam Islands' calm water, whose chunks the GBA reader writes after every matrix's from the floor's other layout, `GbaWorld.Alternates`).

A bridge over the sea whose import is its deck and nothing round it (plan 22 · U6, part b: the Driftveil Drawbridge) says how far the sea lies under it: `"seaBelow": 5`. Every blocked tile that isn't the deck's planks becomes the sea that many tiles down, every other tile a deck over it (`WorldMapBuilder.DeckOverTheSea`), and the four columns either side of a way in stay ground at the deck's height; the original's models there are left out, and whoever its area file stands on the deck stands on it (style guide, "Relief", "A bridge over the sea").

A cave nothing leads into names the tiles it is come to by (plan 06 · R16a: the Underground, which only the Explorer Kit leads into), since a cave's floor is found by walking in from its ways in: `"arrivals": [[15, 23]]` lists tiles of the map, and `"arrivalsInEveryChunk": [[8, 8], [23, 8], [8, 23], [23, 23]]` tiles of every one of its chunks (the four that whoever digs down lands on; the tunnels are six systems that don't join, so each needs one).

### `overlays/<key>.json`

- `doors` and `locked` refer to the area's `warps` by their number, counting from 0. A warp whose other side is an open area needs neither: it leads there by itself, and whoever takes it is put down one step out of the warp at the far end (`WorldMapBuilder.Arrival`). A `door` sends one to a hand-made map instead (a room) and says where in it. A `locked` warp leads nowhere yet: a house door stays shut, a wall with a door painted on it. A warp may be both a `door` and `locked`: shut until the story opens it, it says where it will lead (the Valley Windworks', locked from inside until the Works Key). Any other warp whose far side isn't in the game is left out without a word, but a *door* that is neither open nor listed fails `WorldTests.ADoorIsOpenOrListedAsLocked`. A way in that is no door (a cave's mouth, a gate house, a hall's porch) and leads nowhere is closed: its tile is blocked, so nobody walks into an opening and stands in the wall.
- A gate house is a room like any other since plan 01 · M11, part d: the warps into it are `doors`, and the room's own warps lead out to either side. (Until then an overlay could pass it through, `through`, to the warp on its far side; no overlay does any more, and the builder no longer reads it.)
- More than two ways may share a tile (plan 02 · S15, part c: Mt. Coronet's way out onto its summit is four, to Spear Pillar as it stands, the torn pillar, Dialga's and Palkia's). A way that is there `from` a flag takes the tile from one that is only there `until` another (`Map.GetWarpAt`), so the plain way keeps its one flag and each further way has a flag of its own, which the place's arrival script sets and clears as the original's moves its warps.
- `switches` (plan 02 · S10) are warps that are there for part of the story only. Where the original keeps two warps on the same tiles and moves one away by script (the way into a lake that is one map before Team Galactic comes and another after: Valor Lakefront's, Verity Lakefront's, Valor Cavern's way out, and Acuity Lakefront's since plan 02 · S11), each says the flag it is there `until` or `from` (`Warp.Until`, `Warp.From`); `Map.ApplyDoors` shuts the one the story isn't at whenever a flag changes (`Warp.Shut`), `Map.GetWarpAt` gives the one that is open, and `Map.WarpsAt` both.
- `heldBack` leaves objects of the area file out for now, by id: an item on a ledge reached only through a place that isn't open yet (Solaceon's Rare Candy, over the ruins, is reached through Maniac Tunnel, which plan 02's Ruin Maniac digs), or someone the imported collision closes in. Their overlay entries (lines, a trainer) may stay, ready for when they come back.
- `national` (plan 03 · D12a) is the area's wild Pokémon of our own once the National Pokédex is had: `"land"`, two species for the grass's slots 8 and 9 (where the original put a second game in the console's species; 4 in a hundred each, at the slots' own levels), and `"superRod"`, one for the Super Rod's slot 3 (`{ "land": [ "Stunky", "Skuntank" ], "superRod": [ "Clamperl" ] }`, Route 221's). They go in the places Diamond and Pearl have the species. The content check holds the species and the counts; `special.json` tells the Pokédex's area page of them.
- `ours` (plan 03 · D15, design B) is the area's species of our own past level 100, met once the region's Hall of Fame is entered (Sinnoh's once Kanto's is; `OursSlots.GateFlag`): `"land"`, up to two for the grass's slots 10 and 11, `"surf"`, one for the water's slot 4, and `"superRod"`, one for the Super Rod's slot 4 (1 in 100 each), each written `[species, lowest level, highest level]` at its own levels (its evolution level to four above), which the region's levels never map (`{ "land": [ ["Ratchisel", 105, 109] ], "superRod": [ ["Medusarch", 115, 119] ] }`). Only a species of ours of the region's own batch that is no legendary set's end may stand there (the content check holds it), and the Pokédex's area page shows it as a way of its own.
- `alphas` (plan 10 · F10) is the area's alphas, met once the region's Hall of Fame is entered: `"land"`, up to three `[species, lowest level, highest level]`, species of the games, each at levels above the area's grass (`{ "land": [ ["Machamp", 55, 57] ] }`). One step in a hundred through the area's tall grass (and a Sweet Scent drawn there) meets one, larger by half, with three perfect IVs and a move ahead of its level (`Overworld/Alphas.cs`). Only the post-game zone has any (Routes 225 to 227); none of Platinum's own areas lists one (`CompanionAlphaTests`).
- `exits` are warps of our own: where the open world ends at a hand-made map (none is, today: Jubilife City, which Route 202 led onto until plan 01 · M5, is part of the map of Sinnoh), or where the original moves the player with a script that has no counterpart yet. Iron Island's lifts are two each (plan 01 · M7): until a lift's platform moves, stepping onto its top or its foot is a warp to the other level of the same map, through a fade. An exit may say `"openedBy": "FLAG_…"` (plan 39 · CR1: Sunyshore City's workshop, whose way is a dead end of the town's alley, the original's two unused doors there standing inside blocked ground): its tile is a wall, like a locked door's, until a script sets the flag (`Warp.OpenedBy`, `Map.ApplyDoors`).
- `people` is keyed by the `id` of an object in the area's file. Only the people listed appear; each takes its place and facing from the import, its character from the object's `looks` (`WorldMapBuilder.CharacterFor`, or `npcType` to choose; looks that name a Pokémon, `machop` or `giratina_origin`, make a Pokémon of the map of that species, `WorldMapBuilder.SpeciesFor`, unless the person is a trainer or given an `npcType`), and its name, `dialog`, `trainer` block or `isStarterBriefcase` from here. Give `id` when something refers to the person. The original's script text is never imported: every line is ours. A trainer sees as far as the object's `sight` in the area's file says (0: they battle when spoken to). The two halves of a route list each other's people and signs near the border (Route 204's boys by the Ravaged Path); each is placed once, by the half it stands in, so give it its line in that half's overlay.
- A person stands where the import has them, unless the overlay says otherwise: `"at": [72, 104]` is the tile, where the original itself stands them elsewhere than its map's file does (plan 06 · R16a: the Underground's hikers, whom the original places by a table of its code as the map is come to).
- Someone the overlay makes a Pokémon by `"npcType": "Pokemon"`, where the original gives the object its looks by script, names the `"species"` it stands as until a script dresses it (plan 06 · R16c: Amity Square's `follower_mon`, which is the Pokémon of the team let out of its ball there and is off the map behind its flag otherwise). An object whose looks are a species needs neither (`WorldMapBuilder.SpeciesFor`).
- Someone or something the original lays on furniture has a `"level"`, how far over the floor under it it stands in tiles (plan 19 · K3: the three Poké Balls on Oak's table at 1.45, the height the room draws its desk at), drawn there and spoken to from the floor as anyone is.
- `"name"` at the top of an overlay is what the place is called, where the original gives its map no name of its own (`"name": "The Underground"`); left out, it is the area file's.
- `signs` gives the text of the area's signposts and mailboxes, by the same ids. A signpost without one reads the area's name. In a GBA game's world (plan 19 · K1: Kanto) a sign is a sign event of the area's file, not an object: `signs` names it by the script it runs (`PalletTown_EventScript_TownSign`), every tile of the event reads the words, and a blocked tile of the open country that does nothing stands as a signpost; in a room the tile stays what the room has there (a bookshelf, a PC). An overlay's person may also be `isHealingNurse` (Kanto's Mom, whose house is a room of the import with no map file to say so): a white-out wakes the player beside them.
- `climate` (plan 01 · M10, part b) gives a place a light of its own beyond the hour and the weather: `Volcanic` (Stark Mountain, in the open and inside) or `Frozen` (Snowpoint Temple). It is light and grade alone (`ArtLook.InClimate`); the ground is drawn as everywhere.
- `callsForHelp` (plan 14 · B5) makes every wild Pokémon met alone there an SOS battle's (`BattleFormat.Sos`): it may call for help, and an ally answers into the empty place beside it ("SOS allies", below). Under rules without calls (Platinum's) the battle is a single. No overlay of Sinnoh sets it (plan 14, decision 1); plan 23 · Z6's totems are its first use.
- `props` are things of ours laid down on the area: street furniture a town lacks, or what the original builds into the ground's own model and only blocks (the Battle Frontier's booths, the model of its grounds, its statues). They are laid down after the people, and someone kept `still` inside one keeps their tile (a booth's attendant).
- A person may have a `script` of the area's own file (`scripts/<key>.txt`, see `docs/scripts.md`) in place of `dialog`: `"clown_1": { "name": "Clown", "script": "Clown1" }`. Scripts call the area's people by their ids here. Whoever the area file hides by a flag (`hiddenBy`) is hidden by that flag in the game without the overlay saying so; `"hiddenBy": ""` keeps someone whatever the flag, another flag's name replaces it, and `shownBy` names a flag they wait for.
- `signScripts` gives a signpost a script instead of plain reading (a signpost object, or a sign event of the area by its script's name, whose words the script still has as its own: Ilex Forest's shrine, plan 29 · LS11b), and `triggers` gives the area's own triggers their scripts by their number in the area's file, counting from 0. The tiles, the variable and its value stay the original's (`triggers` in `areas/<key>.json`); a trigger not listed does nothing. A trigger on the tile in front of a warp also starts as the player comes out of the warp onto it, as the original's step off a door's mat does, and one on a warp's own tile goes before the warp (plan 02 · S6).
- `read` gives the area's things that are read where they stand (the original's background events of its own scripts, `signs` of type 0 in the area's file: the Valley Windworks' door, Eterna City's statue) a script of the area's file, by the original's script number (`Map.TileScripts`). Facing the tile and pressing the button runs it.
- `zones` (plan 21 · H4) are stretches of the area, rectangles of tiles of its matrix, with weather (by the header's names: `Sandstorm`, `SlowAshfall`) or a song of their own, where the original sets them by script by the player's row or column (Route 111's desert, Route 113's ash): the first zone that holds a tile wins, and the music changes as a step crosses a zone's edge (`MapArea.ZoneAt`, `Map.WeatherAt`, `BgmTrackAt`). A zone with `while` a story flag holds only while the flag is set (plan 21 · H8, part b: Emerald's abnormal weather over the eastern sea, `"weather": "Abnormal", "while": "FLAG_SYS_WEATHER_CTRL"`; `Abnormal` is the downpour and the drought by turns, and `Drought` the drought alone).
- `diveTo` and `emergeTo` (plan 21 · H8, part b), each `{ "area": "underwater_sootopolis_city", "x": 9, "z": 6, "facing": "Up" }`, are where Dive goes down to from anywhere in the area and where coming up from anywhere on its floor comes up, where the original names one place by the map's own script (Emerald's `setdivewarp`) instead of a floor laid tile for tile under the sea (the area file's `dive` and `emerge`, which are asked first).
- `emergeAt` (plan 21 · H12, part a), a list of `{ "x": 12, "z": 44, "to": { "area": "sealed_chamber_outer_room", "x": 10, "z": 19, "facing": "Up" } }`, is the sea-floor tiles of the area that come up somewhere of their own and not at `emergeTo` (Emerald's Sealed Chamber corridor, whose `OnDive` script surfaces its last tile into the chamber above and the rest to Route 134). `braille` (the same part), a list of `{ "x": 10, "z": 3, "move": "Dig", "script": "DigThrough" }`, is the tiles a field move works a puzzle of the place on: chosen in the party menu on that tile, `Dig`, `Rock Smash` or `Flash` (whatever the place would say of it, the region's badge asked all the same) runs the area's own script in place of the move's usual one (`Map.BrailleSpots`, `FieldSpot.Braille`; Emerald's braille puzzles of the Sealed Chamber and the three Regis).
- `camera` (plan 22 · U6, part b), the field's camera by the names `WorldMapBuilder.CameraOf` knows (`Drawbridge`, `Cave`, `ZoomedIn`), for a place whose header names one this game has no reading of (Black 2's camera 9 over the Driftveil Drawbridge); left out, the area file's own.
- `holeTo` (plan 21 · H8, part c), a map's name (`"SkyPillar_3F"`), is where a cracked floor of the area (`CrackedFloor`) drops whoever walks onto it, onto the same tile (Emerald's `setholewarp`); left out, a cracked floor is walked like any other.
- `darkWater` (plan 21 · H12, part e), a list of rectangles each with a `while` flag (`{ "x": 10, "z": 291, "width": 2, "depth": 2, "while": "FLAG_HOENN_ABNORMAL_WEATHER_ROUTE_105_NORTH" }`), is sea that is dark water, which Dive goes down from, only while its flag is set (Emerald's abnormal weather lays it by setmetatile); a map fresh from the builder has it all, and `Map.ApplyDoors` follows the story.
- `readAt` (plan 02 · S12) gives a tile a script to be read by where the original has an object nobody sees answering for a place (a pit of the Distortion World looked into, Cynthia answering from the stone behind the player's), and `pits` names the tiles a pushed boulder falls into, each with the script that runs as the boulder comes to rest there (`Map.BoulderPits`; the boulder is the script's `self`). A boulder is pushed onto a pit whatever the tile is. On a map of nothing (`"setting": "Void"`) a pit's tile is made nothing itself, as deep as the void round the islands: it is the tile the original drops its boulder through, which the import knows only as blocked.
- A way that the story opens and that shares its tile with no other way (`switches` with one warp on the tile: the hole behind Mt. Coronet's cave painting, there `from` the flag that takes the painting away) is a wall until then: `Map.ApplyDoors` shuts the tile itself, and opens it with the flag.
- A person of `npcs` or `people` may stand at a `level` of their own, in tiles (`NPC.Level`): the height they are spoken to and drawn at where it isn't the ground's. Over nothing in the Distortion World that is the height they hang at (Giratina before the last stepping stone, a guardian beyond its pit); over an island's rim they stand on the rim. In a room's file a person's `level` on a tile the room's `solid` shuts is someone on a piece of furniture (the sailor's boy in his bed, `CanalaveCitySailorEldritchHouse`, plan 02 · S15, part b): nobody takes them for standing in a wall, they are spoken to from the open tile beside them, and a room without relief draws them on the floor inside the furniture's box.
- A door may wait for the story: `"openedBy": "FLAG_UNLOCKED_VALLEY_WINDWORKS_DOOR"` keeps its tile shut, a wall like a locked door, until a script sets the flag (`Warp.OpenedBy`, `Map.ApplyDoors`, plan 02 · S6); the door's own `read` script is what sets it.
- People move about as the area file says (plan 02 · S6): an object's `movement` with its `rangeX` and `rangeZ` is read by `PersonMovement.Parse` (those who look about turn, those who wander step within the box round where they stand, those who walk a loop go round it; `Overworld/Wandering.cs`). `"still": true` keeps a person standing where a scene of ours counts on finding them. Someone kept still whom the original stands inside a model that blocks the way (the swimmer in the Resort Area's pool, plan 01 · M10) keeps their tile open: they are what is in the way there, and in a pool they stand on its floor, in water to the chest (`NPC.Wading`).

### How a chunk becomes tiles

Every tile of a map of the world keeps the original's behaviour (`Map.BehaviourAt`), which is what the rules of walking go by (`FieldMovement`, `docs/tile-behaviours.md`), and its height. `WorldMapBuilder.Look` turns its cover, behaviour and blocked flag into what the game draws:

| In the world | Drawn as |
| --- | --- |
| `LedgeSouth`, `LedgeWest`, `LedgeEast` | `LedgeDown`, `LedgeLeft`, `LedgeRight`; the blocked corner pieces at a side ledge's south end are drawn as more of it |
| `Door` | `Door`, solid until a warp opens it |
| `River`, `Sea`, `Waterfall` | `Water`. Open water is not blocked: it is its behaviour that asks for Surf. A blocked tile of it is a rock standing in the water, and so is a `Boulder`-cover tile that stands in water at the water's level |
| `Ice` | `Ice` |
| `Grass`, `Flowers`, `TallGrass`, `Sand`, `CaveFloor`, `Snow`, `Rock`, `Marsh` | The tile of the same name |
| `Path` | `Path` |
| `Paving` | `Paving`: the slabs of a city |
| `Bridge`, `Steps` | `Planks`, `Stairs` |
| `Walkway` | `Walkway`: a raised deck of steel and solar panels, walked like a bridge |
| `Cliff` | `Rock`, solid: bare rock nobody walks on. It stands above the ground (below) |
| `Boulder` | `Dirt` with a `Boulder` standing on it, solid |
| `Fence` | `Grass` with a `Fence` on it, solid. One tile of it by itself in paving is a street lamp, on the paving (Jubilife City's lamps, whose texture the import takes for a fence) |
| `Lamp` | A `LampPost`, solid, on the ground of the tiles round it |
| `Building` | Whatever the model standing there makes of it (below) |
| `Tree`, `Broadleaf`, or anything blocked the importer couldn't name | `Tree`. An area with more broad-leaved trees than pines has round trees (`MapArea.Trees`) unless its overlay says otherwise. A chunk of an area that isn't open has the trees its own cover shows (`Map.SetChunkTrees`: the forest that fills the world between its places is one area, pines but round the Battle Zone), and a cell of the matrix with no chunk at all, which is forest too, round trees where every wooded chunk beside it has them (plan 01 · M10) |
| `CaveMouth` | `CaveMouth`: the dark in the mouth of a cave, at the level of the ground under the rock that stands round it. Blocked where it is the hole in the rock, open where it is the way in. Inside a cave it is rock or floor |
| Anything open it couldn't name | The ground of the tiles round it; lawn if they don't say |

**From models to buildings.** What stands on a chunk is its `props`: the original's models by their short names. `Data/WorldModels.cs` is the catalogue that says what each is here (`docs/world-models.md` lists those that stand outdoors or in a cave the game has opened), and `WorldMapBuilder.PlaceModels` puts it on the map:

- A **building** takes the tiles the world blocks under its model's box, in whichever chunks they lie. Where boxes overlap, a tile is the smaller model's. The tiles are cut into rectangles, the largest first (`WorldMapBuilder.Rectangles`): each rectangle two tiles or more each way is a block of the building (roof tiles, with a row of wall along its front), the block with the way in being the main one and the others its wings. Thin pieces on the row before the main block's front, beside a way in, are its **porch**; other thin pieces become a `Fence` or, in a town of brick or stone, a `LowWall`. A model whose catalogue entry says its thin pieces are conveyors (`WorldModel.Thin`: the mine's yard, the machine in the mine) has them laid as belts and gantries instead (`WorldMapBuilder.Conveyors`: a straight run of three or more carries a belt; two pieces across one open tile are a gantry, and the belt runs through it to whatever blocked thing lies within eight tiles either way).
- Its **doors** are where the world has a `Door` tile or a warp: in the front wall, in the porch's front, or on open ground between a porch's two sides (the door is then in the wall behind). A gate house on a road that runs east and west has them in its ends (`BuildingInfo.SideDoors`).
- Its **kind**, the **town** it is built like, its **storeys** (0 goes by the model's height) and the **name** over its door come from the catalogue; a Gym takes its leader's type from the area it stands in (`WorldModels.GymTheme`). A model the catalogue doesn't know is a house if it is as large as one. The result is the map's `PlacedBuildings`, which `MapStructures.BuildingsOf` returns as it returns the buildings found on a hand-made map.
- A **prop** (a fountain, a ship, a wind turbine) covers the tiles whose middle lies in its model's box; a ship leaves out a row or column of them that lies on a pier's planks (the Fight Area's ferry, whose box reaches over the pier its sailor stands on), and a swimming pool's tiles are paving from edge to edge. The world's own data says which of them block the way (a turbine's blades turn over grass one can walk through); those show the ground of the tiles round them, made or bare ground before lawn, and a ship's are water.
- An area's fences and walls are built like the houses that stand in it, unless its overlay names an `architecture`.

**Heights.** A tile's height is the plate under its middle, plus the altitude its matrix gives the chunk (in half tiles); stairs and ramps keep the plate's slope. Where two plates lie one over the other at a bridge, the lower is the ground and the upper the bridge's deck (`Map.DeckAt`), and whoever comes onto the tile stays on the one nearer their own height. Sinnoh's lowlands are at one tile (`WorldMapBuilder.GroundLevel`, which the field draws at zero) and its water half a tile lower. A step of 1.25 tiles or more can't be walked, which is what makes a cliff.

**Rock that stands up.** The plates say how high the ground is for whoever walks on it; rock nobody walks on (a blocked `Rock` tile: a mountainside, a sea cliff) has no height of its own in them and would lie as flat as the road beside it. `WorldMapBuilder.RaiseRock` stands it up: each such tile takes the height of the nearest ground that isn't rock, plus two tiles (`CaveWallRise`), or three quarters of a tile (`CaveLipRise`) where open ground lies right behind it to the north, so it hides no more than the feet of whoever walks there. The mouth of a cave stays at the level of the ground, under the rock.

**Caves.** A map whose entry says `"setting": "Cave"` is rock where the open country is forest. Whatever blocks the way without being water or part of a model is `CaveWall`, and so is everything that can't be come to from one of the map's warps: the space past the walls, which the original leaves open and empty, and any pocket of floor the walls close in. Open ground the import couldn't name is `CaveFloor` where it can be reached. The rock stands above the floor by the same rule as outdoors. The field looks at such a map with the original's camera for caves and lights it without the clock (`ArtLook.CaveFieldRig`); no weather comes in but fog. A dark cave (`Map.IsDark`, from a header whose weather is `DarkFlash`) shows only a circle round the player until Flash is used there (`Darkness`; plan 02 · S2).

**Rooms built from a layout** (plan 18 · W7). A map whose entry says `"setting": "Indoors"` (a GBA game's house, which keeps its rooms as maps of their own and not as models) is wall where no chunk says otherwise; within its chunks, blocked ground the import couldn't name is `Wall` and open ground `Floor`. Sinnoh's rooms are map files instead, and such a room has no `interior` look of its own yet: the region plan that opens one lays its look out. **Buildings found at their doors**: a `world.json` that says `"buildingsAtDoors": true` (the GBA writer's; left out for Sinnoh, whose buildings are its models) has the builder find each building of its open country at a warp on a tile of the behaviour `Door` (`WorldMapBuilder.BuildingsAtDoors`): the blocked ground nobody named in the door's row, as wide as the row behind it, and the rows behind as long as each is about as wide (at most 16 by 12), its kind what the door leads into (`MapStructures.KindOfDoor`: a Pokémon Center, a Mart, a Gym, a lab, else a house).

**The Distortion World.** A map whose entry says `"setting": "Void"` (each floor of the Distortion World, plan 01 · M8) is islands over nothing where the open country is forest. What blocks the way without being rock, water, grass or a tree is `Void` (`TileType.Void`, blocked, with no ground and no face drawn), and so is a gap the original jumps across (its behaviours `0x5A` to `0x5D`, jumped the way they face to the third tile on: `FieldMovement.LongJumpDirection`); open ground the import couldn't name, and the original's marks on its floors, are the islands' stone (`DistortionGround`), the tile of every warp on it a slab (`DistortionSlab`), and the tile of every pit an overlay names nothing, let into the island (plan 02 · S12). Water that reaches the map's edge is a sea under the islands (B3F's), one level under the lowest of them; the void lies three tiles under the island nearest it (`WorldMapBuilder.VoidDrop`), so each island shows its underside, and the rock that rims an island stands three quarters of a tile above it. The field lights such a map without the clock (`ArtLook.VoidFieldRig`), with no weather and no sky. Past its edge is nothing too (`GroundBaker.TypeAt`).

The original keeps two things of the Distortion World outside its map files, in `res/prebuilt/fielddata/tornworld/tw_arc.narc` and `tw_arc_attr.narc`, and the importer reads both (`tools/MapImporter/DistortionWorld.cs`): where each floor lies in the Distortion World's own space, which its events are given in (subtracted from every warp, person, sign and trigger of its area, so they are in tiles of the floor's own map), and the floors that float over a floor's own ground (the stones of B2F's upper level), which it writes into the chunks as open tiles with the original's behaviours and a plate at their height wherever the ground under them is blocked. Its walls and ceilings, walked with sideways gravity, are not kept. Where the original carries the player between floors or across a gap by a moving platform or a script, the floors' overlays join them with `exits` (warps of their own), each named in a comment after the table it was read from.

**Obstacles.** Objects of an open area whose looks are `cut_tree`, `rock_smash` or `strength_boulder` become obstacles without the overlay naming them (plan 02 · S2): things of the map (`npcType` `CutTree`, `CrackedRock`, `StrengthBoulder`; `Map.AddObstacle`), drawn as cards, in the way like a person, that run their common script when faced (`docs/scripts.md`). Each keeps its id and the original's flag that hides it once cleared, made its area's own (`FLAG_MAP_LOCAL_HIDE_OBSTACLE_1_ETERNA_CITY`): a local flag, cleared when the player leaves, so a tree that was cut is back on the next visit, and a boulder that was pushed is put back where it stood (`Map.ResetObstacles`).

**Things the story leaves.** Two more things of the map are named by an overlay's person or `npcs` entry (plan 02 · S12), drawn as cards and in the way like an obstacle, which no field move clears: `"npcType": "Rubble"`, a heap of broken stone (the shards of Mt. Coronet's cave painting), and `"npcType": "CavePainting"`, the painting itself, a slab that stands on the tile of the way it shuts and is tall enough to cover the dark behind it. Each takes its place and its `hiddenBy` flag from the area's object and runs its `script` when faced. A boulder of our own is an `npcs` entry of `npcType` `StrengthBoulder` with flags of the story's (the Distortion World's three, which are where the puzzle's flags say from one visit to the next, where an imported boulder's flag is its area's local one). Two more came with Stark Mountain (plan 02 · S15, part b): `"npcType": "MagmaStone"`, the dark stone veined with fire at the head of the mountain's heart, and `"npcType": "FalseRock"`, a cracked rock that is no rock (Looker in hiding), drawn as a rock to be smashed and cleared by nothing. The original lays both out with the looks of a Strength boulder and a cracked rock, which an area's file would make obstacles of: such an object is named in the overlay's `heldBack`, and an `npcs` entry of the story's type stands on its tile with the original's flag (`{ "id": "magma_stone", "name": "Magma Stone", "npcType": "MagmaStone", "x": 7, "y": 8, "script": "MagmaStone", "hiddenBy": "FLAG_HIDE_STARK_MOUNTAIN_ROOM_3_MAGMA_STONE" }`).

### `chunks/NNN.json`

```json
{
  "id": 0,
  "behaviours": [ "0000…", … ],
  "solid": [ "####…", … ],
  "cover": [ "TTTT…", … ],
  "heights": [
    { "x": 0, "z": 0, "width": 32, "depth": 27, "height": 1, "slopeX": 0, "slopeZ": 0 },
    { "x": 12, "z": 27, "width": 8, "depth": 5, "height": 0.5, "slopeX": 0, "slopeZ": 0 }
  ],
  "props": [
    { "model": 22, "name": "t1_h01", "x": 21, "y": 1, "z": 10.5, "boxX": 19, "boxZ": 9.125, "width": 4.1875, "depth": 2.75, "height": 4.4375 }
  ]
}
```

(From chunk 0, Twinleaf Town: its ground is one tile up, its pond half a tile, and `t1_h01` is one of its two small houses.)

- `behaviours`: 32 rows of 64 characters, two hexadecimal digits per tile: what the tile does, a `TileBehavior` value. `docs/tile-behaviours.md` lists them.
- `solid`: 32 rows of 32 characters: `#` blocked, `.` open.
- `cover`: 32 rows of 32 characters: what the tile looks like, one code of the table below.
- `heights`: rectangles of ground ("plates"). The ground is `height` at the rectangle's north-west corner and rises by `slopeX` per tile eastward and `slopeZ` per tile southward. Where plates overlap, as under a bridge, a walker stays on the one nearest their own height (`WorldChunkFile.HeightAt`). A step of 1.25 tiles or more up or down can't be walked.
- `props`: what stands on the chunk. `model` and `name` are the original's id and short name for it (`pc` is a Pokémon Center, `fs` a Mart, `t1_h01` a Twinleaf house), by which the game chooses what to build there ("From models to buildings" above); `x`, `y`, `z` is its origin, and `boxX`, `boxZ`, `width`, `depth`, `height` the space it takes. In a GBA world's room (plan 19 · K2) a prop named `gba:<PropType>` (`gba:Table`, `gba:Bookshelf`) is a piece of furniture the importer's interior table read from the room's metatiles, laid by `WorldMapBuilder.Furnish` in its box.
- `areas` (plan 18 · W6; left out on every chunk of Sinnoh): where a chunk holds more than one area, as a Game Boy Advance map's edge does (its maps are not cut to the chunks' grid), 32 rows of 32 characters, one a tile: `-` where the tile is its matrix cell's own area, otherwise an index into the matrix's `areaKeys`, written with the characters of `WorldChunkFile.AreaCodes` (`0` to `9`, `a` to `z`, `A` to `Z`, then punctuation: 89 areas at most). `WorldMapBuilder` gives such tiles their area one by one (`Map.SetTileArea`), and `Map.AreaAt` asks a tile's own area before its chunk's; a map none of whose chunks has the list keeps no grid of tiles.

### `matrices/NNN.json`

```json
{
  "id": 0,
  "width": 30,
  "height": 30,
  "chunks": [ "  -   -  52 174 …", … ],
  "areas": [ " 0  0 12  0 …", … ],
  "areaKeys": [ "everywhere", "jubilife_city", … ],
  "altitudes": [ " 0  0  2  0 …", … ]
}
```

One string per row of the grid, cells separated by spaces. `chunks` holds chunk ids (`-` for none), `areas` an index into `areaKeys` for the area each chunk belongs to, `altitudes` how high the chunk sits in half tiles. Matrix 0 is the overworld. A matrix without `areas` belongs whole to the area that names it; one without `altitudes` sits at zero. The same chunk can fill many cells: the overworld's 468 filled cells use 176 different chunks.

`seasons` (plan 22 · U2, part b; Unova's alone, left out everywhere else): the chunks another season lays in place of the matrix's own, which are spring's, as a list of `{ "season": "Autumn", "chunks": [ … ] }`, each grid in the layout of `chunks` with `-` wherever the matrix's own chunk serves. `WorldMatrixFile.ChunkAt(x, y, season)` gives a season's chunk at a cell; nothing reads it until plan 22 · U4 builds a season's layout.

### `areas/<key>.json`

One area: a town, a route, a cave floor, a room. `key` is its name in lower case (`twinleaf_town`), `index` the original's number for it, `name` what a player reads on arriving, `matrix` the grid it lies on. `kind` is `Town`, `Outdoors`, `Cave`, `Indoors`, `PokemonCenter` or `Underground`; `sign` the style of the arrival sign; `weather`, `camera` and `battleBackground` name the original's settings (the field shows the weather: `Weathers.Of` reads the name, and the five places whose weather follows Platinum's calendar have, until the calendar is imported, what they have on most days); `dayMusic` and `nightMusic` the roles of its music; `encounters` its table of wild Pokémon (left out when it has none); `bike`, `running`, `escapeRope` and `fly` what is allowed there.

`land` (left out when nothing lives in its grass) is the area's wild Pokémon on land: Platinum's twelve slots in order, each `{ "species", "level" }`. A slot's chance is fixed by its place: 20, 20, 10, 10, 10, 10, 5, 5, 4, 4, 1 and 1 in a hundred (`WorldAreaFile.LandSlotWeights`). These are the base slots, the morning's; `day` and `night` are the two species the day (10:00 to 19:59) and the night put in slots 2 and 3, `swarm` the two a swarm puts in slots 0 and 1 (and, for Johto's, `surfSwarm` the species a swarm puts in the first slot of the water and `fishSwarm` in the rods' slots: plan 20 · J13, part c), and `radar` the four the Poké Radar's hard shake puts in slots 4, 5, 10 and 11, each slot keeping its level (plan 06 · R13, `EncounterSlots`); `dualSlot` is an object of `ruby`, `sapphire`, `emerald`, `fireRed` and `leafGreen`, each the two species that game in the console's second slot puts in slots 8 and 9 (plan 08 · P23, with the National Pokédex and the options' SLOT 2 GAME); each list is left out where the area has no grass. `water` is the same for whoever surfs there: five slots, met 60, 30, 5, 4 and 1 times in a hundred, each `{ "species", "level", "maxLevel" }` with a range of levels. `landRate` and `waterRate` are Platinum's rates for the two tables: how often, out of a hundred, an attempt that gets through meets something (`EncounterSteps` has the whole rule). `oldRod`, `goodRod` and `superRod` (plan 02 · S2) are what bites on each rod there, five slots each like `water`'s, with `oldRodRate`, `goodRodRate` and `superRodRate` the chance in a hundred that anything bites at all: the Old Rod's slots bite 60, 30, 5, 4 and 1 times in a hundred, the other two's 40, 40, 15, 4 and 1 (`WorldAreaFile.RodSlotWeights`, the original's `GetRodEncounterSlot`). Each is left out where the rod catches nothing.

A GBA game's area (plan 18 · W7) writes its tables the same way, and a slot whose chance isn't the one its place gives in Platinum's table of the kind carries its own `weight` (the GBA's Old Rod's two slots are met 70 and 30 times in a hundred, its Good Rod's three 60, 20 and 20), which the builder takes over the place's. It may also have `rockSmash` and `rockSmashRate` (what a smashed rock lets out: five slots, 60, 30, 5, 4 and 1; nothing reads it until a region plan gives Rock Smash its encounters) and `dive` and `emerge` (the area Dive goes down into from its deep water and surfacing comes up into, tile for tile: plan 21's Dive reads them). Its objects are the original's `object_events` (`looks` and `movement` in lower case without their prefixes, the GBA's up, down, left and right as north, south, west and east; a trainer's `script` is the trainer its script battles as, `TRAINER_YOUNGSTER_BEN`; an item's ball has its `item` from the original's item scripts), its signs its `bg_events` (a sign of type 0 with its script's label, a hidden item of type 2), its triggers its `coord_events`, and a warp to `MAP_DYNAMIC` keeps its place with no `to`.

`eastSea` (plan 06 · R10; left out when false) says the area's Shellos and Gastrodon are met in the east sea's colours, as the original's encounter data says by its form rates. `unownTable` is which of the original's Unown tables its Unown are drawn from (`FormRules.UnownTables`): 1 to 8 as the encounter data numbers them (most letters, F, R, I, N, E, D, the two marks), left out for none, which reads as the first.

Then what stands on it, in tiles of its matrix (on the overworld: tiles of the whole region):

- `warps`: `{ "x", "z", "to", "toWarp" }`: the tile leads to the warp numbered `toWarp` (counting from 0) of the area `to`. Six lifts lead to `dynamic`: a script decides where. A GBA game's warp may carry `"landing": true` (plan 19 · K6, part b): it is only where someone falling through a hole above comes down, leads nowhere and shuts nothing, and is still counted among the warps the holes name.
- `objects`: people and things: `id`, `looks`, `movement`, `x`, `z`, `facing` (0 north, 1 south, 2 west, 3 east), `rangeX` and `rangeZ` (how far they wander), `trainer` (how a trainer watches for the player) and `sight` (how many tiles ahead they see: the first number the original keeps for the object; 0 for one who battles only when spoken to), `hiddenBy` (the flag that hides them), `y` (the height the original stands them at, left out on the ground: someone on a bridge's deck stands over whoever walks under it, the Cycling Road's riders over Wayward Cave's way in) and `script`. An object that is an item in its ball (`looks` "pokeball" and a script from 7000 on) also has the `item` it holds, by the game's own name for it, and a `count` when it is more than one: the game lays it there without the overlay saying anything. A patch of soft soil (`looks` "berry_soil") has the `patch` it is, the number of the original's berry patch (0 to 117, its object's first number; Floaroma Town's lone patch names none and is 0), and the game lays the soil there with what grows in it (plan 06 · R14a). The tile under it, blocked in the chunk and painted with nothing, is taken as the ground round it and left open: the patch itself is what stands in the way.
- `signs`: `{ "x", "z", "type", "script" }`: something read or found by facing the tile. Type 2 is a hidden item, which also has its `item`, a `count` when more than one, the `flag` set once it is found and the `range` the original's Dowsing Machine notices it from; `"buried": true` (plan 23, Alola) is an item dug up only on a ridden Stoutland's back.
- `triggers`: `{ "x", "z", "width", "depth", "script", "variable", "value" }`: a rectangle that starts a script while a story variable has a value.

Scripts are numbers into the area's script file in the decompilation; plan 02 writes ours (`docs/scripts.md`) and the overlay says which of the area's people, signs and triggers run them. No dialogue is imported.

### `habitats.json`

Where every wild Pokémon of the region lives, read by `Habitats` for the Pokédex's area page (plan 03 · D10). Unlike the area files it covers every area with wild Pokémon, open or not, since the Pokédex shows the whole region.

```json
{
  "map": [ "                              ", "        .....              ~~~", "        ...T.         ...  ~.~" ],
  "areas": [
    { "key": "route_201", "name": "Route 201", "cells": "3,26 4,26",
      "morning": [ "Starly", "Bidoof", "Kricketot" ], "day": [ "Starly", "Bidoof" ], "night": [ "Starly", "Bidoof", "Kricketot" ] }
  ]
}
```

`map` is the overworld's matrix as a picture: one string per row and one character per chunk, `~` where most of the chunk is water, `.` land, `T` a town or a city, a space where there is no chunk. Each area has its `key` and `name`, the chunks of the overworld it is shown on (`cells`, `"x,y"` each: an outdoor area's own, the chunks whose warps lead into a cave or a building, through any rooms between; the Great Marsh, which a script lets the player into, is shown at Pastoria City, and rooms reached only by scripted warps where the rest of the place is), and the species met there, each list left out when empty: in the grass or the cave in the `morning` (the table's own twelve slots), by `day` and at `night` (the two species each puts in slots 2 and 3), `surf`ing, and with the `oldRod`, `goodRod` and `superRod`. Swarms, the Poké Radar and the species a second game in the console calls up are not in it, as Platinum's Pokédex leaves them out.

### `encounters.json`

What the original keeps beside the areas' tables (its `encdata_ex` and the `res/field/encounters/` files of the honey trees, the Great Marsh and the Trophy Garden), read by `SpecialEncounterTables` (plan 06 · R13).

```json
{
  "honeyTrees": { "common": [ "Combee", "Wurmple", ... ], "uncommon": [ ... ], "rare": [ "Munchlax", ... ] },
  "greatMarsh": { "local": [ "Wooper", ... ], "national": [ "Toxicroak", ... ] },
  "trophyGarden": [ "Eevee", "Bonsly", ... ],
  "feebas": { "area": "mt_coronet_b1f", "species": "Feebas", "tiles": "9,1 10,1 ..." },
  "swarms": [ { "area": "route_201", "name": "Route 201", "species": "Doduo" }, ... ]
}
```

`honeyTrees` is the six slots of each group a honey tree draws (`HoneyTrees`); `greatMarsh` the 32 species the six areas of the marsh draw their daily one from, `local` before the National Pokédex and `national` after (`GreatMarsh`); `trophyGarden` the sixteen Mr. Backlot can bring (`TrophyGardenRules`). `feebas` is the lake Feebas lives in: every tile of it in the original's own order (in tiles of the area's matrix), which the day's number cuts into four groups and picks one tile of each from (`Feebas`). `swarms` is the places swarms come to (Sinnoh's 22, Johto's 20, in each game's order), with each one's name, the species that swarms there and, in Johto's, the `way` it is met (`surf` or `fish`; left out for the grass), open or not, for what is told of the day's swarm (`Swarms`).

`outbreaks` (plan 38 · EX1; Hoenn's alone, left out of every other region's file) is Emerald's television outbreak, one row each of `sPokeOutbreakSpeciesList` in `src/tv.c`: `{ "area": "route_102", "species": "Seedot", "level": 3, "moves": [ "Bide", "Harden", "Leech Seed" ], "share": 50 }`, an open area of the region with a land table of twelve slots, a species, the level it is met at (nought leaves the slots' own), up to four moves and the meetings in a hundred it takes (`Outbreaks.SlotsFor` fills the grass's first slots to it). The rows of our own that a region adds are `outbreaks.json` beside it, written by hand and read strictly (`{ "rows": [ ... ] }`, the same rows; Hoenn's three are Surskit, which Emerald's table has none of). The content check holds every row. Every region has two outbreaks a day besides, generated from its areas' own tables by `Outbreaks` (no file).

### `calendar.json`

Platinum's weather calendar (`sYearlyWeather` in the original's `src/field_overworld_weather.c`, plan 01 · M7), read by `World.Calendar`. Five places name the calendar in their header instead of a weather: the south of Route 212, Route 213, Route 216, Acuity Lakefront and Snowpoint City. For each day of a leap year from the first of January, the file gives the weather of each, by the names the headers use.

```json
{
  "places": [ "Route212South", "Route213", "Route216", "AcuityLakefront", "SnowpointCity" ],
  "days": [ [ "Raining", "Clear", "HeavySnow", "Snowing", "Snowing" ], ... ]
}
```

An area whose header names one of the places takes its weather from the row of today's date (`MapArea.WeatherOn`, by the computer's clock as the original goes by the console's). The table has a row for the 29th of February, so in any other year the days from March on are counted one further (`Weathers.CalendarDay`, as `FieldSystem_GetWeather` does).

### `townmap.json`

The map of the region as the game shows it (plan 01 · M12), read by `RegionMap` (`Data/RegionMap.cs`; `RegionMap.Of(region)`, `ForMap(mapName)`) for the Town Map, Fly's choice, the Pokédex's area page and whatever else shows the region. A region without the file has no map, and the Town Map says nothing there. The cells, the kinds, the flags and the hidden places are the original's (`res/town_map/town_map_data.json` and `src/applications/town_map` of the pinned decompilation); the names are the world's own and every line of text is ours.

```json
{
  "map": "Sinnoh", "columns": 30, "rows": 30,
  "view": [ 0, 5, 30, 24 ], "cursor": [ 1, 6, 28, 28 ], "start": [ 3, 27 ],
  "hidden": [ { "key": "fullmoon_island", "variable": "VAR_HIDDEN_LOCATION_FULL_MOON_ISLAND", "value": 520, "sea": true } ],
  "areas": [ { "key": "twinleaf_town", "flag": "FLAG_FIRST_ARRIVAL_TWINLEAF_TOWN", "about": "A small town..." },
             { "key": "route_201", "about": "..." } ],
  "landmarks": [ { "key": "oreburgh_gate", "name": "Oreburgh Gate", "flag": "FLAG_FIRST_ARRIVAL_OREBURGH_GATE",
                   "arrival": [ "oreburgh_gate_1f" ], "about": "A short tunnel..." } ],
  "blocks": [ { "x": 7, "y": 23, "kind": "Route", "name": "Route 203", "area": "route_203", "landmark": "oreburgh_gate" } ],
  "fly": [ { "spawn": 1, "cells": "3,27" }, { "spawn": 19, "cells": "12,27", "flag": "FLAG_FIRST_ARRIVAL_POKE_PARK_FRONT_GATE", "unseen": true } ]
}
```

- `map` is the streamed map the grid lies over: a cell is one of its chunks, `columns` by `rows` of them (`RegionMap.CellOf` turns a tile into its cell). `view` is the part of the grid the screen shows (left, top, columns, rows), `cursor` the cells the cursor may stand on (left, top, right, bottom) and `start` where it stands when the player is nowhere on the map.
- `hidden` are the places left off the map until `variable` holds `value`; `sea` has the picture show open water there until then, where the world has land all along.
- `areas` are what the cursor reads about: a `key`, the words (`about`) and, for a town, the `flag` its words wait for (the original tells of a town only once it has been visited, and of a route from the start).
- `landmarks` are the places inside a cell (a cave, a building, a lake): a `name`, the `flag` its words and name wait for, and `about`. `arrival` names the areas or maps whose first visit sets that flag, for the places no script of ours sets it for (`RegionMap.ArrivalFlags`, `GameEngine.NoteArrivalOnTheMap`).
- `blocks` are the cells with something under the cursor: `kind` (`Route`, `Town`, `Landmark`: the tag the screen shows), the `name` shown, its `area`, and where there is one its `landmark` and the `hidden` place it belongs to.
- `places` (optional) are the rooms and maps of the region that no warp of the overworld leads into (dived, surfed, flown or ridden to, or stepped into by a script) with the cell each is shown at, `"x,y"` (plan 38 · EX10): `RegionMap.PlacesOf` takes them first, and the rooms that open off one have its cell too. The cell must have a block.
- `islands` (optional; plan 23, wave 91) lays the region's other overworld maps onto the same grid, each at the cell of its north-west chunk, `"x,y"`: `map` is at 0,0, and a tile of an island is shown at the island's cell plus its own chunk (`RegionMap.Overworlds`, `IsOverworld`, `CellOn`). The islands' rectangles of chunks may not overlap. Alola's file lays its four islands so (Melemele at 0,0, Ula'ula at 17,0, Akala at 17,12, Poni at 1,16, a grid of 30 by 27 with a cell of sea round the land); `RegionMapArt` paints all of them into the one picture, the region's habitats are on the same grid (MapDraw's `island` on a map of the layout), and the way come, Fly and the rooms' cells all go by it.
- `fly` are Fly's places: a `spawn` of `Data/SpawnLocations.cs` and the `cells` it covers; `flag` where the place opens by another flag than the spawn's own first arrival, and `unseen` where nothing of it is drawn until it is open. In an open region (plan 32 · OW10) a place may name a `marker` of its places' files (`"marker": "town_square"`): Fly puts the player down on it, facing as it does, in place of the spawn's tile (`RegionMap.FlyMarkerOf`, `GameEngine.OpenFlyLanding`).

An open region (plan 32) whose `map` is an open map rather than a grid map has its picture painted from above out of its bake (`RegionMapArt.PaintOpen`, OW10): a texel a metre and a cell a chunk of 32 metres, the grid's cell 0,0 on the land's north-west chunk (`RegionMapArt.OpenCellOf`), each texel the highest ground facing up there (a cave under a hill is never seen), in the Town Map's own colours by its cover and lit from the north-west, and the baked water over it.

Unova's file (plan 38 · EX10) is made the way Kalos's is: a cell for each chunk of the overworld matrix (Black 2's 29 by 27), its blocks on the cells of each open area, Fly's places the Pokémon Centers of spawns 2 to 11 of `regions/unova.json`. Castelia City, a map of ours off the overworld's grid, stands on two cells of empty sea.

A file that contradicts itself is refused with its fault as it loads (a block outside the view, two blocks on one cell, a block that names an area, a landmark or a hidden place the file doesn't have, one of Fly's places on a cell with no block, an `arrival` with no flag to set). `RegionMapTests` holds the file to the world: every block is named as its place on the map of Sinnoh is, every place is on the map and says something the font can write, every flag a place waits for is set by something in the game, and Fly's places are the spawn locations on the cells of their towns.

## Characters

`Data/characters.json` (`CharacterStyles`, GPU-free) is the table of looks (plan 11 · C1): one name for a person in the field, in battle and in the files. It is hand-written; every look is our own design after the character.

```json
{
  "looks": {
    "lass": { "hairColor": "#603c34", "hair": "Long", "top": "#ec6884", "accent": "#fcf0f4", "bottom": "#485ca8",
              "shoes": "#543834", "skirt": true, "shortSleeves": true, "lashes": true, "blush": true },
    "rowan": { "hairColor": "#bec2d2", "hair": "Swept", "mustache": true, "coat": true, "build": "Adult", "height": 1.04 }
  },
  "same": { "trainer": "player", "lucas": "player" },
  "fallbacks": { "barry": "Rival", "school_kid_f": "Lass", "hiker": "Gentleman", "briefcase": "StarterBriefcase" },
  "fallback": "Trainer",
  "classes": { "Lass": "lass", "Galactic": "grunt", "Leader": null, "Ace Trainer": [ "ace_trainer_m", "ace_trainer_f" ] }
}
```

- `looks`: a look by its name, matched without regard to case (a map's `"npcType": "Lass"` is the file's `lass`). Its fields are `CharacterStyle`'s, camelCase, each left out where it is the plain default (`Graphics/CharacterStyle.cs`): `skin`, `hairColor`, `hatColor`, `hatBand`, `top`, `accent`, `bottom`, `shoes`, `eyes`, `sole` and `bag` are colours written `#rrggbb` (no `bag` is no bag, no `sole` the shoes' own colour darker); `hair` is `Short`, `Spiky`, `Long`, `Swept`, `Afro`, `Bun`, `Braids` or `Bunches` (`HairCut`; the ties of braids and bunches are `hatBand`'s colour), `hat` `None`, `Beret`, `Cap`, `NurseCap`, `Bow`, `Mask` or `Fedora` (`Headwear`), `build` `Kid`, `Adult`, `Little` or `Elder` in the chibi kit and `Teen`, `Grown`, `GrownBroad`, `Child` or `GrownElder` in the grown (`BodyBuild`; a build is read in the kit the look is made in, so a `Kid` asked grown is a `Teen`: style guide, "Grown characters") and `height` a scale (1 the kids'); `kit` is `Chibi` (left out) or `Grown`, the kit the look is made in (plan 30 · FM4: from Kalos on, people are not chibi), and `curved` gives a grown body a waist, hips and a bust (`true`) or not (`false`), left out following `lashes`; `glasses` is a colour too (the frames', drawn on the face; no `glasses` is none); `skirt`, `shorts`, `mustache`, `beard`, `stripes`, `coat`, `shortSleeves`, `scarf`, `bushyBrows`, `lashes` and `blush` are switches. A field the kit doesn't have is an error, not a look left plain. A name the table doesn't have is the plain default (a green shirt, short brown hair, a child's build). New parts of the kit (a hair cut, a hat, a build) are code in `CharacterModels` and a value here.
- `grown`: looks of the grown kit under a name `looks` has already (plan 30 · FM4, FM10: Kalos's whole cast, its own people's and the classes the older regions share with it, 81 twins, each with its build of the grown kit; style guide, "Grown characters", "Kalos's cast"), asked for first when a look is asked in the grown kit (`CharacterStyles.Get(name, kit)`); each is read as `kit` `Grown`. A name here that `looks` lacks, or whose look is of the grown kit already, is an error: a look of the grown kit alone stands among the `looks` with `"kit": "Grown"`. A look is asked in a build by its name and the build after a `~` (`PLAYER~Teen`, beside an outfit's `@` and a held thing's `+`, in either order), which is how a region's kit reaches the player crossing into it (`CharacterStyle.InKit`).
- `same`: names that are another look (`TRAINER` and `LUCAS` are the boy).
- `fallbacks`: the original's object looks (`OBJ_EVENT_GFX_*` as the map importer names them, the `looks` of an area file's objects) that have no look of their own yet, and the look each stands as until it has. `WorldMapBuilder.CharacterFor` gives a person their own look when the table has it, else this, else `fallback` (the boy). Plan 11 · C2 to C4 draw the rest and take their lines out of here; `CharacterTableTests` lists the people of the open areas who still stand as the boy.
- `classes`: the look a trainer class stands in on the battle's platform, as the original shows the class whatever look the person wears in the field (an Aroma Lady stands as a Breeder). Several looks are picked between by the person's own (`Trainer.Look`, their `npcType`), the first for anyone else; `null` is a class that names nobody in particular ("Leader", "Commander", "Champion"), whose person's own look stands. A class not named here stands in the plain default until it has a look (`CharacterStyles.InBattle`).

The engine builds the rigs of the people within two chunks of the player in the background as they come near, and on a warp lets go of every other (`CharacterModels.Trim`); the disk cache keeps the meshes.

## Clothes

`Data/clothes.json` (`ClothingDatabase`) is every garment the player can wear and every boutique that sells them (plan 11 · C9, C10). It is hand-written, not imported.

```json
{
  "garments": [
    { "id": "red_cap", "name": "Red Cap", "slot": "Hat", "price": 800, "hat": "Cap",
      "color": "#dc3c3c", "accent": "#f6f6f8", "description": "..." },
    { "id": "winter_coat", "name": "Winter Coat", "slot": "Top", "price": 3600,
      "color": "#2e4e88", "accent": "#f4ecd8", "coat": true, "scarf": true, "description": "..." },
    { "id": "no_hat", "name": "No Hat", "slot": "Hat", "price": 0 }
  ],
  "boutiques": { "jubilife": [ "navy_beret", "red_cap", "..." ] }
}
```

- `slot` is `Hat`, `Top`, `Bottoms`, `Shoes` or `Bag` (`ClothingSlot`). A garment changes only its own slot's fields of the look's `CharacterStyle` (`CharacterStyle.Dressed`); the build, the face and the hair stay the look's.
- `color` is the garment's main colour and `accent` its second (a hat's band, a top's sleeves or stripes, a shoe's sole, a bag's flap). A garment with no `color` takes the slot's part off (`No Hat`, `No Bag`).
- What a slot's garment may say: a hat its shape (`Beret` or `Cap`); a top `coat` (long, over the hips), `shortSleeves`, `stripes` (in the accent) and `scarf` (in the accent); bottoms `skirt` or `shorts`.
- `price` is in the game's money; a price of 0 is free, owned from the start.
- A boutique (`boutiques`) is a counter's key and the garments it sells, in the order its rows show them. A script opens it with `wardrobe "<key>"`; `wardrobe` alone opens the player's own clothes at home.
- A set (`sets`, plan 11 · C10) is offered in the new-game introduction, after the look's own clothes: `{ "id": "trail", "name": "Trail Set", "garments": [ "red_cap", "forest_hoodie", "..." ], "for": "Boy" }`, at most one garment to a slot, a slot left out being the look's own; `for` (`Boy` or `Girl`) keeps a set to one look, and left out offers it to both. A set chosen there is given for nothing (`Wardrobe.Start`): its garments are the player's from then on, worn and taken off at the wardrobe at no cost.

What the player wears is an `Outfit` (`Core/Outfit.cs`): a garment's id in each slot, or nothing for the look's own. It is saved as `SaveData.Outfit` with the garments bought (`SaveData.Wardrobe`); a save without them loads in the look's own clothes. A character dressed in an outfit is named `PLAYER@<hat>.<top>.<bottoms>.<shoes>.<bag>` (a dash for the look's own), so its rig, sprites and mesh cache follow the outfit, and an outfit with nothing chosen is plain `PLAYER` or `DAWN`.

## Pokédex entries

`Data/dex-entries.json` (`Data/DexEntries.cs`) is what the Pokédex's INFO page says of a species (plan 08 · P13; the design's "Written entries" in `docs/plans/finished/08-platinum-details.md`). It is hand-written, our own words in the manner of a Pokédex, never the games' text nor a paraphrase of it.

```json
{
  "Turtwig": {
    "habitat": "Forest",
    "entries": [
      "First entry ...",
      "Second entry ..."
    ]
  }
}
```

- One object keyed by a species' name exactly as `species.json` writes it (`Mr. Mime`, `Porygon-Z`), and from plan 08 · P17 a form's by its Showdown name (`Rotom-Heat`), in National Pokédex order (a form after its species), two spaces to an indent as `GameDataFiles.Serialize` writes.
- `habitat` is one of Platinum's nine kinds of place by its name: `Grassland`, `Forest`, `WatersEdge`, `Sea`, `Cave`, `Mountain`, `RoughTerrain`, `Urban`, `Rare` (`DexHabitat`; the page says "Water's edge", "Rough terrain"). It is the kind of place, not where the species is met: that is `habitats.json`, for the AREA page.
- `entries` are two, of 15 to 45 words each, saying different things (they share no run of five words), consistent with the species' types, category, size and evolutions in `species.json`. The page shows them in turn, one more at each opening of the entry.
- A name the file lacks shows the sentence the importer generates (`dexEntry` in `species.json`). `DataFileTests` holds the shape and the floor: every species of the Sinnoh Pokédex, and every species to `WrittenEntriesThrough`, which each batch raises.
- Before a batch is done, `dotnet run --project tools/DataImporter -- --check-entries` must report no run of six words shared with any English flavour text PokeAPI keeps (its README, "Checking the written entries").

## Trainers' lines in battle

`Data/trainer-lines.json` (`Data/TrainerLines.cs`) is what a trainer of the other side says in battle, at the three moments the rules mark (plan 11 · C12; `TrainerCue`): `lastPokemon` (the last of their Pokémon able to fight comes out; never the opening's send-out), `firstDown` (the first of theirs falls, with others still able to fight) and `lowHp` (their last one is in the red, a fifth of its HP or under). It is hand-written, our own words, never the games' text.

```json
{
  "leader_roark": {
    "firstDown": { "text": "A crack in the rock... You hit harder than you look." },
    "lastPokemon": { "text": "My last stone. Let's see if you can break bedrock." },
    "lowHp": { "text": "Steady now... Rock holds firm until the very last blow." }
  },
  "rival_route_203_*": {
    "lowHp": { "text": "No, no, no! I ran the whole way here for this!", "mood": "...", "act": "..." }
  }
}
```

- A key is a trainer's id in `trainers.json` (`leader_roark`), or a stem ending in `*` that every id beginning with it takes (`rival_route_203_*`: the rival's three teams there, one for each starter). An id of its own wins over a stem, and a longer stem over a shorter one. A rematch is a trainer of its own (`leader_roark_rematch`), with nothing unless it is given its own lines.
- Each moment has up to one line, `text`, which may hold `{player}` and `{rival}`; a moment left out says nothing. A line must fit two lines of the battle's message box (a hundred characters with both names at their longest) and is Latin-1 only, as the interface's font is.
- `mood` and `act` are the face and the gesture the line is said with, by plan 11 · C11's names (`Mood`: `neutral`, `happy`, `surprised`, `sad`, `angry`; `Gesture`: `wave`, `nod`, `cheer`, `surprised`, `bow`, `shrug`, `point`, `laugh`, `jump`; either may be left out), which the trainer shows as they step back onto their platform to say it (plan 11 · C13; `TrainerLine.Face`, `Gesture`). A name the game doesn't know is a failed test.
- The lines are read only where the `TrainerLines` option holds (On, or Rules in a game played by the modern rules). Whoever is beside the player (a partner) never speaks: the moments are the other side's.

## Text

What the interface says (plan 17 · N1) is a table of strings by key, read by `Core/Text.cs`; the code holds the keys only (`Text.Of("options.title")`).

- `text/languages.json`: a row to a language: `tag` (its IETF tag, the folder's name), `name` (the language's own name for itself, which the options' first row shows), `script` (`Latin`; a language whose script the faces don't draw is listed but not offered until plan 17 · N5, and neither is one with no `ui.json` of its own, as French has none yet), `plural` (`english`, `french` or `none`: how a word's form is chosen by a number), `decimal` (the mark before a fraction), `breaks` and, for the pseudo-language `xx` only, `pseudo` (English with every string in brackets, for the tests and the harness's `SHOTS_LANGUAGE`; never offered).
- `text/<tag>/ui.json`: sections, a screen to each (`shared` holds the words several say), flattened into dotted keys (`options.textSpeed.label`). A value is a string, or an object of forms: `one` and `other` (also `zero`, `two`, `few`, `many`), chosen by the argument `n` under the language's rule, or `boy` and `girl`, chosen by the player's look. Arguments are named in braces (`"Delete slot {slot}?"`), so a translation may move them; a brace no argument is named for is left for whoever fills it later (`{player}`). A language's file holds only what it translates: a key it lacks is English's. The battle's sections (plan 17 · N2) are `battle` (the log's lines; a few, kept in tables built once, are said through `string.Format` and name their arguments `{0}` and `{1}`), `battleInfo`, `battleMenu`, `evolution`, `intro` and `classes` (a trainer class by its English name's letters and digits, `PokeKid` for "Poké Kid", and `master` for a Master Trainer's). English's is the one every key must be in, and every entry of it must be asked for (`TextTests`).
- `text/languages.json` may give a language `nameGroups` (plan 17 · N3): the groups of first letters the Pokédex search offers for its names, a string of the letters of each (kana rows for Japanese, initial consonants for Korean); a language without them offers the Latin alphabet's nine. `plural` also has `romanian` (`one`, `few`, `other`).
- A pseudo-language may give `brackets` (plan 17 · N5), the two strings it puts round every string in place of `[` and `]`: `xx-cjk`'s are `［あ字한` and `］`, so the harness can draw every screen through the CJK face. A language of the scripts `Japanese`, `Korean` or `Chinese` is drawn by the CJK face and is offered once it has its own `ui.json`; every CJK character of its files must be in the face (`CjkFontTests`), which `dotnet run --project tools/TextTool -- font <NotoSansSC-Bold.ttf> <NotoSansKR-Bold.ttf>` cuts again from the whole faces when a translation brings new ones.
- `text/<tag>/names.json` (plan 17 · N3; written by `tools/DataImporter`'s `NameFiles.cs` from PokeAPI's names tables, never by hand; `Data/LocalNames.cs` reads it): the names of the game's own things in a language, every entry keyed by the English name the data and the save go by. Sections `species`, `categories` (PokeAPI's genus whole, by species: `Pokémon Graine`), `forms` (where PokeAPI names the form; `LocalNames.Form` puts the species' local name before the English words of the rest), `moves`, `abilities`, `items`, `types`, `natures`, `places` (PokeAPI's English location name to the language's), and a `descriptions` block (`moves`, `abilities`, `items`) of PokeAPI's contributors' short prose where the language has it (French for moves, abilities and items, German for part of the abilities). A name the file lacks is the English one, and a language with no file shows English names. Written for the eight languages PokeAPI names things in (`fr`, `de`, `es`, `it`, `ja`, `ko`, `zh-Hans`, `zh-Hant`); `docs/mechanics/coverage.md` counts what each has. Nothing of the games' own text is read.
- `text/<tag>/names.<kind>.json` (plan 17 · N6; written by hand, never by the importer): a language's own names and descriptions in the shape of `names.json`, holding only the sections it writes, e.g. `names.moves.json` = `{ "moves": { "Tackle": "Izbitură", … }, "descriptions": { "moves": { "Tackle": "Se izbește cu tot corpul de țintă.", … } } }` (`names.abilities.json` with `abilities` and `descriptions.abilities`, `names.items.json` with `items` and `descriptions.items`). `LocalNames` reads them after `names.json`, in the ordinal order of their names, and their entries win (`LocalNames.FilesOf`). Romanian, which PokeAPI doesn't name, has these alone: its moves' names and descriptions are made up by us.

### Translating the scripts, the overlays and the rooms

The scripts, overlays and hand-made maps keep their English files; a language's sibling files (plan 17 · N4; `Data/TranslationFiles.cs`, `Core/Text.Lines.cs`, `Data/TextSources.cs`) hold the same lines in order, and a line is translated as it is shown. A file a language lacks is a language that says that file in English, and a line with no text says its English. Every entry is `{ "en": [...], "text": [...] }`: the English the entry translates (one string to a line it covers) beside the translation (the same count, `""` where a line isn't translated). **An entry whose `en` is no longer the English the game has is stale**: the scene was rewritten, the game shows the English, and the report counts it. `tools/TextTool extract <tag> --update` refreshes `en` and keeps the old text in `was` beside it.

- `text/<tag>/scripts/<file>.json`: an object of script names, each a list of entries, one to each text-bearing instruction of the script in the order they are written (`Instruction.TextIndex`: `say`, `text`, `ask`, `choose` with its answers, and `speaker "Name"`). A `say` of several lines is an entry of several. Scripts with nothing to say are left out.
- `text/<tag>/world/<key>.json` (an area's overlay) and `text/<tag>/maps/<Name>.json` (a hand-made map): `displayName` (an entry; an overlay's `name` or a room's `displayName`), `people` (by the id the file gives them, or `x,y` of the tile they begin on where it gives none: `name`, `dialog`, `before` and `after`, each an entry; the last two are a trainer's words) and `signs` (by the sign's id, or `x,y`, each an entry). A place's name is looked for there first, then in `names.json`'s `places`, then in `ui.json`'s `places` section (keyed by the English name's letters and digits, as `classes` are, for a place PokeAPI doesn't name), then English.

- `text/<tag>/data.json` (plan 17 · N6): the lines a data file gives in English and the game says through `Text.OfData` (a region's professor and his lines of the introduction, `regions/<id>.json`, every trainer's lines in battle, `trainer-lines.json`, said through it by `BattleEngine.Show`, and the Pokégear's radio, `world/johto/radio.json`, whose line `RadioShows.Line` draws from the English and says through it, plan 17 · N6f; `TextSources.DataLines` lists them all), an object of the English line to the language's own (`"Welcome to the world of Pokémon!": "Bun venit în lumea Pokémonilor!"`). Being keyed by the English itself, an entry whose English is rewritten matches nothing and the line shows in English until it is translated again; `RomanianTests` holds every key a line of some region file, of `trainer-lines.json` or of the radio, and every trainer line translated and fitting the battle box. A line the table lacks is English.

A trainer's `before` and `after` that begin with the trainer's title (`"Youngster Tristan: ..."`) begin with it as the language's `classes` and `battle.fullTitle` say it (`"Puști Tristan: ..."`), so the box drops it and shows the title on its tag. The content check (`tools/ContentCheck`, `Translations`) holds every translated line, answer and name to the text box and the faces as it holds the English.

Placeholders (`{player}`, `{var:VAR_X}`) and plan 09's markup pass through untouched: they are filled after the lookup, and a translation may use only the ones its English line has (`TextSources.Problems`). The save never holds a translated word: the place a Pokémon was met at and the name of whoever gave an Egg stay English identifiers, and `Map.DisplayNameAt` is that name while `Map.ShownNameAt` is the language's.

## Regions

`regions/<id>.json` (plan 18 · W1; `Data/RegionFiles.cs`, read by `RegionDatabase`) is what a region is made of beyond its place in the chain: `id`, `folder` (its world's and its music's), `models` (the catalogue its props are looked up in), `map` (its overworld, where Fly and the way out noted for Dig land), `dex` (the PokeAPI identifier of its regional Pokédex; plan 23 · Z4: `dexes`, a list, for a region with several, Kalos's `kalos-central`, `kalos-coastal` and `kalos-mountain`, `dex` being the first: entering the region gives the player all of them and each has its own diploma) and `nationalUnlock` (the rule that opens the National one: `HallOfFameAndSeenAll`, Sinnoh's, the region's Hall of Fame with every species of its Pokédex seen; `Caught60`, a number of species caught, Kanto's sixty, any number after `Caught`; `HallOfFame`, which is also what an empty or unknown text means; `NationalUnlock.Parse`), `hasStory`, `rival` (the name the introduction offers; absent where the rival is the other hero), `heroes` (`boy`, `girl`: character types), `starters` (three, in the briefcase's order; the rival takes the next one round), `badges` (names in the card's order, none to eight: the story keeps a mask of them for the region, plan 18 · W2, and a script names them in any case) and `gates` (the badge each field move asks for there, by the move's name, `RockSmash`, to one of the region's badges: `FieldMoveRules.BadgeFor(move, region)`), `professor` (`name`, `look`, `species` shown, and `lines`: sixteen strings of the introduction, `{name}` and `{rival}` filled as it plays) and `spawns` (the places Fly lands: `id`, `area`, `x`, `y`, `room`, `unlockedOnArrival`, `flag`, and `map` for a place on a map of its own rather than the overworld, plan 21 · H8, part c: Sootopolis City; a GBA import's `spawns.json` has the same shape), and last `levels` (plan 18 · W4; `LevelMapping`): pairs `[own, met]` of a level of the region's own game and the level it is met at by a team that crossed into it, by the region's own level, never lower for a higher one; a straight line between pairs and on past them, rounded half up and held to 1 to 260. Absent for Sinnoh. The world files keep the game's own levels: the mapping is applied as a Pokémon is met, in a game begun in another region. A region with no file has an empty record. `caps` (plan 18 · W4b-1; `LevelCaps.TableOf`) is the level cap by Badges held, nine levels of the region's own game (none held to all eight, then the Champion's), mapped by `levels` in a region crossed into. `movement` (plan 30 · FM1; `RegionMovement`) is how the player moves there: `directions` (`Four`, the default; `Eight`, stage 1; `Free`, stage 2), `speed` (`Paces`, the default; `Analog`, stage 2 only) and `modes` (the region's own ways of travelling: `Skates`, `Ride`, `RotomBike`, `Mount`; each listed by one region alone); left out, the four-way grid of every region before Kalos. `kit` (`Chibi`, the default, or `Grown`) is the body kit its people are drawn in (plan 30 · FM4). A map entry of `world.json` may give a `movement` record of its own, which wins for the place (`RegionMovement.Under`: its directions and speed, its modes where it lists any, an empty list barring them); `GameEngine.MovementHere` lays the options' MOVEMENT row over the two.

`field` (plan 32 · OW1; `RegionFile.FieldKind`, read as `Region.Field` and asked through `GameEngine.Here`) is what the region's outdoors are walked on: `"open"`, the open world's engine in millimetres (Galar's and Paldea's, plans 33 and 34), or `"grid"` and left out, the tile grid every region from Sinnoh to Alola has; anything else is refused (a pack's region by `--validate-packs`). A region's rooms are grid maps either way (plan 32 · decision 6). No region's file says `open` yet.

A region file may also name the sound its music plays in, `"sound": "modern"` (plan 31 · MU2; left out, or `"ds"`, is the DS sound every region before Kalos has; anything else is refused as the songs are read). A song's own header `sound` wins over it (`docs/music-format.md`, "Which sound a song plays in").

`battleCamera` (plan 45 · BC1) names the manner its battles are filmed in, a row of `CameraManners` (`"sinnoh"`, and the rows later sessions add; style guide, "The battle's manners"); left out, the region is filmed as Sinnoh is. A name the table lacks is refused by the pack check (`--validate-packs`) and the tests; in a battle it films as Sinnoh does. The options' BATTLE CAMERA row decides whether it is used (REGION, the default) or passed over (CLASSIC, LIVELY).

**A region a pack adds** (plan 16 · T14; `docs/packs.md`, "A region of your own"; `RegionDatabase.With`, `RegionChain`) is a region file in a pack whose `id` is no region of the game's. The game's regions keep their place in the chain, start, arrival and maps in code; such a file says them itself, in fields the game's files leave out: `name` (shown; the id when left out), `generation` (nought when left out), `start` and `arrival` (`{ "map", "x", "y", "facing" }`, `RegionSpot`; the arrival is the start when left out), `maps` (every map of the region, each belonging to it alone), `after` (the region it joins after, which must be the last with maps; left out or another, the region stands outside the chain with nothing sailing to it) and `link` (`RegionLinkFile`: `transport`, `vessel`, `departureMap`, a map of the region before, and `attendant`, a person as a room writes one with `isTransportAttendant`, which `MapDatabase` lays onto the departure map as the maps load). The chain is worked out again whenever other packs are put in use (`RegionDatabase.Chain`), and with none it is the game's own arrays. Its world folder, index included (`DataLayers.IsFixed` holds only for a region the game has), its rooms, songs, scripts and trainers are read through the layers (`DataLayers.FoldersIn`, `World.LoadAll`, `TrainerDatabase.LoadRegions`). A save's `packs` entry for the pack whose region the player stands in carries `region` (`SavedPack.Region`); without that pack the title says where the game was saved, and the save wakes at the start of the region its game began in (`SaveData.WhereAPackLeftIt`).

## Open worlds

An open region's land (plan 32 · OW2; `tools/OpenWorldBake`) is written as data in `Data/world3d/<region>/` and baked by `dotnet run --project tools/OpenWorldBake -- <region>` into `baked/` beside it, which is what the game loads. The baker's own test world is `tools/OpenWorldBake/worlds/lab/` (OW0's hill, its tunnel and cavern, a river with a rock bridge, a pond with a stack, a ledge and two roads), whose bake is git-ignored; a region of the game commits its bake. Every length is in metres; x runs east, z south and y up, as `OpenPos`'s do; a point on the ground is `[x, z]` and a point in the air `[x, y, z]`. The files are read strictly (a key no field has, a value of the wrong type or a field the schema marks required stops the bake, naming the file, the JSON path and the key), then held to what JSON can't say (a point's length, a polygon of three points or more, a grid's rows as long as one another), and may hold comments; their schemas are `docs/schemas/open-*.schema.json`. A brush's or a shape's `kind` is written first.

- **`world.json`** (`OpenWorldFile`): `region` (its id), `chunkSize` (32, the only size there is), `chunks` (`[west, north, east, south]`, chunk numbers as `OpenMap.ChunkOf` counts them, 32 m columns from nought, the last two included), `baseHeight` (where no grid lies), `cover` (`highland`, `snow`: the heights over which open grass is highland and open ground snow, none unless given; `shore`, how far over the water its edge is sand, 0.6), `areas` (`key`, `name`, `polygon`, `minY` and `maxY`, so a cave under a hill is an area of its own, `music`, `weather`: OW5 reads them, the baker draws them) and `maps` (`name`, no grid map's, and its `areas`).
- **`terrain/*.json`** (`TerrainFile`), read in the order of their names: a `grid` of control points (`origin` `[x, z]` of the north-west point, `spacing`, `heights` a row a row north to south, read between them by Catmull-Rom; a column takes its height from the first file's grid that holds it), `brushes` laid on the heights in order (`dome`: `at`, `radius`, `rise`; `ridge`: `points`, `width`, `rise`; `plateau`: `polygon`, `height`, `falloff`, level inside and eased into the land outside; `crater`: `at`, `radius`, `depth`, `rim`; `channel`, a river's bed: `points`, `width`, `depth`; each with a `name` for messages) and `paint` (`cover`, a `polygon` or an `at` and `radius`, `minY` and `maxY`: the first that holds a vertex of the land's own surface wins over the rules).
- **`carve/*.json`** (`CarveFile`): `carves`, what is cut out (air), and `additions`, what is joined (rock), each a shape: `capsules` (`points` in the air, `radius`, and a flat `floor` if it says: a tunnel, a rock bridge), `ellipsoid` (`centre`, `radii`, `floor`: a cavern, a boulder), `box` (`min`, `max`, `round`: a cliff's notch) and `pillar` (`at`, `radius`, `bottom`, `top`: a sea stack, a shaft). The land is one signed-distance field: the heights as a surface, the carves taken out of it, the additions joined to it.
- **`water.json`** (`WaterFile`): the `sea` (`level`, and a `polygon` if it isn't everywhere), `lakes` (`name`, `polygon`, `level`) and `rivers` (`name`, `points` in the air whose y is the surface there, `width`).
- **`roads.json`** (`RoadsFile`): `roads`, each a `name`, `points` (`[x, z]`, graded at the land's height there under the brushes, or `[x, y, z]`, at y), `width`, `shoulder` (where it eases back into the land, 3) and `surface` (a cover, `Path`). A road is a Catmull-Rom spline through its points, its grade running straight between them by the length along it; the land is level across its width at that grade.
- **`places/<key>.json`** (`OpenPlaceFile`, plan 32 · OW5; `OpenWorld/OpenPlaceFiles.cs`, schema `docs/schemas/open-place.schema.json`), an overlay's role at metre scale: what stands in the area of `world.json` its file is named for, read strictly **by the game** as it loads the region (a file named for no area of a map stops the load) and never baked. `markers` (`name`, letters, digits and `_` as a script names it, unique across the map's places; `at` `[x, y, z]`, the feet's; `yaw` in degrees, nought south and 90 east), `triggers` (`name`, a `box` (`min`, `max`, `[x, y, z]`) or a `cylinder` (`at` `[x, z]`, `radius`, `bottom`, `top`), never both; `script`, looked for in the place's `scripts/<key>.txt` and then in `common`; `var` and `value`, the story's variable it waits for at that value, none for always: it runs once as the capsule's middle, 0.8 m over its feet, crosses into it), `doors` (`name`, a `box` or a `cylinder`; `room`, a grid map; `at` `[x, y]`, the room's tile it lands on, facing `facing`, `up` unless it says; `out`, the marker the room's way back out lands on, which is any warp of the room whose `targetMap` is the open map's name; `openedBy`, a flag it waits for), `zones` (`name`, `polygon`, `minY`, `maxY`, `slots` of `species`, `minLevel`, `maxLevel` (to 260), `weight` and `time` (`Any` unless it says, `Morning`, `Day`, which takes twilight with it, or `Night`, which takes late night), `density` to a thousand square metres and `cap`: plan 32 · OW7's wild Pokémon come out of them, as many at once as the zone's area by its density, at least one and at most its cap, each walking by the temperament `open-wild.json` gives its species), `water` (`name`, `min` and `max` `[x, z]`, `level`: the hand-laid water OW3's capsule swims, `OpenWaterBox`), `solids` (`name`, `min`, `max`: boxes the capsule collides with, `OpenBox`; the baker bakes the navigation mesh with them and with `water`, so people walk round a house, plan 32 · OW6; and `building`, how one is drawn, plan 32 · OW9, `OpenBuildingFile`, `OpenBuilding`: `kind`, a `BuildingKind`, `house` unless it says; `town`, the `Architecture` whose way of building a house follows, `timber` unless it says; `storeys`, nought for its style's own, six at most; `faces`, the way its front and door face, `down` (south) unless it says; `sign`, the name over its door. The kit's building at 1.5 m a tile stands over the box, its door in the bay of the place's door nearest its front; a solid with no `building` is drawn as a block of stone), `trees` (plan 32 · OW9, `OpenTreeFile`, `OpenTree`: `at`, `[x, z]` on the highest ground there or `[x, y, z]` on the ground nearest y; `kind`, `pine` or `round`, `pine` unless it says; `size`, a share of the kind's own, more than nought and 4 at most, 1 unless it says; `trunk`, `true` for a tree walked round, its trunk a post of the collision and of the navigation mesh, `OpenTrunks`: scenery the capsule walks through unless it says), `groves` (plan 32 · OW9, `OpenGroveFile`, `OpenGrove`: `name`; `polygon` `[[x, z], ...]`; `spacing`, the jittered grid's in metres, 1.5 to 50, 6 unless it says; `fill`, the share of the grid's cells that hold a tree, more than nought and one at most, 0.8; `pines`, the share of those that are pines, 0.5; `size`, `[least, most]`, `[0.9, 1.25]`; `seed`; `trunks`, `true` for a forest walked round, every tree of it with a solid trunk as a tree's `trunk`: a grove is the same trees on any machine, its grid laid on the world's own so two groves side by side never crowd their border) and `people` (plan 32 · OW6, `OpenPersonFile`, `OpenPerson`: `name`, as a script's `walkto`, `placeat` and `faceto` name them, unique in the map and never a marker's name nor `player`; `at` and `yaw` as a marker's; `look`, `displayName`, `script` (in the place's file or `common`), `lines` (a trainer's first two are their words before the battle and once beaten, as an overlay's trainer's two lines are: plan 33 · GL1); `trainer`, an id of the trainers' table (the map's region first), and `sight` (`length` in metres, 30 at most, and `angle`, the cone's width in all in degrees, 60 unless it says), a trainer only; `moves`, `still`, `lookAbout` or `wander`, and for a wanderer a `radius` in metres (3 unless it says) or a `bounds` polygon `[[x, z], ...]`, never both; `hiddenBy` and `shownBy`, flags). **`open-wild.json`** (beside the other data files, not in a region's folder; `OpenWildFile`, `OpenWorld/OpenWildKinds.cs`, schema `docs/schemas/open-wild.schema.json`; ours) is how the wild Pokémon of an open map take the player (plan 32 · OW7): `kinds`, a row for each of the four temperaments `Calm`, `Shy`, `Curious` and `Aggressive` with `sight` (metres ahead, 30 at most), `angle` (the cone's width in all, in degrees), `scent` (metres all round, whatever lies between; twice as far for a player who runs) and, for a curious one, `keep` (how near it comes before it stands and watches); `species` (a species' or a form's temperament by its name), `types` (a type's, for a species not named: its first type with a row decides) and `default`. A calm one pays no heed and is met only when walked into; a shy one runs once it senses the player; a curious one comes and watches; an aggressive one charges and meets them by running into them. A place's area is the later of `world.json`'s areas that holds a spot, so a cave's area listed after the hill over it is the cave's; its scripts are `scripts/<key>.txt`, and their own `OnEnter` runs as the player comes out of a door or a `warpto` onto one of its markers. The game reads `world.json`'s `areas` and `maps` itself (leniently: the baker reads the whole file strictly) to make each open map with its areas, and opens `baked/` beside them for the land where it is there. `tools/ContentCheck` holds them to their rules ("The content check" in CLAUDE.md, the tool's README).
- **What a Galar place adds** (plan 33 · GL2): a zone's `rustle` (rows of a table as `slots` are, met by touching a patch of its grass that shakes now and then, never seen walking about; left out where the grass never rustles), a row's `weather` (the `FieldWeather` names it is met under, `["Rain", "Thunderstorm"]`; left out, under any) and a place's `evolutionSites` (`["Stone Arch"]`: a level-up anywhere in the area counts as at them, as a grid overlay's do). **`calendar.json`** (`WorldCalendarFile`, read by `OpenWorld/OpenWeather.cs`'s `OpenCalendar`) is an open region's weather by area and day: `places` (area keys of `world.json`) and `days`, 366 rows from the first of January, each with a `FieldWeather` name for every place; an area it keeps no column for keeps `world.json`'s `weather`. Galar's is the Wild Area's eight parts, a table of ours made once by a rule of seasons and runs of days (`docs/mechanics/rulings.md`, "The Wild Area"). The baker reads none of these.

**What is baked** (`RegionBake`; the game's side is `OpenWorld/BakedChunk.cs` and `BakedRegion.cs`): every chunk of the extent at three levels, as `baked/<level>/<x>_<z>.chunk`, and `baked/index.json` (`BakedIndexFile`: the format, the region, the chunk's side and level 0's cell in millimetres, each level's error bound in millimetres, every chunk as `[x, z]`, and `source`, a hash of the descriptions' text). Level 0 is surface nets at half a metre (every vertex pulled onto the surface and rounded to the millimetre, the chunk's own ground, an apron of a cell's row on its west and north that overlaps its neighbours' last row, and skirts hung under its border, decision 13); levels 1 and 2 are level 0 thinned to 6 cm and 30 cm (decision 16: edges collapsed onto one of their ends by Garland and Heckbert's quadrics, never across a hole, a turned face, two covers the level keeps apart, or the two rows of cells along the chunk's border, so every vertex is level 0's and a chunk meets a neighbour of any level on the very same vertices; they carry no apron and no skirts). A chunk file is "OWCK", the format (1), then whole numbers only in zigzag seven-bit bytes: its column and row, level, error, the vertices (places as steps from the one before, starting at the chunk's north-west corner, then three normals a vertex in 127ths, then a cover a vertex, `OpenCover`), the triangles (their count, the ground's, the apron's, then the indices as steps; the rest are skirts) and the water (a byte for any, then sixteen by sixteen cells of 2 m, each nought for none or its surface's step from the last plus one). The collision mesh is level 0's own ground. `--check` bakes again and compares to the byte, ending with 1 where they differ (a file changed, missing, or a chunk the region doesn't have); the bake is the same on any machine.

**The navigation mesh** (plan 32 · OW4; `tools/OpenWorldBake/NavBake.cs`, the game's side `OpenWorld/OpenNavMesh.cs`) is `baked/navmesh.bin`, baked with every chunk (a bake of some chunks alone writes none) from level 0's own ground and the map's boxes' tops: the places a body stands with its clearance, one to a column of the world's own 0.5 m grid and layer (a cave's floor and the hill over it are two), joined east and south through the walked surface, covered by rectangles of up to 4 m a side within one chunk (each a polygon, exactly convex, its corners at the columns' edges at the height of the ground there and its hub at its middle), linked across the sides two share and, off the mesh, by a step up or a drop down probed past an open side (one way: a ledge is a way down only). The file is "OWNM", the format (1), then whole numbers only in zigzag seven-bit bytes: the vertices (places as steps from the place before), the polygons (each its chunk's column and row, a byte for whether it is swum and then the water's surface, its hub, its corners' count and the corners as steps from the corner before) and the links (each its polygon from as a step from the link before's, its polygon to as a step from that, its flags, `OpenNavFlags`: walk, swim, drop, climb, glide, a byte for whether it is off the mesh, and its two places: the shared side's ends, or where it is taken and where it lands). `--check` holds it to the byte as it holds the chunks; `--nav <out dir> [area or box:x0,z0,x1,z1] [x,z x,z ...]` prints what was found and draws the mesh and the paths between the pairs of places given over the region's plan.

## Voice packs

Plan 26 · V2. A pack is the spoken lines of one language, never in git (`/voice/` is ignored): a folder `voice/<language>/` beside the executable, or at the repository's root while the game is developed (`VoiceLibrary.FoldersFor`), or anywhere with the switch `--voices <folder>` (the folder with `pack.json` in it, or one holding a folder per language), which also sets the options' VOICES row to ALL for that run (ALL is the row's own default since 2026-11-12; with no pack the text shows as ever and the log says so once); `docs/voice/playing.md` says what to run. It holds a file to a take and `pack.json` listing them (`Audio/VoiceLibrary.cs`, `VoicePackFile`):

```json
{
  "version": 1,
  "language": "en",
  "lines": [
    { "key": "script:Twinleaf.Rival#3.0", "voice": "rival", "file": "rival/script+Twinleaf.Rival#3.0.wav", "textHash": "1f0c9a2e", "fileHash": "7d03b1c4" }
  ]
}
```

- `key` is the line's key in `Data/voice/lines.json` (plan 17 · N4's address), or a variant's (`…~starter=Piplup`, `…~assistant=Dawn`), which the game asks for where the line names those.
- `voice` is the cast's voice. A line said by whoever is spoken to (`@self` in the catalogue) may have one take, which any voice speaks, or a take for each voice, of which the game plays the speaker's.
- `file` is relative to the pack: `<voice>/<key>.<ext>`, the colons of both written `+` (`VoicePacks.FileOf`). The game reads WAV (16- or 24-bit PCM or 32-bit float, mono or stereo, any rate, taken to the DS's 32,768 Hz); and Ogg Vorbis (`.ogg`, any rate, mono or stereo, through NVorbis, `Audio/OggVoiceDecoder.cs`) the same way; a file with no decoder, or one that cannot be read, counts as missing.
- `textHash` is the hash of the English the take was made from (`VoiceCatalogue.HashOf`, the catalogue's `hash`). A line whose English has changed since is **stale** and never played: the text shows alone until the take is made again.
- `fileHash` (FNV-1a of the bytes), `engine`, `seed` and `sample` are kept for `VoiceTool check` and for making a take again; the game reads none of them.

`dotnet run --project tools/VoiceTool -- manifest [folder]` writes `pack.json` from the files a folder holds (each stamped with its line's English now), `check [folder]` holds a pack to the game (every key a line's, every file there, decoding, within the voice bus's loudness window of −24 to −18 dB with its peak under −1 dB, starting and ending at nothing; stale takes listed apart) and `vowels [folder] --voice <id>` or `--all` writes a pack of stand-in takes of synthesised vowels, to try the voices in the game before anything is recorded.

## Recorded battles

A battle kept on the Vs. Recorder (plan 08 · P10; `Battle/Sim/BattleRecordFile.cs`) is a JSON file in `replays/` beside the saves (`SavePaths.Replays`), written by the game and never by hand, named by the day and the foe (`20261020-YoungsterTristan.json`, then `-2`, `-3` for more the same day). It holds everything the rules need to fight the battle again with no screen:

- `fileVersion` (the file's shape, 1; a later one is refused) and `dataVersion` (`GameDataFiles.Version`: sixteen hexadecimal digits of a hash of `species.json`, `moves.json`, `abilities.json`, `items.json` and `trainers.json`; a file of any other is refused, since a move or a species that changed would play another battle);
- `rules` (`Platinum` or `Modern`), `format`, `kind`, `playerName`, `day`;
- `player` and `wild`: each Pokémon as it began, `{ "pokemon": <a save's Pokémon>, "sleepTurns", "toxicCounter" }`; `trainers` and `partner`: `id`, `name`, `trainerClass`, `prizeMoney`, `doubleBattle`, `ai`, `items`, the four flags of the later mechanics, `look` and `party` (its Pokémon the same way), with `firstTrainerIndex` for the one that leads;
- the battle's own flags (`cannotFlee`, `firstBattle`, `specialBalls`, `frontier`, `noBattleItems`, `offersSwitch`, `keyStone`, `zRing`, `dynamaxBand`, `teraOrb`) and `conditions` (`terrain`, `night`, `weather`, `trickRoom`, `badges`, `money`, `player`, `inverse`, `fishing`, and `caught`, the National numbers of the wild species already caught);
- `seed` (the battle's `BattleRandom`), `answers` (each request's choices: `side`, `slot`, `kind`, `move`, `targetSide`, `targetSlot`, `switchTo`, `item`, and the later mechanics' flags), `result` and `turns`.

A file that can't be read is `Damaged`; `BattleRecordFile.Read` says which of the three refusals it is and `Notice` the line a list shows for it.

## Remix files

`Data/remix/<region>/` (plan 13 · V15 to V19; `Models/Remix.cs`; `docs/mechanics/rulings.md`, "Remix"), Sinnoh's today, read through `GameDataFiles` only in a run with `RunRules.Remix` on. Written by hand, in our own designs: nothing of a game's or of a fan hack's is transcribed. `Data/trainers.json` and the area files are never patched.

- **`trainers.json`**: `{ "trainers": [ ... ] }`, a team to an entry, in the order their Pokémon are seeded by. `id` is the trainer's id in `trainers.json` (`leader_roark`, a rival's battle once for each starter the player can choose: `rival_route_201_piplup` is the team against a player who chose Piplup). `batch` names the session that wrote it (`V15` to `V19`), `note` says what the team is built round. `ai` (the trainer AI's flags by name) and `items` (what the trainer uses in battle) are the table's own when left out. `party` is one to six Pokémon, each `species`, `form` (left out for the species' own), `level` (never above the table's own highest for the trainer: Remix is harder by species, not by levels), `moves` (one to four, each one the species can know by `Models/Legality`), `item`, `ability` (one of its own; left out, the one its personality gives), `nature`, `gender` (left out, its personality's), `ivs` and `evs` (six numbers each, HP, Attack, Defense, Sp. - **`wild.json`**: `{ "areas": { "<area key>": { ... } } }`, an area's tables by the key of its area file (`route_201`), each with its `batch` and the area file's own tables, `land`, `water`, `oldRod`, `goodRod`, `superRod`, in the area files' shape: the same number of slots as the area's own table, each slot `species`, `level` and (on the water and the rods) `maxLevel`, the same levels as the area's own slot, weighted by its place as the area's are. ## Battle puzzles

`Data/puzzles/*.json` (plan 13 · V26; `Models/Puzzle.cs`, `PuzzleFile`), one puzzle to a file, read in the order of the files' names. `id` (letters and digits: the table's words are `puzzles.<id>.title` and `.hint`), `rules` (`Platinum` or `Modern`), `goal` (`Win`, `Survive` or `Catch`) and `turns`, `seed`, `player` and `foe` (each a list of Pokémon with `species`, `form`, `level`, `nature`, `ability`, `item`, `moves`, and the `hp` and `status` it starts with), `wild` (the foe a wild Pokémon), `foeName`, `foeClass` and `foeLook` (the trainer), `foeMoves` (the foe's move each turn by name, the last for every turn after), `weather`, `trickRoom`, `balls` (Poké Balls in the bag), `rolls` (each a `kind` of `RollKind` and the `values` it comes out as in turn), and `solution` and `decoy` (lists of answers, each a `move` by name, a `switch` by the party's place, or an `item` by name, given to whatever the battle asks next). `PuzzleTests` plays every solution and decoy and replays the solution's record; `Puzzles.Problems` names what the core couldn't play as written.

## The gift box

`Data/gifts.json` (plan 40 · GE7, `Models/GiftBox.cs`, schema `docs/schemas/gifts.schema.json`; ours, read strictly). `gifts` is a list, each gift with its `id` (its words in `ui.json` are `giftBox.gift.<id in camel case>.sender`, `.title`, `.card`), `when` (every term given must hold: `dates`, days of the year `MM-dd` or windows `MM-dd..MM-dd`; `festival`, a festival's id; `hallOfFame`, a region's id; `caught`, species caught; `hours`, hours played; `anniversary`; `words`, two phrase keys told at a Gift Runner), `yearly` (taken once a year, or once each anniversary, instead of once for good), `picture` (`kind` `pokemon`, `egg`, `item`, `seal`, `backdrop` or `ribbon`, and its `name`) and `script`, a script of `scripts/gifts.txt`. **A pack's `gifts.json` is laid over the game's by id**: a gift of an id the game has replaces it, a new id is added after the game's (with the pack's own `scripts/gifts.txt` and `text/<language>/ui.json` words).

## The trade board

`Data/trade-board.json` (plan 40 · GE8, `Models/TradeBoard.cs`, schema `trade-board.schema.json`; ours, read strictly): the made-up trainers' `names`, their `looks` (of `characters.json`), the `languages` a trainer from abroad speaks and the `items` a Pokémon offered may hold. The board draws from them by the date and the region with `SplitMix`, never `Dice`.

## Festivals

`Data/festivals.json` (plan 40 · GE9, `Models/Festivals.cs`, schema `festivals.schema.json`; ours, read strictly; not the weather's `calendar.json`). Each festival has an `id` (its flag is `FLAG_FESTIVAL_<ID>`), a `region` and `town` (none for the days every region keeps), its days (`from` and `to`, `MM-dd`, both kept, a window that may cross the year's end; or `easter`, two counts of days from Easter Sunday), `night` and its `stall`, a counter of `marts.json`. No festival keeps 1 June, the harness's day (`FestivalTests`).

## Pennant Hall

`Data/plaza.json` (plan 41 · EG3; `Models/Plaza.cs`, `PlazaFile`; its schema `docs/schemas/plaza.schema.json`) is the hub's plaza, written by hand and read strictly: `ranks` (the pennants earned each rank asks, the first 0), `pitches` (how many pitches each rank has), `places` (each pitch's booth by its north-west tile in `Hub_Plaza`, three tiles by three, its keeper standing in its middle and its counter the tile south of them), `spots` (where the day's guests stand, `x`, `y` and `facing`), `guestClasses` (the trainer classes a guest is drawn from, as the regions' tables write them), `guestLooks` (the look a guest of a class stands in, one of each list by the guest's spot), `guestWord` and `guestWin` (the pennants a guest's first word of the day and a battle won pay), and `stalls`, each with `id` (its words `hubPlaza.stall.<id>.name` and `.keeper` in `ui.json`), `keeper` (a look of `characters.json`), `region` (whose Hall of Fame opens it; none for any), `rank`, `awning` (its booth's colour, `#rrggbb`) and `goods` (what it sells for pennants: `item`, `price`, and the `rank` it is sold from). A stall's service is its keeper's script in `Data/scripts/Hub_Plaza.txt`, told apart by the stall's place in the list (`plaza stall`), so a new stall is a row at the end and a label of that script. Comments are allowed.

## The Week's Work

`Data/missions.json` (plan 41 · EG6; `Models/MissionBoard.cs`, `MissionsFile`; its schema `docs/schemas/missions.schema.json`) is the hub's board of missions, written by hand and read strictly: `plain` and `timedCount` (how many missions a week posts with no clock and timed), `timedBonus` (the pennants a timed one pays more), `kinds` (each a `kind` of `MissionKind`, its `weight`, the `least` and `most` it asks, `perOne` pennants for each thing asked and a `bonus`, whether it may be `timed` and for how many `minutes`, and `regions` alone it may be drawn in, every finished one where none are named), `types` (the types a catch of a type may ask for), `places` (each region's by its id: `catch`, areas with wild Pokémon in their grass, and `landmarks`, areas to come to, by area key) and `items` with `itemOneIn` (what a mission may pay beside its pennants, one in that many). Comments are allowed.

## Charms

`Data/charms.json` (plan 40 · GE5, decision 3; `Models/Charms.cs`, `CharmsFile`; its schema `docs/schemas/charms.schema.json`) is which charm a Pokédex filled earns, written by hand and read strictly: `regional`, the charms the regional Pokédexes seen whole earn in the order they are earned (the first regional Pokédex the player completes, whichever region's it is, earns the first; a diploma held counts as its Pokédex seen whole), and `national`, what the National Pokédex caught whole earns. Each is an item of `items.json` by its name, a key item; one in the bag has been given, so nothing else is saved. Comments are allowed. The script's `charm due` and `charm give` read it (`docs/scripts.md`, "The charms"), and what each charm does is the rules' (`docs/mechanics/rulings.md`, "The charms, critical captures and EXP for a catch").
