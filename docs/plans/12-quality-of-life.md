# Plan 12 · Quality of life

Written 2026-10-06, before any session.

**Goal**: what a PC game in 2026 is expected to have and this one lacks: a save that can't be lost to a crash and lives in the player's own folder, keys and a controller that can be rebound with hints that follow the device in hand, a mouse that works, accessibility settings for sight, light, motion and hearing, helpers in battle (what a move will do, what the field is doing, a turn the game plays for you), the conveniences later games added (the boxes from anywhere, a sorted bag, a line that says what to do next, a jukebox) and a screenshot key with a photo mode. Nothing here changes what a Platinum save plays like unless the player switches it on: every addition is a row of the options, off by default or following the game's rules preset, and the default shots of the harness draw what they drew.

## Where we are

- **Saves** (`Core/SaveManager.cs`): `SaveGame` writes `savegame.json` in the working directory with one `File.WriteAllText`, so a crash in the middle of it leaves half a file, and `LoadGame` turns every exception into null, so a damaged file shows the title with no CONTINUE card (`TitleScreen(SaveData?)`, read once in `GameEngine.Initialize` as `titleSave`). `GameSettings.Load` falls back to defaults with a console line, `Save` writes `settings.json` wherever the game started, and `FirstRun` says there was none. `Program.Main` loops on `WindowShouldClose()` and `QuitRequested` and saves nothing when the window's close button is pressed (Esc is not the exit key: `SetExitKey(Null)`). The engine saves through `SaveCurrentGame(quiet)` from the save screen (`SaveScreen.TakeRequest`) and SAVE AND QUIT; `BuildSave` makes the data. No test round-trips a whole `SaveData`. `SavedPokemonData` has no EVs, id, original trainer or met data: plan 07 · O2 owes those and `SaveVersion`; plan 02 · S1's `StoryMigration` and `SaveData.Place()` bring old content up to date. `tools/ShotHarness` makes its out dir the working directory so it never touches a real save.
- **Input** (`Core/InputManager.cs`): every binding is a literal. `GameAction` is Up, Down, Left, Right, Confirm, Cancel, Menu and Run, and since plan 02 · S2 `Item` (the registered key item), `Poketch` and `PoketchApp`; the arrows and W A S D, Z and Space, X and Esc, Enter and Tab, Shift, C, P and O, and pad 0's d-pad, four face buttons, Start and the right shoulder (the Pokétch's next app), with no stick. Three reads bypass it: F11 and Alt+Enter in `Program.cs`, Enter in `BattleEngine.Menus.cs` (`ConfirmPressed`) and M in `GameEngine.Update` (mute). Hints are `ModernUi.Hints(rightX, y, (Key, Label)[])` with literal names ("Z", "Esc") in seven `ModernUi.*.cs` files, `OptionsScreen` and `EvolutionScreen`, and the title's "PRESS Z OR ENTER". `UiIcons` has 17 `UiIcon`s, none a pad button. Plan 08 · P1 owns Platinum's Battle Scene and Battle Style rows and leaves Button Mode to rebinding; plan 08 · P2 owns the speed-up, auto-advance, autorun and text frames.
- **Mouse**: no file reads it. Every screen's logic is in methods that take no input (`StartMenu.Move` and `Confirm`, `BagScreen.MoveCursor`, `PCScreen.Move`, `ChoiceBox.Move`, `Confirm` and `Back`, `BattleEngine.MoveCursor`), `ModernUi.RowRect` gives a list row its rectangle and `UiNav.Window` a list its window; the battle's command buttons, move cards and targets (`ModernUi.Battle.cs`: `MoveMenu`, `TargetMenu`) and the choice box have no rectangles of their own. `GameEngine.Draw` fits the 3840×2160 `virtualScreen` into the window (`scale`, `destX`, `destY`) and keeps nothing of that transform.
- **Accessibility**: no setting. HP is told by `ModernUi.HpColor` alone and a type by `TypePill`'s colour (`Palette.GetTypeColor`); the type scale is fixed (style guide, "Typography"). The battle's screen flash (`FxList.Flash`, drawn in `BattleRenderer.DrawField`), the two flashes before a battle (`SceneTransition.FlashSeconds`), lightning (`WeatherFx.Lightning`, `GameEngine.LightningNow`), the evolution's `Appearance.Flash` and the ball's are unconditional; so are `WorldRenderer.ShakeCamera`, the shake and the `ShotKind.Critical` punch-in in `BattleCamera.Update`, `UiReveal`'s slides and `QualityProfile.DepthOfField`. A sound has `SoundEntry.PlayedWhen`, documentation rather than a caption; tests hear sounds through `AudioManager.Listen`.
- **Help in battle**: `MoveMenu` draws name, type, PP, category, power, accuracy and description, and the matchup is learned from the line afterwards (`HitSounded`, `MoveShown.SuperEffective`). `DamageCalculator.Effectiveness(attacker, defender, move, rules)` answers with `Battler.Types` and the defender's ability; `BattleEngine.Pokedex.IsCaught(dexNumber)` and `EnemySlots` are at hand. `BattleMenuState` is Main, Moves, SwitchPokemon, SelectBagItem, SelectTarget and Message. The field's numbers are in `FieldState` (`Weather`, `WeatherTurns`, `TrickRoomTurns`, `GravityTurns`), `SideState` (`ReflectTurns`, `LightScreenTurns`, `TailwindTurns`, `Spikes`, `ToxicSpikes`, `StealthRock`) and `Battler.Volatile`, mirrored to the screen's battlers at every menu (`BattleMirror.Publish`); no event of the log names an ability or an item, only `Said`'s text, which plan 06 · R9's `AiMemory` reads for the AI (the ability and item a line has named for the Pokémon in a place, as the original's `BattleAI_SetAbility` and `BattleAI_SetHeldItem` record them, and the moves seen, forgotten on a switch). `CoreSetup.PlayerController` is fixed at setup; `TrainerAi.Instance` is the opponents' and a tag partner's chooser, Platinum's own since R9: a Pokémon thinks with its trainer's flags (`Battler.Trainer`, `Trainer.Ai`, through `AiThinking`), one with no trainer (the player's own places have none) picks a usable move at random as a wild one does, and every roll it makes is `RollKind.AiChoice` on the battle's own generator. The engine answers each asked place through `BeginChoosing` and `Commit`, so whatever it submits is in `BattleRecord`.
- **Conveniences**: `GameState.PCStorage` is entered by `common.PC` alone; `StartMenuChoice` is Pokedex, Pokemon, Bag, Trainer, Save, Options, SaveAndQuit and Quit, in Platinum's order (style guide, "Field menus and notices"). `Inventory.AllItems` is insertion order and `GetPocketItems(pocket)` copies it; `BagScreen` scrolls with `HeldKey`. Nothing tells a returning player where to go: `StoryState` has flags, variables, badges and the starters, `OverlayPerson.Dialog` is fixed lines, and `Op` has no command for it; plan 06 · R12's Journal records the past. `MusicLibrary.Ids` (each `Song` with its `Title` and `HasNight`), `SoundBank.Entries`, `Cries` in every `CryMode` and `Ambience.Entries` are heard only where the game plays them.
- **Pictures**: only the harness writes one (`Capture` reads `virtualScreen` by reflection, then `ExportImage`). `WorldRenderer.PanCamera` and `ReleaseCamera` are plan 02's scene entry points; `MapScene.ViewOf(map)` gives a map its `FieldView` (pitch, FOV, distance: the three `FieldCamera`s); `BattleCamera` has `Current` and `Kind` and no way to hold a shot; of `Emote`, Wave, Nod and Cheer are played by nothing yet (style guide, "Known gaps after G11").

## Design

**Platinum's defaults don't move.** Each addition is an `OptionRow` kept by `GameSettings` in `settings.json`, with its `Describe` line and `Change` case in `OptionsScreen` and its `ApplySettings` push, and is off unless chosen. The four that the newest games made their default (move hints, the boxes from anywhere, the bag's sort key shown, the next-objective line) are three-valued, `Off`, `Rules`, `On` (a new `RulesDefault` enum), where `Rules` follows the save's `RulesPreset`: a Platinum game gets Platinum, a Modern game gets Sun and Moon's and Sword and Shield's ways, and either player can override. That keeps rulings.md's first ruling (no option changes the ruleset) while the ruleset still decides the default. Every existing harness shot is taken with the defaults, so `diff` against a run before each session is the test that nothing moved; the new rows get shots of their own. Whatever is drawn stays a function of `FrameClock` and `Dice`.

**Saves** (`Core/SaveManager.cs`, a new `Core/SavePaths.cs`). A save is written to `savegame.json.tmp` and moved over the old file (`File.Move(…, overwrite: true)`, which is a rename on every platform), after the old one has been copied to `savegame.bak`. The file carries `Checksum` (FNV-1a of the rest, a field like any other, so old files without one still load) and a loader that says what happened (`SaveLoad`: `Ok`, `None`, `Recovered` from the backup, `Damaged` with both gone), which the title turns into Platinum's notice before its menu, in our own words. Where the files live is `SavePaths.Folder`: portable (the working directory) when `portable.txt` or a `savegame.json` is there, otherwise the per-user folder (`%APPDATA%\PokemonPlatinum`, `$XDG_DATA_HOME/pokemon-platinum`, `~/Library/Application Support/PokemonPlatinum`); `--portable` and `--save <folder>` on the command line win over both. `settings.json` and the screenshots folder go with it. The harness writes `portable.txt` into its out dir first, so it stays where it is. Slots are files `save1.json` to `save3.json` plus `autosave.json`; a `savegame.json` found in the folder becomes slot 1 on first run. The title's CONTINUE card becomes a column of `ModernUi.SaveSummary` cards, one per slot that exists, with DELETE behind a prompt; the save screen writes the slot the game was loaded from. Autosave is an option (off, Platinum's way) that writes `autosave.json` through `SaveCurrentGame(quiet: true)` after `EndBattle`, a heal, a badge, a catch and `ArriveOnMap`, with a small disc in the corner while it writes. The close button asks (a `WindowClosing` state over whatever is on screen: SAVE AND QUIT, QUIT, KEEP PLAYING) instead of ending the loop. `tools/SaveTool` dumps, validates, migrates and diffs saves without the game. All of it is GPU-free: the paths, the checksum, the loader and the slot list are plain classes tests drive on temp folders; only the cards draw.

**Input** (`Core/InputBindings.cs`, `InputManager`). A binding set is data: for each `GameAction`, a list of keys and a list of pad buttons, plus the pad index, the stick's dead zone (0.35 by default; `GetGamepadAxisMovement` on the left stick feeds Up to Right) and a `ButtonMode` (Platinum's Normal, Start=X and L=A as three presets that set the lists). `GameAction` gains `PageLeft` and `PageRight` (the shoulders and Q and E, for the pockets, the boxes and the Pokédex's pages; the right shoulder is `PoketchApp`'s too, which only the field reads), `Fullscreen` (F11, Alt+Enter), `Mute` (M) and `Screenshot` (F12). `InputManager` reads the set, remembers which device pressed last (`LastDevice`) and gives each action its glyph (`Glyph(GameAction)`: a key cap with the key's name, or a pad sign drawn by `UiIcons` from new `UiIcon`s, `PadA` to `PadStart` and `Dpad`, our own simple signs). The three stray reads go through it: `Program.cs` asks for `Fullscreen`, the mute asks for `Mute`, and the battle's Enter becomes the `Menu` action, which Enter is bound to, so the style guide's "Enter answers a battle's menu" holds and follows a rebinding. `ModernUi.Hints` takes `(GameAction, string Label)` pairs and the old overload goes, so no literal key name can be written; `NameEntry` stays cursor-driven (plan 04 · G10, decision 6). The Controls page is a screen of its own under OPTIONS (`ControlsScreen`, `GameState.Controls`): one row per action with its keys and buttons, press-to-bind capture (the next key or button pressed; Esc never binds), a conflict shown in red, and RESET TO DEFAULTS. Bindings round-trip through `settings.json` with the other rows. Capture and the device memory are the only raylib reads; the set and its checks (every action bound, no two actions read in the same state on one key) are GPU-free.

**The pointer** (`UI/Kit/UiPointer.cs`). The window-to-layout transform is the one `GameEngine.Draw` computes, kept as a new `GameEngine.Viewport` and inverted by `UiPointer.ToLayout`. Each screen gains a `HitTest(Vector2) → int?` that says which card, row, button or answer lies under a point, built from the same rectangles it draws with (`ModernUi.RowRect`, the cards' rectangles, new `ModernUi.Battle` rectangles for the four commands, the move cards and the target cards, `ChoiceBox` rows); hovering moves the cursor through the screen's own `Move`, a click confirms, a right click cancels, the wheel scrolls through `UiNav.Window`, and a click anywhere reads a line on. The pointer is drawn (an arrow of `UiShapes`) only after it moved, hides after two seconds still or any key, and its last device counts as `LastDevice`, so the hints turn to key caps when the mouse is in use. The hit tests are GPU-free and `MenuScreenTests` drives them with points.

**Accessibility.** Sight: a `ColourVision` row (Normal, Protan, Deutan, Tritan) remaps every colour the kit draws through one function on the way out (`ModernUi.Seen(Color)`, applied in `UiShapes` and `UiFonts`, never per screen), using the Machado matrices scaled to the palette; HP bars under half gain hatching and the numbers are always shown; every `TypePill` carries its type's sign, 18 new `UiIcon`s of our own; a `TextSize` row (Normal, Large) that `DialogueManager` and `ModernUi.MessageBox` follow, wrapping to three lines where the panel has room; and a `HighContrast` row that draws the text box opaque with white on black. Light and motion: `ReduceFlashes` holds every flash to a quarter and makes it a fade (`FxList.Flash`, `SceneTransition`'s two, `WeatherFx.Lightning`, the evolution's and the ball's), and `ReduceMotion` stills `ShakeCamera`, the battle camera's shake and punch-in, snaps `UiReveal` to its end, turns `DepthOfField` off and stills the sway through a new scale on `SwayOffset` in `FieldShaders` (the `wind` uniform only adds gusts to a breeze). Hearing: `Captions` shows a pill beside the text box naming each sound as it plays, from a new `Caption` field on `SoundEntry` written in our own words ("a Poké Ball is thrown"), a cry by species ("Starly cries") and the field's thunder and doors, hooked in `AudioManager.PlaySound` and `PlayCry` behind a caption queue. Motor: `HoldToConfirm` asks for a 0.4 s hold on dangerous answers (deleting a save, releasing a Pokémon, a new game over a save). The interface's whole scale is not changed: every screen lays out in 1920×1080 units anchored to its edges, and a factor over the 2D camera would push panels off the picture; the text size covers what reads small. The style guide changes before the code: "Typography" for the second scale, "Shapes and components" for the hatched bar, the signed pills and the opaque box, "Move effects" and "Camera" for the reduced flash and shake, each with the setting named.

**Help in battle.** The move card shows the matchup as a pill (`MoveHints`, `Off`, `Rules`, `On`): "Super effective", "Not very effective", "No effect", worked out by `DamageCalculator.Effectiveness` on the screen's battlers (the same as the core's whenever a menu is open) for each foe in `EnemySlots`, and per target in the target menu; nothing for a status move; only once the foe's species is caught (`Pokedex.IsCaught`), as Sun and Moon have it, with a type-only answer for a species merely seen as a choice of the row. An inverted battle, when plan 14 brings one, shows the inverted answer because the function does. The information panel is `BattleMenuState.Info`, opened from the main menu with the `Menu` action: a GPU-free `Battle/BattleInfo.cs` turns `Core.Field`, each side's `SideState`, each battler's stages and `Volatile` and a `Revealed` set into lines; the set is filled from two new log events, `AbilityShown(Place, string)` and `ItemShown(Place, string)`, emitted by the core where the original names them (Trace, Frisk, Forewarn, a berry or Leftovers going off), which `AiMemory` then reads in place of matching the lines' text, so the panel never says what the player hasn't been told (a test holds it). `ModernUi.Battle.cs` draws it as a two-column sheet over the dimmed stage with a strip of the field's state under the foe's box. Auto-battle is a fifth command, AUTO, drawn under RUN only while `AutoCommand` is on (the style guide's "Battle panels" and the layout line change first), and never in R9's battles of their own where a ball or bait is all there is to choose (`BattleKind.Safari`, `PalPark`; the `CatchingLesson` plays itself): one press has R9's AI choose for every asked place as it chooses for a tag partner, thinking with the flags decision 5 names (`TrainerAi` alone picks at random for the player's places, which have no trainer), and answers through `Commit`, so the answers are the player's in `BattleRecord`. It rolls on a generator of its own, seeded from the battle's seed and the turn, and thinks on a copy of `AiMemory`: `AiThinking` draws from the battle's generator today, and a roll taken from outside would move it, so a replay would part from the battle. Holding it keeps answering turn after turn until Cancel is pressed, a Pokémon of the player's faints or the battle ends, and it never throws a ball or switches. The rules are not touched for it; the core only lets its AI be asked from outside with flags, a generator and a memory of the caller's. A HELP page under OPTIONS shows the type chart as a grid of the signed pills (`TypeChart.GetEffectiveness`), the current controls from the bindings, and a few how-to notes of our own.

**Conveniences.** `StartMenuChoice.Boxes` (`BoxLink`, `Off`, `Rules`, `On`) opens `PCScreen` over the field everywhere but where the story forbids it (a new `Map.NoBoxLink` flag, for the Distortion World and the League's rooms); the style guide's "Start menu" paragraph changes first. The bag keeps a sort key per pocket in the save (`SaveData.BagOrder`; `Inventory.Order(pocket)`: as stored, by name, by kind, by quantity), cycled by the `Menu` action in the bag and shown as a `Tag`; the TM pocket by name is by number since the names carry it; left and right jump to the next letter when sorted by name; plan 06 · R11's bag in battle reuses the order. A `Story/Objectives.cs` gives one line for what to do next from an `objective` Op that stores an id in `VAR_OBJECTIVE` (a save carries it), each chapter of plan 02 adding its own; the line shows on the start menu's panel, the Trainer Card and `SaveSummary` (`NextObjective`, `Off`, `Rules`, `On`), and Mom and the nurse say it as an aside when asked (`common.Healer`, `common.Nurse`, through an `{objective}` placeholder). Rumours are `common.Rumour`: a script whose `if badges` and `if flag` branches give townsfolk without a script of their own lines that follow the story, in our own words; an overlay opts a person in with `"script": "Rumour"`. The jukebox is `JukeboxScreen` (`GameState.Jukebox`, from the title's menu and from OPTIONS): tabs of songs, sounds, cries and ambience from the four catalogues, the night and low-HP arrangements selectable, every entry unlocked from the title and, in a game, as heard (`SaveData.Heard`).

**Pictures.** `GameAction.Screenshot` has `GameEngine.CaptureScreen()` write the 3840×2160 `virtualScreen` as a PNG named by the time into `screenshots/` beside the saves, with a toast and a shutter sound (a `SoundBank` entry with its row in `docs/sound-effects.md`); the harness's `Capture` reads through the same method. Photo mode is `GameState.Photo` (`UI/PhotoScreen.cs`, GPU-free state: pan, zoom, tilt, time, weather, filter, frame, pose), opened from the start menu over the field and from the main menu over a battle. The clocks stop (`FieldLife.Now` already does under a menu; `BattleAnimator.Update` isn't called), the interface hides, and the renderer takes a `PhotoView`: in the field a pan of up to six tiles through `PanCamera`, a `FieldView` of the photo's own between the three `FieldCamera`s' lenses and a tilt of ±8° (`WorldRenderer` asks `ViewOf` everywhere, so the photo's view goes through it), a time of day through `ArtLook.FieldRigFor` and a weather over `Map.WeatherAt` without changing the game's, filters and frames as `PostSettings` of our own, and the player and the follower posed with Wave, Nod or Cheer; in battle a `BattleCamera.Hold(CameraShot)` and each Pokémon's `PokePose` stepped through its clip. The Poké Finder's side (marked spots, a rating, a reward) waits for plan 10 · F5's wild Pokémon in sight.

## Risks

- **The save's folder moves.** A player who ran the game from a folder and keeps running it there must find their save: the portable rule (a `savegame.json` beside the game wins) is tested first, and `SaveTool` can move a save by hand.
- **Removing the hints overload touches every screen.** Q5 is wide but shallow; the compiler finds every caller, and `diff` of `menus` shows the hints redrawn and nothing else.
- **The mouse pulls layout into logic.** Hit rectangles must be the drawn rectangles: a screen that computes them twice drifts. Each screen's layout moves into a GPU-free method that both the drawing and `HitTest` call.
- **Flash and motion settings reach into the renderers.** Each is a number read once a frame from `Settings`, never a branch inside a shader; with the rows off, the numbers are today's, which `diff` holds.
- **The AUTO command and the move hints change how a battle feels.** Both are off in a Platinum game unless chosen; the auto answers are ordinary answers in the record, so the fuzz and the replays are untouched.
- **New log events.** `AbilityShown` and `ItemShown` add to the log of every battle that reveals one; the harness's shots don't read them, but a test that counts events would.
- **Captions need a line for every sound** and a cry's name for 1025 species; the text is our own and short.

## Decisions for the user

1. **Where saves live.** *Recommended:* the per-user folder, with the portable rule for old installs and the harness. Alternative: stay beside the game, with only the backup and the checksum.
2. **How many slots.** *Recommended:* three and an autosave. Alternative: one save and one backup, as Platinum has.
3. **The three-valued rows.** *Recommended:* `Off / Rules / On`, so a Modern game gets the newest games' conveniences by default and a Platinum game none. Alternative: every row plain off, whatever the rules.
4. **The information panel.** *Recommended:* always available, like the summary: it shows what was said and changes no rule. Alternative: behind a row of its own.
5. **AUTO.** *Recommended:* a fifth command behind `AutoCommand`, off, thinking as Platinum's Gym Leaders do (`Basic`, `EvalAttack`, `Expert`). Alternative: leave it out and let plan 08 · P2's speed-up carry the grinding.
6. **Captions' wording.** *Recommended:* a short caption per sound of our own ("a door opens"). Alternative: the sound's name alone.
7. **The jukebox in the game.** *Recommended:* under OPTIONS with the save's unlocked set, and on the title with everything. Alternative: a radio in the player's room, once plan 08 · P8 furnishes it.
