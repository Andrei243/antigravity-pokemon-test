# Plan 14 · Battle formats of the later games and the venues that use them

Written 2026-10-06, before any session.

**Goal**: the formats Platinum never had, each a change to the rules first and to the screen second: inverse battles and, optional, sky battles (a flag and a filter), hordes (five against one), SOS calls (a second foe joining mid-battle), triple and rotation battles (three places a side, reach by position), and, last and optional, the Battle Royal (four sides; decision 7 asks whether to keep it and the sky battles at all); then the venues that put them to use: Emerald's five other Frontier facilities as a second wing of plan 06 · R18's Battle Frontier, the Pokémon World Tournament as a bracket played at level 50 against Sinnoh's leaders first and every later region's as its plan adds them, and Let's Go's Master Trainers, a master for every species fought one-on-one with that species alone, in a hall of the same wing. Platinum's own battles don't move: Sinnoh's routes and trainers keep Platinum's formats, and every new format is met in the post-game, in a facility, in plan 13's Versus, or in the region it came from.

## Where we are

- **Two places a side, two sides.** `BattleFormat` (`Battle/BattleSetup.cs`) is `Single` and `Double`; `BattleCore`'s constructor makes `PlayerSlots` and `EnemySlots` of the same count from it, and falls back to a single when a side can't field two. `Place.Number` (`Battle/Sim/BattleLog.cs`) counts places as the original does, `Slot * 2 + side`; `FieldState.Places` (`BattleField.cs`) is an array of four read by `FieldState.At` with the same arithmetic, `FieldState.Sides` two `SideState`s, and `BattleCore.ControllerOf` picks a place's chooser by its side (`enemyController`, `playerController`, and since plan 06 · R9 `partnerController` for a partner's place), with `BattleSide` declared in `Battle/BattleAnimator.cs` as `Player, Enemy`. `Battler.Turn.PhysicalFrom` is indexed by `Place.Number` too.
- **Targets by kind, not by distance.** `ResolveTargets` (`BattleCore.Moves.cs`) picks targets from `MoveTarget` and `SlotsOf(Other(side))`; `Reaches` marks `MoveHit.OutOfReach` only for a target that is elsewhere (`Battler.IsElsewhere`). `MoveFlags` (`Data/Enums.cs`) has no flag for reach. `TrainerAi` is Platinum's since plan 06 · R9 (`Battle/Sim/TrainerAi.cs`, its routines in `Battle/Sim/Ai/`): `ChooseAction` scores a trainer's moves against the first of `ActiveFoes(mine)` in a single and against each of the other three on the field in a double (`AllBattlers`, `TrainerAI_MainDoubles`), draws a move at random for a place with no `Trainer` behind it (a wild Pokémon other than a roamer, and the player's own), and `ChooseReplacement` sends in the one `BattleAI_PostKOSwitchIn` picks, party order only as a fallback.
- **What may be chosen** is `WhyNot`, `WhyNotMove` and `UsableMoves` (`BattleCore.Choices.cs`); `WhyNotItem` (`BattleCore.Items.cs`) already refuses a ball when more than one foe stands ("There are two Pokémon out!").
- **The chart** is `TypeChart.GetEffectiveness(attack, defender1, defender2, rules)` (`Data/TypeChart.cs`), read type by type by `DamageCalculator.Matchups` (for `Effectiveness` and `Calculate`), by Conversion 2 in `BattleCore.Unique.cs`, by Stealth Rock in `BattleCore.Switch.cs`, and by the AI's own sums in `TrainerAi` and `AiThinking`; it is given the `Ruleset` and nothing of the battle. `BattleConditions` (`Battle/Sim/Formulas.cs`) carries what the rules need of the world: `Terrain`, `Night`, `HasCaught`, `Weather`, `TrickRoom`, and since plan 06 · R10 of the player: `Badges`, `Money`, `Player`; `GameEngine.BattleConditionsHere()` builds it.
- **Where a format comes from.** `GameEngine.StartTrainerBattle(npc, second, partner, first)` makes a double of `Trainer.DoubleBattle` (`Models/Trainer.cs`; the overlay's `MapFile.TrainerRecord.DoubleBattle`, or the trainer table's, which `TrainerDatabase.Fill` adds), of two trainers at once and of a partner beside the player (plan 06 · R9). `GameEngine.StartWildBattle(WildEncounterEntry)` makes one Pokémon of the entry `Map.RollWildEncounter` returned (a species, a level range, a weight; `Overworld/Tile.cs`) from the engine's `fieldRandom` (a `Dice.New()`), in the form its place gives it (`FormRules.WildForm`, plan 06 · R10), and hands it to `MeetWildPokemon(pokemon, kind, cannotFlee)`, as a Safari battle (`BattleKind.Safari`) while plan 01 · M7's Safari Game is on in the Great Marsh (`SafariGame.Active`); a script's `wildbattle` and `catchinglesson` start theirs there too; `BattleSetup.WildPokemon` is a list already. A script's `battle a and b with c [first] [canlose] [as "id"]` is `Op.Battle` (`ScriptParser`; `Instruction.Other`, `Partner`, `FirstBattle`, `Option`, and `AsTrainer` since plan 02 · S4: the person fights with a team of `trainers.json` by id and is that trainer from then on, as the rival is) and `IScriptHost.Battle(trainer, second, partner, mayLose, first)`; `TrainerDatabase` reads `trainers.json` alone, and no command lets a script choose one Pokémon of the team.
- **The screen.** `BattleAnimator.Slots` is one count for both sides and `views[side, slot]` holds two a side; `BattleRenderer.Spot(side, slot, slots)` lays platforms round `BattleStage.EnemySpot` and `PlayerSpot` (radii 2.4 and 1.2), `SlotFit` shrinks a Pokémon to 0.78 in a double; `ModernUi.Battle.DrawBattle` draws a `CompactBox` for `slot < 2`; `BattleEngine.TargetLayout` is four cards two to a row and `TargetStep` walks them; `BattleCamera.Overview` orbits at `OverviewFov` 24° and `WidestFov` is 40. `BattleArena` (`Overworld/Tile.cs`) is Grass, Forest, Cave, Water, Snow, Sand, Indoors, Gym, League; `BattleArenas.Build` switches on it, `SkyPainter.Draw` paints every outdoor stage's sky, `ArtLook.ArenaRig(spec, hour)` lights it.
- **Replays and the fuzz.** `BattleRecord(Seed, Answers)`, `BattleCore.Record` and `Replay`; `BattleCoreTests.ARecordedBattleReplaysTheSame` plays the game's own menus, and `RandomBattlesAlwaysEndAndLeaveNothingOutOfRange` plays 150 battles, singles and doubles, both sides chosen by `TrainerAi`. `CoreScenario` (`PokemonPlatinumTests`) has `Wild`, `Against`, `Doubles`, `Turn`, `DoubleTurn`, `SwitchTo`, `SendIn`, `Calm` and the places `Mine`, `Foe`, `Mine2`, `Foe2`.
- **Venues.** `Data/world/sinnoh/world.json` opens 85 areas, as far as Celestic Town, Pastoria City and the Great Marsh, Canalave City and Iron Island (plan 01 · M7); the Battle Zone's buildings (`d31_s01` the Tower, `d32_s01` to `d32_s04`) are plan 01 · M10's stand-ins (`docs/world-models.md`) and their rooms M11's. Plan 06 · R18 owns Platinum's five facilities, Battle Points and the Brains, and none of it exists; plan 07 · O6 names Flat 50 and its clauses and plan 13 · V12 `VersusRules` with the same three, and no level scaling exists; the original's trainer table is `Data/trainers.json` (plan 06 · R9, written by the data importer from `res/trainers/data` and read by `TrainerDatabase`: every leader's, the Elite Four's and Cynthia's teams with their rematches, AI flags and items), plan 08 · P7 takes the last hand-typed teams out of the overlays, and P10's record file carries a battle's format, rules and conditions; plan 13 · V13 has a gauntlet of the leaders and V14 the Factory-style rental draw. `MusicRole` (`Audio/MusicDirector.cs`) has no role for a facility or a tournament; `ScriptScreen` is `Starter, Shop, Pc, Travel`; `GameState` has no state beyond the menus; `CharacterStyle.For(npcType)` has the named cast and a few classes, the rest plan 11 · C4 to C6.
- **Pokémon copies**: `BattleMirror.Copy` clones (`Pokemon.Clone()`); `Pokemon.Level` has a private setter and `RecalculateStats()` is public.

## Design

**One core, more places.** `BattleFormat` gains `Horde`, `Sos`, `Triple` and `Rotation` (`Royal` only in B13), and a new `PlacesOf(side)` beside it (an extension method, since `BattleFormat` is an enum) says how many places each side has (1/1, 2/2, 1/5, 1/2, 3/3, 3/3). `BattleCore`'s constructor makes each side's slots from it in place of one `slots`, and its fallback (a double a side can't fill) becomes a new `FallBack` beside `PlacesOf`. `Place.Number` keeps its formula and a new `Place.MaxNumber` (10) sizes `FieldState.Places` and `Battler.Turn.PhysicalFrom` instead of 4. `BattleAnimator.Slots` becomes `SlotsOf(side)` with `views` of five a side, and `BattleEngine` sets both from the core. Nothing a single or a double does changes: `BattleCoreTests` run unchanged, and the harness's `battle`, `doubles` and `demo` modes diff clean against a run made before.

**Every new kind of choice or request** (a shift, a rotation, a place opened by a call) is a value of `ChoiceKind` or a field of `BattleChoice`, refused by `WhyNot` where it can't be made, played by `ARecordedBattleReplaysTheSame`, and given a turn of the fuzz, which plays every format in rotation. Nothing in a format may draw from anything but the battle's `BattleRandom` (a new `RollKind.Call` for an SOS, `RollKind.Target` as now).

**Where a format comes from, and Platinum's defaults.** A trainer's format is a new `Format` on both `TrainerRecord`s (the trainer table's in `Data/TrainerDatabase.cs` and the overlay's `MapFile.TrainerRecord`) and on `Trainer` (`"double"`, `"triple"`, `"rotation"`, `"sky"`; `DoubleBattle` stays and means `"double"`), a wild horde a new `WildEncounterEntry.Count` in the table, an SOS a new `callsForHelp` in the area's overlay (described in `docs/data-files.md`), an inverse battle a word on the script's line or a facility's rule. Platinum's own trainers are single or double in plan 06 · R9's `Data/trainers.json`, and the imported overlays carry Platinum's trainers and tables, so no Sinnoh route gains a later format unless an overlay is edited by hand; the later formats are met at the Battle Zone's post-game characters, in the facilities, in the new zone plan 03 · D12 opens, in plan 13 · V12's Versus, and in the regions they came from (plan 22's Unova, plan 23's Kalos and Alola). `docs/mechanics/rulings.md` gets a section "Formats of the later games" for each rule taken from a later game and each stand-in.

**Flags on the conditions.** A new `BattleConditions.Inverse` inverts the chart in one place, `TypeChart.GetEffectiveness`, which gains an `inverse` argument beside `rules` (2 becomes ½, ½ becomes 2, 0 becomes 2, as X and Y have it), passed through by `Matchups`, Conversion 2, Stealth Rock and the AI's sums, so Anticipation, Wonder Guard, the resist berries, an Expert Belt, `HitSounded`, the lines and the AI's scores follow with no code of their own. A new `BattleConditions.InTheAir` is a sky battle: `WhyNotMove` refuses the grounded moves (those Showdown's move table flags `nonsky`, kept in a new `Data/sky-battle.json` by the importer), and `BattleCore` refuses a side that fields nobody eligible before it starts. Both are kept by plan 08 · P10's record file.

**Reach.** A new `BattleCore.InReach(user, target)` is adjacency by slot: on the other side a place faces the one across from it (slot `2 − theirs` in a triple) and its neighbours, on the user's side the neighbours; a new `MoveFlags.Distance` (Showdown's `distance` flag, read by the importer) reaches anywhere. `ResolveTargets`, the menus' `TargetChoices` and a spread move's list are filtered by it; a move left with no target fails as one whose target is gone does today ("But there was no target...", `BattleCore.Moves.cs`). In a single or a double everything is in reach, so nothing there changes. A `Shift` is a new `ChoiceKind` of its own: the Pokémon at an end trades places with the centre and that is its turn. A side down to one Pokémon has it moved to the centre, so a battle can't stall out of reach (the fuzz holds it). In a rotation battle the three places exist but only the front one `IsActive` for targets, the end of the turn and entry checks; a new `BattleChoice.Rotation` (−1, 0, +1) goes with a Fight, rotations happen at the turn's start in speed order, and a Pokémon rotated out keeps its stages and volatiles, as Black and White have it.

**Hordes and calls.** A horde is five wild Pokémon of the entry's species at levels rolled each (`WildEncounterRules.Level`), one of the player's, every foe chosen for by `TrainerAi`; spread moves take their ×0.75 (`Formulas.BaseDamage`), a ball is refused while more than one stands (`WhyNotItem`, generalised from two), EXP comes per faint as now. An SOS battle is a single whose enemy side has a second, empty place: `BattleCore.Turn.cs` gains a step after the end-of-turn effects where a wild foe may call (`RollKind.Call`: the species' call rate, doubled when its HP is low or its ally fell this turn, the Alola games' numbers), answered by `Arrive` into the empty place with an `Entered` event (`FromBall` false), which the screen shows as a send-out without a ball. A new `FieldState.SosChain` (`BattleField.cs`; `BattleConditions` is what the world hands the battle, and stays so) counts the calls answered this battle, and a new `SosChain.Bonus(chain)` (in `Overworld/WildEncounterRules.cs`, GPU-free) gives the ally's guaranteed IVs, its hidden-ability chance (under the modern `Ruleset` alone, as plan 10 · F12's DexNav gives one) and its extra shiny rolls against the denominator plan 13 · V5's `ShinyDenominator` gives (Platinum's 1 in 8,192 when no run rule changes it). It is a chain of its own beside plan 06 · R13's Poké Radar chain and F12's DexNav chain, sharing none of their counters. The Adrenaline Orb is a hold effect for plan 06 · R28. A caller never calls at a place that is full, so there are never more than two.

**The screen.** `BattleRenderer.Spot` lays n places on an arc round each side's spot (three at 0.6 of a single's size, five at 0.45; style guide, "Stage"); `BattleCamera.Overview` widens its lens by the places in view (up to `WidestFov`), a target shot frames whoever a spread move hits; `ModernUi.Battle` draws a `CompactBox` per place (a horde's five stacked small on the left); `TargetLayout` becomes rows of the format's width and `TargetStep` walks a grid (style guide, "Battle panels": the six-card and the five-and-one target menus, the SHIFT button and the rotation arrows beside FIGHT); the rotation's platforms sit on a turntable that turns as the rotation is shown. `BattleArena.Sky` is the sky battle's stage: platforms of cloud over `SkyPainter`'s sky, a row of the "Arenas (G8)" table, an `ArenaRig` of its own. Everything drawn is a function of `BattleAnimator.Time` and the cue's seed, so two runs draw the same; the frame cost of five Pokémon is measured with `profile` before B4 is called done (the budget is 8 ms on High).

**The venues.** Emerald's facilities are GPU-free rules each (a new folder, `Models/Frontier/`), their screens on the kit, their buildings our own: the Dome's bracket (a new `Bracket`: sixteen entrants, four rounds, drawn by `Dice`, each trainer's card from its team), which the World Tournament reuses at eight; the Palace's `PalaceController : IBattleController` on the player's side (`CoreSetup.PlayerController`, which `BattleEngine` doesn't pass on today, so `BattleSetup` gains a `PlayerController` it hands to the core), choosing by the Pokémon's nature from Emerald's table of attack, defence and support moves, so the player's menus never open; the Arena's judge (a new `ArenaJudge` reads three turns of the log: Mind from the moves chosen, Skill from the hits landed, Body from the HP left) with a new `CoreSetup.TurnLimit` ending a bout and a new `Judged` event naming the winner; the Pike's rooms of chance as a small hand-made map whose doors a script sends through (`Dice` picks the room); the Pyramid's floors generated on a `BattleRandom` seed from pieces of our own, dark under `Overworld/Darkness.cs` with a circle that grows per foe beaten, items as `Map.HiddenItems`, seven floors. Battle Points, streaks, the rental draw and the Brains' challenges at 21 and 49 are R18's; this plan adds its five to R18's record. The World Tournament is a new `Models/Tournament.cs` (GPU-free): an event, a bracket of eight, the player's three at level 50 by `LevelScaling.At(pokemon, 50)` (a clone with its level set and `RecalculateStats`, in `Battle/Sim/LevelScaling.cs`, written once for plan 07 · O6 and plan 13 · V12 to use, or taken from whichever lands first), species and item clauses at the pick, the other matches of a round played headless by the core with `TrainerAi` on both sides so the bracket is real (the entrant in the player's places given as a trainer behind them, since plan 06 · R9's AI thinks only for a place with a `Trainer` and draws at random for the player's own), nothing written to the save but the record and the Battle Points; its teams in a new `Data/tournaments.json` in the shape of the trainer table's `TrainerRecord` (`Data/TrainerDatabase.cs`, AI flags and items included), Sinnoh's eight leaders and Cynthia first (their teams taken from plan 06 · R9's `Data/trainers.json`), every later region's as its plan adds its table.

**Master Trainers** (B14, B15) are Let's Go's: after the Hall of Fame a master for every species battles one-on-one, its Pokémon against one of the player's of the same species, neither side may use the bag, and a master beaten gives a title. The masters are data: a new `Data/master-trainers.json` in the shape of the trainer table's `TrainerRecord`, one record per species (1025), its Pokémon at level 75 as Let's Go's masters' are, written by `tools/DataImporter` from Pokémon Showdown's random-battle sets (the source the importer already pins, MIT), whose nine generations' files at the pinned commit give about two thirds of the species a set (673 by name); the rest know what their level taught them, as `TrainerDatabase.Build` gives any trainer's Pokémon with no moves chosen. The rule is one flag of the core, a new `CoreSetup.NoBattleItems` refused in `WhyNotItem` for both sides, which plan 13 · V5's run rule of the same name sets for a whole run; one-on-one is the parties: the master's holds one Pokémon, and the player's is a `Party` of its own holding the member put forward. A script chooses that member with a new `choosemember` (the party screen in a choosing mode) and fights with a new `master`, which finds `master_<species>` as `battle <who> as "id"` (plan 02 · S4) finds a trainer of the table. A win is that trainer beaten (`StoryState.Defeat`), so the titles need no field of their own: they are the beaten ids that begin with `master_`. No master stands in the imported world (decision 8): one hall of the Battle Frontier's annex calls the master of whichever species the player puts forward, so the overlays, the maps' load and the seeded shots don't move, and nothing of it is met before the Hall of Fame, under either rule preset.

## Risks

- **`BattleSide` as two values** is in the log's `Place`, the field's arrays, the animator's views and the renderer's player-or-enemy split. B3 to B7 keep two sides and only widen the slots, so the risk stays in B13, which is optional; everything before it is checked by the unchanged tests and a clean `diff` of the old modes.
- **Reach** touches `ResolveTargets`, the menus and every spread move; a rule that reads "the other side" without asking reach is a bug only a triple shows. B6's fuzz with triples is the net, and `InReach` is the one place reach is decided.
- **Balance and Platinum's defaults**: a horde slot or a sky trainer on a Platinum route would move Platinum's play; this plan places them only where decision 1 says, and a new test in `WorldTests` (written in B2, or in B3 if decision 7 leaves B2 out; widened in B3) holds every open area's trainer formats and tables to what the import gave.
- **1025 masters from random sets.** Showdown's sets are built for its random battles, not for a species' best showing, and about a third of the species (most that still evolve; 64 of Sinnoh's 210, by name at the pinned commit) have none, so their masters know only what their level taught them. B14's report counts them, the legality check keeps out any move the species can't learn, and a master is only ever as strong as its data; a set written by hand for a species the user cares about goes in a new `tools/DataImporter/Overrides/master-trainers.json`, as the data's other corrections go in that folder.
- **Frame cost**: five Pokémon, five boxes and a wider lens fill more of 4K; B4 measures with `profile` and `SlotFit` shrinks models before anything is drawn twice.
- **The facilities' rules** are Emerald's, read from pret/pokeemerald at the commit plan 18 · W6 pins in `tools/DataImporter/Sources.cs` (B9 adds that pin itself if it comes first), as the importers read Platinum's decompilation (numbers and layouts only, never a line of its text); where a number can't be read it is our own and the rulings say so.
- **Two tournaments' data** (the leaders' teams) exist in plan 06 · R9's `Data/trainers.json` and will in `tournaments.json`; B11 reads Sinnoh's from the table and writes nothing twice.

## Decisions for the user

1. **Where the later formats are met in Sinnoh.** *Recommended:* only after the Hall of Fame: the Battle Zone's characters, the facilities, the World Tournament, plan 03 · D12's new zone, and plan 13's Versus; Platinum's routes and trainers keep Platinum's formats under both rule presets. Alternative: sky trainers and horde slots on Sinnoh's routes under the modern preset, with the balance that costs.
2. **Where Emerald's five facilities stand.** *Recommended:* an annex of Sinnoh's Battle Frontier, one building of our own beside Platinum's five with the five inside, so they are played before Hoenn exists and plan 21 reuses their rules and rooms. Alternative: wait for Hoenn and build them there only.
3. **Where the World Tournament is held.** *Recommended:* Jubilife's Global Terminal, whose door plan 01 · M11 opens and which plan 07 leaves empty (its counter is in the Pokémon Centers), unlocked by the Hall of Fame. Alternative: a hall of its own in the Fight Area.
4. **The triple's shift.** *Recommended:* Black and White's rule, the shift is the Pokémon's turn, and a side's last Pokémon moves to the centre by itself; the session checks what X and Y changed against Showdown's code and the rulings record the choice. Alternative: X and Y's rule throughout.
5. **The Battle Royal.** *Recommended:* B13 stays optional and last, built only when plan 23's Alola is near, since it alone makes `BattleSide` more than two. Alternative: build it with B7 so Versus has it early. (Decision 7 asks whether to keep it at all.)
6. **Who the SOS allies are.** *Recommended:* the species' own evolutionary family plus the Alola games' pairs where PokeAPI has them, as data of our own. Alternative: the family only.
7. **Whether to keep the sky battles and the Battle Royal.** A second check, by both a fit and a value lens, judged B2 and B13 not worth their sessions: Sinnoh has no sky trainers, so B2's only use here would be trainers added to the imported world, and B13 rebuilds the two-sided core for a format few remember. *Recommended:* drop B13, and keep B2 optional, built only when plan 23 · Z3 is next, where Kalos's own sky trainers use it and no trainer is added to Sinnoh; B3 then writes the `WorldTests` case B2 would have. The alternatives: keep both as they stand (optional, B13 last as decision 5 has it), or drop both, and plan 23's Z3 and Z8 leave out the sky trainers and the Royal.
8. **Where the masters stand.** *Recommended:* one hall in the Battle Frontier's annex (decision 2), opened by the Hall of Fame, which calls the master of whichever species the player puts forward: no person is added to the imported world, so Platinum's places and the seeded shots don't move, and all 1025 masters are there the day the file is. The alternative is Let's Go's way, Sinnoh's 210 standing near where each lives and the rest in the hall: 210 people the original never had in its places, carrying no team on the map so their loading draws nothing, each an entry of an overlay.
9. **What a master battle leaves.** *Recommended:* the player's own Pokémon fights, in a party of one, so EXP, friendship, an evolution's counts and the PP it used stay as after any trainer battle; no prize money; a loss heals the team and the player may try again. The alternative fights with a copy (B11's tournament way), so a master battle changes nothing but the title.
