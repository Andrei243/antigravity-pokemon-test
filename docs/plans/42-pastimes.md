# Plan 42 · Pastimes: the stage, the studio, cabinets and rides

Written 2026-11-07, before any session, at the user's request: one of ten plans (35 to 44) from [the study of what else to add](studies/what-else-to-add-study.md).

**Goal**: small games inside the world that a long journey through every region can stop for, none of them a story beat. A stage show in Nimbasa's theatre, where the player dresses a Pokémon of the team in props of our own and performs to a song of ours against three rivals before a crowd (Black and White's musical as a mechanic: the show plan 22 left behind the stage's door); a film studio behind Virbank's gate, where scripted battles are shot as films with lines, choices and endings and shown again at a premiere (Black 2's Pokéstar Studios as a mechanic); a card-flip deduction game at the Game Corner tables HeartGold played it at, on HeartGold's rules with pieces of our own; sports days on Nimbasa's two fields, where Black 2's athletes battle by the day of the week; an arcade of two puzzle cabinets, a match-three on the team's species and a picture-logic puzzle of every species seen; and trick surfing off Alola's beaches for Battle Points. A riding race, the two link games whose record boards stand blank in Two Island's and Mossdeep's game corners (played alone), a pinball table and a café of orders are optional and last. Each is a GPU-free state that a test plays to its end from a seed and the player's presses, with a screen on the kit and a harness mode, and everything new in them is our own.

## Design

**What every pastime shares.** Its rules are a GPU-free class of `Models/` (a folder of its own where it has several files) that takes plain inputs, draws its chance from the generator it is handed (`Dice` in the game, a seed in a test), counts in frames of thirty a second as the slot machine and the Pokéathlon do, and never reads a clock. What it keeps is a field of `SaveData` that is null while empty, so no version is raised and an older save reads as empty. Its screen is a `GameState` at the enum's end whose logic is in methods that take no input, drawn with the kit by a `ModernUi.<Pastime>.cs`, the style guide's section written first; where a board suits a click, it joins the pointer (plan 12 · Q6: `HitTest`, `PointAt`, `GameEngine.PointableNow`). A script opens it through a command at `Op`'s end with both hosts, `ScriptTests.EveryCommandHasItsLine`, `ScriptRunner.StopsAHurry` (which names every `Op`) and `docs/scripts.md`, or through a new word of `gamecorner` and `PastimeAct`. Words are `ui.json` keys through `Text.Of`, in English and Romanian; the scripts' and overlays' Romanian goes in plan 17 · N6's next slice. Sounds are `SoundBank`'s and listed in `docs/sound-effects.md`, a rumble pulse where a sound has one (`Haptics.Pulses`); songs are MML of ours, checked by `MusicRender` and given to the user as MP3s. Each has a harness mode, not part of `all`, a section of `docs/mechanics/rulings.md`, and its numbers there where they are ours.

**Content of our own.** Each pastime takes a game's mechanic and makes everything in it ours: the props and their shapes, the shows and their songs, the rival performers, the films with their genres, scripts, lines and posters, the card game's pieces and art, the cabinets' boards, stages and pictures (silhouettes of this game's own models), the surf courses and race tracks, the rope and the berry race of the link rooms, the pinball table and the café's menu. HeartGold's card game is played by HeartGold's rules, read from the decompilation as Platinum's and Emerald's machines were, and Black 2's athletes are Black 2's imported trainers, as every trainer of Unova is: numbers and layouts, never art or words. Names on screen are plain words of ours (a stage show, props, a film studio, a card-flip game, an arcade, trick surfing); the games' names say only where an idea came from. No character, story, place, host or mascot of a game is recreated for what is new: the theatre's owner is the lobby's own gentleman, the studio's people are ours beside Virbank's captain and its talent scout, whom the game already has, no film is any of Black 2's, and no costumed mascot appears. A place the region already has, or imports as all of Unova was imported, is used as it stands (the theatre's lobby, the stadiums, the studios' lot, Goldenrod's hall); a place that is new (the arcade) is a room of ours.

### The stage show (PS1, PS2)
- **Props** are a table of ours (`Data/show-props.json`, hand-written, `ShowPropDatabase` through `GameDataFiles`): about forty pieces, each with a slot (`PropSlot`: `Head`, `Face`, `Neck`, `Back`, `Hand`, `Tail`), one of four moods of ours (`PropMood`: bright, dashing, gentle, eerie; none of them a contest's quality), a trick (`PropTrick`: a spin, a wave, a toss, a shine, a bounce, a ring), two colours and a sculpt: a top hat, a crown of paper, round glasses, a bow, a scarf, a short cape, a folding fan, a baton, a bell, a ribbon for the tail. Each is sculpted in the SDF kit at a unit size (`Graphics/ShowPropModels.cs`) and meshed through `SdfCache`.
- **Worn on the Pokémon's own model.** `Graphics/PropAnchors.cs` (GPU-free) finds where each slot sits on a built model's mesh, as `Footprints.Make` finds a foot: from the vertices weighing at least 0.3 on a bone of the slot's role, the top of the head's surface for a hat, the face's front between the eyes for glasses, the ring where head meets body for a neck piece, the back's highest point for a cape, the end of an `Arm` bone for a hand, the end of the last `Tail` bone; each with an up, a forward and a size by the head's. A slot the body lacks (a hand on a fish) is not offered. The pieces ride their bones through every pose: `PokemonSprites.Render` takes the pieces worn, drawn on the posed bones' matrices, and with none, as every bake hands it, draws what it drew. Props are worn only on the stage and in the dressing room.
- **The trunk and the dressing room.** `PropTrunk` keeps how many of each piece (nine at most); `Dressing` is a Pokémon's pieces, one to a slot and four at most; `ShowRecords` (saved as `SaveData.StageShow`) holds the trunk, each Pokémon's last dressing by `Pokemon.Id` (plan 07 · O2), the shows played and won, each show's best and the fans owed. The theatre's owner gives the trunk and a first handful at the first visit; the desk (person 6) opens `DressingRoomScreen` (`GameState.DressingRoom`): the team to choose from (no Egg), the slots down the left, the trunk's pieces for the slot under the cursor at the right, and the Pokémon on a dais in the middle, turning, wearing what is on it, with the show's mood and how many pieces match.
- **The show** is `ShowRun` (`Models/StageShow/`), on the Pokéathlon's shape: thirty frames a second, `Step(press)`, `Advance(dt, press)`, the rivals' `Think` on the show's own generator from its seed, `Autopilot`. A show of `Data/stage-shows.json` (ours) names its song, its tempo (held equal to the song's `Song.Tempo` by a test), its mood, its bars and its cues: the bars in which each of the four performers has the spotlight, three each. In a cue a press on the beat does the performer's trick (`TimingAssists.Window`, `Scored`, so Automatic counts as Generous); the crowd's eyes, a share of a hundred among the four, move toward whoever tricks on the beat, further for a piece of the show's mood, less for a trick repeated, and a trick off the beat earns nothing. Between cues the four dance on their own. The rivals are three of the show's cast (species, dressing and knack, ours). The score is the eyes at the curtain and the dressing's match with the mood, our own numbers in the rulings.
- **On the stage** (`StageShowScreen`, `GameState.StageShow`, `ModernUi.StageShow.cs`): the choice of show, the curtain, a stage in the kit (curtains, boards, footlights, the crowd's heads in silhouette along the foot, the spotlight), the four performers rendered each frame into pictures of their own in `Render`, before the 2D pass, as the Hall of Fame's ceremony renders its figures, posed by `PokemonAnimation` at the show's time (the body plan's own clips: idle, the cheer of plan 25 · Y5, and for a trick a move's or the entry's) with their props on their bones and a trick's piece turned or tossed by its age; the beat along the foot; the results with each one's share.
- **Fans and gifts** (`ShowFans`): after a show one of the lobby's three fans (persons 1, 4 and 18) waits with a gift the next time the theatre is come to, a piece drawn from a table of ours by the score and the shows played. Two shows come with PS2, each with a song of ours in `Data/music/unova/`; the table takes more as songs are written.

### The card-flip game (PS3)
- **HeartGold's rules, in its order** (`src/voltorb_flip/voltorb_flip_game.c`, which the importer's sparse checkout does not fetch yet: PS3 adds it and Goldenrod's script to `Sources.HeartGoldFolders`, with the pinned commit unchanged): `CardFlipLevels` is its eighty configurations (busts, twos, threes, the free multipliers allowed in a row or column and in all) and each level's draw among them; `CardFlipBoard` is twenty-five face-down cards, the sum and the busts along each row and column, drawn up to a thousand times until the limits hold, the last kept as it falls, as the original keeps it; `CardFlipGame` is a sitting: the round's payout multiplied card by card, a bust losing the round, quitting keeping it, every multiplier found winning it, the next level chosen from the last five rounds by `CalcNextLevel`'s branches, four memo marks on a card. Every draw is on the generator it is handed; the coins go into `Pastimes.Coins`. A sitting keeps nothing but its coins, as HeartGold keeps nothing.
- **Pieces of our own**: cards of 1, 2 and 3 in faces of ours and a bust card of ours (a cracked tile), in the Game Corner's look; `CardFlipScreen` (`GameState.CardFlip`; `Move`, `Flip`, `Memo`, `Quit`; the pointer), `ModernUi.CardFlip.cs`, its sounds and a pulse for a bust.
- **Where it is played**: Goldenrod's tables (`gamecorner cards`, read from the tiles they stand on), its prize counter opened with HeartGold's own list read from its script (the TMs and held items at their coins, and its three Pokémon at level 15 through `givepokemon`, which the randomiser already asks) in `GameCornerPrizes.Goldenrod`, its clerk selling coins and giving a Coin Case to whoever has none, as Celadon's does; and HeartGold's Celadon's tables (decision 3).
- **The lucky number draw** (HeartGold's weekly draw of trainer numbers, claimed at the Radio Tower's counter): a draw once a week on `Models/Lottery.cs` (a `Lottery.WeeklyTicket` beside `TicketOf`, read from the week's number of `Models/DailyEvents.cs`, matched with `Matching` and `Check` against the ID numbers of the player's Pokémon, prizes by digits in HeartGold's own list where its script gives one, else ours in `Lottery.WeeklyPrize`); a station of `radio.json` that announces the week's number in lines of ours (a new `RadioStation` value at the enum's end with its `RadioShows` entry and `ui.json` words, `Models/Pokegear.cs`'s dial and the signal that picks it up in Johto); and the counter's script in `goldenrod_radio_tower_1f.txt`, which checks the player's Pokémon, hands the prize once a week (a `FLAG_WEEKLY_` flag cleared by `DailyEvents`) and replaces the man's line in the overlay. Johto only; Sinnoh's Lottery Corner is unchanged.

### The film studio (PS4)
- **A film** is a file of ours (`Data/studio-films/*.json`, `docs/data-files.md` gains "Studio films"): its genre and title key, the cast lent to the player, the co-star and the foe (people of ours with looks of `characters.json`), the battle in a battle puzzle's shape (`PuzzleFile`'s sides, field, fixed rolls and the foe's moves turn by turn), its **beats** and its **endings**. A beat is a condition judged on the core as each menu opens (a turn reached, a Pokémon's HP under a share, a Pokémon down, a move of a type used, the weather) and what follows it: a line of the co-star or the foe, or a line the player chooses among two or three, the script's and an ad-lib. An ending is a condition on the take (won or lost, by which turn, the beats met, the lines chosen) with its closing lines and its stars.
- **The rules** (`Models/Studio/StudioFilms.cs`, GPU-free): `FilmScript`, `FilmBeat`, `FilmEnding`, and `FilmTake`, what a take did (the beats met, the lines chosen, the ending, the stars); `FilmRecords` (saved as `SaveData.Studio`): the films shot, each one's best stars and best take, the studio's rank (the stars summed), which opens the next films.
- **Shot on the game's own battle**, made as the battle room makes a puzzle's (`Puzzles.PlayerParty` for the cast, a `PuzzleFoe`, the fixed rolls, `NoRewards`), with `BattleSetup.StudioFilm`: `BattleEngine.BeginChoosing` asks the film's beats, their lines go on the interlude a trainer's line takes, and a line to choose is a `LearnStep.Line` at the enum's end. The rules never know a film is shot; the player's own team is never in it.
- **The studio** is Black 2's lot behind `PokestarGate`'s far door, imported with `--game black2 --data` as every place of Unova was (the door opened by the flag Roxie's scene already sets), with people of ours: a director, the casting desk, crew, a poster wall; Virbank's captain, back from his screen test, as a co-star of a film; Virbank's talent scout at the gate. Four films of ours to begin (a ghost story, a heist, a detective story, a race against a storm). The **premiere** in the lot's theatre plays the best take again: the take is kept as a Vs. Recorder file beside the player's (`SavePaths.Replays`) whose new field `BattleRecordFile.StudioFilm` names the film, so a replay takes its foe and fixed rolls (`Read` refuses a film the data lacks, a `ReplayRefusal` at the enum's end), watched through `BattleEngine.ForReplay` with the crowd's reaction and the box office (a number of ours from the stars and the genre). No Vs. Recorder is needed.

### The arcade (PS6, PS10)
- **A room of ours** (decision 2: behind one of Lumiose's shut doors) in the Game Corner's look (`interior: GameHall`), with a prize counter of ours and cabinets read on their tiles (`gamecorner cabinet …`); `CabinetRecords` (saved as `SaveData.Cabinets`): stages cleared, pictures solved by species, best scores.
- **The match-three** (`Models/Cabinets/MatchThree.cs`; an idea from Pokémon Shuffle): a board six by six of the icons of four species the player picks from the team, and a stage's foe of ours (`Data/cabinets.json`, stages by region, ours); swapping two neighbours that makes a line of three or more clears it and deals damage by the cleared species' type against the foe's (`TypeChart.GetEffectiveness`, the battle's own chart), more for four or five and for a cascade; refills drawn on the board's own generator from the stage's seed, so a stage is a pure function of its seed and the swaps; a board with no swap left is shuffled; a stage has HP and a number of swaps. It pays coins.
- **The picture-logic puzzle** (`Models/Cabinets/PictureLogic.cs`; an idea from Picross): a grid of 10, 15 or 20 cells square whose filled cells are a species' silhouette (`Graphics/Silhouettes.cs`, made from its model's mesh through the menu sprite's own framing, GPU-free, kept by signature and borrowed as `Footprints` are: `PokemonModels.Borrow`, `Return`, `Patient` for the harness), with the runs of each row and column at its edge. A picture is offered only if line logic alone solves it to one answer (`PictureLogic.Solve`), at the smallest grid that does; one for each species the Pokédex has seen, so the puzzles grow with the Pokédex, and a solved one shown in the species' own colours. It pays coins and fills a gallery page.
- **A pinball table** (PS10, optional; an idea from Pokémon Pinball): `Models/Cabinets/Pinball.cs`, a ball on a table of ours (segments and circles: two flippers, bumpers, lanes, a spinner) stepped eight times a frame in single precision, the bumpers' kick on the table's own generator, so a ball is a pure function of its seed and the flipper presses; a lane lit three times shows a species of the Pokédex's seen on the centre target for a bonus. Coins only.

## Risks

- **A prop in the wrong place.** A piece placed inside the surface vanishes and one off it floats (CLAUDE.md's lesson of hats and hair). Anchors are found on the mesh's surface, never guessed; a slot whose anchor can't be found is not offered; `ShowPropTests` looks at every body plan, and the harness's `sh04` shots at each.
- **Four models a frame.** Rendering four posed performers into pictures every frame costs what a double battle's four do; PS2 adds a stage scene to `profile` and holds it to 8 ms on High.
- **The song and the show's frames.** The show counts its own frames and the song plays on the mixer's thread; both start together and run in real time, but a slow frame can part them by a frame or two, which the beat's window absorbs. The show never reads the song's position.
- **Films that move the battles.** A film's beats live on the face and the rules never know of them; `battles-*.txt` must not move, and `BattleRecordFile.StudioFilm` is null for every other battle, so the Vs. Recorder's files read as before.
- **The card game's thousandth board.** HeartGold keeps the last of a thousand draws even when it breaks the limits; the port keeps it too, and the test says so instead of holding every board to the limits.
- **Pictures with two answers.** A silhouette at a coarse grid often has several; only those line logic finishes alone are offered, and a species with none at any grid has no picture (the gallery counts it, a test lists them).
- **Places that are other plans'.** Lumiose's doors and Alola's beaches are plan 23's, Goldenrod's and Celadon's plan 20's, Nimbasa's and Virbank's plan 22's: each session writes only its own rows of their overlays and scripts, merged by the hot files' rules. Lumiose's 27 shut doors are wanted by plans 37 (its streak house, three restaurants, warp 22 first), 43 (a salon branch) and this one's arcade: each takes its own door, and whichever lands second reads `lumiose_city.json`'s `locked` list first.
- **Songs wait for the user's ear.** The shows' and the studio's songs are checked by `MusicRender` but are the user's to approve; a show is not called done on a song the user hasn't heard.
- **Coins and Battle Points.** The card game pays HeartGold's; the cabinets pay less than it for the same time, and a course's best pays no more than a round of seven at the Battle Tower; the rulings keep the numbers.
- **Small games grow.** Each cabinet and ride is a few hundred lines on a known shape; one that doesn't fit its shape is dropped before it bends it, as plan 15's events were.
