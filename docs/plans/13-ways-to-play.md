# Plan 13 · Ways to play: difficulty, challenge runs, a randomiser, medals, timers and local versus

Written 2026-10-06, before any session.

**Goal**: choices made once, at NEW GAME, that change how a run is played and never move afterwards: a difficulty (Challenge and Easy, a level cap by badge, trainers scaled to the team), the rules of a challenge run (a Nuzlocke preset, monotype, a party of one, no items in battle, no Pokémon Center, no catching, no running, Set forced, the shiny odds) and a seeded randomiser; and around them what a run leaves behind (counters, a statistics page, medals, a run timer with splits, a New Game+), a seeded daily run (those two optional since a second check judged them low value), two people battling at one screen; and after them Remix (a second take on Sinnoh's trainers and wild Pokémon, written by us and chosen at NEW GAME like the rest), a friend in the partner's place and four players at one machine, friends' trainers brought in by code or file, a kit for streaming a run, and battle puzzles. Normal with nothing on is today's game byte for byte: Platinum's defaults never move, and every choice here is the player's at the start of their own game, as plan 06 · R1's choice of rules is.

## Where we are

- **The pattern to follow** (plan 06 · R1): whose rules a game is played by is asked on the title screen after NEW GAME (`TitlePhase.ChooseRules` in `UI/TitleScreen.cs`, `TitleScreen.Rules`), handed through `GameEngine.StartNewGame(name, look, rules, rival)` (the engine keeps it as `newGameRules` between the title and the introduction, which since plan 02 · S4 also asks the rival's name: `IntroPhase.AskRival`, `PlayerIdentity.RivalName`), kept in `SaveData.Rules` and never changed. `Ruleset.Use` is called by the engine alone and tests hand rules to what they drive. `TitleScreenTests.TheRulesAreChosenBeforeANewGameAndPlatinumsAreOffered` walks it.
- **The save** (`Core/SaveManager.cs`): `SaveData` has `PlayTimeSeconds`, `Money`, `Badges`, `SeenSpecies`, `CaughtSpecies`, `Diplomas`, `BoxStorage`, `RivalName` (plan 02 · S4), `Safari` (a Safari Game under way, plan 01 · M7) and the story's fields; no counters, no run state, no graveyard, no splits. `SavedPokemonData` keeps a Pokémon whole. The title's CONTINUE card and the save screen draw `ModernUi.SaveSummary`; the Trainer Card draws a `TrainerCardInfo` (name, id, money, Pokédex, time, badges, the day) on one page, and plan 06 · R12 owes its back, the stars and the signature.
- **Trainers** are made as maps load: `MapFile.BuildNpc` turns each `MapFile.TrainerRecord`'s `PartyMember`s into `new Pokemon(species, level)` with the chosen moves, drawing from the seeded `Dice`, which is why the harness's battles repeat. A trainer of the imported world whose overlay names the same team as plan 06 · R9's `Data/trainers.json`, or none, is then filled from the table (`TrainerDatabase.Fill`: `Trainer.Ai`, `Trainer.Items`, the team built as the original builds it from each Pokémon's personality, and the prize money of its class's formula); the table also holds Platinum's rematch teams (`leader_roark_rematch`). `GameEngine.StartTrainerBattle` hands the NPC's `TrainerData` to the `BattleSetup` as it is, with `Trainer.PrizeMoney`, `TrainerClass` (a string; "Leader" is already matched by name for the transition) and `DoubleBattle`, and a second trainer or a partner when a script names them. The rival's battles are a script's `battle rival as "rival_route_201_turtwig"` (plan 02 · S4): one of the table's three teams for the battle, chosen by `if starter`, made a `Trainer` whose `Id` is the table's. Elsewhere `Trainer.Id` is the overlay's, the table's own for most trainers and made up for some (`trainer_tristan` on Route 202) until plan 08 · P7 replaces those.
- **What may be chosen in a battle** is one place, `BattleCore.WhyNot` (`Battle/Sim/BattleCore.Choices.cs`): running is refused by a trap and by `CoreSetup.CannotFlee` (plan 06 · R9), an item by `WhyNotItem`, and the menus ask it first. `CoreSetup` carries `Rules`, `Conditions`, `Kind`, `CannotFlee`, `FirstBattle`, `Partner`, `PlayerController`, `EnemyController` and `PartnerController` (`TrainerAi.Instance`, Platinum's AI since R9, which thinks for a place with a `Trainer` behind it and draws a move at random for a wild Pokémon or the player's own; null makes the core ask both sides, which plan 07 · O6 uses for a link battle). The partner of a tag battle is asked from outside the same way: `BattleCore.ControllerOf` gives a player-side place with a `Trainer` the `PartnerController`, so a null one puts the partner's place in the `ActionRequest`, and `BattleEngine` already gives that place the partner's `Roster`. `TrainerAi` thinks only for a place with a `Trainer` behind it: the player's own place, which has none, draws its moves at random. `BattleEngine.Menus.cs`'s `BeginChoosing` keeps only the places whose `Side == BattleSide.Player`, and `InputManager` reads gamepad 0 alone. The harness's `versus` mode already shoots a battle between two named species.
- **EXP** is given in `BattleCore.AwardExp` (`Formulas.ExpShares`, `ExpFor`, then `Pokemon.GainExp`) and by a Rare Candy in `BagScreen` (`ExpForNextLevel`, `Pokemon.GetExpForLevel`); nothing caps a level.
- **Shininess**: `Pokemon`'s constructor rolls `rng.Next(8192) == 0` and nothing reads the result (plan 09 · L1 draws it, and keeps the roll at 1 in 8192 under both presets, leaving a chosen rate and the Shiny Charm to V5). The Shiny Charm isn't in `items.json`: the data importer brings in only the later items used or held in battle (`LaterItemCategories` in `tools/DataImporter/Importer.cs`), and the later games' other items are plan 06 · R28's.
- **Wild Pokémon** come from `Map.RollWildEncounter` through `WildEncounterRules.Meet` (and since plan 02 · S2 from a rod's bite and Sweet Scent), whose `WildEncounterEntry.SpeciesName` `GameEngine.StartWildBattle` turns into a Pokémon; in the Great Marsh, while plan 01 · M7's Safari Game is on (`Models/SafariGame.cs`), the same encounter becomes a Safari battle (`BattleKind.Safari`), whose SAFARI BALL is a `BattleChoice.UseItem` committed like any item. `Map.WildAt(x, y, water)` is the one place a land or water table is chosen (the tile's `MapArea`'s), `Map.Fish` a rod's; an area's tables are its area file's (`WorldAreaFile.Land`, `Water`, `OldRod`, `GoodRod`, `SuperRod`: lists of `AreaEncounter` over Platinum's slot weights), and `habitats.json` lists every area of the region, open or not, with what lives there (`HabitatArea`). The open areas meet 87 species; Generations 1 to 4 have 458 that are neither legendary nor mythical (`PokemonSpecies.Legendary`, `Mythical`), 212 of them first stages. The starters are `StarterSelectScreen.Starters`, which since plan 02 · S4 the briefcase's script opens (`common.Briefcase`, `ScriptScreen.Starter`): the one chosen joins the team and `StoryState.ChooseStarter` keeps its name, from which the rival's (`RivalStarterFor`), the scripts' `if starter` and `{starter}` follow; an item ball's item is `NPC.Item`, a hidden one `HiddenItem.Item`; the Pokédex's area page reads `Data/Habitats.cs`. Nothing remaps any of them.
- **Counting**: `GameEngine.OnStep` counts steps for friendship, `Evolution.CountStep`, the Pokétch's pedometer (`Poketch.Step`, plan 02 · S2: saved, and reset by the player) and the Safari Game's 500 (`SafariGame.Step`, plan 01 · M7); `BattleEngine.Show` sees every `Caught`, `ExpGained`, `LevelRose`, `Won` and `Ended`; `StoryState.GiveBadge` gives a badge; `GameEngine.PlayEvolutions` plays what `Evolution.Evolve` does. Beyond the pedometer, none writes a number anyone reads later. `Toast` (`UI/FieldNotices.cs`) shows one line; `AudioManager.PlayFanfare(MusicRole)` plays a fanfare by role; `OptionRow` has twelve rows.
- **Scripts** ask twenty-three questions (`Query` in `Story/Script.cs`: `flag`, `badge`, `item`, `knows`, `money`, `starter`, `safari`...), none about how the game is being played; the nurse heals on a yes (`common.Nurse`). `IScriptHost.GivePokemon` is how a script gives one.
- **Chance and time** for tools: `Core/Dice.cs` (`Seed`, `New`, `Shared`) and `Graphics/FrameClock.cs` (`Fixed`, `Now`). The day is `GameClock.Today`, the computer's date unless `GameClock.FixedDate` is set; the harness has set it to the first of June since plan 01 · M7, for Platinum's weather calendar. `GenomeRandom` (SplitMix32 in `Graphics/PokemonGenome.cs`) is the game's one generator that gives the same numbers on every machine; it is internal to `Graphics`.
- **Teams as text**: nothing in the engine reads or writes a team as text. `SavedPokemonData` is the save's JSON for one Pokémon, `TrainerPokemonRecord` the table's, and `tools/DataImporter/Showdown.cs` reads Showdown's data files (moves, items, species), not a team. raylib's `GetClipboardText` and `SetClipboardText` are there; nothing calls them.
- **Outside the window**: the game shows nothing beyond its window and files it writes for itself (the save, `settings.json`). Nothing in `PokemonPlatinumEngine` opens a socket, a pipe or a connection of any kind. `StoryState.Revision` moves on every change of the story; the Trainer Card's `TrainerCardInfo` carries the trainer ID it draws.

## Design

**One record, chosen once.** A new `Models/RunRules.cs` (GPU-free): a `RunRules` record with `Difficulty` (a new enum `Difficulty`: `Easy`, `Normal`, `Challenge`), `LevelCap`, `ScaleTrainers`, `Nuzlocke` and its clauses (`DupesClause`, `ShinyClause`, `GiftsCount`), `Monotype` (a `PokemonType?`), `PartyLimit` (6; 1 for a solo run), `NoBattleItems`, `NoCenter`, `NoCatching`, `NoRunning`, `SetStyle`, `ShinyDenominator` (8192, Platinum's; 4096, 1024 or 512 when chosen) and `Random` (a `RandomizerOptions` with the seed and what it touches; null for none). `RunRules.Normal` is `new RunRules()`, and `IsNormal` says nothing is on. The presets (`RunRules.NuzlockePreset`, `Hardcore`) are records with some fields set; the title shows them on the first row and the rows under it follow the preset. It is kept whole in `SaveData.Run` (null in older saves, read as Normal), shown on the CONTINUE card, the save screen's summary and a page of the Trainer Card, and never changed. It is passed wherever it is read: `BattleSetup.Run` to `CoreSetup.Run`, `IScriptHost.Rules` to the runner, a new `Party.Limit` on the party. One rule has no caller to hand it to, the shiny roll in `Pokemon`'s constructor: `RunRules.Current` is static for it alone, set by the engine (`StartNewGame`, `ApplySaveData`) as `Ruleset.Use` is, and tests never set it.

**What a run remembers** is a new `RunState` (`Models/RunState.cs`, GPU-free), saved as `SaveData.RunState`: the areas where a wild Pokémon has been met (by `MapArea.Key` on a streamed map, a hand-made map's name otherwise), the graveyard (each fallen Pokémon as `SavedPokemonData` with where and when), whether and why the run ended, the clears (New Game+), the timer and its splits. `SaveData.Counters` (`Dictionary<string, long>`) is everything counted, named in `Models/Statistics.cs`.

**Where each rule is enforced**, once each: a battle's in `BattleCore.WhyNot` and `WhyNotItem` from `CoreSetup.Run` (running, items, balls, revives); the party's size in `Party.Limit` (a property, 6 by default, read wherever the constant `Party.MaxSize` is read today, so a catch under a solo run goes to the box through the core's own `Caught.ToBox`); a trainer's strength in `StartTrainerBattle`, on a copy; EXP's ceiling in `AwardExp` and the Rare Candy; the field's in the scripts (a new `Query.Rule`) and the screens (the PC's withdraw, a gift). A new test, `RunRulesTests.EveryRuleHasItsPlace`, drives each rule through the bare core (`CoreScenario`) or `HeadlessScriptHost` and holds that Normal refuses nothing.

**Trainers are raised on a copy.** The map's `TrainerData` stays as the import, `TrainerDatabase.Fill` and the seeded `Dice` made it, so the harness's shots and every test that loads a map are untouched. A new `DifficultyRules.Prepare(trainer, rules, leadLevel)` (`Models/DifficultyRules.cs`, GPU-free) returns the `Trainer` the `BattleSetup` gets: when no rule touches trainers (Normal, no scaling, no randomiser) the same object; otherwise a new one. Challenge raises levels by a table by class (our own numbers: leaders, the Elite Four and the Champion most, the rival next, everyone else a little), gives leaders and the League one more Pokémon from the species the original's rematch team gives them (the rematch teams in plan 06 · R9's `Data/trainers.json`, `leader_roark_rematch` and the rest), gives the important trainers held items (plan 06 · R8's effects run already), sets every trainer routine of R9's `AiFlags` on `Trainer.Ai` (`Basic` to `Harassment`; not `TagStrategy`, which the battle adds itself, nor `Roaming`, `Safari` or `CatchTutorial`), and pays prize money ×1.5. Easy lowers levels by the same table, gives EXP ×1.5 in `AwardExp` and takes a quarter off `ShopScreen`'s prices. The copy's Pokémon are built at their new level as `TrainerDatabase.Build` builds the table's (plan 06 · R9: personality, IVs, nature, ability, item and the chosen moves kept, nothing left to chance, as the original builds its rematch teams); a team an overlay types by hand is made `new Pokemon` at its level with the chosen moves kept, drawn from `Dice.New()`. `ScaleTrainers` re-levels the copy's party round the lead's level keeping its spread, at any difficulty.

**The level cap** is the next leader's highest level from plan 02's pacing targets (14, 22, 26, 32, 37, 41, 44, 50, then 62 for the League), by `StoryState.BadgeCount`, lifted by the region's `StoryCompleteFlag`. It is Sinnoh's; a region without a table is uncapped until its own plan writes one. `AwardExp` clamps the amount at `Pokemon.GetExpForLevel(cap, growthRate)` and says once a battle that the Pokémon can't grow further yet; the Rare Candy refuses the same way.

**The randomiser never edits the data.** A new `Models/Randomizer.cs` (GPU-free) is built from the seed with `GenomeRandom`, moved to `Core/SplitMix.cs` and made public so one seed gives one mapping on every machine (never a string's `GetHashCode`), and asked at the point of use: a species to one of similar base-stat total (within a tenth, widening until one is found) and the same stage of its line, legendaries among legendaries, a line mapped from its first stage so it stays coherent unless "random evolutions" is on. It is asked for a wild Pokémon in `StartWildBattle`, a trainer's team in the copy `Prepare` makes, the starters in `StarterSelectScreen` (the briefcase shows the mapped three, while `StoryState.ChooseStarter` keeps the slot's own name, so `if starter` and the rival's team ids still choose, and `{starter}`, `{rivalstarter}` and `{assistantstarter}` are filled with the mapped names), a gift in `IScriptHost.GivePokemon`, an item in `find` and the hidden items, and in reverse by `Habitats` for the area page. What belongs to a species (abilities, learnset, behind its own toggle the types) is swapped in memory at the start the way `Ruleset.Use` swaps the modern values (`Randomizer.Use`) and put back on a new game or a load. The harness and `Dice.Seed` are untouched: the randomiser has a seed of its own, typed on the title's keyboard or rolled once from `Dice.Shared`.

**Counters and what stands on them.** Every count is one line, `Statistics.Count(key, n)` on the engine's counters, from `OnStep` (steps by `Player.Mode`), `EndBattle` (battles by kind and `BattleResult`, whiteouts), `BattleEngine.Show` (catches by ball, `ExpGained`, `LevelRose`, the player's `Fainted`), `GiveBadge`, `PlayEvolutions`, money in and out, items found, `EnterArea` (time by area) and `StartWildBattle` (encounters by species). Medals (a new `Models/Medals.cs`) are a catalogue of predicates over the counters and the save, with our own names, texts and signs (nothing of the games' Medal Rally but the idea), checked when a counter moves; one earned is kept with its day in `SaveData.Medals` in every game, and told by the `Toast` with its sign and a fanfare of our own (a new `MusicRole.FanfareMedal`, named as the other fanfares are) only when a new `OptionRow.MedalNotices` is on, off by default, so a game played as Platinum shows nothing over the field that Platinum doesn't. The statistics and the medals are pages of the Trainer Card (a new `CardPage`: Front, Run, Statistics, Medals; plan 06 · R12's back takes its place after Front when it comes), flipped with left and right and drawn in `ModernUi.Records.cs` with `ListRow` and `Tabs`.

**The timer** (a new `Models/RunTimer.cs`, GPU-free): real time (the wall clock, read through plan 15 · E3's `Core/WallClock.cs` once it exists so the harness can fix it, paused on the title, in the options and while the window has no focus) and the game's own time (the sum of `dt`, which the harness's fixed clock makes repeatable) side by side; splits taken by itself at `GiveBadge`, at named flags (each commander beaten, Spear Pillar) and at the region's `StoryCompleteFlag`; a corner of the field and the battle (`ModernUi.Field`, top right, where nothing stands) behind a new `OptionRow.RunTimer`, off by default; `splits.json` beside the save in a plain shape a spreadsheet or an autosplitter reads.

**Two people at one screen.** `BattleSetup` gains `SecondSide`, a `Trainer` whose choices come from outside (`CoreSetup.EnemyController = null`). `BeginChoosing` then asks every place of the `ActionRequest`, the player's first, and the HUD's menus open for the side whose turn it is, from its own side; a `ReplacementRequest` for an enemy place is asked the same way. Between the sides a curtain (a new `CurtainScreen`: "Pass the controller to {name}", any button) hides the choice just made; with two pads `InputManager` takes a pad index, the second side reads pad 1 and there is no curtain. Against the AI the second side keeps `TrainerAi.Instance`. The battle is fought on copies of both teams (`Pokemon.CopyStateFrom` into fresh ones, levelled for Flat 50 and Flat 100 by `LevelScaling.At`, the clone plan 14 · B11 writes in `Battle/Sim/LevelScaling.cs` for plan 07 · O6 and this plan, written here instead if V12 comes first), so neither save changes, and it is reached from VERSUS on the title (new: `TitleChoice.Versus`, `GameState.Versus`, `VersusScreen`). This is plan 07 · O3's menu change delivered early, built once for a remote player to use too.

**Remix: a second take, written by us.** `RunRules.Remix` (new, off by default) is a row of V1's panel, chosen once like the rest. Under it the trainers who matter fight teams of our own design and every area's wild Pokémon come from tables of our own, so that each of the 458 species of Generations 1 to 4 that are neither legendary nor mythical can be met, or evolved from one met, before the Hall of Fame. The rules, the formulas, Platinum's AI, the slots' weights, the areas' rates and the story stay Platinum's. The files are new and apart, a folder for each region: `Data/remix/sinnoh/trainers.json` (a team for each trainer it changes, by plan 06 · R9's id; a rival's battle has a team for each of the three starters, as the table's ids do) and `Data/remix/sinnoh/wild.json` (tables by area key, in the area files' own shape and slots). Plan 06 · R9's `Data/trainers.json` stays Platinum's and Remix never patches it, since a patch would change every game. The team goes on the copy `DifficultyRules.Prepare` makes in `StartTrainerBattle` (V2's seam), so the maps load as before; the tables go through a new `Map.Tables` that `Map.WildAt` and `Map.Fish` ask first. With Remix off neither is touched and a `diff` of `all` is empty. On the copy, Remix's team comes first, then the randomiser's mapping (V6 and V7 map what Remix wrote, so there is one way to widen what is met, not two), then the difficulty's levels and AI and `ScaleTrainers`; a Remix team is written whole and equipped, so Challenge adds it no member and no item. The leaders keep plan 02's pacing levels and V3's cap table holds under Remix: Remix is harder by species, moves, natures, items and full teams, not by levels (decision 8). A species from outside the Sinnoh Pokédex caught before the National Pokédex is kept by its number, as `Pokedex.RegisterCaught` does already, and shows once the National Pokédex opens on Platinum's condition (plan 03 · decision 5); the area page reads Remix's tables in a Remix run. Every team and table is our own work: "nothing taken from the games" covers the community's hacks too, and none is transcribed from one. Balance is checked headlessly, because nobody here can play-test by feel: for each leader, a reference team (plan 02's pacing party, made of what Remix's tables offer by then, at the cap) fights the leader's Remix team over twenty seeds with Platinum's AI on both sides, and its share of wins is held in a band of our own; the user's play is the last word. Today `TrainerAi` plays the player's own place at random, so the reference side gets a `Trainer` through a new `CoreSetup.PlayerTrainer`, set by tests and tools only. Remix covers the story to the Hall of Fame; the post-game's trainers keep Platinum's teams until a batch is asked for. It is written in batches, as plan 03's models were: V15 the choice and the files, then V16 to V19 the teams and tables by the story's regions in plan 01's order (M5's south-west, M6's centre, M7's east and sea, M8's north and the League), each batch raising a floor of species met, as `CoverageTests` holds its floors.

**A friend beside the player.** Platinum often puts someone beside the player (plan 06 · R9's tag battles, which plan 02's chapters script: the assistant against the grunts in Jubilife City, Cheryl, Mira, Riley, Marley, Buck, the rival at Spear Pillar), and its Battle Tower has multi rooms. With a new `OptionRow.Coop` on (off by default), a tag battle asks before its fade whether a friend takes the partner's place. A yes sets `CoreSetup.PartnerController` to null (through a new `BattleSetup.FriendPartner`), so the partner's place joins the player's `ActionRequest`. The friend plays the partner's own team and has no bag, since the partner brings none; the menus say whose turn it is, and with a second pad (V12's pad index) pad 1 answers the partner's place. Partners see each other's choices, so there is no curtain between them. Every answer goes through `Commit`, so `BattleRecord` holds both players' and replays agree; with the option off, or a no, the battle is today's. In VERSUS the same pairs two players against an AI pair (V20) or two against two (V21: a curtain between the sides only, four pads once plan 12 · Q4 binds pads 2 and 3). The Tower's multi with a friend comes with plan 06 · R18, an online room of three or four with plan 07 · O8, and raids with plan 23 · Z10 and Z15.

**Friends' trainers as text, without a server.** Platinum's Battle Tower had a room of trainers other players had sent, and Emerald's record mixing brought friends' trainers into its world; plan 06 · decision 7 leaves the Tower's room out as online play, and plan 07 never lists it. Here a friend's trainer travels as a file or a short code that friends swap however they like, so no server is needed and plan 07's friends-only posture holds. A new `Models/ShowdownPaste.cs` reads and writes a team in Pokémon Showdown's paste format, the plain text the community shares teams in; a new `Models/TrainerFile.cs` holds a name, a `PlayerLook`, a trainer class, two lines picked with plan 08 · P9's phrase picker (a short list of our own until P9) and up to six Pokémon as a paste, with the data version (plan 08 · P10's `GameDataFiles.Version`) and a checksum. A file or a code is untrusted: it is read whole or refused, held to its checksum, its version and the one legality check V13, plan 14 · B11 and plan 07 · O5 share (written by whichever comes first). It carries no free text, so a friend's file can't put words on screen that we didn't write. An imported trainer thinks with every trainer routine of R9's `AiFlags` and fights on copies at Flat 50 (`LevelScaling`), so neither save changes.

**The streamer kit stays on the machine unless switched on.** Challenge runs are mostly played on stream, with trackers and autosplitters that read an emulator's memory, and this game shows nothing beyond its window. A new `Core/LiveFeed.cs` writes what a run is doing to `stream/state.json` beside the save, a bundled `stream/overlay.html` of our own shows it as an OBS browser source with no server, and a link to LiveSplit Server takes V10's splits (V24). Every row of the kit is off by default, the harness turns none on, and plan 16 · T3's `--safe-mode` turns them all off. The feed and the overlay are files, and LiveSplit is reached at `127.0.0.1` alone, so V24 sends nothing off the machine, as T3 promises. What does leave it (Discord's presence, which Discord shows to the user's friends, and Twitch's chat) is V25's, optional: each a row of its own that says where its data goes when switched on, and nothing is sent before it is. A stream-safe row hides what a viewer shouldn't copy: the trainer ID on the card, plan 07's friend and link codes once they exist, and paths with the user's name in them; the feed never carries any of them. The feed draws nothing from chance and writes nothing with its row off, so two runs still draw the same pictures.

**Battle puzzles.** A position with a goal, solved on the real core: a puzzle file (`Data/puzzles/*.json`, new) sets both sides, the field and the rolls (fixed by kind through `BattleRandom.Force`), names the `Ruleset` it is written for and its goal (win this turn, win within N turns, survive N turns, catch the Pokémon), and carries its solution and a decoy, a plausible wrong answer. A new `Models/Puzzle.cs` builds the `CoreSetup` and judges the goal from the log (`Fainted`, `Caught`, `Ended`, the turns). A test replays every solution through `BattleCore.Replay` and checks that the decoy fails, so a later rules session that breaks a puzzle shows in the test run. The puzzles are ours, about ten to start, each turning on a rule of Platinum's the core runs; packs add more through plan 16 · T14's layers.

**GPU-free and drawn.** `RunRules`, `RunState`, `DifficultyRules`, `Randomizer`, `Statistics`, `Medals`, `RunTimer`, `DailyChallenge`, the team builder's state, `Remix`, `ShowdownPaste`, `TrainerFile`, `FriendsHall`, `LiveFeed`, `LiveSplitLink`, the messages of the presence and chat clients, `Puzzle` and every change to `BattleCore`, `Party`, `Pokemon` and the scripts take no window. What draws: the title's run panel, the card's pages, the medal notice, the timer's corner, the curtain, the versus and team-builder screens, the co-op question and turn line, the export and import screens, the friends' hall, the stream-safe card, the chat poll's panel and the puzzles' list and goal line, all on the kit, each with harness shots, and two runs must still draw the same pictures. Screens a Normal game already has take their shots in the harness's `title` and `menus` modes, which run with nothing on; everything that needs a choice on takes them in a new mode, `ways` (not part of `all`), which starts a game for each choice through `StartNewGame`'s new argument and names its shots `wy_*`.

## Sessions

### V1 · The choice at NEW GAME
- `Models/RunRules.cs` with `Difficulty` and the presets; `TitlePhase.ChooseRun` after `ChooseRules` (`TitleScreen.Run`; rows like the options', the preset first; Confirm on Normal leaves at once, Cancel goes back to the rules); `GameEngine.StartNewGame(name, look, rules, rival, run)` and `newGameRun`; `SaveData.Run` and `RunState`; `RunRules.Current` set in `StartNewGame` and `ApplySaveData`; the run on `SaveSummary` (a mark on the badges' row beside the MODERN RULES mark it already draws, nothing when Normal) and on `CardPage.Run` of the Trainer Card; `docs/mechanics/rulings.md` gains "Ways to play": each choice, what it changes, Normal changing nothing.
- Style guide first: "Opening and title screen" gains the step after "which rules?", "Menu screens (G10)" the card's pages.
- Tests: `TitleScreenTests` walk both prompts and hold Normal as the default; `RunRules` round-trips through JSON and an old save reads as Normal; `IsNormal`.
- Shots: in `title`, `title_13b_run_preset` and `title_13c_run_flag` after `title_13_rules_modern` (the panel on its preset and on a flag); in `menus`, `28b_trainer_card_run`.
- **Done when** a game started with the Nuzlocke preset is saved, continued and seen on its card, and plays exactly as before because nothing enforces it yet.

## Risks

- **A rule enforced in one place and forgotten in another** (a script's `wildbattle`, a gift, the PC): each rule's one place is named above and `EveryRuleHasItsPlace` holds it; a new way to get a Pokémon or use an item asks `RunRules` there.
- **Two runs must still draw the same pictures**: Normal leaves the maps as loaded, the copy in `StartTrainerBattle` is made only when a rule asks for it, the randomiser rolls from its own seed, the daily takes a date, Remix's tables are read only through `Map.Tables` in a Remix run, the friends' hall reads only the files in its folder (fixtures copied into the harness's out dir in `ways`), the streamer kit writes nothing with its rows off, and the harness's modes in `all` run with nothing on (`ways`, outside `all`, starts games of its own). A rule that reached into map loading would move every shot of `all`.
- **Static state**: `RunRules.Current` is one more static beside `Ruleset.Current` and `PlayerIdentity`; tests run in parallel and must never set it. `Map.Tables` on `MapDatabase`'s maps is the engine's to set, as `Ruleset.Use` is; tests give it to maps of their own.
- **Overlap**: Set belongs to plan 08 · P1, the second pad to plan 12 · Q4 (and pads 2 and 3 with it), the screenshot folder to Q16, both sides' menus to plan 07 · O3 and the rule sets to O6, the Factory's pool, the Tower's streak and its multi rooms to plan 06 · R18, the stars to R12, the Shiny Charm's place among the later items to R28, `LevelScaling` and a team's legality check to plan 14 · B11 (shared with plan 07 · O5's), `WallClock` to plan 15 · E3, the trainers' table ids to plan 08 · P7, the phrases to P9 and the data version to P10, the log's paths and `--safe-mode` to plan 16 · T3 and packs to T14, the post-game's obtainability to plan 03 · D12, rooms of three or four online to plan 07 · O8. Each session builds on the owner's work or hands its own over, never a second version.
- **Balance**: Challenge's numbers are ours. A table that makes Roark unwinnable at level 12 shows in a story walk under Challenge with the pacing party (plan 02's chapters, when they exist). Remix's teams are judged by the headless balance band and then by the user's play; a band that holds but feels unfair is the user's call, and the band is moved, not the test dropped.
- **Remix is mostly writing**: about 150 teams and a table for every area with one. The batches keep it moving region by region and each stands on its own; a team or table must be ours, which no test can tell, so each batch's notes say what each leader's team is built round (its type, a plan of play), never copied from a hack's sheet.
- **Untrusted files**: friends' files and codes, and puzzle and Remix packs once plan 16 · T14 reads them, come from outside. Each is read whole or refused, held to its checksum, its data version and the legality check, never followed to a path, and never shows text of its own.
- **What leaves the machine**: only V25's rows (Discord's presence, Twitch's chat), each off by default and saying where its data goes when switched on; V24's feed is a file and its LiveSplit link stays on `127.0.0.1`, as plan 16 · T3 promises.
- **Scope**: twenty-six sessions. V1 to V5 are the value, Remix (V15 to V19) and V12 and V13 the second, then V20, V22 and V24; V21, V23, V25 and V26 come as their plans' places appear; V11 and V14 are optional and last (decision 7).

## Decisions for the user

1. **Where the choice is made.** *Recommended:* on the title after "which rules?", one panel, left by Confirm when nothing is wanted. The alternative is the professor asking in the introduction, which costs a phase of `IntroScreen` and lines for every flag.
2. **What a Nuzlocke's end does.** *Recommended:* the run ends, the save stays, can be loaded to look at the card, the graveyard and the boxes, and can't be saved over. The alternative deletes it, as the strictest players do by hand.
3. **VERSUS on the title.** *Recommended:* yes, since it needs no save; Platinum's menu has no such entry, so the style guide says it is ours. The alternative is the Pokémon Center's upstairs counter, which plan 07 builds for online play.
4. **New Game+ and the chain.** *Recommended:* start the finished region again with the carry-over; the chain's default (Kanto onward) stays what a plain NEW GAME does. The alternative restarts the chain at Kanto with the team, which plan 18's crossing has to answer first.
5. **The daily run.** *Recommended:* after plan 06 · R18, local results only, and dropped if the Factory's own format satisfies. The alternative builds it now on a pool of its own.
6. **How many medals.** *Recommended:* about 120 in six categories, our own names and signs, grown with later plans, kept in every game and told over the field only with `OptionRow.MedalNotices` on. The alternative is Platinum's five stars only, from R12.
7. **Keep New Game+ and the daily run?** A second check, by a fit lens and a value lens, judged V11 and V14 low value (their sessions say why). *Recommended:* keep both written, optional and last, built only if the user asks once V1 to V10 are in and a post-game exists, and the daily run folded into plan 06 · R18's Factory as a daily seed if the Factory's own format satisfies. The alternative drops both sessions, and decisions 4 and 5 with them.
8. **How Remix is harder.** *Recommended:* the leaders keep plan 02's pacing levels (V3's cap stands), and Remix is harder by its species, moves, natures, items and full teams; Challenge raises a Remix team's levels and AI on top and adds it no member or item. The alternative raises levels too, as the community's hacks do, with a cap table of Remix's own.
9. **Where Remix ships.** *Recommended:* with the game, in `Data/remix/sinnoh/`, read only in a Remix run, covering the story to the Hall of Fame. The alternative ships it as a first-party pack through plan 16 · T14, which waits for T14 and lets a player take it out; either way `Data/trainers.json` is never patched.
10. **How a friend is offered the partner's place.** *Recommended:* `OptionRow.Coop`, off by default, asking before each tag battle when on, so a game played as Platinum asks nothing. The alternative asks whenever a second pad is connected, with no option.
11. **Where friends' trainers stand.** *Recommended:* upstairs in Jubilife City's Pokémon Center (Platinum has an upper floor there), a hand-made room reached early in the story, until plan 01 · M11 builds the Centers. The alternative is the Battle Zone's annex (plan 01 · M10), reached only after the Hall of Fame.
12. **The streamer kit's networked half.** *Recommended:* build V24 (the file, the overlay, LiveSplit on `127.0.0.1`, stream-safe), and V25 only on the user's word, under a Discord application the user registers in the project's name. The alternative builds both now, or drops V25.
