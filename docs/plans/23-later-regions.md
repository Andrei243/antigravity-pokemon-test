# Plan 23 · The later regions: Kalos, Alola and Hisui

Written 2026-10-06, before any session.

**Goal**: the regions of the 3DS and Switch games on this game's tile grid, after Unova: Kalos with its free movement reduced to the four-way grid and Mega Evolution's home, Alola with trials instead of gyms and Ride Pokémon instead of HMs, Hisui as Sinnoh's past, a side region off the chain (optional, decision 9), and Legends: Z-A's Lumiose as a side era off Kalos, with its night league, the Z-A Royale; each on plan 18's framework and its decision on where a region without a decompilation comes from, each section starting with the decisions its region forces on the engine, each short and honest about what isn't known, because these are the furthest off. Galar and Paldea were part of this plan until 2026-11-04, when the user moved them to an open-world engine of their own: [plan 32](32-open-world-engine.md), [plan 33](33-galar.md) and [plan 34](34-paldea.md).

## Design

**One grid, four ways.** Every later region is a tile grid walked by `FieldMovement.Step`'s four directions. The free movement of X and Y and of the Switch games is not rebuilt: eight directions would leave `player_move.c`'s rules, which `FieldMovement` follows, and every sprite has four facings. What a region's movement adds is a `TravelMode` or a `FieldMoves` flag with a rule in `FieldMovement`, GPU-free and tested by walking a map built in the test; `Player.Advance` plays it out as it plays out surfing. A curved street is an octagon of stepped streets on the grid, and the user judges the first town of each region before the rest is laid (plan 01 · M4's decision 8: no picture of the original is kept).

**Field powers from two sources.** `FieldMoves` grows the later games' powers (`Cut`, `RockSmash` and `Strength`, which plan 02 · S2 asks in the obstacles' common scripts, `CutTree`, `Rock` and `Boulder`, with `if knows` and not through `FieldMoves`, so those scripts ask the region's grant beside the move; `Fly`, which opens S2's `FlyScreen` with no Pokémon of the party knowing it; then `Charge` for breakable rocks, `Climb` for any cliff, `Glide`, `Float` for a bike over water, `Jump`). A new `StoryState.Grants(region)` is a `FieldMoves` value per region (a new `SaveData.FieldGrants`, a dictionary by region id, empty in older saves) that scripts set with a new `grant <power>` command and ask with a new `granted <power>` question beside `knows` (an `Op` and a `Query`, parser, runner, both hosts, `ScriptTests.EveryCommand`, `docs/scripts.md`), and `GameEngine` sets `player.Moves = MovesOf(playerParty) | story.Grants(Here)` (`Here` is plan 18 · W1's). `usemove` names the Pokémon that knows the move; for a power that is granted and not known, the obstacle's script plays the ride's species (named with the power in the region's `Powers`) on the same cut-in band. The badge gates of plan 02 · S2 (`FieldMoveRules.BadgeFor`, the scripts' `if badge`) apply to the party's moves alone. Sinnoh grants nothing, so `MovesOf` keeps giving what it gives.

**Where a region's layout comes from.** *Decided for Kalos by the user on 2026-11-02: (b), hand-drawn.* There is no decompilation of X and Y and no dump is read: Kalos is drawn by hand on the four-direction grid, in its shape from public descriptions of the maps (the order and shape of the places at the level Serebii and Bulbapedia describe them), never copying a picture, a tile, a model, a line of text or a script's text. Z1 built the way to do it: `tools/MapDraw`, drawings of a character to a tile (`tools/MapDraw/kalos/*.txt`, placed by `layout.json`) written out as the chunks, the matrix and the habitats, the houses found at their doors (`buildingsAtDoors`), the rooms hand-made map files, the area files and overlays by hand; plan 16 · T9's editor is not built, and the drawings are text. The (a) below is what plan 23 had recommended, kept for the regions that come after if the user rules otherwise for them. Plan 18 · decision 5 (a): `tools/MapImporter/Dump/`, a reader of the user's own dump found by `--dump` and pinned by `Sources.DumpHash`, which plan 22 · U1 starts for the DS container (`DumpFiles.cs`, `Gen5Maps.cs`); the 3DS and Switch readers are two more files of that folder, sharing `WorldWriter`, and nothing of a dump is in the repository but the world files. Kalos and Alola's maps are 3D models over a collision grid, so the reader samples the walkable and the height at one tile per unit of that grid, writes `HeightPlate`s a tile wide (a step over `WeldLimit` is a face, under it a slope) and leaves `Cover` to a table per zone written by hand in the region's session, as plan 18 · W6's `GbaCover` does. Galar and Paldea are not on the grid: since 2026-11-04 they are hand-built on plan 32's open-world engine (decision 1). Nothing of this is known to work: the containers are documented by the community and not by a decompilation, and the fallback at every step is (b), hand-authored layouts in the region's shape with plan 16 · T9's editor, which plan 01 · decision 1 keeps for fixes alone and this plan would widen. Either way the rule holds: layouts and numbers, never a tile's graphics.

**A region's rules live in its file.** Plan 18 · W1's `Region` gains `Challenge` (`badges`, `trials`, `royale` or `none`: what the card shows and what plan 13 · V3's cap counts; not W1's `Gates`, the badge each field move asks for), `Dex` as a list (Kalos has three) and `Powers` (the grants its story hands out, in order, each with the species that carries it), and fills the `Arrival` it already has: where plan 18's plane sets the player down. None of these games has an airport the player walks through, so each region's first session names a spot of its own in the town where the original's story begins (the player's new home), confirming plan 18's crossing table and plan 22's departure from Mistralton. `GameEngine.Here` (plan 18 · W1) is who every rule here asks; a game that stays in Sinnoh never meets one, and Sinnoh's shots stay what they are.

**The later mechanics are at home.** The player arrives with the four key items from Sinnoh (plan 02's beats; plan 18's crossing keeps the bag), so this plan gives no mechanic and rebuilds none: each region's story keeps the original's scene where the original has it (the Tower of Mastery, the Z-Ring as the island challenge begins, the Dynamax Band from the professor, the Tera Orb at the academy), with the mechanic's rules R20 to R23's and the one-of-each rule of plan 06 · decision 3 unchanged. Its script gives the key item only to a bag without one (`if not item`), as a game whose Sinnoh beat moved to the post-game may have skipped it, and otherwise gives what the region adds (a Mega Stone, a trial's crystal, a Tera Shard). Where a region's own venue was optional in plan 06 (the dens, the raid crystals), this plan builds it here as that session's optional part.

**Wild Pokémon seen, by the area's own rule.** A new `MapArea.WildVisible` (from the area's header through the overlay) makes plan 10 · F5's `FieldPokemon` the area's way whatever `OptionRow.WildPokemon` says, because the Wild Area, Paldea, Hisui and Z-A's Wild Zones are played that way; Sinnoh's grass follows the option as F5 leaves it. A touch meets the Pokémon seen through `MeetWildPokemon`.

**Art and sound are ours.** A town's look is an `Architecture` of its own (`BuildingArt.Styles.cs`), a stadium a `BuildingKind`, a Noble's arena a `BattleArena`, each a row of the style guide's "Buildings", "Arenas (G8)" or "Areas (targets)" before the code; music is `Data/music/<region>/` by `MusicDirector.Resolve`'s roles (plan 05 · A6's way); every line is ours on the original's beats. Field Pokémon, weather and a stadium's crowd cost the frame: each session with one measures it with `profile` on High against 8 ms, and `Dice` and `FrameClock` keep two runs the same.

#### The engine for Alola (2026-11-07, the user's question: does Alola need an adapted engine?)
- **What plan 30 already gives.** Eight directions on the grid (FM1, FM2: Alola's file says `Eight`, as Kalos's does);
  continuous movement over the same grid behind the region's record (FM7 to FM9: `Overworld/FreeMovement.cs`,
  `Player.SteerFreely`, the stick, the save's offset, the bot and the fuzzer free), proven on test regions and switched
  on for no region (the user's gate, untouched here); the `freemove` harness mode's three looks and two cameras (Platinum's
  59° beside a 50° Kalos camera). Every Alola drawing is a grid drawing, so switching Alola's file to `Free` later
  redraws nothing: `FieldMovement.Step` stays the only wall.
- **The camera.** Sun and Moon's field camera is no camera the player turns: it follows from a fixed yaw at a lower,
  nearer angle than Platinum's, and a place or a scene changes its framing (closer in towns, wider on routes, a few
  scripted angles in scenes and interiors). So **Alola needs no orbiting camera**; it needs (a) a camera of the region's
  own, a row of `MapScene.ViewOf` like plan 30's decision 3 `FieldCamera.Kalos` (about 50°, a wider, nearer lens), which
  is plan 30 · FM6's and should be judged there with the grown cast, and (b) per-area framing, which the area files'
  `camera` field and the camera kit (`CameraRig`: zoom, pans, paths, bars) already give scenes. **A yaw that turns**
  (scripted or free) is the large part, and is a proposal only: the upright straightening in the shared vertex shader
  (`SetUpright`, `WorldRenderer.Straighten`) assumes a camera looking north; field sprites are baked at fixed facings
  seen from the south (a yaw would remap facings and bake more); `WorldRenderer.VisibleRects` and the chunks' streaming
  cull an axis-aligned trapezoid; the pixel ground is baked in whole texels on the grid's axes (a turned view would show
  the texel grid shimmering diagonally); houses are painted for a southern view (their backs are plain); and steering
  would need to be relative to the camera. Recommended: none of it for Alola; if the user wants turned angles in scenes,
  a session of its own after plan 30 · FM3 (live rigs make facings free) that generalises the straightening to any yaw
  and culls by the frustum.

## Risks

- **The readers may not be possible.** No decompilation documents the 3DS or Switch containers as Platinum's does; a session that can't read a dump within its first day falls back to hand-authored layouts in the region's shape (decision 1's (b)) and says so in the plan.
- **A grid reading of a 3D city may not be the place.** Lumiose's ring, Hau'oli's beach: the user judges each region's first town before the rest, as plan 01 · M4 did, and a region whose first town fails stops there.
- **Generated species.** Kalos from Inkay on, Alola, Galar and Paldea (and much of the Lumiose era's dex) are played with generated models until plan 03's batches reach them; a region's plan may ask for its batch first (plan 03 · decision 3's order), and this is the real cost of the later regions.
- **Field Pokémon and weather on screen** are new per-pixel and per-model costs: Z18 and Z22 measure before they are called done and drop the crowd, the far Pokémon or the second mist layer if the frame goes over.
- **The level curve.** Plan 18 · decision 2 (a) lands the player with none; the fixed levels of each region's leaders then play as the original's, with plan 13 · V3's cap table a row per region.
- **The Lumiose era has no source of its own**: no decompilation, and a Switch game's city with no grid. It leans on Z2's Lumiose (decision 11), so if Z2's city fails the user's judgement, Z21 to Z24 stop with it; the Royale's numbers and the Wild Zones' tables are ours where nothing can be read, and `rulings.md` says which.
- **These plans are far off** and will be rewritten when their time comes, after Unova; what is fixed here is the decisions each region forces on the engine, not its sessions' contents.
