# Plan 11 · People: a look for every trainer class and the named cast, poses, and a player who chooses their own look

Written 2026-10-06, before any session.

**Goal**: everyone the player meets looks like who they are: Platinum's trainer classes and its named cast each get a model of their own in place of the fourteen looks that stand in for all of them today, in the field and across the battle's platform; people sit on benches, lie in beds, swim in the sea and hold a rod out over the water instead of standing on and in all of it; and the player chooses skin, hair and clothes in the introduction and changes them later at a boutique, while a game in which nothing is chosen stays byte for byte the boy or the girl of today. Every look is our own design after the character (the official art is looked at, nothing from it is kept), reviewed by the user on the harness's turntables, sprite sheets and faces; nothing here changes a rule of play.

## Where we are

- **Looks are a table of values with fourteen rows.** `CharacterStyle` (`Graphics/CharacterStyle.cs`) holds skin, hair colour, a `HairCut` (Short, Spiky, Long, Swept), a `Headwear` (None, Beret, Cap, NurseCap), top, accent, bottom, shoes, eyes, the flags `Skirt`, `Shorts`, `Mustache`, `Stripes`, `Coat`, `ShortSleeves`, `Scarf`, `BushyBrows`, `Lashes`, `Blush`, a `Bag`, a `Sole`, a `BodyBuild` (Kid, Adult) and `Height`. `CharacterStyle.For(npcType)` is a switch: PLAYER (which TRAINER and LUCAS share), DAWN, RIVAL, ROWAN, NURSE, MOM, LADY, CLERK, YOUNGSTER, LASS, CLOWN, LOOKER, GENTLEMAN, and for any other name a plain default (a green shirt, short brown hair, a child's build). `CharacterModels.Build(npcType)` sculpts the style in `BuildHumanoid` (hair in `BuildHair`, hats in `BuildHat`) on the 19-bone `HumanBones` skeleton and meshes it through `SdfCache.Get`, whose key hashes `SdfModel.Describe()`, so a changed style meshes afresh by itself. `Get(npcType, shaders)` keeps every rig for good in `Cache`; `Preload` at start-up (`GameEngine.Initialize`) builds the type of everyone on every map, plus PLAYER, DAWN and ROWAN. `CharacterKitTests.Humans` lists thirteen types; `MenuScreenTests` holds that the boy's and the girl's styles differ.
- **A person has three names.** The world's objects carry the original's `looks` (`AreaObject.Looks`: the importer's `OBJ_EVENT_GFX_*` names trimmed in `tools/MapImporter/WorldWriter.cs`), which `WorldMapBuilder.CharacterFor(looks)` (`Data/World.cs`) folds onto the fourteen: sixty-two names onto Lass, Youngster, Lady, Gentleman, Clerk and the rest (thirteen of them added by plan 01 · M6 "as the nearest of the characters there are", so a Black Belt stands as a Gentleman and a Psychic as a Youngster), and everything else onto the default "Trainer". The open areas' files hold 55 people looks, 38 of them placed by the overlays; twelve fall to the default (`grunt_m`, `grunt_f`, `roark`, `mars`, `tuber_m`, `tuber_f`, and since M6 `cheryl`, `cynthia`, `cyrus`, `gardenia`, `mira`, `baby_in_pram`), none of them placed yet. An overlay may name a type instead (`OverlayPerson.NpcType`), as may a map file (`MapFile.NpcRecord.NpcType`). In battle the rig is the trainer's **class**: `BattleCore` emits `TrainersStand(Trainers[0].TrainerClass, …)` (`Battle/Sim/BattleCore.cs`, `BattleLog.cs`), `BattleEngine.Show` writes it to `BattleAnimator.EnemyTrainer`, and `BattleRenderer.DrawTrainers` asks `CharacterModels.Get(t.Type, …)` with it, so a Hiker, an Aroma Lady and every class whose name is not one of the fourteen stands on the platform in the default style. `Data/audio/sound-map.json` already names 68 classes of ours (`MusicTests.TestEyeThemesFollowTheSoundMap`); the overlays use twenty-two. The importer's `TRAINER_TYPE_*` is how a trainer watches for the player (`AreaObject.Trainer`), not a class: the checklist of looks is the `OBJ_EVENT_GFX_*` list, and of classes the `TRAINER_CLASS_*` of `res/trainers/data`, which plan 06 · R9 imported: `Data/trainers.json` gives each of its 927 trainers a class by name (`TrainerRecord.Class`, 70 in all), without the class's gender ("Ace Trainer" for both of the original's; `Importer.Trainers.cs` reads the genders only to build the personality) and with every Gym Leader's class "Leader".
- **Poses are standing, walking and five emotes.** `CharacterPose` is `Walk`, `WalkBlend`, `Running`, `Hop`, `Time`, `Blink`, `Emote` (Wave, Surprised, Nod, Cheer, Throw), `EmoteTime` and `Expression`; `CharacterAnimation.Apply` makes the clips; `CharacterSprites.KeyOf` turns a pose into a `SpriteKey` of a `SpriteAnim` (Idle, Walk, Run, Hop, the four emote strips), `BakePose` the frame back into the pose it is baked from, and `OverrideName` the file a hand-drawn PNG replaces it under. `WorldRenderer.GatherActors` builds every person's pose from `NPC.WalkCycle`, `WalkBlend`, the start under the "!" and a blink seeded by `SeedOf(name)` (a stable FNV hash). Nobody sits, lies, swims or fishes: a person placed on a bench stands on it, Route 219's two swimmers are not placed (plan 01 · M5's outcome), and the rods with their cast and bite (plan 02 · S2: `Overworld/Fishing.cs`, `FishingAttempt`, played by `GameEngine.UpdateFishing`) put no rod in anyone's hand: the player stands still with a "!" over them on a bite. Nor does anyone ride: S2 gave the Bicycle its rules and left the player walking at its pace until a riding sprite is made. Each object's `movement` (`look_around`, `look_south_and_east`, `wander_around`…; 321 are `none`) is in the area files and read by nothing; plan 02 · S1 left wandering to S4. `PropType` has `Chair`, `Sofa` and `Bench` (nine benches on the hand-made maps, and since plan 01 · M6 the world's own in Hearthome City and Amity Square, `c5_o01` and `d11_o01a`) and no bed.
- **The player is a look and a name.** `PlayerLook` is Boy or Girl (`Core/PlayerIdentity.cs`), chosen in `IntroScreen` (`IntroPhase.ChooseLook`, `ConfirmLook`), kept as `SaveData.Look` and set by `PlayerIdentity.Set(name, look)` on a new game and a load. `PlayerIdentity.Character` is "PLAYER" or "DAWN", and nine places draw by it: the field (`WorldRenderer`), the battle (`BattleRenderer`, `BattleAnimator.PlayerTrainer`), the introduction's figures and sprite (`IntroScreen.Render` through `CharacterStudio.Render` and `CharacterSprites.Portrait`), the Trainer Card's and the Pokédex's portrait (`WorldRenderer.Portrait`), and the preload. No outfit, hair or skin can be chosen. Plan 06 · R17 dresses the Pokémon for contests, never the player; R11's Veilstone Department Store sells Platinum's items.
- **Review** is the harness's `lineup` mode: a turntable of each of fifteen types (`37_turntable_*`, `CharacterStudio.Turntable`), sprite sheets of a few strips (`38_sheet_*`, `CharacterSprites.Sheet`) and the faces (`39_faces_*`). The style guide's "Characters" section is the rule for all of it; its "Known gaps after G11" still say only the trainer's start and the throw are played.

## Design

**One table of looks** (`Data/characters.json`, read through `GameDataFiles` like the rest; a new GPU-free `Data/CharacterStyles.cs` with a static constructor like `PokemonDatabase`'s). Each look is an entry under its name with the style's fields (colours as hex, enums as strings), so a look is values and an overlay author or a mod (plan 16) can add one without a case in a switch. `CharacterStyles.Get(name)` gives a style or the default, `Has(name)`, `Names`, and `OfClass(trainerClass, female)` the look of a trainer class from the file's `classes` block ("Ace Trainer" → `ace_trainer_m`, `ace_trainer_f`). `CharacterStyle.For` reads it; the fourteen move into the file as they are, and a test pins every field of PLAYER and DAWN to today's values. A person's `NpcType` becomes a look's name: `WorldMapBuilder.CharacterFor(looks)` is `CharacterStyles.Has(looks) ? looks : fallback`, the old folding kept as the file's `fallbacks` block until every look is in, and a test counts the people of the open areas who fall back, which every batch lowers. The decision between a file and a grown switch is the user's (decision 1).

**The battle draws the person, not the class.** `Trainer` (`Models/Trainer.cs`) gains `Look` (null: the class's), set by `MapFile.BuildNpc` from the person's `NpcType`; `BattleMirror` copies it; `BattleEngine.Show(TrainersStand)` sets `Anim.EnemyTrainer` from `Trainers[i].Look ?? CharacterStyles.OfClass(class)` in place of the event's class string. The core and its log change nothing, so `BattleCoreTests.ARecordedBattleReplaysTheSame` holds untouched. Plan 06 · R9's table names a class without its gender and every Gym Leader's "Leader", so the person's look is what tells the two Ace Trainers and the Leaders apart, and `OfClass` is the fallback for a trainer no person carries.

**Rigs come and go.** A rig is some 40,000 vertices and its shell; a hundred looks can't all stay on the card. `CharacterModels.Trim(keep)`, like `PokemonModels.Trim`, frees every rig not among the current map's people, the player's and the battle's as a map is left (the disk cache keeps the mesh); `Preload` builds the looks of the map being entered and its neighbours' chunks, not the whole game's, and the people of a map arrived at have their idle frames baked behind the fade, as `PokemonModels.Request` is asked before a battle. Measured with `profile` on the town scenes before and after C2.

**The kit grows in code** (`CharacterModels`), the looks only in the file. New parts, each a field of `CharacterStyle` and a shape of `BuildHumanoid`: hair cuts (bowl, bun, ponytail, braids, curls, bald, a quiff), headwear (helmet, hard hat, beanie, bandana, headband, wide hat, cowboy hat, visor), glasses and a beard, clothes (overalls, lab coat, long coat, dress, robe, swimsuit, vest, apron, a uniform with a mark that is not their logo), things in hand on the hand bone that swing with the arm (rod, parasol, guitar, net, pickaxe, float ring, camera), and two builds beside Kid and Adult: `Little` (a head shorter, for the toddlers and the Tubers) and `Elder` (shorter, stooped, the head a little forward). The two tests of `CharacterKitTests` that hold every type to one smooth skinned body and its hat over its hair run over every look in the file. The style guide's "Characters" section ("Proportions" and "Parts") changes before each batch that adds a build or a part. A look is designed after the character so that it is known at a glance by hair, hat and colours; nothing is traced, and the user judges each batch on its turntables (decision 2).

**Poses** are a `Stance` on the pose (`Graphics/CharacterAnimation.cs`: Stand, Sit, SitGround, Lie, Swim, Fish) with a `StanceTime` for the rod's tug. `Apply` lays the stance under the idle so breathing, blinking and an emote play on top: a sitter's hips drop to the seat with the thighs forward and the hands on the knees; a ground sitter is cross-legged; a lier is turned flat on their back, the head on the pillow; a swimmer is sunk to the shoulders with the arms stroking on the walk's cycle; a fisher holds the rod out in both hands and, on a bite, leans back as the rod dips for 0.3 s. `SpriteAnim` gains Sit, SitGround, Lie, Swim, Fish and Tug (2, 2, 2, 4, 2 and 3 frames a facing); `KeyOf`, `BakePose` and `OverrideName` (`down_sit_0.png`) extend, and the theory `EveryFrameIsBakedFromAPoseThatShowsThatFrame` takes the new rows. A lying frame's facing is the way the head points. A swimmer is baked with the rig lowered so the frame's foot line is the water, and `GatherActors` sets its feet at the water's drawn height (`Relief.At`): what shows is the head and the shoulders, and a ring spreads behind every stroke through a new `FieldLife.Stroke(map, x, y)`, timed by `FieldLife.Now` like `Splash`, so two runs draw the same rings. A sitter is drawn a quarter tile nearer the camera than the seat's card, so a bench's back is behind them; a room's chair and sofa are boxes, and the depth test does it.

**Who poses.** `NPC.Stance` is set three ways, in this order: the overlay's or the map file's `pose` (`OverlayPerson.Pose`, `NpcRecord.Pose`; a line in `docs/data-files.md`); when none is given, what is under them (`Map.Props` covering their tile: Chair, Sofa and Bench sit, a new `PropType.Bed` lies, `Map.IsDeepWater` swims); and a script's `pose <who> sit|lie|swim|fish|stand` (`Op.Pose`, `IScriptHost.Pose(NPC?, Stance)` in `GameEngine.FieldHost` and `HeadlessScriptHost`, a row in `docs/scripts.md`, a line in `ScriptTests.EveryCommand`, the path plan 09 · L4's camera commands take too). The `look_*` kinds of `movement` become `NPC.Gaze` (the directions and a period): the engine turns them every two to four seconds with `Dice`, and someone spoken to keeps their gaze afterwards. The `wander_*` and `walk_*` kinds stay plan 02 · S4's. The player gets `Player.Stance` for fishing alone, set by the engine while a cast plays (plan 02 · S2's `FishingAttempt`, through `GameEngine.UpdateFishing`) and tugged on its `Hooked` stage; the player never swims (they ride a Pokémon, `SurfMount`) and never sits, as in Platinum. The Bicycle's riding pose would be the same mechanism; S2 brought the Bicycle without one, and no session owns it yet.

**The player's outfit** is an `Outfit` record (GPU-free, `Core/Outfit.cs`): `Skin` (one of six tones), `Hair` (a `HairCut`), `HairColor` (one of eight), `Eyes`, `Set` (a clothes set by name) and `Hat`. `Outfit.DefaultFor(look)` is today's PLAYER or DAWN; `Outfit.Style(look)` builds the `CharacterStyle`: the body's build and height are the look's, skin, hair, eyes and clothes the outfit's. Sets live in `characters.json` under `sets`: Platinum's two as the free defaults and a dozen of ours, each on both builds. `PlayerIdentity.Outfit` stands beside `Look`, and `Character` returns the name the outfit's style is registered under (`CharacterStyles.Register("PLAYER", style)` gives `PLAYER@<eight hex of the style's hash>`; `CharacterModels.Build` finds it in the registry; `SdfCache` meshes each outfit once by its own key; `CharacterFaces.Paint` takes the eyes and lashes from the style as it does now), so the nine places that draw the player change nothing. The default registers as plain `PLAYER` and `DAWN`, which is what keeps a game with nothing chosen byte for byte today's. `SaveData.Outfit` (null in older saves and where nothing was chosen) and `SaveData.Wardrobe` (the sets owned) need no migration. Nothing but drawing reads the outfit: the trainer id, `{player}`, `{assistant}` and the rules stay the look's. The outfit's place in a link battle's trainer record is plan 07 · O6's line, not this plan's.

**Choosing it.** `IntroScreen` gains `IntroPhase.ChooseOutfit` between `ConfirmLook` and `AskName`: four rows (skin, hair, colour, set) over the figure rendered live by `CharacterStudio.Render` through a trial registration, the defaults preselected so pressing on plays the introduction as it plays today; `IntroLook` gains what the phase shows and the tests play it through. A `WardrobeScreen` (`UI/WardrobeScreen.cs`, its logic in `Move`, `Confirm` and `Cancel` taking no input; drawn by a new `ModernUi.Wardrobe.cs` from `ListRow` and `Stepper`, with the figure from `CharacterStudio.Render` as the introduction does it; style guide, "Menu screens") opens through `GameState.Wardrobe`, `ScriptScreen.Wardrobe` and a `wardrobe` command whose arguments are the sets for sale, so the boutique's clerk sells sets for money and the mirror in the player's room (`Data/maps/PlayerHouse.json`, a `Mirror` prop with a script) changes hair and the sets owned for nothing. The boutique is a floor of the Veilstone Department Store, whose shops are plan 06 · R11's and whose interior plan 01 · M11's.

## Risks

- **Volume and judgement.** A hundred looks, each reviewed by the user on a board, because Claude can't judge a likeness; batches of fifteen to twenty-five entries keep a session reviewable. A look that doesn't read is a change of values, not of code.
- **Likeness without copying.** A named character must be known at a glance and still be ours: hair, hat and colours carry it; nothing is traced and no proportion is measured from the art. Where a look lands too close, the user says so on the board.
- **Memory and first runs.** Every rig meshes once (seconds) and reads from `cache/models` afterwards; a first run of a town with twenty new looks is slow once, and plan 16's CI runs cold. `Trim` and the narrower `Preload` keep the card's memory to a map's people.
- **Three names.** The looks, the types and the classes name the same people; C1 puts them through one table and a test, or an overlay's `npcType` and a class's look drift apart again.
- **Depth.** A sitter and their bench, a swimmer and the water's drawn height, a lier and a bed: each is a card against a card or a box, and the field's steep camera forgives little. The shots of C7 and C8 are the check.
- **Determinism.** A gaze turns on `Dice`, a stroke's ring on `FieldLife.Now`, a blink on `SeedOf`: nothing here may take a number from `Random` or a string's `GetHashCode`, or two harness runs differ.

## Decisions for the user

1. **Looks as a data file or as code.** *Recommended:* `Data/characters.json`, with the kit's parts in code; a look is values, and the file is what a chapter's overlay, a test and a mod read. The alternative is `CharacterStyle.For` grown to a hundred cases.
2. **How close the cast comes to the original.** *Recommended:* known at a glance by hair, hat and colours, with the official art looked at and nothing kept, as the Pokémon models were done. The alternative is a freer redesign, which would make the Gym Leaders strangers.
3. **Where the outfit is chosen.** *Recommended:* in the introduction, after the look, with the defaults preselected so a press goes on as today, and at the mirror and the boutique afterwards. The alternative is the boutique alone, which keeps the introduction as Platinum has it.
