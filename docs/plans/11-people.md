# Plan 11 · People: a look for every trainer class and the named cast, poses, a player who chooses their own look, acting on cue and trainers who speak in battle

Written 2026-10-06, before any session.

**Goal**: everyone the player meets looks like who they are: Platinum's trainer classes and its named cast each get a model of their own in place of the fifteen looks that stand in for all of them today, in the field and across the battle's platform; people sit on benches, lie in beds, swim in the sea and hold a rod out over the water instead of standing on and in all of it; the player chooses skin, hair and clothes in the introduction and changes them later at a boutique, while a game in which nothing is chosen stays byte for byte the boy or the girl of today; people in the story's scenes wave, bow, point and laugh with a face to match; and, where the player's game asks for it, the leaders, the rival and the bosses speak during their battles as the later games' do. Every look is our own design after the character (the official art is looked at, nothing from it is kept), reviewed by the user on the harness's turntables, sprite sheets and faces; nothing here changes a rule of play.

## Where we are

- **Looks are a table of values with fifteen rows.** `CharacterStyle` (`Graphics/CharacterStyle.cs`) holds skin, hair colour, a `HairCut` (Short, Spiky, Long, Swept), a `Headwear` (None, Beret, Cap, NurseCap), top, accent, bottom, shoes, eyes, the flags `Skirt`, `Shorts`, `Mustache`, `Stripes`, `Coat`, `ShortSleeves`, `Scarf`, `BushyBrows`, `Lashes`, `Blush`, a `Bag`, a `Sole`, a `BodyBuild` (Kid, Adult) and `Height`. `CharacterStyle.For(npcType)` is a switch: PLAYER (which TRAINER and LUCAS share), DAWN, RIVAL, ROWAN, NURSE, MOM, LADY, CLERK, YOUNGSTER, LASS, CLOWN, LOOKER, CYRUS (plan 02 · S4's: spiky blue hair, a long grey coat), GENTLEMAN, and for any other name a plain default (a green shirt, short brown hair, a child's build). `CharacterModels.Build(npcType)` sculpts the style in `BuildHumanoid` (hair in `BuildHair`, hats in `BuildHat`) on the 19-bone `HumanBones` skeleton and meshes it through `SdfCache.Get`, whose key hashes `SdfModel.Describe()`, so a changed style meshes afresh by itself. `Get(npcType, shaders)` keeps every rig for good in `Cache`; `Preload` at start-up (`GameEngine.Initialize`) builds the type of everyone on every map, plus PLAYER, DAWN and ROWAN. `CharacterKitTests.Humans` lists thirteen types; `MenuScreenTests` holds that the boy's and the girl's styles differ.
- **A person has three names.** The world's objects carry the original's `looks` (`AreaObject.Looks`: the importer's `OBJ_EVENT_GFX_*` names trimmed in `tools/MapImporter/WorldWriter.cs`), which `WorldMapBuilder.CharacterFor(looks)` (`Data/World.cs`) folds onto the fifteen: sixty-three names onto Lass, Youngster, Lady, Gentleman, Clerk and the rest (thirteen of them added by plan 01 · M6 "as the nearest of the characters there are", so a Black Belt stands as a Gentleman and a Psychic as a Youngster, and `cyrus` onto his own since plan 02 · S4), and everything else onto "Trainer", which `CharacterStyle.For` draws as the boy (TRAINER shares PLAYER's case). Since plan 01 · M7 the open areas' files hold 69 looks of people (the Pokémon that stand in the field, Poké Kid Danielle's `pikachu` among them, left out), 54 of them placed by the overlays; eighteen fall to "Trainer": seven placed by M7 (`sailor`, `policeman`, `roughneck`, `swimmer_m`, `swimmer_f`, `tuber_m`, `tuber_f`) and eleven not placed yet (`baby_in_pram`, `cameraman`, `cheryl`, `crasher_wake`, `cynthia`, `expert_f`, `gardenia`, `grunt_m`, `mira`, `riley`, `roark`). An overlay may name a type instead (`OverlayPerson.NpcType`), as may a map file (`MapFile.NpcRecord.NpcType`). In battle the rig is the trainer's **class**: `BattleCore` emits `TrainersStand(Trainers[0].TrainerClass, …)` (`Battle/Sim/BattleCore.cs`, `BattleLog.cs`), `BattleEngine.Show` writes it to `BattleAnimator.EnemyTrainer`, and `BattleRenderer.DrawTrainers` asks `CharacterModels.Get(t.Type, …)` with it, so a Hiker, an Aroma Lady and every class whose name is not one of the fifteen stands on the platform in the default style. `Data/audio/sound-map.json` already names 68 classes of ours (`MusicTests.TestEyeThemesFollowTheSoundMap`); the overlays use twenty-two. The importer's `TRAINER_TYPE_*` is how a trainer watches for the player (`AreaObject.Trainer`), not a class: the checklist of looks is the `OBJ_EVENT_GFX_*` list, and of classes the `TRAINER_CLASS_*` of `res/trainers/data`, which plan 06 · R9 imported: `Data/trainers.json` gives each of its 927 trainers a class by name (`TrainerRecord.Class`, 70 in all), without the class's gender ("Ace Trainer" for both of the original's; `Importer.Trainers.cs` reads the genders only to build the personality) and with every Gym Leader's class "Leader". A trainer's object in the field often wears another class's looks: the open areas' three Aroma Ladies stand as `pokemon_breeder_f`, Veteran Grant as `expert_m`, Poké Kid Danielle as `pikachu` (her overlay makes her a Lass), Ty and Sue of the Young Couple as `beauty` and `guitarist`, and since plan 01 · M7 Route 210's Bird Keeper, Dragon Tamer and Double Team and Route 212's two Pokémon Rangers as `ace_trainer_m` and `ace_trainer_f`, Belle & Pa as `cowgirl` and `rancher`, and Route 214's PI as `rich_boy`; the original's battle shows the class all the same. `CharacterFor`'s `little_boy`, `little_girl` and `aroma_lady` name no object of the original.
- **Poses are standing, walking and five emotes.** `CharacterPose` is `Walk`, `WalkBlend`, `Running`, `Hop`, `Time`, `Blink`, `Emote` (Wave, Surprised, Nod, Cheer, Throw), `EmoteTime` and `Expression`; `CharacterAnimation.Apply` makes the clips; `CharacterSprites.KeyOf` turns a pose into a `SpriteKey` of a `SpriteAnim` (Idle, Walk, Run, Hop, the four emote strips), `BakePose` the frame back into the pose it is baked from, and `OverrideName` the file a hand-drawn PNG replaces it under. `WorldRenderer.GatherActors` builds every person's pose from `NPC.WalkCycle`, `WalkBlend`, the start under the "!" and a blink seeded by `SeedOf(name)` (a stable FNV hash). Nobody sits, lies, swims or fishes; the only thing that changes how someone stands is the ground, deep snow and marsh mud sinking them a few rows (`WorldRenderer.SinkRows`). A person placed on a bench stands on it; Route 219's two Tubers are not placed (plan 01 · M5's outcome), while plan 01 · M7 placed thirteen Swimmers on Routes 213, 220 and 221, who stand upright on the sea in the boy's look, and two Tubers on Route 213; and the rods with their cast and bite (plan 02 · S2: `Overworld/Fishing.cs`, `FishingAttempt`, played by `GameEngine.UpdateFishing`) put no rod in anyone's hand: the player stands still with a "!" over them on a bite. Nor does anyone ride: S2 gave the Bicycle its rules and left the player walking at its pace until a riding sprite is made, which it points at plan 04, whose sessions are all done. Each object's `movement` (`look_around`, `look_south_and_east`, `wander_around`…; 321 are `none`) is in the area files and read by nothing; plan 02 · S1 left it to S4, and S4 (done) moved the `wander_*` and `walk_*` walks on to S5, the first chapter with a town full of people; the `look_*` turns are this plan's C7. `PropType` has `Chair`, `Sofa` and `Bench` (fifteen benches on the hand-made maps, two in every Pokémon Center and one in Pallet Town, and since plan 01 · M6 the world's own in Hearthome City and Amity Square, `c5_o01` and `d11_o01a`), and since plan 02 · S4 a `Bed` in the player's and the rival's rooms (`PlayerHouse2F`, `RivalHouse2F`), with a `Computer` in the player's.
- **Acting is a bubble.** `NPC` and `Player` carry only a `Bubble` (`NPC.ShowBubble`, `TickBubble`, aged by the field's `dt`), which a script's `emote <who> exclaim` shows; `Emote` and `Expression` are internal to `Graphics` (`Graphics/CharacterModels.cs`), so nothing outside it can name one, and `GatherActors` sets no expression on anyone. Of the emotes the field plays only the trainer's start; Wave and Nod are the introduction's, Throw the battle's, Cheer nobody's. A script's `walk` and `move` take directions alone (`ScriptParser`'s `Path`, played by `NpcWalk` and `PlayerWalk` in `Overworld/ScriptedWalk.cs`), though the player already walks on the spot behind a boulder that slides (`Player`). The original's scenes walk people on the spot some 1,200 times (`WalkOnSpot`) and step them back with their facing kept (`LockDir`, in sixteen scripts).
- **In battle a trainer is seen at the start alone.** The core emits `TrainersStand`, the face stands them on the platform, and they step off at the send-out (`BattleAnimator.PlayerTrainerExit`, `EnemyTrainerExit`, `TrainerExitTime` 0.9 s, after which `EnemyTrainer` is cleared); a trainer's lines are `Trainer.DialogueBefore` and `DialogueAfter`, said in the field. The face plays the log as `steps` behind an `interlude` queue that goes first (the move prompt's lines, `BattleEngine.Learning.cs`), and `playingTheLog` holds until both are empty. `BattleCamera`'s shots (`ShotKind`) frame the Pokémon and the ball, never a trainer after the opening. No option row follows the save's rules yet; plan 12 · Q10 brings the three-valued `RulesDefault` (Off, Rules, On) that does.
- **The player is a look and a name.** `PlayerLook` is Boy or Girl (`Core/PlayerIdentity.cs`), chosen in `IntroScreen` (`IntroPhase.ChooseLook`, `ConfirmLook`), kept as `SaveData.Look` and set by `PlayerIdentity.Set(name, look)` on a new game and a load. Since plan 02 · S4 the introduction also names the rival (`IntroPhase.AskRival`, `EnterRival`, `ConfirmRival`; `PlayerIdentity.RivalName`, `SaveData.RivalName`, `{rival}` in lines), whose look stays `RIVAL` whatever he is called. `PlayerIdentity.Character` is "PLAYER" or "DAWN" (`CharacterOf(look)`), and five places draw by it: the field (`WorldRenderer`), the battle (`BattleRenderer`, `BattleAnimator.PlayerTrainer`) and the Trainer Card's and the Pokédex's portrait (`WorldRenderer.Portrait`, opened by `GameEngine`). The introduction's figures and sprite ask `CharacterOf(look)` themselves (`IntroScreen.Render` through `CharacterStudio.Render` and `CharacterSprites.Portrait`), a person of the map typed `Player` or `Assistant` goes through `PlayerIdentity.CharacterFor`, and the preload names "PLAYER" and "DAWN". No outfit, hair or skin can be chosen. Plan 06 · R17 dresses the Pokémon for contests, never the player; R11's Veilstone Department Store sells Platinum's items.
- **Review** is the harness's `lineup` mode: a turntable of each of fifteen types (`37_turntable_*`, `CharacterStudio.Turntable`), sprite sheets of a few strips (`38_sheet_*`, `CharacterSprites.Sheet`) and the faces (`39_faces_*`). The style guide's "Characters" section is the rule for all of it; its "Known gaps after G11" still say only the trainer's start and the throw are played.

## Design

**One table of looks** (a new `Data/characters.json`, read through `GameDataFiles` like the rest; a new GPU-free `Data/CharacterStyles.cs` with a static constructor like `PokemonDatabase`'s). Each look is an entry under its name with the style's fields (colours as hex, enums as strings), so a look is values and an overlay author or a pack (plan 16 · T14 patches `characters.json`) can add one without a case in a switch. `CharacterStyles.Get(name)` gives a style or the default, `Has(name)`, `Names`, and `OfClass(trainerClass, look)` the look of a trainer class from the file's `classes` block: "Aroma Lady" → `aroma_lady`; "Ace Trainer" → `ace_trainer_m` or `ace_trainer_f`, the person's look telling which; a class of two (Twins, Young Couple) two looks; and null for a class that names nobody in particular ("Leader", "Elite Four", "Commander", "Pokémon Trainer"). `CharacterStyle.For` reads it; the fifteen move into the file as they are, and a test pins every field of PLAYER and DAWN to today's values. A person's `NpcType` becomes a look's name: `WorldMapBuilder.CharacterFor(looks)` is `CharacterStyles.Has(looks) ? looks : fallback`, the old folding kept as the file's `fallbacks` block until every look is in, and a test counts the people of the open areas who fall back, which every batch lowers. The decision between a file and a grown switch is the user's (decision 1).

**The battle draws the class, and the person where the class says too little.** `Trainer` (`Models/Trainer.cs`) gains `Look` (null where no person carries the trainer), set by `MapFile.BuildNpc` from the person's `NpcType`; `BattleMirror.Copy(Trainer)` copies it; `BattleEngine.Show(TrainersStand)` sets `Anim.EnemyTrainer` (and `EnemyTrainer2`) from `CharacterStyles.OfClass(class, Trainers[i].Look) ?? Trainers[i].Look` in place of the event's class string. That is the original's way: its field objects borrow other classes' looks (an Aroma Lady stands as a Breeder), its battle shows the class. Plan 06 · R9's table names a class without its gender and every Gym Leader's "Leader", so the person's look is what tells the two Ace Trainers apart and what draws Roark rather than "a Leader". The core and its log change nothing, so `BattleCoreTests.ARecordedBattleReplaysTheSame` holds untouched.

**Rigs come and go.** A rig is some 40,000 vertices and its shell; a hundred looks can't all stay on the card. A new `CharacterModels.Trim(keep)`, like `PokemonModels.Trim`, frees every rig not among the current map's people, the player's and the battle's as a map is left (the disk cache keeps the mesh); `Preload` builds the looks of the map being entered and its neighbours' chunks, not the whole game's, and the people of a map arrived at have their idle frames baked behind the fade, as `PokemonModels.Request` is asked before a battle. Measured with `profile` on the town scenes before and after C2.

**The kit grows in code** (`CharacterModels`), the looks only in the file. New parts, each a field of `CharacterStyle` and a shape of `BuildHumanoid`: hair cuts (bowl, bun, ponytail, braids, curls, bald, a quiff), headwear (helmet, hard hat, beanie, bandana, headband, wide hat, cowboy hat, visor), glasses and a beard, clothes (overalls, lab coat, long coat, dress, robe, swimsuit, vest, apron, a uniform with a mark that is not their logo), things in hand on the hand bone that swing with the arm (rod, parasol, guitar, net, pickaxe, float ring, camera), and two builds beside Kid and Adult: `Little` (a head shorter, for the toddlers and the Tubers) and `Elder` (shorter, stooped, the head a little forward). The theories of `CharacterKitTests` over `Humans` (`EveryCharacterIsOneSmoothSkinnedBody`, `EveryHatShowsOverTheHair`, `HairIsLitAsOneMass`) run over every look in the file. The style guide's "Characters" section ("Proportions" and "Parts") changes before each batch that adds a build or a part. A look is designed after the character so that it is known at a glance by hair, hat and colours; nothing is traced, and the user judges each batch on its turntables (decision 2).

**Poses** are a new `Stance` enum (Stand, Sit, SitGround, Lie, Swim, Fish) on `CharacterPose` (`Graphics/CharacterModels.cs`), with a `StanceTime` for the rod's tug. `CharacterAnimation.Apply` lays the stance under the idle so breathing, blinking and an emote play on top: a sitter's hips drop to the seat with the thighs forward and the hands on the knees; a ground sitter is cross-legged; a lier is turned flat on their back, the head on the pillow; a swimmer is sunk to the shoulders with the arms stroking on the walk's cycle; a fisher holds the rod out in both hands and, on a bite, leans back as the rod dips for 0.3 s. `SpriteAnim` gains Sit, SitGround, Lie, Swim, Fish and Tug (2, 2, 2, 4, 2 and 3 frames a facing); `KeyOf`, `BakePose` and `OverrideName` (`down_sit_0.png`) extend, and the theory `EveryFrameIsBakedFromAPoseThatShowsThatFrame` takes the new rows. A lying frame's facing is the way the head points. A swimmer is baked with the rig lowered so the frame's foot line is the water, and `GatherActors` sets its feet at the water's drawn height (`Relief.At`): what shows is the head and the shoulders, and a ring spreads behind every stroke through a new `FieldLife.Stroke(map, x, y)`, timed by `FieldLife.Now` like `Splash`, so two runs draw the same rings (a swimmer who moves, once plan 02 · S5 walks people, also leaves `Footstep`'s ring, as the surfing player does). A sitter is drawn a quarter tile nearer the camera than the seat's card, so a bench's back is behind them; a room's chair and sofa are boxes, and the depth test does it.

**Who poses.** A new `NPC.Stance` is set three ways, in this order: the overlay's or the map file's `pose` (new `OverlayPerson.Pose` and `NpcRecord.Pose`; a line in `docs/data-files.md`); when none is given, what is under them (`Map.Props` covering their tile: Chair, Sofa and Bench sit, a `PropType.Bed` (plan 02 · S4's) lies, `Map.IsDeepWater` swims); and a script's `pose <who> sit|lie|swim|fish|stand` (a new `Op.Pose` and `IScriptHost.Pose(NPC?, Stance)` in `GameEngine.FieldHost` and `HeadlessScriptHost`, a row in `docs/scripts.md`, a line in `ScriptTests.EveryCommand`, the path plan 09 · L4's camera commands take too). The `look_*` kinds of `movement` become a new `NPC.Gaze` (the directions and a period): the engine turns them every two to four seconds with `Dice`, someone spoken to keeps their gaze afterwards, and a sitter or a lier keeps the facing their seat or bed gives them. The `wander_*` and `walk_*` kinds stay plan 02's (S4 left them to S5). The player gets a new `Player.Stance` for fishing alone, set by the engine while a cast plays (plan 02 · S2's `FishingAttempt`, through `GameEngine.UpdateFishing`) and tugged on its `Hooked` stage; the player never swims (they ride a Pokémon, `SurfMount`) and never sits, as in Platinum. The Bicycle's riding pose would be the same mechanism; S2 brought the Bicycle without one and pointed its sprite at plan 04, which is closed, so no session owns it yet (decision 6).

**The player's outfit** is a new `Outfit` record (GPU-free, `Core/Outfit.cs`): `Skin` (one of six tones), `Hair` (a `HairCut`), `HairColor` (one of eight), `Eyes`, `Set` (a clothes set by name) and `Hat`. `Outfit.DefaultFor(look)` is today's PLAYER or DAWN; `Outfit.Style(look)` builds the `CharacterStyle`: the body's build and height are the look's, skin, hair, eyes and clothes the outfit's. Sets live in `characters.json` under `sets`: Platinum's two as the free defaults and a dozen of ours, each on both builds. A new `PlayerIdentity.Outfit` stands beside `Look`, and `Character` returns the name the outfit's style is registered under (a new `CharacterStyles.Register("PLAYER", style)` gives `PLAYER@<eight hex of the style's hash>`, a stable hash of the style's values, never a string's `GetHashCode`; `CharacterModels.Build` finds it in the registry; `SdfCache` meshes each outfit once by its own key; `CharacterFaces.Paint` takes the eyes and lashes from the style as it does now), so the five places that draw by `Character` change nothing; a person typed `Player` (`CharacterFor`) and the preload ask `Character` too, while `CharacterOf(look)`, the assistant and the introduction's two figures before `ChooseOutfit` stay the plain look. The default registers as plain `PLAYER` and `DAWN`, which is what keeps a game with nothing chosen byte for byte today's. New `SaveData.Outfit` (null in older saves and where nothing was chosen) and `SaveData.Wardrobe` (the sets owned) need no migration. Nothing but drawing reads the outfit: the trainer id, `{player}`, `{assistant}` and the rules stay the look's, and `{rival}` the name the player gave him. Drawing an online opponent in their outfit belongs to plan 07 · O6's server-hosted battles, which would send the record with the team; this plan only keeps it a plain record that can be sent.

**Choosing it.** `IntroScreen` gains `IntroPhase.ChooseOutfit` between `ConfirmLook` and `AskName`: four rows (skin, hair, colour, set) over the figure rendered live by `CharacterStudio.Render` through a trial registration, the defaults preselected so pressing on plays the introduction as it plays today; `IntroLook` gains what the phase shows and the tests play it through. A `WardrobeScreen` (`UI/WardrobeScreen.cs`, its logic in `Move`, `Confirm` and `Cancel` taking no input; drawn by a new `ModernUi.Wardrobe.cs` from `ListRow` and `Stepper`, with the figure from `CharacterStudio.Render` as the introduction does it; style guide, "Menu screens") opens through a new `GameState.Wardrobe`, a new `ScriptScreen.Wardrobe` and a new `wardrobe` command whose arguments are the sets for sale, so the boutique's clerk sells sets for money and the mirror in the player's room (`Data/maps/PlayerHouse2F.json`, plan 02 · S4's bedroom, a new `PropType.Mirror` with a script) changes hair and the sets owned for nothing. The boutique is a floor of the Veilstone Department Store, whose shops are plan 06 · R11's and whose interior plan 01 · M11's.

**Acting on cue.** The rig's `Emote` and `Expression` stay internal to `Graphics`, so the field gets words of its own: a public `Gesture` (Wave, Nod, Cheer, Surprised, Bow, Shrug, Point, Laugh, Jump) and `Mood` (Neutral, Happy, Surprised, Sad, Angry) in a new `Overworld/Acting.cs`, kept on `NPC` and `Player` (`Gesture`, `GestureAge`, `GestureTimes`, `Mood`) and aged by the `dt` the field is handed, as `TickBubble` ages a bubble, so `FrameClock.Fixed` repeats them. `Emote` gains Bow, Shrug, Point and Laugh; Jump is the hop clip played on the spot. `WorldRenderer.GatherActors` turns a gesture into `CharacterPose.Emote` and `EmoteTime` (their length `CharacterAnimation.Duration`) and a mood into `Expression`; the start under the "!" still wins, and C7's stance lies under a gesture when C7 is in, an emote playing on top of the idle as "Poses" says, so either session can come first. A script says `act <who> wave [times]`, which waits for the end as `walk` does, and `mood <who> happy`, held until changed or the place is come to again; `mood` and not `look`, because `look` would read as a way to turn beside `face` and C7's `look_*` gaze. Beside `walk`'s directions come the two movements the original's scenes are built from after walking: `onspot`, the next direction walked on the spot (`WalkOnSpot`, some 1,200 times in its scripts), and `back`, the next step taken with the facing kept (`LockDir` … `UnlockDir`, in sixteen of its scripts). Its list of movements also has a jump on the spot (`JumpOnSpotFast`, `JumpOnSpotSlow`) that no Platinum script uses; `act <who> jump` stands for it. A mood is also the face plan 09 · L3's portrait shows, if its decision 1 gives the box portraits.

**Trainers who speak in battle.** From Black and White on, leaders, rivals and bosses speak during their battles; Platinum's are seen at the start alone. Here they speak where the player's game asks for it, at three moments: their last Pokémon comes out, the first of theirs falls, their last one is brought into the red. The option is a row of plan 12 · Q10's three-valued kind (`RulesDefault`: Off, Rules, On; Rules is on in a Modern game and off in a Platinum one), so a Platinum game shows nothing new. The rules say when, the face says what:
- **The moment is an event of the log** (CLAUDE.md, "Battles: the rules and their face": something new on screen is an event in the log and a case in `BattleEngine.Show`). A new `TrainerReacts(Place Place, TrainerCue Cue)` (`LastPokemon`, `FirstDown`, `LowHp`) is attached by the core with `With` to the line that tells of the moment ("X sent out Y!", "Y fainted!"), and with `AtImpact` to the hit that takes the trainer's last Pokémon to a fifth of its HP or under (the bar's red, `BattleAnimator.LowHpRatio`, which a test holds equal to the core's own number): once each per trainer per battle, never after `Ended`, with no roll, so `BattleRecord`, `Replay` and the random battles hold. The core writes it whatever the option says; the face ignores it when the option is off.
- **The words are ours, in data.** `Data/trainer-lines.json` (hand-written; decision 7), keyed by `trainers.json`'s ids or a stem ending in `*` (`rival_route_203_*` for the rival's three teams there), gives a trainer up to one line a moment, each with a `mood` and an `act` of C11's words if wanted, read by a new GPU-free `Data/TrainerLines.cs`; `{player}` and `{rival}` are filled as the line shows. Plan 17 · N4's text tool reads the file beside the scripts and the overlays.
- **The face reads it out.** `BattleEngine.Show(TrainerReacts)` finds the trainer at the place among `BattleSetup.Trainers` and, with the option on and a line for the moment, puts the line in the `interlude` queue, so it is read straight after the line it came with and `playingTheLog` holds until it has been: the screen never takes the rules' state in the middle of it. The message box shows the trainer's name on a pill over the line.
- **The trainer comes back.** `BattleAnimator` keeps the look of who stepped off (`EnemyTrainer` is cleared once they have) and walks them back to the platform's edge while their line is read, with the line's face (`Expression`, from the battle's smooth faces) and gesture (`Emote`), then off again; `BattleCamera` gains `ShotKind.Trainer`, which frames them and eases in and out as its other shots do. Its timings are the animator's, never the battle's `Random`.

## Sessions

### C1 · The table and one name for a person
- `Data/characters.json` with the fifteen looks and the `fallbacks` and `classes` blocks; `CharacterStyles`; `CharacterStyle.For` reads it; `WorldMapBuilder.CharacterFor` looks the file up first; `Trainer.Look`, `BattleMirror`, `BattleEngine.Show`; `CharacterModels.Trim` and the narrower `Preload`; a "Characters" section in `docs/data-files.md`.
- Tests: PLAYER and DAWN pinned field by field; every `looks` of every open area resolves, with the eighteen that fall back counted and pinned; every class of the sound map and of `Data/trainers.json` has a look, the person's (null) or a fallback, those with none yet standing in the plain default as they do today; `OfClass` picks an Ace Trainer's look by the person's and gives null for "Leader"; a Hiker's battle draws the Hiker's look once C2 gives it one (the test names the fallback until then); the replay test; `Trim` keeps what it is told to keep.
- Harness: the `lineup`, `battle`, `world` and `story` diffs against a run before are empty.
- **Done when** nothing drawn has changed and every look, type and class resolves through one table.

**Done (2026-10-09).** Decision 1 taken as recommended (a data file). What was built, and where it differs from the above:
- `Data/characters.json` holds 25 looks, not fifteen (plan 02's chapters had added Cyrus, Roark, Fantina, Maylene, the black belts, the grunts, Mars, Charon, Cynthia, Gardenia, Jupiter and the scientist to the switch since this plan was written), under lower-case names matched without regard to case, plus a `same` block (`trainer` and `lucas` are the boy). `CharacterStyles` is an instance with a `Default` read from the file and `Parse` for the tests' own tables; `Get`, `Has`, `Names`, `CharacterFor` (the old folding, now the file's `fallbacks` and `fallback`), `OfClass` and `InBattle` (`OfClass ?? look ?? class`). A look's fields are `CharacterStyle`'s own, read straight into it; an unknown field is an error.
- Proof that nothing drawn moved: for all 25 looks, the two aliases and three names that fall to the default, every field of the style and a hash of the sculpt's `SdfModel.Describe()` were dumped before and after: identical. In the field no person changes either: of the open areas' people placed with no `npcType`, none has a look the table now names that the old switch folded elsewhere (Cynthia, Gardenia, Mars, Jupiter and Maylene's objects are placed with their type, or not placed).

## Risks

- **Volume and judgement.** A hundred looks, each reviewed by the user on a board, because Claude can't judge a likeness; batches of fifteen to twenty-five entries keep a session reviewable. A look that doesn't read is a change of values, not of code.
- **Likeness without copying.** A named character must be known at a glance and still be ours: hair, hat and colours carry it; nothing is traced and no proportion is measured from the art. Where a look lands too close, the user says so on the board.
- **Memory and first runs.** Every rig meshes once (seconds) and reads from `cache/models` afterwards; a first run of a town with twenty new looks is slow once, the kit's theories over a hundred looks take that once per clean test run, and plan 16 · T4's CI, which restores `cache/models` keyed by the model and mesher sources, meshes every look afresh after a batch that grows the kit. `Trim` and the narrower `Preload` keep the card's memory to a map's people.
- **Three names.** The looks, the types and the classes name the same people; C1 puts them through one table and a test, or an overlay's `npcType` and a class's look drift apart again.
- **Depth.** A sitter and their bench, a swimmer and the water's drawn height, a lier and a bed: each is a card against a card or a box, and the field's steep camera forgives little. The shots of C7 and C8 are the check.
- **Determinism.** A stroke's ring runs on `FieldLife.Now`, a blink on `SeedOf`, an outfit's registered name on a stable hash of its values, a gesture on the field's `dt`: nothing here may take a number from `Random` or a string's `GetHashCode`, or two harness runs differ.
- **A line inside a battle.** C12 adds an event to the rules' log, written with no roll and never after `Ended`, so the replay test and the random battles hold it; the line itself is the face's own, read from the `interlude` queue, so the screen never takes the rules' state while it waits.

## Decisions for the user

1. **Looks as a data file or as code.** *Recommended:* `Data/characters.json`, with the kit's parts in code; a look is values, and the file is what a chapter's overlay, a test and a mod read. The alternative is `CharacterStyle.For` grown to a hundred cases.
2. **How close the cast comes to the original.** *Recommended:* known at a glance by hair, hat and colours, with the official art looked at and nothing kept, as the Pokémon models were done. The alternative is a freer redesign, which would make the Gym Leaders strangers.
3. **Where the outfit is chosen.** *Recommended:* in the introduction, after the look, with the defaults preselected so a press goes on as today, and at the mirror and the boutique afterwards. The alternative is the boutique alone, which keeps the introduction as Platinum has it.
