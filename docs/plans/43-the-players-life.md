# Plan 43 · The player's life: the card, the look, photographs and a home

Written 2026-11-07, before any session, at the user's request: one of ten plans (35 to 44) from [the study of what else to add](studies/what-else-to-add-study.md).

**Goal**: what the player makes of their own trainer once the journey runs through every region. A Trainer Card they design (a pose, a background, a frame, a title, a greeting, the six Pokémon they show and their own signature), and if wanted the cards of the Leaders and Champions they beat; a salon for the hair, the eyes and the skin and a dye house for the clothes plan 11 sells; poses in photo mode, a photo book of the player's own pictures, photo spots at the landmarks of every region and a studio with backdrops, stickers and frames; a phone of our own for Unova and every region after it, beside the Pokégear and the PokéNav; a home of their own, the secret base, opened from a spot in any region and kept as it was left, with the bedroom at home decorated in every region; cafés to sit in with the lead, and travelling folk whose songs and stories change with the journey; and if wanted, neighbours whose bases raise the home's rank and a film desk that cuts a recorded battle into a film. None of it changes a rule of play: it reads the story, the counters, the team and the recordings and never moves them, and a game that touches none of it draws and plays as today.

## Design

### Content of our own

Every feature here takes a mechanic of the later games and makes the content ours: the cards' backgrounds and frames, the salon's cuts and colours, the dyes, the studio's backdrops and frames, the photo spots' places, the phone and its look, the cafés' drinks, the travelling folk, the neighbours and every line. Each is named in plain words: a card designer, a salon, a dye house, a photo book, photo spots, a photo studio, a travel phone, a home, cafés, travelling folk, neighbours, a film desk. Nothing of the games is taken, and no host, mascot, place or character of a particular game is made again for what is new: the phone has no Pokémon living in it, the studio no photographer of Alola's, the folk none of Mauville's five, the salon and the dye house no shop of Lumiose's or Malie's. Where this game already has a character, the plan uses them as they stand: the rivals, friends and professors call on the phone, and the Leaders, the Elite Four and the Champions are on their cards. The games' names appear only to say where an idea came from.

### The card

`Models/CardDesign.cs` (GPU-free): a `CardDesign` is a background and a frame of `Data/card-looks.json` (ours, hand-written, read through `GameDataFiles` as strictly as plan 24 · X4 reads what a person writes, the Fashion Case's backdrops owned offered too), a pose (a `Gesture` held at a share of its round, or none; a `Mood`; standing or sitting on the ground), the title shown (a Hall of Fame's region, a master's title, a medal won), up to six Pokémon by `Pokemon.Id` from the team and the boxes or the team as it stands, the greeting (a phrase `CARD` kept by key in `StoryState.Phrases`) and the signature. `CardLooks.Unlocked(CardFacts)` says what each look waits for (the start, a region's Badges, its Hall of Fame, a category of medals whole, the Pokédex diplomas held (`Pokedex.Diplomas`; plan 40 · GE4 adds the regions' papers and a Pokédex of our own, which then count too), and later this plan's photo spots and cafés' cards). `SignaturePad` is a grid of 160 by 48 cells: a stroke between two points fills the cells on its line, with an eraser and a clear, kept as packed bits. The front draws the design when there is one and today's front when there is none; the figure is the player's look and outfit rendered posed in the offscreen phase (as `IntroScreen.Render` does), never in a game with no window. PRINT writes the card's two sides into the screenshots folder as one picture.

### The look: the salon and the dye house

`Outfit` gains the look's own parts the salon changes, `Hair` (a cut and its colour), `Eyes` and, by decision 2, `Skin`, each null for the look's own; `CharacterStyle.Dressed` lays them over the look as it lays the garments. **The key takes the new parts only when one is chosen**, so every outfit worn today keeps its name, its sprites and its meshes. A dye is a row of `clothes.json`'s `dyes` (sixteen of ours, each a colour, an accent and the berry it is made with); a dyed garment is its garment's id with the dye's (`striped_tee~mint`), owned beside the plain one and read by `ClothingDatabase.Get`, `Wardrobe.Owns` and `CharacterStyle.Dressed`. The salon is `WardrobeScreen` opened by `wardrobe salon` (tabs CUT, COLOUR, EYES and SKIN, each change paid at the chair); the dye house is the boutiques' second counter, `wardrobe dye "jubilife"`, which takes a garment owned, a berry picked from the bag (`BagScreen.OpenToPick`) and a fee. The cuts are those both kits draw: the chibi kit's twelve, and on the grown kit as plan 30 · FM4 makes each.

### Photographs

**Poses** (Q16's leftover): `PhotoRow` gains `Pose` and `Face` at its end, the player's gesture held at its height or sitting on the ground and a mood, with a follower turned to the camera; the field still stands still and nothing of the game moves. **The photo book** (`Models/PhotoBook.cs`, GPU-free) keeps a hundred photographs (decision 5) as `PhotoPrint`s (the picture's file, the place, the region, the day, the lead's look, a spot's id, how it was taken), saved as `SaveData.PhotoBook`; the pictures stand beside the saves in `photos/` (`SavePaths.Photos`, beside `Screenshots`), each at 1920 × 1080 with a 480 × 270 copy for its page, named as `Screenshots.NameFor` names and never written over a file. Photo mode's A keeps its 4K picture in the screenshots folder as today and puts a copy in the book while it has room. A picture whose file is gone is drawn as Cameron's prints are (`PhotoScenes`). **Photo spots** are `Data/photo-spots.json` (ours: five in each built region, each the tile the player stands on and the way they face, the tile the camera looks at, its zoom and its name's key) marked by a plate on the ground (`PropType.PhotoSpot`); reading it opens photo mode on the spot's view with the POSE and FACE rows alone, and A puts the picture in the book. A region's five taken unlock its card background.

### The studio

`Models/StudioShot.cs` (GPU-free) is a composition: a backdrop (the Fashion Case's owned and the studio's own of `Data/photo-studio.json`), the player's pose and face, one Pokémon of the team with its clip and moment, where each stands, up to twelve stickers (a Seal kind owned in the Seal Case each, with its place, size and turn, never used up) and a frame of the studio's. `StudioScreen` (`GameState.Studio`) renders the figures in the offscreen phase as the gallery does (`CharacterStudio.Render`, `PokemonSprites.Render`; the model asked for with `PokemonModels.Request` and the still sprite until it is there), and A writes the picture without the panel into the book and the screenshots folder. Photo mode's filters grade the field's light rig and so have no place here.

### The phone

`Models/TravelPhone.cs` (GPU-free): one phone of our own (decision 4) that works in every region whose file says `"phone": true`, Unova and every region after it, once `FLAG_GOT_TRAVEL_PHONE` (ours) is set; the Pokégear and the PokéNav stay Johto's and Hoenn's. Its books are a new `PhoneBook.Of(region)` over each region's `Data/world/<region>/phone.json` in the Pokégear's shape (`PhoneBook` today holds Johto's `All` and Hoenn's `Hoenn` and no more; a contact's id is in one book only, `PhoneBook.Get` and `phone.txt` going by it, so the new books' ids carry their region's name), `addcontact` registers any number, a call is the contact's script of `phone.txt`, and `PhoneRinger` rings on a step as it does for the Pokégear. `PhoneRematch` asks a contact's own row (from what its rematches open) rather than a branch per region, and plan 41's rematches for the later regions' leaders fill it. The screen (`GameState.Phone`, the start menu's PHONE where it works) has three cards: CALLS (the numbers, and a rematch waiting), NEWS (the programme of `Broadcasts` a television would show today, picked by the day's number) and MAP (the Town Map's `TownMapMode.Card` where the region has a `townmap.json`, which shows plan 38's day's report once that lands); a fourth, POWERS, opens plan 36's panel of the powers in force once its TR8 stands, as the C-Gear carried its own. A call shows the caller's face, rendered as the bands' busts are, its mouth moving with a voice where one plays (`VoiceTalk.MouthOpen`, plan 26). The phone is given by a scene of ours early in each region's story and owed to a save already past it as it loads (`GameEngine.OweTheTravelPhone`, as `OweTheFashionCase` is), so no story version is raised.

### A home that goes with the player

**Spots in every region**: an overlay's `baseSpots` (`{ x, z, kind }`, tiles of the map as `braille`'s are) puts an entry in `Map.SecretBaseSpots` as Hoenn's area files do now (an area sign whose script `SecretBaseLayouts.IdOf` reads), with ids of their own beyond Emerald's 24 rooms, and `PartyScreen.ActionsFor` lists Secret Power wherever the region has a spot. **The room goes with its door** (decision 3): `SecretBaseSave.Room` is the room the base was first made in, and every later spot in any region opens into it with every piece where it was; MOVE IN HERE at the base's PC (`openbase movein`) takes the spot's own room, and each room's last layout is kept (`SecretBaseSave.Layouts`) for coming back to it. The six kinds get their own looks and the way in is drawn at a spot (H10's leftovers). **The bedroom at home** in every region is decorated from the same decoration PC (Emerald's own, H10's leftover): twelve pieces at most, each region's room its own layout (`SaveData.Bedrooms`), a piece standing in a bedroom not in the base at the same time, opened at the bedroom's computer (`openbase bedroom`).

### Cafés and the travelling folk

**Cafés** (`Data/cafes.json`: a menu of drinks of ours for each café, with prices and lines): the drink is paid, the player is seated at the café's table with the lead beside them if it follows (`pose player sit`), and its line is said; once a day at each café the lead's friendship rises a little (a `FriendshipEvent` of ours at the enum's end) and a stamp goes on the café's card (`SaveData.CafeCards`), the tenth giving the café's gift (a decoration, a dye, a studio backdrop). The cafés are the rooms there are (the Café Cabin, Celadon City's restaurant, Olivine City's café, Join Avenue's café, Lumiose City's café) and a stand of ours on Lilycove's Department Store roof; in Celadon City and Olivine City the drinks' counter stands beside plan 39 · CR7's waiter, who serves its dishes with powers in the same room. **The travelling folk** (`Models/TravellingFolk.cs`, GPU-free, remembering in the story's own variables and phrases as the Lilycove Lady does) are five people of ours: a singer, a storyteller, a swapper, a chatterer and a word-collector. Each week, by the week's number of `GameClock.Today`, one of them sits in five of the six cafés. The singer makes a song of two words the player gives (`phrase SONG`), its tune one of five short tunes of ours (`Data/music/common/folk_*.mml`) chosen by a stable hash of the words' keys (FNV-1a, never a string's `GetHashCode`); the storyteller tells a tale of ours about the player that grows as the counters and medals pass its marks; the swapper trades a decoration of the PC for one of three by the week; the chatterer chatters with the player's last phrase; the word-collector teaches a word of a new group of the phrase picker at each visit.

### Neighbours and the film desk (optional)

**Neighbours** are the Underground's diggers across the regions: by the day's number up to three neighbours' bases stand in a region at spots the home isn't at, each a person of ours in a room laid out by us with a team drawn from a table of ours at the region's levels (`GameEngine.LevelHere`); a win takes their flag once a day, and the flags raise the home's rank, which lets more pieces out. **The film desk** cuts a recording: `Highlights.Of(BattleRecordFile)` replays it with no screen and offers the turns that hold its moments; a film is a title, a song heard, up to six cuts with captions of two words, played through the watch mode with the turns between run unseen behind a title card.
