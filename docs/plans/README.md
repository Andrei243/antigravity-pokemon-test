# Roadmap: the full Platinum and the regions after it

Forty-nine plans, each worked through over several sessions. Plans 01 to 07 build Platinum and play it with friends; plans 08 to 25, written on 2026-10-06 before any of their sessions, add what those seven leave out, the other regions of the chain, and the guards and polish around them; plan 26, written on 2026-10-07 at the user's request, voices the characters' lines; plan 27 moves the game to Unity at the end; plan 28, written on 2026-10-12, makes the battles ready for two players online; plan Y dresses Dynamax; plan 29 stages the legendaries' meetings; plans 30 and 31, written on 2026-11-02, change how the regions from Kalos on move, look and sound; plans 32 to 34, written on 2026-11-04, build Galar and Paldea on an open-world engine; and plans 35 to 44, written on 2026-11-07 at the user's request, add what the main series and its spin-offs offer that the game neither had nor planned, a plan for each category ([the study they came from](studies/what-else-to-add-study.md)); plan 45, written the same day, gives the battles a camera that moves; and plan 46, written on 2026-11-09 at the user's request, builds Carpathia, a region of our own after Romania, with new species, a Sound type and the Tales.

**Where things stand (2026-11-07):** twenty-one plans are finished (below). The stories of Sinnoh, Kanto, Johto, Hoenn and Unova are whole, Kalos is drawn to its Hall of Fame and Alola is being drawn (plan 23), plan 32's open world waits at its prototype's gate for the user, and plans 35 to 44 have just been written. [00 · Overview](00-overview.md) lists what waits on the user at its top and lays every wave still to come (waves 90 to 112, then the open world's column, the voices' column, the optional sessions and plan 27); its Status has every wave so far.

## Order

**The order now is [00 · Overview](00-overview.md)**: it reorders what is left so that several sessions run at once and merge together, and since 2026-11-07 lays every wave still to come, plans 35 to 44 among them as first-class as the regions: the cheap fixes of what is built and unreachable first (38 · EX10, 36 · TR1 to TR3, 40 · GE1 to GE3, 41 · EG5), the journey and the team next, plans 42 to 44 last, all of them around the spine of plan 23's last regions, plan 30's grown kit and plan 32's open world with Galar and Paldea after its gate. What follows is the order as first written, kept for its reasons.

The plans depend on each other, so interleave them rather than finishing one before starting the next.

1. **Look and sound first.** Everything built later inherits them.
   - 04 · G1–G3: style guide, rendering foundation, interface kit.
   - 05 · A1–A3: audio engine, music director, sound effects. Independent of the rest; fit them in whenever convenient.
2. **Foundations for content.**
   - 03 · D1: species, moves and learnsets from data files.
   - 06 · R1–R3: the mechanics catalogue, the new battle core, status conditions and field effects.
   - 01 · M1–M3: world import, chunk streaming, terrain; then 04 · G4–G5: the nature and building kits.
   - 02 · S1–S2: scripting and story flags, field moves and obstacles.
   - 06 · R4–R10: Platinum's moves, abilities, held items, double battles, trainer AI and what happens after a battle. The story needs these before its first gym.
   - 04 · G6–G8: characters, Pokémon models, battle presentation; 05 · A4–A5: cries and the core music.
3. **Chapters.** Region by region: the map (01), its story (02), its species (03), its music (05 · A6). Fit in Platinum's systems (06 · R11–R18: the bag and shops, storage, encounters, daily events, breeding, side activities, the Battle Frontier) as the story reaches the places that use them. End every session with something playable.
4. **Endgame.** Pokémon League, National Pokédex, post-game areas, remaining species; then the later generations' mechanics (06 · R19–R30: Mega Evolution, Z-Moves, Dynamax, Terastallization, every remaining move, ability and item); polish with 05 · A7. (Plan 04 was finished early, on 2026-10-05; its G11 says what to time again as the cities and the later regions are built.)

5. **Beside the chapters, plans 08 to 17, 24 and 25.** Plan 24's guards are best early, before more of Platinum is built on rules nobody checks by machine; plan 25 goes with the chapters whose battles it dresses. None of them blocks Sinnoh's story, and most of their early sessions need nothing first: 12 · Q1 (a save that survives a crash) and 16 · T1–T3 (the front door, release builds, the crash handler) are the cheapest and protect players soonest; 08's options, summary and level-up panel, 09 · L1 (shiny Pokémon) and 10 · F1 (Pokémon in the field) are the most visible; 11's looks should come before the chapters that meet each character. 13, 14 and 15 belong with the post-game, and 17 whenever a second language is wanted. Everything in them that changes how the game plays is behind an option, a choice at NEW GAME or the modern rules, so Platinum's defaults never move.
6. **The other regions, plans 18 to 23**, after Sinnoh's Hall of Fame: 18 first (it makes the engine's Sinnoh-only parts regional and decides where Generation 5 onward comes from), then Kanto, Johto, Hoenn, Unova and the later regions in the chain's order.

Each plan's "Needs and gives" section lists the exact dependencies.

Read docs/plans/README.md and docs/plans/23-later-regions.md, then do the next unchecked session. Verify with the build, the tests and the screenshot harness, show me before/after screenshots, and tick the session off in the plan.

The short forms work because Claude's memory points to these plans; the long form spells everything out. At the end of a session, the plan's status checklist is updated and any decision taken is written into the plan, so the next session starts from the truth.

## Shared conventions

- **Build and test**: `dotnet build PokemonPlatinum.sln`, `dotnet test PokemonPlatinumTests`. Keep tests green; add tests for new rules (movement, scripts, battle effects, data completeness, music switching).
- **Visual checks**: `dotnet run --project tools/ShotHarness -- <out dir> [all|field|lineup|battle|flow|menus|intro|look|title|terrain|buildings|lab|life|world|times|sheets|story] [before dir]` renders the game in a hidden window and saves PNGs (`story` shows scripts at work in the field: questions, a scene, a trainer's challenge; `menus` shows every menu screen in its states and `intro` plays the new-game introduction step by step; `life` shows what moves in the field: prints, dust, leaves, splashes, doors, bubbles, every weather, camera moves, the ways into a battle, waterfalls, fountains and turbines;`look` renders the style guide's reference frames and, given the folder of an earlier run, before/after boards; `times` renders the five times of day and the quality presets; `terrain` and `buildings` render the field's nature, and its buildings, props and rooms by day and after dark; `lab` shows every terrain feature on a map made for it: a mountain with stairs and a waterfall, bridges, ice, snow, marsh, ledges, surfing, obstacles; `world` shows the imported towns and routes and times a walk across them, which is where streaming shows if it can't keep up; `area <key>` takes one shot of an area by its key; `cities [key ...]`, which is not part of `all`, builds the whole of Sinnoh from the importer's last full run and takes a shot before each different building and prop of every town, with a sheet of them per town: how a town is looked at before its area is open). Add shots for every new area or feature and look at them before calling it done; graphics sessions show before/after pairs. Two runs of the harness draw the same pictures (plan 04 · G11), so a change to anything that is drawn is checked by running a mode before and after and comparing the two folders: `dotnet run --project tools/ShotHarness -- <dir> diff <other dir>` lists the shots that changed, `crop` enlarges a rectangle of one, `contact` puts a whole run on sheets, and `profile` says where a frame's milliseconds go (the budget is 8 ms on the high preset; CLAUDE.md has the details). The harness is a file per mode (`tools/ShotHarness/Modes/`) and reaches the engine through `GameEngine.Drive` (plan 16 · T5), so the build catches whatever a change to the engine breaks in it.
- **Map checks**: `dotnet run --project tools/MapImporter` reads all of Sinnoh from the decompilation in about twelve seconds and writes, into `tools/MapImporter/out/`, a mosaic of the overworld, a picture of every area and chunk, the world files and a report of what it could not sort and of how much of each open area is in the game. It also rewrites `docs/tile-behaviours.md` and `docs/world-models.md` (the models that stand outdoors and what the game builds for each). Look at an area's picture (`out/areas/<key>.png`) before building it and compare the game's screenshot with it afterwards.
- **Content checks**: `dotnet run --project tools/ContentCheck` (plan 16 · T8; in the solution, two seconds, no window) reads every overlay, room and script and names what is wrong, a line each with the file and the thing: what an overlay names that its area hasn't, ways to no map or onto a blocked tile, fields the formats don't have, scripts, looks and flags that aren't there, and lines that run out of the text box or can't be drawn. Run it after writing or changing any of them; `ContentTests` holds the repository to it. `-- --plan <out dir> <Room or area key>` draws a room or an open area from above with its props, warps, people, signs and triggers tagged and named, and `-- --rows "<line>"` shows how the text box breaks a line.
- **Opening an area**: add its key to `PokemonPlatinumEngine/Data/world/sinnoh/world.json`, run `dotnet run --project tools/MapImporter -- --data` (it writes the chunks and area files the game needs and nothing else), and write `overlays/<key>.json` by hand: music, where the doors lead, who the people are and our own lines for them (`docs/data-files.md`, "World files"). A place that is a matrix of its own (a cave floor, a lake, a meadow) also needs an entry in `maps` there, with `"setting": "Cave"` for a cave, and its map's name in `RegionDatabase`. A trainer's overlay entry is their lines alone: who they are, their team and their prize money come from `trainers.json` (the decompilation's `res/trainers/data`, by the trainer the area file's object names), and how far they see from the area file (plan 08 · P7). `WorldTests` checks the result, and the tests that walk the whole game (`WorldWalk`) that every warp and everyone in it can be come to.
- **Writing a scene**: scripts are text files in `PokemonPlatinumEngine/Data/scripts/`, one to a place plus `common.txt` ([`docs/scripts.md`](../scripts.md) is the language). Read the original's `res/field/scripts/scripts_<map>.s` for what happens and in what order, keep its flags' and variables' names, and write every line in our own words. Give the people their scripts and the triggers theirs in the place's overlay or map file, and play the scene to its end in a test with `HeadlessScriptHost` (`StoryTests` already plays every script on every way through it and holds every name to something that exists).
- **Sound checks**: Claude can't listen. Render sounds to WAV files and spectrogram images for checks (levels, clipping, timing, loops), and have the user listen to approve.
- **Art direction**: follow [`docs/art/style-guide.md`](../art/style-guide.md) ("Sinnoh Diorama", chosen in 04 · G1): an HD-2D pixel-art overworld, full 3D battles, and a vector interface that shows Pokémon as 2D sprites.
- **Faithfulness**: follow Platinum, not Diamond/Pearl, wherever they differ (gym order, HM locations, badge effects, the Distortion World, encounter tables, music roles).
- **Rules**: Platinum's rules come from the decompilation's battle code (`src/battle/`, `res/battle/scripts/`), later generations' from Pokémon Showdown (<https://github.com/smogon/pokemon-showdown>, MIT). Rulings where generations disagree go in `docs/mechanics/rulings.md`; `docs/mechanics/coverage.md` lists what is implemented (both start in 06 · R1).
- **Reference data**: the pret/pokeplatinum decompilation (<https://github.com/pret/pokeplatinum>) has most of the game's data as JSON: `res/field/events/events_<map>.json` (NPCs, warps, triggers, signs), `res/field/encounters/encounters_<map>.json`, `res/trainers/data/<trainer>.json`, `res/pokemon/<species>/data.json`, map scripts in `res/field/scripts/scripts_<map>.s`, the world grid in `res/field/matrices/`, per-chunk land data in `res/field/maps/data/`, and the names of every music track and sound effect in `res/sound/pl_sound_data.json`. Raw files download from `https://raw.githubusercontent.com/pret/pokeplatinum/main/<path>`. Bulbapedia blocks automated fetching; pokemondb.net and Serebii work.
- **Assets**: our own art, models, music, sound effects and dialogue (written in our own words, following the original beats), plus free-licensed assets where they help (OFL fonts, CC0 textures, models and sounds, MIT-licensed libraries), each credited in `docs/art/CREDITS.md`. Nothing taken from the games themselves: no sprites, models, textures, music, sound effects, cries or script text. Files the user supplies can go in the `overrides/` folder.
- **Commits**: commit or push only when the user asks; commit on `main`.

## Regions

Decided 2026-10-01 and changed on 2026-10-12 (plan 18 · W10): the game grows into every generation as a chain of separate regions. Every new game begins in Sinnoh, and NEW GAME asks nothing about regions; finishing a region's story (its Hall of Fame) opens the way to the next one, like HeartGold and SoulSilver's trip to Kanto but in the other direction, and the chain after Sinnoh runs in generation order: Kanto, Johto, Hoenn, Unova, Kalos, Alola, Galar, Paldea. Sinnoh to Kanto and Kanto to Johto are by boat; how the player reaches each later region is decided when that region is built. Since 2026-11-03 (plan 18 · W8, decision 9 (a)) a region's harbour sails both ways once that region's Hall of Fame is set: on to the next region and back to every region already entered, with the whole team; Fly goes back to any region already entered too (plan 18 · W9). `--region <name>` starts a new game in another region for testing. The chain lives in `Data/RegionDatabase.cs`, and `RegionTests` checks it. Since 2026-11-09 (plan 23 · Z17, decision 3) the chain has a branch: a **side region** (`Region.Side`) hangs off a region of the chain by a link of `LinkKind.Side` (`RegionDatabase.SideLinksFrom`), open once its root's Hall of Fame is entered and taken by a scene of the root's own, never an attendant. Hisui, Sinnoh's past, is the first: Celestic Town's ruins lead there and the stone on Prelude Beach leads back. A side region stands after the chain in `RegionDatabase.All`, no harbour sails to it, Fly neither lands there nor leaves it for another region, a pack's region never joins after it, and its Hall of Fame moves no other region's rematches; the chain's link test counts the chain's links alone. The Lumiose era of plan 23 · Z21 will be the second, off Kalos.
