# Plan 09 · Looks: shiny and gendered Pokémon, animated sprites, status on the model, footprints, reflections, cameras, the text box, scene effects and the ways into a battle

Written 2026-10-06, before any session.

**Goal**: what the finished graphics plan still leaves out of the picture: a shiny Pokémon drawn as shiny, the hundred species whose females look different, menu sprites that move, a condition seen on the Pokémon that has it, every species' footprint, reflections in water and on polished floors, a camera kit for the story's scenes and the title's attract loop, a text box with coloured names, pauses and a backlog, the effects and set pieces the story's scenes are shown with (the nurse's machine, a burst of light, a rift, a passing shadow), and Platinum's thirty-one ways into a battle, the leaders' and the Elite Four's portraits among them. None of it changes a rule of play: every session here is a change to what is drawn, held to plan 04's two laws (two harness runs draw the same pictures through `Dice` and `FrameClock`; a frame stays under 8 ms on High, measured with `profile` before and after).

## Where we are

- **Shiny**: `Pokemon.IsShiny` is rolled 1 in 8192 in the constructor (`Models/Pokemon.cs`) and saved (`SavedPokemonData.IsShiny`), and nothing in `Graphics`, `UI` or `Battle` reads it. A model's colours are baked into `SdfMesh.Colors` at sculpt time and its eyes and marks into the decal atlas (`PokemonDecals.Paint(model, EyeState)`); `PokemonModels.Signature(species)` and `PokemonSprites.GetBaked(name, view)` key by name alone. `SkinnedModel.Upload(source, boneCount, uvs, recolor)` already takes a recolour callback, which only the decal patch passes (to whiten it). Plan 06 · R10 (after the battle) lists shininess among its rules: the roll's rules are its, the look is nobody's.
- **Gender**: `Pokemon.Gender` (`Gender.Male`, `Female`, `Genderless`) and `PokemonSpecies.GenderRatio` exist; `PokemonModels.Get(string)` is keyed by species or form, and the only female touch on a species' own sculpt is Pikachu's `HeartTail` inside its costume forms (`PokemonModels.Pikachu.cs`). Plan 03's batches D6–D9 and Kanto 1 each say "not done here", and the batches since (to Unova 4: every species of Kanto, Johto, Hoenn and Unova, 642 hand-built) don't mention it; D11 covers only females that are a form of their own (`Meowstic-Female`, `Pyroar-Female`, `Indeedee-Female`, `Basculegion-Female`, `Oinkologne-Female`, `Frillish-Female` and `Jellicent-Female`, the last two hand-built as forms). `tools/DataImporter` reads `gender_rate` from PokeAPI's `pokemon_species.csv` (`Importer.cs`) and not that table's `has_gender_differences` column.
- **Menu sprites**: `PokemonSprites` bakes one still per `SpriteView` (Front, Back, Icon) into `cache/sprites`, keyed by `Signature` and `BakeVersion`, served by `PixelArtGenerator.GetPokemonSprite` and `GetPokemonIcon`; the Pokédex entry opens with a hop (`ModernUi.Pokedex.cs`) and its cry. `PokemonStudio.Strip(context, species, clip, times, yaws, w, h, …)` already renders a clip at chosen moments for the harness's boards. Plan 04 · G1 decided that menus show 2D sprites, never 3D renders; this plan keeps that.
- **Status in battle**: `CueKind.Status` plays once as the line appears (`EffectCue`, `MoveFx`), and `ModernUi.StatusPill` marks the HP box. `BattleRenderer` builds a `PokePose` (`Time`, `Blink`, `Attack`, `Kind`, `Hurt`, `Faint`, `Entry`) from each `CombatantView` and never reads `Shown.Status`; `PokemonAnimation.EyesOf(pose)` can give `EyeState.Shut`. The log carries `StatusChanged` (a condition given or cured) and `Confused` (a confusion begun or holding); nothing marks infatuation or the end of a confusion, whose lines are plain `Said`s. `Battler.ConfusionTurns` and `Battler.Volatile.InLoveWith` are the volatiles. Plan 06 defers to the battle's art the Substitute's doll, the weather over the stage and a Secret Power or Nature Power that looks like its ground, never a lasting condition.
- **Footprints**: `species.json` has none, and the original's are art the importers may not keep. A `PokeModel` has its `Skeleton`, `Bones` (`PokeBoneInfo(Role, Side, Phase, Front)` with `PokeRole.Leg`), its `Mesh` (`SdfMesh.Positions`, `BoneIndices`, `BoneWeights`) and `Hovers`. Plan 04 · G9's footprints are the player's own (`FieldLife`, `TileBehaviors.KeepsFootprints`). Route 213 is open since plan 01 · M7, its signboard names Dr. Footstep's House and the house's door is locked until plan 01 · M11 gives it a room (`overlays/route_213.json`); he and his lines are plan 02 · S8's, ribbons plan 06 · R17's.
- **Water**: a flat surface over the ground coloured in depth bands by `FieldShaders.Water` from the shore mask `PixelGround` bakes (style guide, "Water": whole texels, nothing a smooth gradient), mirroring nothing. Puddles are done since plan 01 · M7: a cover of their own (`TerrainCover.Puddle`, `TileType.Puddle`, painted by `PixelGround`; style guide, "Puddles"), drawn wherever an open area's tiles behave as `Puddle` (Verity Lakefront, Solaceon Town, Route 212), and a reflection of whoever stands in one (`WorldRenderer.DrawReflections`, which draws `CharacterSprites.DrawReflection`: the walker's baked sprite upside down on the ground, tinted, fading, cut off where the puddle ends to the south), which is what the original's puddles mirror. `TileBehavior.ShinyFloor` is described as mirroring the walker too and is not among the tiles `DrawReflections` asks for; its 444 tiles are the League's and the three ruins' (`docs/tile-behaviours.md`), none open. The terrain lab has two puddles on its beach (`BuildTerrainLab`) and `world` shoots Route 212's (`wd6_route212_puddles`).
- **Camera**: `WorldRenderer.PanCamera`, `ReleaseCamera` and `ShakeCamera`, reached by scripts through `camera pan`, `release` and `shake` (`CameraMove`, `IScriptHost.Camera(move, x, y, seconds)`, `GameEngine.PanCamera`); the lens is the area's preset (`MapScene.ViewOf` giving `FieldView(PitchDeg, FovYDeg, Distance)` from `FieldCamera`); the title's journey uses `WorldRenderer.RenderCinematic(map, focusX, focusZ, hour)` between its own bars. `TitlePhase.Idle` waits for a button forever.
- **Text**: `DialogueManager` types plain strings at `CharactersPerSecond` (`ShowDialogue`, `ShowQuestion`, `Type`, `Advance`) and draws through `ModernUi.DrawDialogue(sw, sh, speaker, visibleText, complete)`; `PlayerIdentity.Fill` substitutes `{player}`, `{assistant}` and, since plan 02 · S4, `{rival}` (`PlayerIdentity.RivalName`) as plain text; `CharacterSprites.Portrait(context, npcType)` renders the face the Trainer Card shows; `UiNav.Window` (`UI/Kit/UiMotion.cs`) and `ModernUi.Dim` are there for a list over the field. The money mark inside a sentence is a known gap.
- **Scenes**: `FieldLife` (`Graphics/FieldLife.cs`, timed by its own `Now`) is told of steps, landings, splashes, rain, doors and an obstacle giving way (`Footstep`, `Landing`, `Splash`, `Rainfall`, `OpenDoor`, `GiveWay`) and draws them from 16-texel cells of `LifeArt.Atlas`: nothing a scene could call for, and a battle's `MoveFx` and `FxRenderer` never draw in the field. `common.Nurse` heals with `heal`, `fanfare heal` and a line before a machine whose tray always holds six balls (`PropModels.HealingMachine`, the face `healer.top`); `common.PC` says the PC is switched on over a screen that never changes (`PropModels.BuildPc` on a `TileType.PC` tile). Art that moves in place moves by the clock alone (`ArtSheet.PaintFrames`, `MapScene.Animate`). The original's scripts call nine set pieces by name (`src/scrcmd.c`); only the healing machine and the PC's two are in places open today.
- **Ways into a battle**: `SceneTransition.ForBattle(trainer, leader, theirLevel, ownLevel)` (`UI/SceneTransition.cs`) picks one of plan 04 · G9's five (`TransitionKind`: `Wild`, `WildStrong`, `Trainer`, `TrainerStrong`, `Leader`, besides `Fade`), a leader being any trainer whose class contains "Leader" (`GameEngine.StartTrainerBattle`); `Covers` is the testable function of kind, phase and point (`FieldLifeTests`), `OutSeconds` and `InSeconds` give a kind its own length, and `life` shoots each (`l30_<kind>_*`). The original has thirty-one (`src/overlay005/encounter_effect_core.c`), picked in `src/enc_effects.c`. `trainers.json` gives every Gym Leader the class "Leader" under the original's name (`leader_roark`, Roark), and has "Elite Four", "Champion", "Galactic Grunt", "Commander", "Galactic Boss", "Rival" and the five Frontier Brains' classes; `GameEngine.TerrainAt` gives the battle's ground (`BattleTerrain`). The style guide's "Known gaps after G11" say the rest "come with their places".
- **Measuring**: `FrameProfiler.Lap(FrameSection)` gives a pass its own line, `QualityProfile(SceneScale, AmbientOcclusion, Fxaa, ShadowTaps, ShadowMapSize, DepthOfField)` is what a preset can switch.

## Design

**One name for a look.** A new `Pokemon.LookName`: `ModelName`, then `+f` when the Pokémon is female and its species or form has a female look, then `+s` when it is shiny (`Gyarados+s`, `Pikachu+f+s`). `PokemonModels.Get`, `Request`, `TryGet`, `Trim` and `Signature` take a look name: they strip the suffixes, build the base model, and for a look upload a second `SkinnedModel` from the same `SdfMesh` through `Upload`'s `recolor`, with a decal atlas painted in the look's colours (`PokemonDecals.Paint` given a palette). The SDF cache is untouched, because the mesh is the same; a female whose shape differs is a model of its own (below). `Signature` hashes the look in, so `cache/sprites` keys by it, and the harness's `dex` and `versus` modes take look names as they take forms. Everything that shows a particular Pokémon (the party, the summary, the PC, the battle, the evolution scene) asks by `LookName`; the Pokédex, which shows a species, asks for the look it wants.

**Shiny palettes** (`Graphics/ShinyPalettes.cs`, GPU-free). A model's shiny palette is a map from each of its own colours (the distinct entries of `SdfMesh.Colors` and the decals' `Color`, `Iris` and `White`): a table of hand-picked palettes for the designs whose shiny is famous, our own colours chosen after each (a black and crimson Charizard, a red Gyarados, the three Sinnoh starters), and a rule for every other species and form: the main and second colours shifted in hue by an angle taken from the species' National number (never a string's hash, which changes from run to run), the belly lightened, the dark and the eye kept. A generated species shifts its `PokeGenome` colours (`Main`, `Second`, `Belly`, `Dark`, `Eye`) the same way; the genome itself is never changed. The sparkle is a `CueKind.Shiny` cue that `BattleEngine.Show` raises on `Entered` when the Pokémon is shiny, made of `FxShape.Star` quads by `MoveFx`, timed to the send-out, seeded by the animator; the star beside the name is a new `UiIcon.Star` drawn by `ModernUi.NameWithGender`. The roll stays 1 in 8192 under every rules preset; a Shiny Charm or a chosen rate is plan 13 · V5's.

**A condition on the model.** `CombatantView` gains `Condition` (the `StatusCondition` shown, with `Confused` and `InLove` flags), set by `BattleEngine.Show` as the line that tells of it is read and cleared on a cure, a recall or a faint: the screen stays a line behind the rules, as it does for HP. `StatusChanged` gives the six conditions; confusion and infatuation need a new event, `VolatileChanged(Place, bool Confused, bool InLove)`, which the core attaches with `With` to every line where either begins or ends (Attract and Cute Charm, the Destiny Knot, the herb and berries that cure them, a confusion running out), with its case in `BattleEngine.Show`. `PokePose` gains `Asleep` and `Frozen`: `EyesOf` returns `Shut` for a sleeper, `Apply` holds a frozen Pokémon's idle at one moment. What hangs about the body is `MoveFx.Condition(condition, age, seed)`: Z's rising, an ice shell of `Shard` quads with the model flashed ice-blue through `SetFlash`, sparks at intervals, small flames, bubbles with a purple pulse, stars circling a confused head, hearts over an infatuated one; a pure function of age and seed, drawn by `FxRenderer` in the 3D pass like any cue, so `BattlePresentationTests` can hold it still.

**Strips** (`PokemonSprites`). Beside the Front still, a strip of eight frames of the idle clip over one breath (`PokeModel.Tempo` sets its length), baked by the same path as the stills, cached as one PNG under the same key with the frame count, requested and served within `Service`'s budget, the still showing meanwhile. Played by `FrameClock` where one Pokémon is looked at: the Pokédex INFO page once its hop has landed, the summary, the starter choice. Lists and icons keep the still, so memory stays (a strip is 512 KB). The evolution scene renders its Pokémon live already and needs no strip.

**Female looks.** `PokemonModels.Females` lists the species and forms with a female sculpt, beside `Species` and `Forms`; a female look is `Paint`, `PaintEll` or `Mark` on the species' own sculpt where the design differs only in colour or a mark (Combee's red mark, Wobbuffet's lips), and a sculpt of its own where the shape differs (Pikachu's tail, Rhyhorn's smaller horn) or every colour of the body does (Hippopotas's swapped colours). The importer writes `genderDifferences: true` from PokeAPI's flag, the only thing taken: a flag, not a picture. A test holds that every hand-built species with the flag is in `Females`; a generated species with the flag gets a female by a rule of `PokemonGenomes` (the second colour a step lighter, the head's parts a tenth smaller, seeded from the same genome), replaced by the sculptor's own touch when plan 03 hand-builds it.

**Footprint icons** (`Graphics/Footprints.cs`, GPU-free). An icon is made from the model: the vertices that follow a `PokeRole.Leg` bone (weight 0.3 or more) and lie within a cell of the model's lowest point are projected onto the ground, one foot of each pair, into a 16×16 `PixelCanvas` of ink; a fish, a floater or a model that `Hovers` has none, as Platinum shows none for them. Cached in `cache/sprites` under `Signature`; drawn in the Pokédex list row and on the INFO page.

**Mirror pass.** Reflections are pixel art like the water they lie in: before the scene pass `WorldRenderer` draws the upright things (people and item balls as sprites, the trees' and buildings' meshes with their winding reversed) once more through a view mirrored about the water plane into a half-resolution target of `RenderContext`, and `FieldShaders.Water` reads it in the mid and deep bands only, darkened to the band's colour, broken by the wave marks and sampled in whole texels of the field, so nothing is a smooth fade. There is no sky to mirror: the field's background is a flat colour. A puddle keeps plan 01 · M7's reflection and takes nothing from the target: what the original's puddles mirror is the walker, which `WorldRenderer.DrawReflections` already draws. A polished floor (`ShinyFloor`) mirrors the walker the same way: it joins the tiles `DrawReflections` asks for, with a tint of its own, since the original mirrors only the walker there too and its rooms have nothing else standing on them. The pass gets its own `FrameSection` lap and a `Reflections` flag in `QualityProfile`, on for High and Medium, off on Low. The style guide's "Water" section changes first, and "Puddles" gains the polished floor's row.

**Camera rig** (`Overworld/CameraRig.cs`, GPU-free). The field camera's target, lens factor, path and bars become one state that is a function of time, driven by the existing pan, release and shake and by the new commands, and read by `WorldRenderer` each frame. A zoom changes the lens's field of view only, never the pitch or the distance, so `SetUpright`'s straightening of upright things holds at every zoom. A follow keeps the target on someone else's feet; a path eases through tiles at a pace; letterbox bars are the title's own (120 units) over the field and under the text box; slow motion scales the `dt` the engine hands the field while a script holds it, never the text. The title's `Idle` returns to `Journey` after thirty seconds, as Platinum's title returns to its opening.

**Rich text** (`UI/Kit/RichText.cs`, GPU-free). A line is parsed once into runs: `{red:…}`, `{blue:…}`, `{name:…}` (the speaker's colour), `{pause 0.4}`, `{speed 2}`, `{br}`, `{sfx select}`, `{money 300}` (the drawn mark, the known gap). `DialogueManager` fills a line's `{player}` as a `{name:…}` run in the boy's blue or the girl's red, which is Platinum's convention for the player's name; `PlayerIdentity.Fill` itself stays plain, because the speaker's pill, the location sign (`Map.DisplayNameAt`) and the headless host use it too. `DialogueManager.Type` honours the pauses and speeds and `DrawDialogue` draws the runs with `UiFonts` in colour. The markup lives in lines only (scripts, overlays, map files' `dialog`): `RichText.Plain` strips it for `HeadlessScriptHost.Transcript`, and `ScriptParser` reports a code it doesn't know with file and line, `ScriptLibrary.Problems` a sound that doesn't exist. Platinum's battle text colours nothing, so the battle's message box takes runs for the money mark alone (the prize line `BattleCore` says). The backlog is a ring of fifty lines with their speakers.

**Scene effects** (`Graphics/SceneFx.cs`, GPU-free). `FieldLife` is told of one thing more, `Effect(map, kind, x, y, seconds, way)`, and keeps it as a piece like a splash: timed by `FieldLife.Now` and seeded by its tile, never by a clock or a string's hash. `SceneFx.Quads(kind, age, length, seed)` gives its quads, as `FieldLife.Quads` does for life and `MoveFx` for a battle's cues, held to the style guide's "Life": whole texels, a few frames, flat shades, alpha in two or three steps. Six kinds cover every scene plan 02 names, each with a sound of our own in `SoundBank`: a **burst** (light thrown out of a point in three frames of rays, with a white flash over the picture), a **beam** (a column of light out of the sky or a machine, its edges stepping a texel at a time), a **rift** (a tear standing in the air, its rim crawling in four frames, dark inside), a **shadow** (a dark shape crossing the ground the way it is sent, flat on it like the blob shadows), a **ripple** (the picture's rows pushed sideways by whole texels on a sine, the original's way of making the screen waver) and **sparkles** (points of light rising and blinking out). The world's pieces are cells of a second atlas of 32-texel cells (`LifeArt.Scene`), drawn by `WorldRenderer` in the 3D pass with the default shader, each corner moved by `Straighten` as life's are; the flash is drawn over the composite as the transitions' is; the ripple is a row offset the composite's own pass reads (a uniform of `FieldShaders`' post program, nought when nothing ripples), so it costs no pass. A script says `effect <kind> X Y [seconds] [way] [wait|hold]`; a held effect (a beam on a machine) lasts until `effect stop X Y`, and coming to another place clears every effect with the rest of life. Plan 12 · Q9's `ReduceFlashes` holds the flash to a quarter and its `ReduceMotion` stills the ripple, whichever session lands second.

**The original's set pieces.** Where the original's scripts call a set piece by name, ours call ours: `anim heal` (`PlayPokecenterHealingAnimation`, given the number of Pokémon on the team), `anim pc on` and `anim pc off` (`PlayPCBootUpAnimation`, `PlayPCShutDownAnimation`), `anim elevator up|down [hums]` (`PlayElevatorAnimation`: a direction and how many times it hums, four in six of its seven scripts), `anim halloffame` (`PlayHallOfFameHealingAnimation`), `shake <who> [times]` (`ShakeObject`) and `flicker <who> [times]` (`FlickerObject`). The counts are the original's; the timing, the art and the sounds are ours. A machine that moves is a held face: `ArtSheet.PaintFrames` given no rate paints its frames (the healing machine's tray empty, with one ball to six, and the six lit; the PC's screen dark, starting and on), and a new `MapScene.Hold(key, frame)` asks for the frame a set piece has reached, which `Animate` redraws as it redraws a fountain's. What each shows is a function of the time since it began (`Overworld/SetPieces.cs`, GPU-free: a machine's frame, a shaken person's offset in whole texels, a flickering person seen or not, the elevator's shudder), which the headless host plays at once and writes in its `Log`. The original's one-off pieces (the Galactic HQ's `InitLakeGuardianContainmentUnits`, the Vista Lighthouse's `UseVistaLighthouseBinoculars`, the Distortion World's `StartDistortionWorldGiratinaShadowEvent`) are their chapters' to write with the effects and L4's camera, a few lines of a scene each. **Platinum's ways into a battle.** `SceneTransition.ForBattle` takes a `BattleMeeting` (new, GPU-free: each trainer's class and name or the wild Pokémon's species, single or double, the `BattleKind`, whether it is the Frontier's or a link battle, the battle's `BattleTerrain` as `GameEngine.TerrainAt` gives it, and the two first levels) and gives a `TransitionKind` and, for a band, whose. It reads as the original's `EncEffects_GetEffectPair` and `CutInEffects_ForBattle` do, their quirks kept:
- A trainer battle: at the Frontier, a Brain's way, else the double's or the Frontier's; then Galactic's (a grunt's for "Galactic Grunt", the boss's for "Commander" and "Galactic Boss"), even in a double; any other double the double's, a leader's too (the original's own slip, met only by Volkner's double in the post-game, its `ENCEFF_DOUBLE_LEADER`); a link battle the Frontier's; a leader's, an Elite Four's or the Champion's band by class and name; anyone else, the rival included, the ordinary way.
- A wild battle: Dialga, Palkia, Uxie, Azelf and Arceus the legendary's; Shaymin, Giratina, Regigigas, Heatran, Darkrai, Rotom and the three Regis (but in Pal Park) the mythical's; a double the double's; anyone else, Mesprit, Cresselia and the three birds included, the ordinary way.
- The ordinary way by the battle's ground: `Water` the water's, `Cave` the cave's, every other the grass's (G9's four), each weaker or stronger by the first levels as today.

Thirty-one shapes in all, drawn by `SceneTransition` and answered for by `Covers`, each our own after the original's beats: on water the picture wavers row by row (L11's ripple) and sinks into the dark; in a cave the dark closes in a ring on the player; a trainer on water or in a cave first sends a Poké Ball of our own drawing spinning in to the middle; a double has four pieces slide in from the corners; the Frontier a mark of ours fade in and shrink before an iris closes; a grunt six shards of a Galactic mark of our own fly in turning (decision 6); a boss the same mark rise out of the picture as it dims; a legendary the camera's lens widen and then rush in before a long fade to white; a mythical the camera jolt through sixteen held lenses before white. The two cameras go through L4's `CameraRig`, driven by `GameEngine.UpdateTransition` while the field is still drawn under the closing: the original's table (`sLegendaryEncounterCameraParams`) gives each held lens's field of view and how many frames it is held, numbers which may be imported; its angles become steps of the rig's target, because the field's upright cards are straightened for one pitch (L4's rule). The original's motion blur is left out: a smear is not pixel art. Each kind's length (`OutSeconds`) is near the original's, counted from its frames.

**Bands.** A leader's band opens across the middle of the picture with a pattern scrolling in it; the leader's portrait slides in from the right as a silhouette and lights up as the picture round the band dims; the name stands beside it in 52 Black; then everything goes to white (decision 5). An Elite Four's and the Champion's bring the player's portrait in from the left as theirs comes in from the right, the name under theirs. A portrait is our own close-up of the person's rig, rendered with `CharacterStudio.Render` into a target of its own before `BeginTextureMode(virtualScreen)` in the transition's first frame, with a face of the battle's set (`Expression`). The rig is the look plan 11 · C5 and C6 make (`roark`), already built because the leader stands on the map (`CharacterModels.Preload` builds a map's people); the player's is `PlayerIdentity.Character`, which plan 11 · C9's outfit reaches without a change here. The thirteen bands' colours are ours, in a table keyed by class and name (`SceneTransition.Bands`); nothing of the original's banners, mugshots or palettes is kept.

## Sessions

### L1 · Shiny Pokémon
- `Pokemon.LookName`; `PokemonModels` and `PokemonSprites` taking look names (`Graphics/PokemonModels.cs`, `PokemonSprites.cs`); `Graphics/ShinyPalettes.cs` with the table and the rule; the second decal atlas; `PixelArtGenerator`'s twelve callers passing the look where they show a particular Pokémon.
- `CueKind.Shiny` and its stars; a `shiny` sound in `SoundBank` with its row in `docs/sound-effects.md`; `UiIcon.Star` beside the name in the battle HUD, the party, the summary and the PC; a new `Pokedex.CaughtShiny` (saved as a set of numbers, empty for old saves) so an entry can show the shiny of a species caught shiny.
- Style guide: "Pokémon (version 2, G7)" gains the shiny rule, "Pokémon in menus" the star.
- Tests: every hand-built species' shiny differs from its normal in its main colour and no two palettes of a line are the same; `Signature("X+s")` differs from `Signature("X")`; the sparkle is the same list for the same age and seed; the saved set round-trips. Harness: `dex --shiny`, `versus Gyarados+s Milotic`, and `96_shiny` in `conditions` for the sparkle.
- **Done when** a shiny Gyarados is red in battle, in the party and in the Pokédex, sparkles as it comes out, and the normal one is drawn exactly as before (`diff` of `battle` and `menus` runs shows no change).

### L2 · A condition on the model
- `CombatantView.Condition`, `PokePose.Asleep` and `Frozen`, `MoveFx.Condition` with the seven looks (`Battle/BattleAnimator.cs`, `Graphics/BattleRenderer.cs`, `PokemonAnimation.cs`, `MoveFx.cs`); the new `VolatileChanged` event in `Battle/Sim/BattleLog.cs` on the core's lines and its case in `BattleEngine.Show`.
- Style guide: "Move effects (G8)", "Marks on a Pokémon", gains the lasting looks and their sizes.
- Tests: `EyesOf` shut for a sleeper; a frozen pose equal at two times; `Condition` empty for `None` and stable for a given age and seed; the view's condition set by the line, not the core (a `BattleCoreTests`-style walk through the log); a `VolatileChanged` on every line where confusion or infatuation begins or ends, and a recorded battle still replaying the same. Harness: `96_status_<condition>` for each, confusion and infatuation included, in `conditions`; `profile`'s battle scene before and after.
- **Done when** a Pokémon put to sleep keeps its eyes shut with Z's until it wakes, and the battle at its menu costs no more than before within the run-to-run noise.

## Risks

- **The frame.** L10 draws the upright things twice where water is in view, and L2 adds quads to every battle frame. Both are measured first and last; L10 is on Medium and High only, and if a town with a shore crosses 8 ms it goes to High alone.
- **Memory and start-up.** Looks multiply sprites: a shiny or a female doubles a species' cache, a strip is eight stills. All are baked on demand like the stills (plan 03 · D5), so nothing is paid for a Pokémon never seen.
- **Taste.** A shiny palette by rule can be ugly; the table grows as the user sees bad ones. A female by rule can be invisible; it is a stand-in until the sculpt.
- **The data's meaning.** Markup in a line is text the headless host must strip and the parser must check, or a typo shows on screen; `StoryTests` holds every line.
- **Determinism.** Everything new is a function of time and seed; a `new Random()` or a string's hash anywhere here breaks `diff`.
- **The whole picture.** L11's flash and ripple cover every pixel: the ripple is read inside the composite's own pass and the flash is one rectangle, and `profile` runs with an effect held still. L15 renders two portraits in a band's first frame, under the flash.
- **The original's art.** Its banners, mugshots, the Galactic logo and its machines' sprites are art: the bands, the marks and the machines here are drawn by us after its beats, and only its counts, frame lengths and camera numbers are read.

## Decisions for the user

1. **Portraits beside the text box?** Platinum has none. *Recommended:* yes, for named characters only, drawn from our own field sprites as the Trainer Card does, the name kept in its pill. Alternative: none, and L3 stays coloured names, pauses and the backlog.
2. **Shiny palettes: a table for how many?** *Recommended:* the thirty best-known designs by hand and the rest by rule, growing as bad ones are seen. Alternative: every hand-built species by hand (642 choices and their 223 forms, several sessions more).
3. **Female looks for generated species?** Today every flagged species to Unova is hand-built, so this decides the net for the batches to come. *Recommended:* the rule, replaced as plan 03's batches hand-build each. Alternative: none until hand-built, and the Pokédex shows one look for them.
4. **Reflections on Medium?** *Recommended:* yes, since Medium is the preset for a slower machine at 4K and the pass is cheap where no water is in view. Alternative: High only.
5. **What closes a band.** *Recommended:* white, as the original's bands, legendaries and mythicals all close, and G9's diamonds go with their test, since no way in of the original's uses them. Alternative: the bands close with G9's diamonds into the dark every other trainer's battle opens from, which keeps G9's shape in the game.
6. **The Galactic mark.** The original's grunt and boss ways in throw the team's logo at the screen, which is art. *Recommended:* one mark of our own design, the same one plan 11 · C2 puts on the grunts' uniforms ("a uniform with a mark that is not their logo"), painted by one GPU-free painter both use, whichever session comes first. Alternative: no mark, and both ways in made of plain shapes.
