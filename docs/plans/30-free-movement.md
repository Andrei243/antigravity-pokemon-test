# Plan 30 · Free movement and grown-up characters, from Kalos on

Written 2026-11-02, before any session, at the user's request.

**Goal**: from Kalos on, the player moves as the 3DS and Switch games let them, and the people look as those games' people look. Two things, each in stages:

1. **Free movement**, region by region, chosen in the region's file: first **eight directions on the tile grid** (stage 1), then **continuous movement over the same grid** with an analog stick (stage 2). Sinnoh to Unova keep the four-way grid of `player_move.c`, and so does every rule the grid carries (encounters, triggers, sight lines, puzzles, scripts).
2. **Characters that are not chibi** ("from Kalos on the characters are not chibi anymore"): realistic proportions, which reaches the character kit's frames, the faces, the clothes and hair, the skeleton, the walk and the turn, the scale of a person to a tile, a door, a prop and a Pokémon, the field's camera and shadows, and the trainers in battle.

3. **Paldea's mount** (added by the user on 2026-11-02): Koraidon or Miraidon ridden as a travel mode of Paldea's alone, with a sprint, swimming, a high jump, gliding and wall-climbing granted along its story, built on stage 2; nothing of it exists in any other region.

What is fixed already: **Kalos is drawn by hand on the tile grid** (another agent's work, plan 23 as the overview's decision of 2026-10-12 has it: no dump is read), so free movement is laid over a grid, never over a world without one. Plan 23's design ("One grid, four ways") and its decision 2 recommended against eight directions; the user has now asked for them from Kalos on, and this plan is where that answer is worked out. Nothing here changes Sinnoh, Kanto, Johto, Hoenn or Unova unless a decision below says so.

## Where we are

- **Walking is four ways and a tile at a time.** `Direction` (`Data/Enums.cs`: `Down, Up, Left, Right`) is used in 95 files, 217 times by name, and many switches over it end in `_ =>` meaning `Right` (`FieldMovement.Delta`, `Opposite`). `FieldMovement.Step(map, x, y, direction, walker)` (`Overworld/FieldMovement.cs`, GPU-free, after `src/player_move.c`) decides a step's `StepKind` (`Walk`, `Hop`, `Land`, `Climb`, `Jump`, `Blocked`), its end tile, height, `Pace` and `Obstacle`; `Carries`, `Pulls`, `Spins` and `IceSlide` move whoever stands on ice or a moving floor; `StepLimit` (1.25) makes cliffs and `SlopedAlong` lets a stair's slope be walked along its run. A `Walker` is how someone travels (`TravelMode` `OnFoot`, `Surfing`, `Cycling`, `Diving`; `BikeKind`; `FieldMoves`).
- **The player** (`Overworld/Player.cs`) holds `GridX`, `GridY`, a `Facing`, and between tiles `PixelX`/`PixelY` interpolated by `moveProgress`; `Advance(dt, map, Direction? want, run, …)` is the steering a tool or test hands it, `Update` turns `InputManager.GetMovementVector()` into the stronger of its two axes (a diagonal is never walked, it is the axis that wins), `Begin` starts a step and `Arrive` ends it on its tile, runs the warp, the `onArrive` callback (a trainer's eye) and the wild Pokémon's roll (`Map.RollWildEncounter`, `EncounterSteps`). `Player.Yaw` is already a continuous angle, eased toward `YawOf(Facing)` in `Settle`; `WalkCycle`, `WalkBlend` and `Stride` drive the legs.
- **A step is a tile entered**, and `GameEngine.OnStep` is where everything that counts steps hears of it: the characters near (`KeepCharactersNear`), the Underground, the statistics, Amity Square's count, Pal Park, the friendship walk (`FriendshipRules.WalkCycleSteps`), evolution's step count, the Pokétch's pedometer, the region map's trail (`MapTrail`), the Vs. Seeker, the music by travel mode and zone, `EnterArea`, ash, the field's marks (`StepLeavesItsMark`: prints, dust, rings), the Canalave lifts, cracked floors, poison and the radar (`EncountersStep`), the Safari Game, the Day Care and Egg cycles (`DayCareStep`), Repel (`EncounterAids.Step`), triggers (`TryStepTrigger`), trainers' sight (`CheckTrainerSight`, `TrainerApproach.CanSee`, which looks straight down a cardinal line), and the phone.
- **Who else moves** goes a tile at a time and four ways: `Follower` (a trail of tiles the player left, `PlayerLeft`, `Gap`), `Wandering` (people of the map, `PersonMovement`, `CanStep`), `NpcWalk` and `PlayerWalk` (`Overworld/ScriptedWalk.cs`: a script's walks, `GiveUpAfter`), `TrainerApproach`, and the skip system's walks walked unseen (`IScriptHost.Elapse`, `Arrive`; plan 12 · Q20).
- **Input**: `ActionFrame(uint Down, uint Pressed)` is a frame of action bits, fed by `InputManager.Feed` and recorded in `InputFrame` (with `Dt`, the clock, window events and, since plan 12 · Q6, a `PointerFrame`) in `.ppmovie` files of `InputRecording.FormatVersion` 2. The stick is `StickReader` (`Core/InputBindings.cs`: a circular dead zone with hysteresis, **eight ways** already, `DiagonalShare` 0.5) turned into the four direction bits; no analog value reaches the game. `StateHash` hashes the game's state a second at a time for `MoviePlayer`. The pointer (plan 12 · Q6, Q7) is a frame too and drives the screens' own moves; it walks nobody in the field.
- **Characters are chibi.** `CharacterModels` (`Graphics/CharacterModels.cs`) sculpts each look with the SDF kit on a 19-bone skeleton (`HumanBones`: root, hips, spine, head, two arms of three, two legs of three, hair, bag, skirt) from one of four `Frame`s (`Kid`: head radius 0.27 at 0.915, about 1.19 units tall, about two and a quarter heads; `Adult`: 0.25 at 1.02, about two and a half heads; `Little`, `Elder` shrunk from those), sized by `CharacterStyle.Height` into `CharacterRig.Scale`, meshed in cells of 1/112 (`MeshCell`), clothes on their own layer (`CharacterRig.Clothes`). `BodyBuild` is `Kid, Adult, Little, Elder`; `Data/characters.json` (plan 11 · C1) holds every look as values.
- **In the field a person is a baked sprite**: `CharacterSprites` renders the rig into 40×58 texels at 32 to the unit, orthographic from 24° above, the pixel face stamped (`CharacterFaces.Stamp`: eyes two texels by three), a one-texel outline (`PixelCanvas.OutlinePass`), cached per `SpriteKey(rig, facing, strip, frame, blink, expression)`; `FacingIndex(yaw)` rounds the yaw to **four** facings. Drawn by `WorldRenderer.GatherActors` / `DrawActors` from `PeopleInSight` as upright cards, straightened by `FieldShaders.SetUpright`, casting card shadows with a contact blob. Hand-drawn overrides are PNGs per facing and frame. Pokémon in the field are `FieldSprites`, four facings, sized `0.3 + 0.75 ×` their height in metres tiles (the player about 1.7 on that scale).
- **In battle a person is the live rig**: `SkinnedModel` (at most 32 bones, `MaxBones`), its outline an offset shell, the 128-pixel smooth face on a patch (`CharacterFaces.Paint`, `FaceLayout`), posed by `CharacterAnimation`, placed at a scale of 2.3 (2.1 for one of two: `BattleRenderer`, `BattleCamera`); the band portraits are `CharacterStudio.RenderBust` (`BustHeight` 0.84, framed for a chibi head).
- **The camera** is Platinum's (pitch 59.05°, FOV 16.18°; `MapScene.ViewOf`, `FieldCamera` in `Overworld/Tile.cs`, `CameraRig` for zoom and scenes), with every upright thing straightened by the vertex shader (`ArtLook.FieldUpright`) and drawn at 32 texels to the tile (style guide, "Pixel grid", "Field art moves in whole texels").
- **A region's file** (`Data/RegionFiles.cs`, `RegionFile`; `Data/regions/<id>.json`) has no word for movement or for a body kit. Kalos has no file yet.

## Decisions for the user

Each has a recommended answer first. Sessions FM0 and FM1 can start on the recommended answers; the prototype (FM0) is made so that questions 1 to 4 are answered from pictures, not from this text.

1. **How a free-moving person is drawn in the field.** *Recommended:* (c) **live 3D rigs, smooth and outlined, at the screen's resolution** in the regions that move freely, as battles draw people today, with the field's light and real shadows. Grown-up proportions at 32 texels to the tile give a head of five or six texels and eyes of one, which no stamp can carry, and eight or any number of facings would multiply the baked strips by two or by infinity. The alternatives: (a) **baked sprites with eight facings** (twice today's strips; works for stage 1 only, and keeps the HD-2D look), (b) **a live pixel layer**: the rigs rendered each frame into a target at 32 texels to the unit, point-scaled and outlined on the GPU, so they keep the pixel look at any yaw (costs a target and a pass, and still has the tiny-face problem). FM0 shoots all three side by side on the same scene; the user chooses there.
2. **How tall a grown-up is, against a tile and a door.** *Recommended:* an adult **1.6 tiles** tall and a teenager (the player, the rival) **1.5**, heads of about a seventh and a sixth of that, with **doors 1 tile wide and 1.75 tall** in Kalos's buildings, which the Kalos map's agent draws to that (a row of the style guide's "Buildings" for Kalos, written in FM6 and handed over before Kalos's first town is built). The chibi player is about 1.2 units of model and reads at about 130 px. The alternative keeps today's height (about 1.25 tiles) with a smaller head, so nothing of the world's scale moves, but a grown body then looks like a child beside a 1-tile door.
3. **The field's camera where people are grown.** *Recommended:* a camera of the region's own (`FieldCamera.Kalos`: pitch about 50°, the lens a little wider and nearer, so a tall body isn't a column of foreshortened head), set per area like `Cave` and `ZoomedIn`, and judged in FM0. The alternative keeps Platinum's 59° camera everywhere.
4. **The player's own look when they cross.** *Recommended:* the player's body follows the region (grown in Kalos and after, chibi again when Fly takes them back to Sinnoh, `PlayerIdentity.Character` resolved through the region's kit), so every region keeps one look throughout; the rival who travels with them, and a partner, do the same. The alternative keeps the chibi player everywhere and grows only Kalos's own people.
5. **Which regions move freely, and how it is written.** *Recommended:* a small record in the region's file (`movement`: `directions`, `speed`, `modes`; "The setting", below), with Kalos `Eight` once stage 1 is built and `Free` once stage 2 is, Alola, Galar and Paldea `Free` with analog speed, and each region's own modes listed there (Kalos's skates, Alola's rides, Galar's Rotom Bike, Paldea's mount); Sinnoh to Unova say nothing and stay four-way. The alternatives: one word per region (`Grid`, `Eight`, `Free`) with the modes left in code; or the option to turn eight-way movement on in the older regions too.
6. **An options row.** *Recommended:* one row, MOVEMENT, `As the region` (default) or `Four ways`, for a player who prefers the grid in Kalos too; a row of `GameSettings` at the end of the settings, before Help, as CLAUDE.md asks. The alternative is no row.
7. **What a step is, when a step can be diagonal or continuous.** *Recommended:* **a tile entered is one step, whatever the way** (a diagonal step, or the centre of the body crossing into another tile), so `OnStep`, `EncounterSteps`, Repel, the friendship walk, Egg cycles, the pedometer, poison and the Day Care keep their numbers per step. Walking diagonally then meets about 30% fewer wild Pokémon per distance crossed; X and Y did not count by tiles at all. The alternative counts distance: a diagonal is √2 of a step on a fractional counter that `OnStep` fires from, which keeps the rate per distance and adds a counter to the save.
8. **Corners.** *Recommended:* **a diagonal step needs both of its cardinal neighbours open** to the walker by `FieldMovement.Step` (no cutting a corner past a wall, a tree, a person, a ledge, water or a cliff), and is refused onto or off anything that is not plain walking: a ledge, a warp, a stair or ramp, ice, a moving floor, water's edge, a puzzle's tile, a Cycling Road slope, a bridge's end. The alternative lets a diagonal slide along a wall (one axis taken when the other is blocked), which feels freer and changes nothing of reach.
9. **People other than the player.** *Recommended:* everyone else stays on the grid and four-way (`Wandering`, `NpcWalk`, `TrainerApproach`, a script's `walk` and `face`, followers' paths), drawn at any yaw by their live rig (so they turn smoothly), until a session needs more. The alternative moves NPCs eight ways too, which reaches every script and the skip system.
10. **Stage 2, and when.** *Recommended:* build stage 1 for Kalos's first towns, and stage 2 (continuous movement, analog speed) once Kalos is playable to its second Gym, so the grid's rules are proven against Kalos's maps first; Alola on starts `Free`. The alternative builds stage 2 straight away and skips `Eight`; or never builds it.
11. **The mouse in the field.** *Recommended:* nothing yet: a click still opens no walk. Click to walk (a route by `Policy.Route` to the tile clicked, walked as steering) is an optional session after stage 1 (FM11). The alternative is no mouse in the field at all.
12. **The grown kit as a new kit or a rebuild.** *Recommended:* **a second kit beside the chibi one**, chosen by a field of the look (`CharacterStyle.Kit`: `Chibi` or `Grown`), its frames, sculpting helpers and face layout of its own, the skeleton the same 19 bones plus seven appended (below), so every chibi look, test, cache entry and shot is untouched. The alternative rebuilds the one kit with a proportion slider, which moves every existing mesh's cache key and every shot of every mode.

Added with Paldea's mount (the user's addition of 2026-11-02: Koraidon and Miraidon ridden, in Paldea alone; "Paldea's mount", below):

13. **How the ridden legendary is drawn.** *Recommended:* the **species' own battle model** (plan 03's hand-built Koraidon and Miraidon, in their ride forms, `Koraidon-Limited-Build`… as `PokemonModels.Paldea5.cs` names them) drawn live in the field at the field's scale with a saddle point, the grown rider's rig attached to it and posed by a ride clip set; the field sprite (`FieldSprites`) only as its impostor beyond the live-rig count. The alternative bakes the mount and rider together as a sprite of eight facings, which can't glide, climb or lean.
14. **Which of the two.** *Recommended:* the one the version would give is not ours to decide by a cartridge: **the player chooses at the story's beat where Paldea's original gives it** (both are offered once, in a scene of ours; the other appears in the story as its counterpart, as the original's does), kept in a story variable (`VAR_PALDEA_MOUNT`). The alternative fixes one (say Koraidon) for every game, or ties it to the player's look.
15. **The mount and the team.** *Recommended:* the mount is **not a party member while ridden** (as in the original: it is the ride, with its battle form a separate story Pokémon for the end of the story), so it takes no party place, gains no EXP and is never in a box; a follower of the team (plan 10 · F3) is put back in its ball while riding and comes out again on getting off. The alternative makes the ridden Pokémon the party's own lead out of its ball, which plan 06's rules for a legendary in a team and Pal Park's would then reach.
16. **The ride's powers' order.** *Recommended:* plan 34 · PD4's order kept (dash first, then `Swim` (its `Float`), `Jump` higher, `Glide`, `Climb`), each a grant (`grant <power>`, `granted <power>`) set by the scene of a Titan beaten as the original gives them, with our own lines. The alternative gives every power at once once the mount is had.
17. **Riding into battle and into the grass.** *Recommended:* riding meets **no wild Pokémon from the tables** (the tables' grass rolls only on foot), only those seen in the field (plan 23's `WildVisible`, plan 10 · F5), touched as on foot; a trainer's eye or a touch dismounts the player behind the battle's fade. The alternative rolls the tables at the ride's steps as a bicycle does (`thick` grass and all).

## Design

### The setting: a small record in the region's file

A region's movement is not one word but a small record, `RegionFile.Movement` (`Data/RegionFiles.cs`, a `RegionMovement`, GPU-free), with `RegionFile.Kit` (`Chibi` when absent) beside it:

| Field | Values | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| `directions` | `Four` (default), `Eight`, `Free` | stage 0 (today), stage 1, stage 2 |
| `speed` | `Paces` (default), `Analog` | Platinum's five `Pace`s by button, or the stick's tilt (stage 2 only) |
| `modes` | a list, empty by default | the region's own ways of travelling beyond `OnFoot`, `Surfing`, `Cycling` and `Diving`: `Skates` (Kalos), `Ride` (Alola's Ride Pager), `RotomBike` (Galar), `Mount` (Paldea's Koraidon or Miraidon) |

What each region's file says, under the recommended answers (plan 23's choices kept where it made them):

| Region | `directions` | `speed` | `modes` | `kit` |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Sinnoh, Kanto, Johto, Hoenn, Unova | (absent: `Four`) | `Paces` | none | `Chibi` |
| Kalos | `Eight`, then `Free` (decision 10) | `Paces`, then `Analog` | `Skates` (plan 23's `TravelMode.Skating`) | `Grown` |
| Alola | `Free` | `Analog` | `Ride` (plan 23 · Z5's Ride Pager: the grants carried by a ridden Pokémon, a rider drawn as a surfer is) | `Grown` |
| Galar | `Free` | `Analog` | `RotomBike` (the Bicycle item with plan 23's `Float` grant) | `Grown` |
| Paldea | `Free` | `Analog` | `Mount` (below) | `Grown` |
| Hisui, Lumiose's era (optional in plan 23) | `Free` | `Analog` | none (Hisui's rides are a later question) | `Grown` |

It is read once, as the region's other fields are, and asked through `GameEngine.Here` (plan 18 · W1) wherever movement, a mode or a look is decided: `GameEngine.MovementHere` is the region's record, with `directions` made `Four` when decision 6's row says four ways. **A mode is offered only where its region's record lists it**: a `TravelMode` of a mode not in `MovementHere.Modes` is refused by `FieldMovement` (a new `Obstacle.NotHere`) and ended by the engine on arrival in another region, so the skates never reach Alola and the mount never leaves Paldea. `RegionTests` holds that only regions of generation 6 and later may say anything but the defaults, and that each mode is listed by one region alone. A map entry of `world.json` may also give a `movement` record of its own for a place that must stay on the grid inside a free region (a Gym puzzle, the Battle Maison's halls, a minigame: `"movement": { "directions": "Four" }`) or where a mode is barred (indoors: `"modes": []`), and that wins for the place.

### Stage 1: eight directions on the grid

**Direction stays four ways.** Adding diagonals to `Direction` would send every `_ =>` branch that means `Right` the wrong way, in 95 files. Instead a new `Heading` (`Overworld/Heading.cs`, GPU-free) is the eight ways the player may step (`N, NE, E, SE, S, SW, W, NW`), with `Cardinal` (the `Direction` it is, null for a diagonal), `Parts` (a diagonal's two `Direction`s), `Delta` and `Yaw`. Only the player's own steering speaks `Heading`; `Player.Facing` stays a `Direction`, the cardinal the player last faced, and is what talking, sight, scripts, the save and the follower read. A diagonal step leaves `Facing` on the part of the two that was pressed last (or the vertical one, decision 8's tests say which), while `Player.Yaw` takes the diagonal's true angle for drawing.

**The rule is `FieldMovement.StepDiagonal(map, x, y, heading, walker)`**, beside `Step` and built from it: the diagonal is open when `Step` from the tile to each cardinal neighbour is a plain `Walk` at the same height band, `Step` from each neighbour on to the diagonal tile is a plain `Walk`, and none of the four tiles is one of decision 8's refusals (`TileBehaviors` gains `RefusesDiagonal`). It gives a `FieldStep` with `Kind = Walk`, the diagonal tile, the height the two-legged path gives, and the `Pace` of a cardinal step; `Player.Begin` lengthens its time by √2 (`stepRate / √2`) so the speed over the ground is the same. Refused, the player falls back to the cardinal part pressed last (decision 8's alternative: slides along), so holding up-right into a wall walks up it. Everything `StepKind`-shaped (hops, landings, climbs, jumps, slides, the Cycling Road's pull) stays cardinal: a diagonal is never one of them. `CanStartSurf`, `Climb`, Strength, ice and every Gym puzzle ask `Facing`, a cardinal, as now.

**Reach does not grow.** Under decision 8 every tile a diagonal reaches is reachable by its two cardinal steps, so `WorldWalk` (the walk of the whole game through warps, by cardinal steps) stays the truth of what can be come to, and a test holds it (below).

**Input for stage 1** is the bits there are: `StickReader` already reads eight ways and the keyboard's two keys make a diagonal; `Player.Update` stops choosing the stronger axis when `MovementHere` is `Eight` and builds a `Heading` from both. Nothing new is recorded: the four bits already say the eight ways, so stage 1 changes no movie format.

**Followers** keep their tile trail (`Follower.PlayerLeft` takes any tile left, diagonal or not; `NpcWalk` learns a diagonal step for a follower alone, its time √2, since a follower must tread where the player trod). **Sight lines** are untouched: a trainer looks down a cardinal line; a player stepping diagonally across it is seen on the tile it enters, as anyone is. **Triggers, warps and doors** fire on the tile entered, as now; a warp is never entered diagonally (decision 8), so a door is still walked into from its front.

### Stage 2: continuous movement over the grid

**The position is fixed-point.** The player's place becomes `SubX`, `SubY`: integers in 1/256ths of a tile (eight to a texel), with `GridX`, `GridY` kept as the tile under the body's centre (`Sub >> 8`) for everything that asks for a tile. Integer positions keep collision and replay exact (floats summed over hours drift, and `CLAUDE.md`'s rules on `Dice`, `FrameClock` and recordings want a run to be the same twice); `dt` is still the frame's float, which recordings already keep. `PixelX`/`PixelY` become derived. The save gains the two sub-tile offsets (`SaveData` and `SaveData.CurrentSave` raised, a `SaveMigration` step that puts an older save at its tile's centre, the corpus of `SaveCorpusTests` given a new fixture by the harness's `saves`).

**Collision is a circle against the grid's own answers.** The body is a circle (radius 0.3 tiles, decision in FM7 by eye) moving by the stick's vector times the pace's speed (`FieldMovement.TilesPerSecond`). Whether a tile's edge is a wall for this walker is **asked of `FieldMovement.Step` from the tile the centre is in toward the neighbour**: a `Blocked` answer makes that edge solid, a `Walk` answer open, anything else a hand-off (below). So rails, closed sides (`ClosedToward`), people, deep water, cliffs, `NoBicycles`, `AcroBike` rails and every rule `player_move.c` gave the grid stay the grid's, in one place; the circle is resolved against solid edges and the corners where two meet (axis by axis, then the corner's point), never against a second collision model. People are solid tiles as they are now (`Map.NpcIn`), or circles of their own if FM8 finds tiles too blunt.

**What isn't plain walking is handed to the grid.** When the circle would cross an edge whose `Step` answer is not `Walk` and the stick points across it (within 35° of the edge's normal), the player is **eased onto the tile's centre line** across that edge (a quarter of a second at walking pace) and the existing step is played as it is today: a `Hop` over a ledge, a `Jump` off a ramp, `Land` from water, a `Climb` up a waterfall or rock face, the Cycling Road's `Pulls`, ice (`IceSlide`: on ice the player is the grid's until they stop on a tile that isn't), a moving floor, a stair met across its run (`SlopedAlong` decides which edges are stairs), a bridge's deck (`Map.SurfaceAt` by height), a warp's tile (a door is walked into from its front and the warp fires when the centre enters it), a Strength boulder (pushed by a press against it, cardinal), a Gym puzzle's tile and anything a map entry keeps on the grid. Heights everywhere else come from `Map.HeightAt` and `SlopeAt` sampled at the centre and eased as `Player.height` is eased now; `Relief.At` stays the drawn height.

**A step is a tile entered** (decision 7): when the centre crosses into another tile, `Arrive`'s part that isn't about ending a step (the warp, `onArrive`, the encounter roll, `JustLanded`, the grass's rustle) runs, and `OnStep` with it, so everything listed under "Where we are" keeps working as it does. A run of the body back and forth over one edge could farm steps; a tile is counted only after the centre has gone 0.4 of a tile into it from the edge it came over (hysteresis), which a test holds.

**Talking and pressing** read a point 0.7 of a tile ahead of the centre along `Yaw`: `TryInteract` asks the tile there, and the tile beside it in the cardinal nearest the yaw if that one has nobody, so a person diagonally in front is spoken to. `Facing` is the cardinal nearest the yaw, which scripts, sight, the save and every cardinal rule read.

**Who else moves.** NPCs stay on the grid (decision 9); they are drawn turning to any yaw. `Follower` gets a trail of the player's positions sampled every 0.25 of a tile (a breadcrumb list in sub-tiles), walked at the player's speed a body's length behind, falling back to its tile trail on a map that is `Grid`. `PlayerWalk` (a script's walk) first eases the player onto the centre of their tile and then walks the tiles as now, so every scene, `Arrive` and the skip system's unseen walks (`IScriptHost.Elapse`) keep their tiles; a script that ends leaves the player there, centred. `TrainerApproach` walks to the tile before the player's, now the tile the player's centre is in. `Wandering.CanStep` asks whether the player's tile and heading are free, as now, with the heading the tile the centre is moving toward.

**The camera** follows the centre with the field's smoothing (the camera kit's `Look`, which a free region feeds a point, not a tile); its target is snapped to texels as now, so the ground still moves in whole texels while the body glides. `CameraRig` and the scenes' camera are untouched.

### Input and recordings

**Stage 2 needs the stick's value**, not only its eight ways. `ActionFrame` grows `MoveX`, `MoveY` (`sbyte`, −127 to 127): what the stick says after `StickReader`'s dead zone, rescaled so the edge of the dead zone is 0 and full tilt 127, **quantised where it is read** so the game sees exactly what a recording keeps; the keyboard's eight ways give 127, 90 on a diagonal. The direction bits stay (menus read them; a frame with bits and no vector is the keyboard's). `InputManager.Feed`, `MoveVector()` (a new `GetMovementVector` that returns the frame's vector in free regions and today's axes elsewhere) and `Player.Update` read it. `InputRecording.FormatVersion` becomes 3 (the two bytes per frame); format 2 still reads, with the vector made from the bits. `StateHash` adds `SubX`, `SubY`. A walk's speed follows the tilt: under half tilt walks slower (`Pace.Slow`), a full tilt walks, the run button or a full tilt held a second runs (decision in FM9 by feel; Autorun, plan 08 · P2, keeps its meaning). The pointer (plan 12 · Q6, Q7) is unchanged; decision 11's click to walk is FM11.

### Characters that are not chibi

**A second kit** (decision 12): `CharacterStyle.Kit` (`Chibi`, `Grown`), set on a look in `characters.json` or by the region (decision 4 for the player). `CharacterModels` keeps `BuildHumanoid` for `Chibi` and gains `BuildGrown` in a file of its own (`Graphics/CharacterModels.Grown.cs`), so the chibi sculpt, its cache keys (`SdfCache`) and every existing shot never move. Its frames, a proposal FM4 tunes by eye (units of the model, before `Scale`):

| Build | Height | Head (radius at centre) | Heads tall | Hips | Shoulders across | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| `Teen` (the player, the rival, students) | 1.50 | 0.105 at 1.38 | 7 | 0.76 | 0.30 | long legs, slim |
| `Grown` (most adults) | 1.60 | 0.105 at 1.48 | 7.5 | 0.82 | 0.36 | |
| `GrownBroad` (hikers, black belts, sailors) | 1.65 | 0.11 at 1.53 | 7.5 | 0.83 | 0.44 | wide chest |
| `Child` (youngsters, lasses) | 1.15 | 0.11 at 1.03 | 5 | 0.56 | 0.24 | still not chibi |
| `GrownElder` | 1.48 | 0.105 at 1.36 | 7 | 0.76 | 0.32 | head forward, as `Elder` |

**The skeleton** keeps `HumanBones` 0 to 18 at their numbers and appends what a grown body needs, within `SkinnedModel.MaxBones` (32): `Neck` 19, `Chest` 20 (the spine in two), `ClavicleL`/`ClavicleR` 21, 22, `ToeL`/`ToeR` 23, 24, `CoatTail` 25 (a long coat's back, apart from the skirt). Hands stay mittens with a thumb on the hand bone (fingers as bones would be six more each, and the hands are a few pixels in the field). `CharacterAnimation`'s clips set the chibi bones as now and leave the new ones at rest unless a grown clip moves them, so a chibi rig is posed exactly as before.

**Sculpting at grown scale** is where the work is: limbs a third as thick as a chibi's are close to the mesher's cell (1/112 of a unit: a forearm of radius 0.028 is three cells), so the grown kit meshes at **1/160** (`GrownMeshCell`, FM4 measures memory over a town's crowd, since `ClothesCell` said finer cost too much), and every rule of the style guide's "Characters" is re-read for the grown body: locks of hair sized to a small head (a lock that is a tenth of a chibi head is a cell), a hat on the hair's surface, hems, a coat's tails on their own bone, a nose (a small ellipsoid, which a chibi face never had), a jaw and a neck, knees and elbows that bend without collapsing (a thigh capsule tapering into the knee). The style guide gains a "Grown characters" section before the code (FM4), and `CharacterKitTests` gets a grown twin of each rule it holds (every hat shows over the hair, every cut inside the frame: the grown frame's limits are its own).

**The faces.** In battle and on the band's portrait the face is still a painted texture on a patch (`CharacterFaces.Paint`), with a **grown layout**: eyes about a fifth of the head's width apart and at its middle, almond-shaped, smaller irises, a nose shaded in the paint over the sculpted one, lips, brows always drawn (a grown face's bangs rarely cover them); 256 pixels square, since a grown head on a battle platform at a scale of 2.3 is still the size a chibi head was. The five expressions and their blinks are kept, so `Expression`, a script's `mood` and trainers who speak (plan 11 · C13) work unchanged. In the field, under decision 1 (c), the face is the same texture at the screen's resolution: a grown head in the field is about 35 pixels tall at 4K under decision 2's sizes, enough for eyes and a mouth; the pixel stamp (`CharacterFaces.Stamp`) is the chibi kit's alone (under decision 1 (a) or (b) a grown face is a two-texel eye pair and nothing else, which is that option's cost).

**Animation** (`CharacterAnimation`, a grown set of clips beside the chibi set):
- **Walk and run blend by speed**: the cycle advances by distance over the ground (`Walk += distance / strideLength`, the stride the build's leg length), so feet never slide whatever the tilt; `WalkBlend` becomes a speed share from standing to walking, and a second blend from walking to running (the run's lean, bent elbows and longer stride come in between 1.2 and 1.8 times walking pace).
- **Turning**: the yaw eases toward the heading at a rate by speed (`Settle`'s 18 a second standing, less while running, so a run curves); a turn of more than 120° from standing is a step-turn clip (0.25 s, the feet planted); the body leans into a turn by the sideways acceleration, at most 8°.
- **Starts and stops**: a weight shift into the first step and a short settle on stopping, both under 0.2 s so the grid's responsiveness is kept.
- **Emotes and stances** re-timed for a grown body (a bow from the hips, a shrug, a wave above the shoulder: the chibi rule "raised hands stay beside the big head" no longer applies); `Stance` sits at grown seat heights (a chair's seat about 0.45 of a grown body's leg, so the field's seat lift is per kit: `Seats` gains the grown heights).

### Paldea's mount: Koraidon or Miraidon (Paldea only)

The user's addition of 2026-11-02: Paldea moves further than any other region, on the legendary as a mount, and nowhere else. Plan 23 · Z13 ("`TravelMode.Ride` with the dash in `PaceOn`, the rider drawn as Z5's are") and Z14 ("`Jump`, `Glide` (`StepKind.Glide` down a face to the first ground), `Climb` and `Float` as grants in the original's order") are its neighbours and were written for four ways; this section is what they become on stage 2. **Since 2026-11-04 Paldea is not on the grid**: it is built on [plan 32](32-open-world-engine.md)'s open-world engine by [plan 34](34-paldea.md), which keeps this section's decisions (13 to 17) and re-expresses its rules on plan 32's capsule (plan 34 · PD2, in place of FM12; FM14's story is plan 34's PD1 to PD8; FM13's rig stands as it is).

**A travel mode of Paldea's own.** `TravelMode.Mount` (at the enum's end, after `Diving`), offered only where `MovementHere.Modes` lists `Mount` (decision 5), which only Paldea's file does. It is a state like surfing: `Player.Mode`, its own `Walker` (a `MountPowers` flags value beside `FieldMoves`: `Dash`, `Swim`, `HighJump`, `Glide`, `Climb`), its own speeds on stage 2's analog model (a trot at full tilt about twice a run, a **sprint** on the run button held about three times, with a stamina of our own only if FM13 finds the sprint unbounded too strong), got on and off by a key (the Item button's place, as the Bicycle's: an action of `GameAction` at the enum's end, `Mount`, bound in every preset) with a short mount and dismount clip behind which nothing moves. Its rules are GPU-free in `Overworld/MountRules.cs`, beside `FieldMovement`, and use stage 2's circle and the hand-offs of FM8, plus:
- **Swim** (granted): open water is a `Walk` for the mount, no Surf asked, the mount's swimming form shown; diving is not offered.
- **High jump** (granted): a press lifts the mount over one face of under `StepLimit` + 1 tile, or onto a ledge from below.
- **Glide** (granted): from a face's top edge or the top of a jump, a press holds a glide: the height falls at a fixed rate while the circle moves at the trot's speed, steered by the stick, landing on the first ground whose height it meets (`Map.HeightAt` sampled along the way, `Relief` drawn); a glide never crosses into another map, and a warp is entered only on the ground. This was plan 23's `StepKind.Glide`, made continuous; plan 34 · PD2 makes it a fall on plan 32's capsule.
- **Climb** (granted): a face marked climbable (a `TileBehavior` of Paldea's own, `ClimbableFace`, written by the Paldea map's sessions on the tiles of a face, as `RockClimb` faces are Sinnoh's) is climbed by pushing into it: the mount sticks to it, moves up, down and along it at walking pace, and comes off at its top edge onto the ground or by a press. Faces not marked stay cliffs. Heights on a face are the face's own, so the climb is drawn against `Relief`'s faces.
- **What the mount refuses**: indoors, Gyms, a `Grid` place, a map entry whose `modes` is empty, scripted scenes (a script's `walk` dismounts first, behind a fade), and the field's puzzles; ice and moving floors are ridden over without sliding (the mount's feet are its own).

**Steps while riding** are tiles entered as on foot (decision 7), so `OnStep`'s counts (Egg cycles, friendship, the pedometer, the Day Care) go on while riding; the grass's tables are not rolled (decision 17).

**The mount as a rig.** The ridden Pokémon is its **battle model** (`PokemonModels`, the form named by the mode: `Koraidon-Sprinting-Build` trotting and sprinting, `-Swimming-Build` on water, `-Gliding-Build` in the air, `-Limited-Build` standing; Miraidon's `Drive`, `Aquatic`, `Glide` and `Low-Power` modes the same way), drawn live in the field (decision 13), scaled by FM6's grown rule for Pokémon in the field, posed by its own clips (`PokemonAnimation`'s walk and run by speed, a glide's held wings, a climb's reach) with a **seat**: a point and a frame on the model's back bone (a new `PokeModel.Seat`, found once from the sculpt's body bone and checked by a test for each of the eight forms), which the rider's rig is parented to every frame. The rider (the grown player's rig) has a **ride clip set** in `CharacterAnimation`'s grown clips (sitting astride: the hips to the seat, the thighs out, the hands forward on the handles the sculpt gives; leaning with the mount's lean; crouched in a sprint; upright in a glide) blended by the mount's speed and pose, the same way the walk blends by speed. Changing form (trot to swim, swim to glide) cross-fades the two models over 0.2 s and the rider's clip with it. Both cast shadows in the shadow pass; reflections (plan 09 · L10) draw both. Its budget is one more live Pokémon model in the field, counted in FM3's `Characters` lap.

**Everything else the mount meets.**
- **Followers**: a Pokémon of the team out of its ball (plan 10 · F3, Amity Square's walk) goes back into its ball as the player mounts and comes out on getting off; a partner of the story (`Follower` with a `TrainerId`) refuses the mount (`MountRules.WhyNot`: "not with company"), as a partner keeps the Bicycle in the bag today.
- **Pokémon in the field** (plan 10 · F5, plan 23's `WildVisible`): seen and touched while riding as on foot; touching one dismounts the player behind the battle's fade; Let's Go (plan 34 · PD5) is sent from the saddle without dismounting.
- **Picnics** (plan 34 · PD7, plan 10 · F6's camp): opening one dismounts the player, and the mount stands at the camp as one of the Pokémon there (drawn by its standing form), outside the team.
- **The camera**: riding, the camera kit's `CameraRig` is given a further lens (the zoom's factor about 0.85, so more is seen ahead at speed) and leads the movement by a share of the velocity, eased; gliding, the pitch comes down by a few degrees toward decision 3's `FieldCamera.Kalos` pitch, so the ground below shows; climbing, the camera pulls back from the face. All through the rig's own moves, never a second camera.
- **Battles**: the mount never fights from the saddle; at a battle's start the player dismounts behind the fade, and the field is back on foot. The legendary's story battle (the end of Paldea's story) is a Pokémon of the story, made then, as plan 34 · PD8 has it.

**Nothing of the mount outside Paldea.** The mode is listed by Paldea's file alone, so `MovementHere.Modes` refuses it anywhere else; `GameEngine.CrossTo` (the one way into a region: plan 18 · W5's vessel and W9's Fly) **dismounts the player before the crossing** and the arrival puts them on foot; the key does nothing outside Paldea (the bag shows nothing, since the mount is no item). A **Champion crossing into Paldea** (plan 18's chain, after Galar) is given the mount at Paldea's own beat like any Paldea game, and **Fly back to Paldea** finds it there: the state (which of the two, `VAR_PALDEA_MOUNT`, and the powers granted, plan 23's `StoryState.Grants(region)`) is the region's story's and is never read in another region. A save made while riding (`SaveData`'s travel mode) wakes riding in Paldea; a save from another region never has the mode (a test holds both).

- **Determinism.** A float position or a stick read unquantised would make recordings and the harness differ; fixed-point positions and the quantised stick are what keep `RecordingTests` and `diff` honest.

- **The mount is the most new thing here**: gliding and climbing are heights the field has never moved through, and a rider on a live Pokémon model is two rigs bound every frame. FM12 proves the rules on boxes before FM13 draws anything.
