# Plan 03 · Every Pokémon: the National Pokédex

**Goal**: all 1025 species (Generations 1–9). The Sinnoh Pokédex (Platinum's 210) during the story; the National Pokédex unlocked after the Hall of Fame; every species obtainable in the game (each in the region of its own games, decision 4), each with its stats, types, abilities, moves, evolutions, Pokédex entry and its own animated 3D model.

## Where we are

- All 1025 species, 847 moves (no Z-Moves or Max Moves), 313 abilities and 642 items are in `PokemonPlatinumEngine/Data/*.json`, generated by `tools/DataImporter` (D1, 2026-10-01). Species 1–493 and moves 1–467 carry Platinum's own values from the decompilation; the rest come from PokeAPI.
- `Data/TypeChart.cs` is Generation 4's chart plus the Fairy type's matchups, which only later species and moves use.
- Pokémon have natures, IVs, six growth rates (Erratic and Fluctuating included), gender by species ratio, abilities, held items, friendship and a personality value. Every evolution method runs (`docs/mechanics/evolution.md`, done 2026-10-02). EVs are gained (plan 06 · R10), species have their forms (D11) and Pokémon breed (plan 06 · R15).
- The game holds all of them at once (D13, 2026-10-12): what it keeps in memory no longer grows with the species shown, the PC has a hundred boxes once the Pokédex is the National one, and the harness's `fulldex` mode weighs a game with every species in it.
- `docs/mechanics/coverage.md` (generated) lists which moves, abilities, held items and evolution methods the engine runs.
- The Pokédex (D10, 2026-10-04): Platinum's Sinnoh Pokédex of 210 species, and the National one after the Hall of Fame, each with its own numbers and counts; an entry's INFO, AREA and SIZE pages; Platinum's search; diplomas (`Models/Pokedex.cs`, `Models/PokedexSearch.cs`, `Data/Habitats.cs`, `UI/PokedexScreen.cs`).
- Every species has a 3D model: all 1,025 hand-built, the whole of Platinum's Sinnoh Pokédex (the 24 of plan 04 in `Graphics/PokemonModels.cs`, Buneary among them for the new-game introduction; D6's 50 in `PokemonModels.Sinnoh1.cs`, the Sinnoh Pokédex from Kricketot to Lopunny; ## Decisions

Decisions 1 and 2 were taken in D1 (2026-10-01), 3 in D5 (changed in D6), 5 in D10 (2026-10-04), and 4 and 6 by the user on 2026-10-12.

1. **Data sources.** *Decided:* as below, read by `tools/DataImporter` from pinned commits. Descriptions of moves, abilities and items use PokeAPI's own short effect texts; Pokédex entries are generated from the data, so no game text is used.
   - Species 1–493 and Generation 4 moves: Platinum's own data from the decompilation: `res/pokemon/<species>/data.json` (base stats, types, abilities, catch rate, EXP, growth rate, gender ratio, egg groups, EV yields, held items, body colour, learnsets by level, TM, tutor and egg) and `res/moves/<move>/` for moves. Find evolutions and Pokédex text in the same tree.
   - Species 494–1025 and later moves and abilities: PokeAPI's CSV files (<https://github.com/PokeAPI/pokeapi/tree/master/data/v2/csv>, BSD licence): `pokemon_species.csv`, `pokemon.csv`, `pokemon_stats.csv`, `pokemon_types.csv`, `pokemon_abilities.csv`, `pokemon_moves.csv` (learnsets by version group), `pokemon_evolution.csv`, `evolution_chains.csv`, `pokemon_forms.csv`, `pokemon_species_flavor_text.csv`, `pokemon_shapes.csv`, `pokemon_colors.csv`, `pokemon_dex_numbers.csv`, `moves.csv`, `move_meta.csv`, `abilities.csv`.
2. **Type rules.** *Decided (D1):* the recommendation. keep Platinum's chart and typings for species 1–493 so the story plays exactly like Platinum, and add the Fairy type's matchups for the later species that need it. The alternative is modern data everywhere (Clefairy becomes Fairy, Steel stops resisting Ghost and Dark), which is consistent with later generations but changes Platinum's battles.
3. **Models for 1025 species.** *Decided 2026-10-04 (D5), changing the plan below:* a **procedural model generator** gives every species without a hand-built model one of its own, and **model files** replace any species' model: a glTF file named after the species in `overrides/models` (ignored by git, so it never enters the repository) is drawn instead, with its textures, skeleton and clips. The user supplies the files for the species they want; the hand-built batches D6–D9 were put on hold. The generator works from body shape (PokeAPI's 14 shapes, refined by the words of the species' category: "Mouse", "River Crab", "Cocoon"), body colour, height, types, egg groups and evolution stage. All art in the repository stays our own; the decomp's sprite files are not used.
   *Originally:* hand-built models for Platinum's 210 Sinnoh species with the SDF kit, the generator for everyone else, popular species upgraded to hand-built models over time.
   *Changed 2026-10-04 (D6):* the generated models of the Sinnoh species looked poor to the user, so the hand-built batches are back on: Platinum's 210 Sinnoh species get hand-built models after all (D6–D9). The generator stays for every other species, and model files still replace any model. *Done 2026-10-05:* all 210 are hand-built since D9.
   *Continued 2026-10-05:* the popular species from outside the Sinnoh Pokédex are hand-built next, a batch of about fifty sculpts at a time in National Pokédex order, starting with Kanto, where a new game begins. Each batch brings its species' forms too: a hand-built species' form shows the species' sculpt until it has one of its own, so leaving them out would show Mega Charizard X as an orange Charizard. The generator keeps every species not reached yet.
4. **Where the non-Sinnoh species live.** *Decided 2026-10-12 (the user's answer, kept in the overview's "Decisions that gate sessions"):* each region holds every species of its own games. In Sinnoh that is every species of Diamond, Pearl and Platinum, on the map or by a quest, with the other two starters after the Hall of Fame; the whole Pokédex can be completed with no link. So the Pal Park-style area and the zone for Generations 5–9 below are dropped: the species of the later generations live in their own regions (plans 19 to 23), and the last session of each region's plan runs for it the test D12a runs for Sinnoh. D12a places the two other versions' exclusives, gives the trade evolutions a way that needs nobody else (they keep their method: a Linking Cord used on the Pokémon already counts as a trade), and proposes where the species the second slot gave go and what Pal Park's Catching Show draws from now that nothing is migrated.
   *As first written:* Platinum's post-game tables (National Pokédex route additions, swarms, the Poké Radar, the Great Marsh and Trophy Garden dailies) cover many species 1–493. The rest need in-game sources, because trading and GBA migration don't exist here:
   - ~~a Pal Park-style post-game area with habitats per region;~~ (dropped 2026-10-12)
   - ~~a new post-game zone for Generations 5–9 (for example Distortion World rifts or new Battle Zone routes) with biome-based tables;~~ (dropped 2026-10-12)
   - gifts: other regions' starters from Professor Rowan or Oak after the National Pokédex;
   - event legendaries as post-game quests (Darkrai on Newmoon Island, Shaymin in Flower Paradise, Arceus at the Hall of Origin, and the like);
   - ~~trade evolutions by another method~~ *Decided 2026-10-02:* the game will have trading (online in plan 07, with characters in the game in plan 06 · R12), so trade evolutions stay trade evolutions. A Linking Cord used on the Pokémon also counts as a trade and already works; whether the game hands them out is still open.
5. **When the National Pokédex unlocks.** *Decided 2026-10-04 (D10):* as in Platinum: after the Hall of Fame, once every species of the Sinnoh Pokédex has been seen. Professor Rowan's scene (plan 02, post-game) asks `Pokedex.CanUnlockNational` and calls `UnlockNational`.
6. **The PC once the Pokédex is the National one.** *Decided by the user on 2026-10-12, after D13:* a hundred boxes from Professor Oak's upgrade on (3,000 places), Platinum's eighteen until then. Eighteen held one of each of Platinum's 493 species; they hold barely half of 1,025, and with a full team and full boxes nothing more can be caught. D13 first made it thirty-six, enough for one of each species; the user asked for a hundred, for a lot more Pokémon (`docs/mechanics/rulings.md`, "The game with every species in it").

## Architecture

- **Data files**: generated JSON, one file to a kind (`PokemonPlatinumEngine/Data/species.json`, `moves.json`, `abilities.json`, `items.json`; D1 decided against a folder each), checked in, so the game never needs a network or the decomp, and loaded into `PokemonDatabase`, `MoveDatabase` and friends at start-up. The C# species and move definitions are gone.
- **`tools/DataImporter`** (console app): reads a local decomp checkout and the PokeAPI CSVs, normalises names (`SPECIES_PIPLUP` → `Piplup`), writes the JSON. Deterministic and re-runnable; hand corrections live in override files it applies last.
- **Species model** gains: abilities (two plus hidden), gender ratio, egg groups, EV yield, base friendship, hatch cycles, held items, shape, colour, forms, regional dex numbers (Sinnoh 1–210), evolution methods.
- **Pokémon** gain: ability, EVs, held item, friendship, form, Pokérus (optional), met location and level.
- **Battle engine**: what moves, abilities and items *do* is [plan 06](06-game-mechanics.md)'s work. This plan supplies their data.
- **Pokédex**: Sinnoh and National modes; National hidden until the unlock flag (plan 02 sets it after the Hall of Fame); sort and search; "area" view built from the encounter tables; forms viewer (optional).
- **Sprites at scale**: bake lazily on first use and cache the PNGs on disk (keyed by species and model version) instead of baking everything at start-up; icons the same.
- **Save format**: a Pokémon's species by its name, with its national number beside it since D13 (found by the number if the data no longer has the name). Older saves need no migration for it.

### D5 · Procedural models for everyone (after plan 04 · G7)
The model generator, built on plan 04's SDF modelling kit, from shape, colour, height, types and feature tags; lazy baking with the disk cache; a harness contact sheet showing every species (extend the `sheets` mode with paging). **Done when** all 1025 species (after D11) have a distinct model and start-up time doesn't grow.
*Ready from plan 04 · G7:* `PokeBuilder` (shapes, paint, eye and marking decals, bones for each body part), the six body plans with their animation clips, and `PokemonModels.Sample(plan)` for the serpent, fish and floating plans that no hand-built species uses yet. Meshing one model takes about a second the first time, so the generator must mesh lazily and keep the start-up preload to the species the story shows.

**Outcome (2026-10-04).**
- **The generator** (`PokemonGenome.cs`, `PokemonGenerator.cs` and `.Parts.cs`): a species' data becomes a *genome*, then a sculpt on `PokeBuilder`. The genome picks one of 18 body kinds (quadruped, upright, humanoid, legs, bird, bat, insect, serpent, crawler, arthropod, fish, ball, armed, blob, cluster, cocoon, jellyfish, tentacled) from the Pokédex shape and the category's words, each sculpted on one of the six body plans so the existing clips move it; a stance (long-legged, low, stocky); colours from the Pokédex colour and the types; and its parts: ears, mouth (muzzle, snout, beak, bill, jaw, trunk, mandibles), tail and its tip (flame, leaf, star), what grows on the head and the back (horns, antlers, crests, sprouts, flowers, flames, antennae, shells, bulbs, spikes, plates, manes), wings (feathered, skin, insect, butterfly), hands (paws, fists, claws, blades, pincers), patterns, ruffs, cheeks and a few bodies of their own (stars, candles, mounds, cones, bivalves, pumpkins, gears, bells, balloons, swords, trees). Choices that run through an evolutionary line (ears, tail, eyes, the shade of the colour) come from a seed of the family's first species, the rest from the species' own, so lines look related and every species differs; the random numbers are our own (SplitMix32), so a species' model never changes between runs or .NET versions. Small species fill less of their frame, by height. All 1025 sculpts are distinct (tested).
- **Model files** (`GltfReader`, `ImportedModels`, `JpegDecoder`): a glTF 2.0 file named after a species in `overrides/models` (next to the game or in the folder it is started from) replaces its model: skinned meshes cut into pieces of at most 32 bones, PNG and JPEG textures (our own decoder: the raylib build reads only PNG), outlines from a shell pushed out along smoothed normals, and the file's own clips played for what their names say (`battlewait`, `attack01`, `damage`, `down`, `appear`…), with the body plan's motion for the rest. Draco and meshopt compression and WebP textures are refused with a message. [`docs/model-files.md`](../model-files.md) is the user's guide. `GltfWriter` writes any sculpted model out (mesh, eyes as a texture, skeleton, every clip baked), so a generated model can be refined in Blender and dropped back in (harness `export` mode).
- **Lazy models and sprites**: models are requested in the background when a battle or an evolution is about to show them and waited for behind the fade (`PokemonModels.Request`, `TryGet`); models the next scene doesn't need are let go after a battle (`Trim`). Menu sprites are baked the first time a menu asks for them (`PokemonSprites.Request`, `Service` once a frame before the 3D passes; the stand-in shows meanwhile) and cached as PNGs in `cache/sprites`, named by a signature of the model (`PokemonModels.Signature`: the file's size and date, or a hash of the sculpt), so start-up bakes nothing it baked before. Start-up still preloads only the hand-built species.
- **Harness**: `dex [--back] [species ...]` boards of every species' 3D model, sixty to a page (from behind with `--back`, as the player's own Pokémon is seen in battle); `sheets` run on its own pages through every species' menu sprites; `pokemon` adds a turntable of one generated species of each body kind and an imported model with its own clips (`91_turntable_imported_riolu`, `92_clips_imported_riolu`); `export [species ...]` writes models as `.glb` files.
- **Tests**: 36 new (`PokemonGeneratorTests`, `PokemonImportTests`; 803 pass with main's G9–G11 tests): every species sculpts, distinctly and the same every time; the genome follows the data; one species of each kind meshes into a sound model (in one piece, on the ground, every bone and decal covered); the glTF round trip, palettes for big rigs, clips by name, refused compression, which surfaces are metal, file names, a broken file falling back, JPEG decoding, background requests and trimming, sprite signatures. `POKEMON_MODELS_ALL=1` meshes and checks all 1025 generated models (about a quarter of an hour the first time).
- **Review of every species**: the boards showed loose parts that the front view mostly hides. Bushy tails, sprout leaves and flowers stopped short of the body, insect wings short of the thorax, a bell's rim beyond its dome, curly tails flat in the air; thin rocky serpents had gaps between their boulders, a pair of bodies only touched, and a crescent grew a lump in its bite. Ninety-two species had one of these besides the bushy-tailed ones; every such part now reaches the body, and the mesh check requires one piece.
- **Decided here**: model files replace hand-building (decision 3); the hand-built batches D6–D9 are on hold (back on since D6).
- **Not done here**: forms (D11) have no models yet; a model file's morph targets, normal maps and texture animation are ignored; generated models look generic (the user found the generated Pikachu odd: the data only says "a yellow, Electric, four-legged mouse"), which model files are for.

### D13 · Balance and polish
Level curves in the post-game areas, start-up time and memory with 1025 species, save migration, a full-dex playthrough check with debug tools (a debug menu to give any Pokémon and set flags).

**Outcome (2026-10-12, done ahead of D12 at the user's asking).** The game was run with every species in it (the harness's new `fulldex` mode: the National Pokédex complete, the boxes full, forty battles against species never met) and weighed. What that found, and what was done:

- **Memory that grew with every species shown.** Walking down a complete National Pokédex asks for a menu sprite of every species, each baked from its model, and the models were kept: the first 300 entries left 275 models and took the process from 1.1 GB to 5.3 GB, so the whole list could not be walked at all. Three things were wrong, and each is fixed:
  - **A model built only to bake a sprite is let go once it is baked** (`PokemonModels.Borrow`, `Return`, `IsBorrowed`; `PokemonSprites.Service`), and at most sixteen are on loan at a time (`PokemonSprites.MostOnLoan`: a box asks for thirty species in one frame). If the game asks for the model meanwhile (`Request`, `Get`, `Preload`), it is the game's and stays.
  - **Every skinned mesh left two buffers behind when it was freed**, in ordinary play too: 2 MB a mesh, so 4 to 5 MB for every battle's foe and every person let go on a warp. `SkinnedModel.AttachBones` kept the ids of its bone index and weight buffers in the mesh's own list at places 7 and 8, but the raylib the game ships with gives that list seven places (`MAX_MESH_VERTEX_BUFFERS` with its GPU skinning compiled out) and frees seven: both writes went past the end of the list, and `UnloadMesh` never freed the two buffers. The ids are the `SkinnedModel`'s own now, freed in `Unload`. Three hundred uploads and frees left 584 MB behind; two hundred now leave 1 MB.
  - **The menu sprites held are bounded** (`Graphics/SpriteLedger.cs`, GPU-free; `PokemonSprites.Kept`, 240 species, about 34 MB of textures where every species and form came to 220 MB): those longest unasked for are freed between frames and read from the cache again when next shown, never one asked for in the last two seconds nor the start's own.

  The walk down all 1,025 now ends with 3 models held and the process at 683 MB, where it stood at 703 MB as the Pokédex opened; forty battles leave it flat (1,141 MB after ten, 1,080 MB after forty).
- **The start.** Its steps are the log's phases (`Log.Phases`, plan 16 · T3, which landed the same day; this session's own log of them gave way to it). With 1,025 species the data itself is small: the species' file takes 41 to 49 ms and 34 MB, the moves' 50 ms, a Pokémon is made in 5 µs. What weighed was models: the start built the model of every Pokémon standing anywhere in the field and kept the opening's twenty-four for the whole game, 46 models and 304 MB of managed memory before the title. Now the field's Pokémon are made ready where the player is, as the people are (`KeepCharactersNear`, and only those whose sprites no earlier run baked: `FieldSprites.Ready`), and no model is kept for good but the stand-in's: the start asks only for the title's Giratina and the introduction's Buneary (`PokemonModels.ShownAtOnce`), and the opening's twenty-four keep only their menu sprites made at the start (`PokemonModels.Preloaded`). After the start: 3 models, 150 MB managed, about 600 MB in all. The team's menu sprites are asked for as the start menu opens, so a late team's icons are there when its screens open. The 1.4 to 1.7 s of the 2.3 to 3.3 s start that go to the maps and the world are plan 01's and plan 16's.
- **The mesh cache on disk** was 1.78 MB a file, two files a model: 5.6 GB once all 1,566 models had been seen. A mesh is now kept packed (`SdfCache`: the same numbers a byte of each at a time, each index as its step from the one before, deflated): 0.52 MB a file, 1.6 GB for all, bit for bit what was meshed. A file kept as it was first written is still read, and is packed as it is read unless another tree shares the folder (`POKEMON_CACHE`); a tree at an older commit can't read a packed file and meshes that model again, overwriting it with one both can read.
- **The PC** held 540 Pokémon, barely half of the National Pokédex; with a full team nothing more could be caught. It has a hundred boxes once the Pokédex is the National one (`PcBoxes.NationalBoxCount`, `Grow`; the script's `nationaldex` gives them, and Professor Oak says so), and Platinum's eighteen until then. The number is the user's: the session made it thirty-six and it was raised to a hundred the same day, with a track and a number on the screen in place of a hundred dots and a key kept down at the box's name running through the boxes. A save keeps as many boxes as its PC has, and one that had the National Pokédex before is given them as it loads. `docs/mechanics/rulings.md`, "The game with every species in it".
- **Saves.** Every species and every form is saved and read back as it was (a test goes through all 1,566, the names with apostrophes, accents and gender signs among them). A Pokémon's species is kept by name and, beside it, by its number in the National Pokédex (`SavedPokemonData.DexNumber`): if a later import spells a name another way the species is found by the number, where it came back a Turtwig. A save of a whole living Pokédex (1,025 in the hundred boxes) is 2.1 MB, written in 72 ms and read in 32 ms. No version was raised: the number is left out of a save that doesn't have it.
- **The full-dex check** (`FullDexTests`): a Pokémon of every species and form is made at levels 1, 5, 50 and 100 with stats, an ability and one to four moves, each a move it learns by then. It found two things:
  - Crabrawler's data began at level 5, so one hatched at level 1 knew a Tackle it never learns (PokeAPI's table lacks its first move; corrected in `tools/DataImporter/Overrides/species.json`).
  - A Pokémon made in a form by `ChangeForm` keeps its species' moves. That is right for one that changes form and is how the original makes a trainer's, so nothing of Platinum's changes; but 86 forms have a learnset of their own, and one that is met in such a form must know the form's moves: `Pokemon.InForm` makes it so, for D12's sources of forms to use.

  No species at any level knows only moves the engine can't run. The level-up sets with no attack in them (a wild Shuppet's or Surskit's at 50, Umbreon's at 100) are the original's own.
- **Levels.** Every level in the game today is Platinum's own, so there was nothing of ours to balance; and since decision 4 (taken the same day) Sinnoh gets no zone of its own for the later generations. What D13 leaves is the yardstick for what D12a places: `PokemonPlatinumTests/Golden/levels.txt` (`GoldenLevelTests`) holds the levels of every place with wild Pokémon or trainers, Platinum's own. After the Hall of Fame the grass and the caves hold levels 47 to 55 and the trainers 51 to 61; the Champion's team is 58 to 62 the first time and 74 to 78 in the rematch.
- **Harness**: `fulldex` (not part of `all`; `Modes/FullDex.cs`) prints the start's steps, the data's cost, what an upload leaves behind, the Pokédex's walk, the boxes, the save and the battles, each with the memory and the counts held, and a line that begins with `!!` for anything out of its bounds; and shoots the screens of a full game (`fd*`). `postgame` prints the PC's boxes after the upgrade.
- **Pictures**: `title`, `intro`, `battle`, `menus` and `evolution` were shot in `main` and in this tree and compared (186 shots), and `title`, `battle`, `menus` and `evolution` again once waves 6 and 7 had been merged in (164 shots): all the same.
- **Tests**: `FullDexTests` (fourteen: every species and form made, their moves, saved and read back, a species found by its number, the boxes and their growing, a save of a hundred boxes, the PC's screen going through them, a complete Pokédex, the sprites' bound, what the start asks for, the packed mesh, the old mesh still read, a damaged one refused), a model on loan in `PokemonImportTests`, `GoldenLevelTests`.
- **Decided with the user**: the PC grows with the National Pokédex, to a hundred boxes (decision 6: the session's thirty-six became the user's hundred on the same day).
- **Not done here**:
  - **The levels of what D12a places** (the other versions' exclusives, the gifts, the other two starters): D12a sets them against `levels.txt` and accepts the file with what it changes. The later regions' curves are their own plans' (plan 18 has the question of the level a journey arrives with).
  - **The debug menu.** Plan 16 · T6 owns it (its console's `pokemon` and `flag`) and says D13 builds none; the check here goes through the harness's `Driver` and the tests instead.
  - **The first time every sprite is baked** a frame takes 11.6 ms at the median (14.1 ms at the 90th percentile, 25 ms at the longest) while the list is walked a species a frame, over the 8 ms budget; afterwards 3.6 ms. Baking is the model's upload, three renders read back and a PNG written, on the main thread: moving the reading back and the writing off it is the place to look.
  - **A player's first meshing.** The mesh cache is made on the player's own machine, about a second of every core for a model the first time it is shown; whether a cache ships with the game is plan 16's decision 5. The numbers here are from a machine with three other sessions building beside it: the megabytes and the counts hold, the milliseconds want a quiet machine.
