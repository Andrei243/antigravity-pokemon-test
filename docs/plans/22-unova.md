# Plan 22 · Unova

Written 2026-10-06, before any session. Rewritten 2026-10-20, when the user found a decompilation of Black 2 and White 2 ([fuddlesworth/pokebw2](https://github.com/fuddlesworth/pokebw2)) and ruled that Unova follows Black 2 and White 2's story ("the second story is better").

**Goal**: Unova as the fifth region of the chain, read from the Black 2 and White 2 decompilation as Johto is read from HeartGold's: its map, events, wild Pokémon, trainers and the order of its scenes from the decompilation's data, its story from Aspertia City to the Pokémon League and the old roads of the post-game on Black 2's beats in our own words, its four seasons (a month each of the real calendar, changing the field's colours, the winter's drifts, the wild Pokémon and Deerling's coat, in Unova alone), its own Pokédex order, the triple and rotation battles plan 14 builds, the Battle Subway, the Pokémon World Tournament in its own hall at Driftveil, the Hidden Grottoes, and a song for every role of its music table, in our own art, sound and words. The player comes in by ship from Hoenn and leaves by plane for Kalos.

## Where we are

- **The chain**: `Data/RegionDatabase.cs` has Unova as a `Region` with no `Start` and no file in `Data/regions/`, so `IsBuilt` is false; its two `RegionLink`s (`Hoenn → Unova` since plan 18 · W10, `Unova → Kalos`) are `Transport.Undecided`. Plan 18 · W1 gave every region its record (`Data/regions/<id>.json`: the professor and his lines, the starters, the badges, the dex and its unlock rule, the spawns, the rival), W2 the badges per region, W3 every regional Pokédex (`PokemonSpecies.DexNumbers`), W4 what crosses and W5 the journey on screen (`common.Crossing`). Plan 18's decision 7 keeps the player's own figure in every region.
- **The source**: until 2026-10-20 no decompilation of a Generation 5 game was known, so plan 18's decision 5 weighed a reader of the user's own dump, drawing by hand with plan 16 · T9's editor, and generated layouts, and the overview took drawing by hand ("a decompilation wherever one exists"; Unova and after drawn). [fuddlesworth/pokebw2](https://github.com/fuddlesworth/pokebw2) is a decompilation of Black 2 and White 2 (USA/Europe), under the GNU GPL v3.0, whose data archives are rebuilt from text sources byte for byte against both ROMs: so by the overview's own rule Unova is read from it, and nothing is drawn and no dump is read. What it holds and what we may take from it is "The source", below. The readers it joins are `tools/MapImporter`'s: `DecompMaps` and `LandData` for Platinum, `Gba/` for FireRed and Emerald (plan 18 · W6, W7), `Hg/` for HeartGold (plan 20 · J1), all writing `WorldChunkFile`, `WorldMatrixFile`, `WorldAreaFile` and `habitats.json` through `WorldWriter` (`Data/WorldFiles.cs`); the pinned commits are in `tools/DataImporter/Sources.cs`, whose `Checkout` makes a sparse checkout of a pinned commit's folders into the importer's git-ignored `.cache`.
- **Seasons** exist nowhere. `GameClock` (`Core/GameClock.cs`) is `TimeOfDay`, `Fixed`, `Hour` from the wall clock and `ForHour`'s table, and the date, `Today`, which `FixedDate` fixes (the harness sets the first of June 2026); `GameSettings.TimeOfDay` and `OptionRow.TimeOfDay` fix it; `ArtLook.FieldRig(hour, map)` gives the light, `ArtLook.Weathered` changes it for the weather, `ArtLook.SnowGrade` grades a snow arena. What is white in winter is the import's: `MapArea.Snowbound` (`Data/World.cs` sets it where snow cover outnumbers green), `TerrainCover.Snow` and `Ice`, the snow behaviours (`TileBehavior.ShallowSnow` to `DeepestSnow`, which `TileBehaviors.KeepsFootprints` reads), `TreeModels.Pine(…, snow:)`; a lawn's tones are `PixelGround`'s own `Grass` and `GrassLight`, a tree's green is a tint `TreeModels.Pine` and `Round` mix over grey leaf sheets (`NatureArt.Leaves`, painted in `NatureArt.TreeTones`), the tall grass and the tufts have `NatureArt`'s own shades, and `SceneTextures.Leaves`, `Needles`, `TallGrass`, `LawnTuft` and `Flowers` are one shared sheet each, made in `SceneTextures.Warm`. Plan 01 · M7's weather calendar (`calendar.json`, Platinum's `sYearlyWeather`, read by `GameClock.Today`) is Platinum's weather by the day of the year, not a season.
- **Deerling and Sawsbuck** are in `species.json` with their coats as forms of `FormKind.Look` (`Deerling-Summer`, `Deerling-Autumn`, `Deerling-Winter`, the spring coat the species itself; Sawsbuck the same) and each coat evolves into its own (`fromForm`, `targetForm`). `Pokemon.ChangeForm` changes a form, `SavedPokemonData.Form` keeps it; nothing changes it with a calendar. Unova's 156 species are hand-built (plan 03's Unova batches, its decision 3: `PokemonModels.Unova1.cs` to `Unova4.cs`) and so are the coats: `Deerling-Summer` to `Sawsbuck-Winter` are in `PokemonModels.Forms`, made in `PokemonModels.Regional.cs` by `DeerlingBuild` and `SawsbuckBuild` (`PokemonModels.Unova3.cs`, on a private `Season` of their own), so a coat that changes changes the model and the sprite with it.
- **Wild Pokémon**: `WorldAreaFile.Land` has twelve slots met 20, 20, 10, 10, 10, 10, 5, 5, 4, 4, 1 and 1 times in a hundred and `Water` five met 60, 30, 5, 4 and 1, the same weights Black 2 and White 2 use; `HabitatArea` lists an area's by `Morning`, `Day`, `Night` and `Surf`; `Map.RollWildEncounter` rolls through `WildEncounterRules`. Plan 02 · S2 brought the rods (`Overworld/Fishing.cs`, `MapArea.RodEncounters`); plan 06 · R13 the time of day's slots and swarms (`EncounterSlots.Grass`).
- **Formats**: plan 14 · B6 and B7 build triples and rotations, B11 and B12 the World Tournament (`Models/Tournament.cs`, `Data/tournaments.json` with a section per region), plan 06 · R18 the Battle Tower's streaks and Battle Points (`Models/Frontier/`).
- **Rules**: every game is played by the modern rules since the user's ruling of 2026-10-12 (plan 06 · R19, `Ruleset.Modern`), so Unova is too; Generation 5's moves and abilities are plan 06 · R24 to R27's, and a move the engine can't yet run is `Partial` or `None` in `docs/mechanics/coverage.md`.
- **Music**: `Data/music/` has `common` and a folder per region built; `MusicDirector.Resolve(role, region, exists)` looks in the region's folder first and plays a stand-in for a role the region has no song for (`MusicDirector.StandIns`); `Data/audio/sound-map.json` gives each trainer class its eye theme.
- **The roadmap's order**: the chain is built in generation order, so plans 19, 20 and 21 stand before this one and nothing in Sinnoh waits for anything here. Everything Unova alone has (seasons, the grottoes, the Subway's lines, the hall at Driftveil) is built only behind Unova's maps, so nothing of the other regions moves.

## Design

### The source

**Black 2 and White 2 over Black and White** (decision 1, taken by the user on 2026-10-20): the larger map (Aspertia, Virbank, Lentimas, Humilau and the sea routes, with the old roads of Black and White as the post-game), the story two years on, the Pokémon World Tournament, the Hidden Grottoes, the Subway's full set of lines, the 301 species of its Pokédex.

**What the decompilation is.** [fuddlesworth/pokebw2](https://github.com/fuddlesworth/pokebw2), read on 2026-10-20 at commit `9ba4760ada88496013d64346af3faf1c67fe9238` (2026-10-19): a work-in-progress decompilation of Black 2 and White 2 (USA/Europe, NDSi Enhanced) aiming at a native PC port, both ROMs rebuilt byte for byte from one tree. Its C code is only partly matched (its README tracks the share), but every data archive the field and the story use is built from text sources and checked against the ROM, so what the data says is exactly what the game holds, whatever the code's progress. Its licence is the **GNU GPL v3.0**, which covers its own work: the code, the tools and the symbol names. Its `res/` folder also holds what was extracted from the ROMs (textures and models as PNG and glTF, sprites, the sound archive, the game's text), which is Nintendo's and which nobody's licence lets us take. So the rule of every other import holds here word for word: **layouts and numbers, never a tile's graphics, a model, a sound or a line of text**, and our reader is our own code, never theirs.

What it has, in its own words (its `docs/data.md`), and what the reader does with each:

| In the decompilation | What it is | Taken |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `res/field/zones/<zone>.json` (616) | Zone headers: the map matrix, scripts, music (`SEQ_*`, or one a season), the encounter table, the area, the place name, weather, camera, battle background, cycling, Escape Rope, Fly and where Fly lands | Every field but the text bank: names, songs by constant (a role in our music table, never the song), weather, camera, the flags |
| `res/field/matrices/<matrix>.json` | Matrices: a grid of map models and the zone of each cell; `overworld` is 27 × 29 | Whole, as `WorldMatrixFile` |
| `res/field/maps/models/<chunk>/terrain_*.csv` (1,065 chunks) | Each chunk's 32 × 32 collision grid as editable integers: shape, slope and height table indices, tile type (behaviour and permission), split tiles; the slope and height tables are in `src/ov036/field_map_chunk_height.c` | Behaviours, permissions and heights (the tables' numbers, read once into our own table), as `WorldChunkFile` |
| `res/field/maps/models/<chunk>/model.glb` | The chunk's model, with an empty node for every field prop placed on it (`bw2_prop_NNNN_id_XXXXX`: position, turn, resource) | The glTF's JSON chunk alone: the props' places and ids, the materials' texture names (a tile's look, through a `Bw2Cover` table) and which tiles a polygon covers (through `TerrainModel`); never a vertex, a texture or the binary buffer |
| `res/field/props/{exterior,interior}/models/<bundle>/<model>/data.json` | Each prop model's resource id and short name | The id and the name, as Sinnoh's models are named in `WorldModels`; never the model |
| `res/field/areas/<area>.json` | Area records: the prop bundle, the texture set and its season variants (`_SUMMER`, `_AUTUMN`, `_WINTER`) | Which areas have seasons and which chunks a season swaps |
| `res/field/events/<zone>.json` (617) | People (look, movement, range, direction, hide flag, script), warps, triggers with their variables, signs and init scripts | Whole but the message ids, as the area file's events |
| `res/field/encounters/<zone>.json` (136) | Wild Pokémon, `all_year` or `spring` to `winter`, each with grass, dark grass, shaking grass, surf, rippling surf, fishing and rippling fishing, split by version where the two differ | Whole, as `WorldAreaFile`'s tables and the seasons' |
| `res/trainers/data/<trainer>.json` (815) | Every trainer: name, class, battle style (single, double, triple, rotation), items, AI flags, money, party with levels, items, abilities and moves | Everything but the name's text is the record; the names are proper nouns, kept as Platinum's are |
| `res/trainers/facilities/` | The Battle Subway's and Trial House's trainers and sets, the Black Tower's and White Treehollow's, the World Tournament's leaders, masters, regulars and rentals | The numbers, as plan 06 · R18 took Platinum's `frontier.json` |
| `res/field/trades/`, `res/field/places/`, `res/town_map/` | In-game trades, place names, the Town Map's layout | The numbers and the layout, never the map's picture |
| `res/field/scripts/<zone>.s` (1,289) | Every field script, disassembled, with flags and variables by name or by value (`EVENT_FLAG_0x02e9`) | **Read, never copied**: what a scene does and in what order (who stands and walks where, the flags and variables it sets, the battles, the items and Pokémon given, the warps), which is how plan 02 used Platinum's scripts. Our scripts are written in our language, in our words |
| `generated/*.txt` | The constant lists: species, items, zones, flags, variables, trainer classes, object codes | Names, to read the rest by |
| `res/text/`, sprites, sounds, PNG and glTF | The game's text and art | **Never**. A message id is made of words of the message (`AspertiaCity_Text_…`), so not even the ids are written into the repository |

**The reader** is `tools/MapImporter/Bw2/`, beside `Gba/` and `Hg/`, sharing `WorldWriter` and `TerrainModel`: `Sources.Bw2Repo` and `Bw2Commit` pin the commit, and `Sources.Checkout` fetches only the folders above (about 30 MB of JSON, CSV and scripts, and the 84 MB of the chunks' `model.glb`, read for their JSON chunk alone) into the git-ignored `.cache`. Since the decompilation's data is already text, the reader is mostly JSON and CSV: `Bw2Maps.cs` (the zones, the matrices, the terrain grids, the props from the glTF nodes), `Bw2Behaviours.cs` (a table from the tile type onto `TileBehavior`: Platinum's value where the meaning is Platinum's, plan 18 · W6's GBA value where it is the GBA's, and a value of its own for what neither has, dark grass among them, each a row of `TileBehaviors.InGame` and of `docs/tile-behaviours.md`; if the `byte` runs out, U1 widens it as "Risks" says), `Bw2Cover.cs` (Unova's texture names in `Cover.Rules`' order), `Bw2Events.cs`, `Bw2Encounters.cs` and `Bw2Trainers.cs`. The command is `--game black2 [--quick] [--data]` into `out/unova/` (the mosaics by cover, behaviour and height, a picture per area, `report.md` with the unknown textures and behaviours counted), and `--data` writes `Data/world/unova/` by its `world.json`. The world files are checked in as Sinnoh's are, so the game builds from the repository alone and the decompilation is needed only to run the reader; CI never runs it. The importer's README gains Unova's table of what is read and what is never kept, as Platinum's has. PokeAPI's pinned checkout (`encounters.csv`, `encounter_condition_values.csv`'s `season-spring` to `season-winter` for `black-2` and `white-2`) stays a second witness for the encounter tables.

**The version.** The decompilation builds both versions from one tree: the zones, matrices and areas are the same in both, and what differs is said where it differs (the encounter tables' `black2` and `white2`, Opelucid's two looks, Black City and White Forest, the version's dragon). Decision 4 picks one.

**If the decompilation went away** (a takedown, a rewrite of its formats): the pinned commit is all a run needs, and the world files already checked in keep the game whole. A chapter not yet imported would then be drawn by hand with plan 16 · T9's editor from the same structure, which is what this plan said before 2026-10-20.

### Unova's world folder

`Data/world/unova/` with the layout of `Data/world/johto/`: `world.json` with `"region": "Unova"`, the generated `matrices/`, `chunks/`, `areas/`, `trainers.json` and `habitats.json`, hand-written `overlays/<key>.json`. The overworld is the decompilation's `overworld` matrix, `Unova` (positions in tiles of the whole region, as `Sinnoh` is), and every other matrix a map of its own: cave floors, tower storeys, the bridges' own, and the rooms, which are matrices with plan 18 · W7's `MapSetting.Indoors` and need no hand-made files (as Johto's, plan 20 · J2, cut to what their ways in reach). Area keys are the decompilation's zone constants in lower case without `ZONE_` (`aspertia_city`, `route_19`, `castelia_sewers`, `giant_chasm`, `aspertia_city_pokemon_center`; its numbered placeholders, `castelia_city_12`, stay numbered until it names them), which collide with none of the other regions'.

Buildings are props placed on the map, as Sinnoh's models are, so `WorldMapBuilder.PlaceModels` lays them out from a catalogue of Unova's own, `Data/WorldModels.Unova.cs` behind `WorldModels.Of(name, region)`, every model a plain stand-in (`StandInUntil`) until the session that builds its town. Unova's towns need ways of building of their own, each a row of the style guide's "Buildings" section **before** `Architecture` grows and `BuildingArt.Styles.cs` paints it: Aspertia's hillside houses, Floccesy's cottages and the ranch, Virbank's docks and the complex's chimneys, Castelia's towers (`BuildingModels.Stages`, the tallest stages the game has), Nimbasa's lights, Driftveil's warehouses and the market, Mistralton's hangars, Lentimas's dry stone, Undella's seaside villas, Lacunosa's walled houses, Opelucid in the version's look (decision 4), Humilau's houses on the water (decks over `Sea`, as plan 21 builds Pacifidlog), and in the post-game Nuvema, Accumula, Striaton, Nacrene's warehouses and Icirrus. The great bridges (Skyarrow, the Driftveil Drawbridge, the Tubeline, Village, Marvelous) are decks `DeckAt` reads with the camera the zone header names (`FieldCamera`); the Marine Tube is a room whose walls are the sea.

### Seasons, in Unova alone

Black 2 and White 2's rule: a month each of the real calendar, round and round (January spring, February summer, March autumn, April winter, May spring), so a season is `(month − 1) mod 4`. Seasons over Sinnoh as well were weighed and refused: Platinum has no seasons, and a season over Sinnoh would move every other region's look. So a season exists only where the region's record says so (`Region.HasSeasons`, Unova true and every other region false), and `GameEngine.SeasonHere` is `null` on any other map. Everything seasonal asks `SeasonHere`, never the clock, which is what keeps the other regions' shots what they are.

- **The clock** (`Core/GameClock.cs`): `Season { Spring, Summer, Autumn, Winter }`, `GameClock.Season` from `GameClock.Today`'s month by the rule above (so `GameClock.FixedDate` fixes it too), `GameClock.FixedSeason` set from a new `GameSettings.Season` through `ApplySettings` (an `OptionRow.Season` among the settings, before `OptionRow.Help`, "Follow the calendar" by default, the Options line of the style guide's "Menu screens" first). The harness fixes the season as it fixes the time of day, always: its fixed date (the first of June) would otherwise give every mode summer, the same each run but not the season the mode means. A season changes as a game is loaded or as the day turns while a game is open; the change is told on the field ("Spring has come to Unova."), and the maps are rebuilt behind the next fade.
- **The look** (the style guide gains "Seasons" under "Overworld" before the code): `Graphics/Seasons.cs` (GPU-free) is `SeasonPalette.For(season)`: the lawn's two tones in place of `PixelGround`'s `Grass` and `GrassLight`, the trees' tints in place of the greens `TreeModels.Pine` and `Round` mix, the tall grass's and the tufts' shades, the flowers' set, and in winter snow lying on every tile of `TerrainCover.Grass` and on every tree, so `PixelGround.Bake` and `TreeModels` take the palette with the map and `SceneTextures` keeps a sheet per season of the tall grass, the tufts and the flowers, made in `Warm`. A chunk's bake is seeded by its tiles' places and the season, so `WorldTests.AChunksGroundIsTheSameHoweverMuchIsBakedWithIt` holds in each; `MapScene.Prepare(map, chunk)` carries the season it baked for, and `WorldRenderer` drops its scenes when `SeasonHere` moves. A winter's snow is a look: the tile's behaviour stays the decompilation's, unless the winter layout below gives the tile a snow behaviour. `ArtLook.Seasoned(rig, season)` grades the light as `Weathered` grades it for the weather (autumn warmer, winter paler, within the style guide's numbers for snow well below white), applied after `Weathered`. The sign on arrival gains a second line in Unova ("Route 19 · Winter"; the style guide's "Field menus and notices").
- **Layouts that change**: where a place has a layout a season (the area records' `_SUMMER`, `_AUTUMN` and `_WINTER` variants; the chunk folders the decompilation names by season, `autumn04_20` and its kin: Icirrus City's marsh frozen in winter, the drifts of Route 7 and Twist Mountain that open a way nobody walks in summer), the reader writes the other seasons' chunks beside the first and `WorldMatrixFile.Seasons` names them (chunk ids per season where they differ; absent, one chunk serves all four, so the other regions' files don't change). `WorldMapBuilder.Build(world, entry, season)` picks them, and `MapDatabase` rebuilds the world's maps at a season's turn behind the fade; positions are the region's tiles, so the save's place stands.
- **Wild Pokémon**: `WorldAreaFile.Seasons` holds, where the decompilation's tables differ, a `Land` and `Water` table per season; `MapArea.TablesFor(season)` gives the one in force and `Map.RollWildEncounter` asks it. Dark grass rolls a wild pair, fought as the wild double battle the core already plays; shaking grass, dust clouds and rippling water are a table each, rolled on a step by `Dice` and shown with plan 10 · F12's rustle. `habitats.json` lists a species under every season it is met in, and the Pokédex's AREA page says which.
- **Deerling and Sawsbuck** (`Models/Seasons.cs`, GPU-free): `SeasonalForms.FormFor(species, season)` (spring the species itself, the other three its coats). A Deerling or Sawsbuck in the party takes the season's coat as a game is loaded and as the day turns, one in the boxes as it is withdrawn, through `Pokemon.ChangeForm`; outside Unova (`SeasonHere` null) a coat stays what it is, as the later games keep it, and an evolution carries the coat over (`fromForm` and `targetForm`, in the data today).

### The region's record

Unova's `Data/regions/unova.json` (plan 18 · W1): Professor Juniper, with our own welcome on Black 2's beats; Snivy, Tepig and Oshawott, handed over by Bianca at the Aspertia Outlook rather than by the professor (a field of the record, `StarterGiver`, names a person of the first area's overlay; the briefcase script, `common.Briefcase`, serves); the badges Basic, Toxic, Insect, Bolt, Quake, Jet, Legend and Wave; the dex `updated-unova` (301, from W3's `DexNumbers`), the National Pokédex opened by the Hall of Fame alone; `HasSeasons` true; the music folder `unova`; the start in the player's room at Aspertia City. Neither Nate nor Rosa appears: the player keeps their own figure (plan 18's decision 7) and Black 2 and White 2 give the other hero no part (decision 5); Hugh is the rival (`rival` in the record, a person of the overlays like any other), Bianca the professor's assistant by name. The gates are none: from Generation 5 a field move asks no badge, so the record's `gates` is empty. The white-out, Fly and Teleport go to the record's `spawns`, which the reader takes from the zone headers' `fly` places.

**Flags and variables.** The decompilation's constant lists name the flags and variables it has worked out (each trainer's `EVENT_FLAG_TRAINER_<NAME>`, the first arrivals' `EVENT_FLAG_ARRIVED_<PLACE>`, the daily flags, the hidden items') and leave the rest as placeholders by value (`EVENT_FLAG_0x02e9`, `EVENT_WORK_0x40a1`). Ours are `FLAG_UNOVA_<what>` and `VAR_UNOVA_<what>`, named by the scene that sets them; the reader writes an object's hide flag as `FLAG_UNOVA_0x<value>` so that a placeholder and a named flag never meet, and `docs/mechanics/rulings.md`'s Unova section lists which of ours is which of the decompilation's, so a later commit's new names can be followed. `StoryTests`' rule that every name asked about is something that is set holds as it is.

### The story

## Risks

- **The decompilation is young and moving.** Its code is partly matched, its constants are placeholders by value where nobody has named them yet, and later commits rename them (its `rename_constant.py` rewrites the data with the new names). The pinned commit is what every run reads, a new pin is a session's decision with `report.md`'s counts compared, and our flags never take a placeholder's name as if it were a meaning ("Flags and variables", above).
- **What it carries that we must not.** Its `res/` holds the games' textures, models, sprites, sounds and text beside the data. The sparse checkout fetches only the folders "The source" lists and the chunks' glTF files, which are read for their JSON chunk alone; nothing but numbers and names is written into the repository, and a message id, being words of the message, is never written. The importer's README says it, and a test holds that no file the reader writes contains a `_Text_` id.
- **Its licence.** The GPL v3.0 covers the decompilation's code, tools and names; reading facts out of its data files into files of our own format, as the other importers do from pret's repositories, copies none of its code, and the reader is written from its documentation, not from its Python. If the user wants it to be held otherwise (asking its author, or keeping even the import's output out of the repository), decision 2 says how.
- **If it went away**: the pinned commit is gone with it, but the world files already checked in keep what was imported, and what wasn't would be drawn by hand from the same structure ("The source", the last paragraph).
- **Generation 5's moves and abilities** are plan 06 · R24 to R27's. Until then Unova's trainers use moves marked `Partial` or `None`; each chapter lists the moves its leaders need and the coverage floor rises as they land.
- **Seasons rebuild the world.** A season's turn rebuilds Unova's maps and drops every baked chunk; behind a fade that is a load, and U3 times it. A change in the middle of a walk waits for the next fade. The other regions pay nothing: no `Seasons` list, no season, the same bake.
- **Two runs, one picture.** A harness mode that forgot to fix the season would draw its fixed date's (summer); the harness sets it beside the time of day for every mode, and `SeasonTests` reads the harness's own setting.
- **What plan 14 owes**: the triples and rotations of the routes and of the Tournament's events, the Tournament's hall. U6 to U8 are written with the beat marked and U11 finishes them.
- **The behaviour byte.** `TileBehavior` is a `byte`, plan 18 · W6 gives the GBA's values the top of it, and Generation 5 has meanings neither game has: U1 counts them before writing any, and widening the enum and the chunk file's rows is its own step with `WorldTests` baking the other regions' chunks unchanged.
- **The version's exclusives** leave the other version's Opelucid, tower and Pokémon out; trading (plan 07) and plan 03 · D12's zone are where the missing species come from, as between the two cartridges.
