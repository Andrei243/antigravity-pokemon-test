# RoomEditor

A window for drawing, furnishing and peopling a room's map file (`Data/maps/<Name>.json`) and for editing an area's overlay (`Data/world/<region>/overlays/<key>.json`). Plan 16 · T9.

```bash
dotnet run --project tools/RoomEditor -- <Room>                          # edit a room of the repository (PokemonCenter, OreburghGym)
dotnet run --project tools/RoomEditor -- --new <Room> <width> <height>   # a new room: floor in a ring of wall, the back wall two rows deep
dotnet run --project tools/RoomEditor -- <Room> --area <key>             # and an area's overlay on the second tab (sandgem_town)
dotnet run --project tools/RoomEditor -- <Room> --data <dir>             # another folder laid out like Data/
dotnet run --project tools/RoomEditor -- --area <key>                    # an area's overlay and its view alone (an empty room beside them, saved only if drawn)
dotnet run --project tools/RoomEditor -- <Room> --shot <out dir>         # each tab drawn once in a hidden window (editor_room.png, editor_overlay.png, editor_preview.png, editor_area.png)
dotnet run --project tools/RoomEditor -- --roundtrip                     # no window: every room and overlay opened and written back as the editor does; exits 1 on a difference (CI runs it)
dotnet run --project tools/RoomEditor -- --check <file>                  # the same of one file (Data/maps/<Room>.json, or Data/world/<region>/overlays/<key>.json)
```

It writes to the repository's `PokemonPlatinumEngine/Data` (the first folder above the program that has one, as `--dev` finds it), never to the copy beside the program.

## The window

- **F1, the room.** The palette on the left is the tile codes' looks (`TileCodes`), or `PropType`'s furniture with the prop tool. Tools on the number keys: `1` paint the ground, `2` solid (Shift opens), `3` the overhead layer (Shift clears), `4` furniture, `5` a warp, `6` a sign, `7` a person, `8` a trigger, `9` select. Left paints or places; a drag of paint is one step. Right takes away the topmost thing on the tile. The wheel zooms and the middle button pans. A tile's behaviour follows from its look on a hand-made map, so only looks are painted.
- **F2, the overlay** (with `--area`). The area file's objects, warps, triggers and signs, each with what the overlay says of it beside it: who a person is and what they say, their script and trainer's lines; where a door leads, that it is locked, or the flag it is there until or from (`switches`); a trigger's script; what a signpost says (`signs`), the script it runs (`signScripts`) and, for a sign the area reads by its script, our script for it (`read`). After them the overlay's own things, which it places itself: its people (`npcs`, with the room's person fields and their tile, species, item, movement and level), its props, its ways out (`exits`), its triggers with tiles of their own and its tiles read (`readAt`). Right takes one of the overlay's own out of its list.
- **F3, the preview.** The content check's plan of the map the game makes of the room (`Plans.OfRoom`, the ground baked by `PixelGround` on the CPU, everything on it tagged).
- **F4, the area** (with `--area`, for an area its world opens). A chunk of the area from above, on the map the game makes of the region's chunks: each tile by what blocks, or by its behaviour (`B`), the tiles that aren't the area's dimmed, or the content check's plan of the chunk under the grid (`P`, `Plans.Of` with the chunk's rectangle: the files as they are on disk, so what the overlay has just been given shows only as the marks drawn over it); `PageUp` and `PageDown` go from chunk to chunk. The overlay's rows stand on their tiles, its own things ringed. With `7` a click places a person of the overlay's own, `5` a way out, `8` a trigger, `6` a tile read, `4` a prop (the palette is the furniture); `9` picks what stands there; right takes the overlay's own thing away. Hovering says what a tile is.
- **The fields panel**, on the right: the room's own fields, or those of the thing selected or placed, or of the overlay's row picked. Click a field, type, Enter sets it (Esc gives up). A list (a person's lines) is written with `|` between its items; a field left empty is left out of the file.
- `Ctrl+Z` undoes, `Ctrl+Y` redoes, `Ctrl+S` saves the tab's file and runs the content check on it; the findings are listed at the foot of the window.

## What it is made of

- `EditorDocument` (no window): a `MapFile` changed a step at a time (paint, fill, solid, overhead, resize, place and take away furniture, warps, signs, people, triggers and tiles read, set a field), each step undone back to the file's text. A room is saved as the game writes the map it makes of it (`MapFile.FromMap` of `MapFile.ToMap`), so what would stop the game loading it is refused before it is written, and every room it saves reads back as it is written, which the content check holds rooms to.
- `OverlayDocument` (no window): an area's overlay listed against its area file and changed a value at a time by `JsonSplice`, which replaces, adds or takes out one value of a hand-written JSON file and leaves everything else as it was written: the comments, the order, the blank lines; a value that was written on one line is written back on one line, and a record added beside records of a line each is a line (`JsonSplice.OneLine`); a list or a dictionary the file hasn't is made where it is first needed. A person given nothing leaves `people`, a door given no map leaves `doors`, a trigger given no script leaves `triggers`, a sign given no words leaves `signs`, a switch with neither flag leaves `switches`. The overlay's own things are added (`AddPerson`, `AddProp`, `AddExit`, `AddTrigger`, `AddReadAt`), changed a field at a time and taken away (`Remove`), each one step.
- `NpcFields`: a person of our own as fields, one set of labels for a room's people and an overlay's.
- `AreaView` (no window): an open area of the folder's worlds from `ContentCheck.GameContent`, a chunk at a time: each tile's blocked flag and behaviour, whether it is the area's, the content check's plan of the chunk, what a tile is (`Describe`) and the overlay's own thing placed on a tile of the area (`Place`).
- `EditorChecks`: the content check's own rules (`ContentCheck.Rooms`, `ContentCheck.Overlays`) asked of the one file being edited, against the rest of the data folder. Never a check of its own.
- `RoundTrip` (no window; `--roundtrip`, `--check`): every room opened and saved must be the text it was read from, and every overlay opened must be its text, with every field of every row written back as the panel shows it leaving the overlay meaning what it meant (a field's parsing, the splice and the game's writing of each record, together). Read in about seven seconds; CI's build job runs it.
- `EditorWindow`: draws them and turns the mouse and the keys into their methods.

`RoomEditorTests` drives the documents with no window: every room of the game opens and saves as it is written; a series of edits undoes back to the file; a Pokémon Center is drawn, furnished, previewed, checked and saved from nothing and loaded as the game loads a room; an overlay is changed with every comment of it kept, and undone; an overlay's own person, prop, way out, trigger and tile read are added, changed and taken away, its signs and switches set, and undone to the file; the area view's layers are the game's map, a pick finds a door, a placed person stands on its tile and the chunks go round; every room and overlay of the game round-trips, and the round trip names a line with a bar in it and a room written otherwise.

Not built yet: the preview through the field's own renderer (`MapScene.Build` with the room's `InteriorStyle` and `MapScene.ViewOf`), which wants the GPU; the plan's top-down picture stands in for it. The window and its `--shot` have still never been run, the area tab included. Of an overlay, what is neither the area's rows nor the own things above (`zones`, `pits`, `holes`, `braille`, `emergeAt`, `darkWater`, `national`, `ours` and the area's own fields: music, trees, climate) is still written by hand.
