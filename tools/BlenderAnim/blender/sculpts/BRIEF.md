Rules learnt since this brief was first written (they overrule anything above):
- Measure proportions as ratios against the reference (head height / total height, limb length / total height, the size of the species' signature part against the head), bring your model's ratios to the reference's BEFORE polishing, and write the ratios (reference and yours) in your report. The skeleton fixes the joints, not the masses: a head can be large about its bone, hips can cover the tops of the legs.
- The face must read small. The game draws models as pixel art with a head some 60 pixels across: render the turntable also at 480 and look. Eyes are simple and large enough: a solid pupil, a rim at least a sixth of the eye's half-size or none, no thin rings, no rows of tiny teeth, no specks; a mouth is one clean shape (a patch on the fused face often reads better than a carved hollow).
- Thin parts (whiskers, antennae, long arms held straight out sideways) make the model wide or tall and its body small in the game: keep them short, and hold arms a little down or forward rather than in a T.
- In the turntable's front view +X is on the viewer's right.
- Limbs in the rest pose: check that arms cannot be mistaken for ears, horns or wings from the front.
- Before you change what this brief or your own instructions say a feature is, look at the reference: the pictures overrule both, and say so in the report.
- A carve's Boolean can fail silently and wipe the body out: after any change to a carved mouth, check the vertex count in the KIT line.
- A fish, or anything flat from side to side: the game shows a model from the front three-quarter and from behind, never side-on. A fin on the midline (dorsal, tail) is edge-on there and reads as a pole: keep it lower than the body is tall and bend or turn it some 40 degrees to one side so its face shows. Eyes on flat sides are not seen from the front: bring them forward and turn them about 40 degrees off straight ahead. Pectoral fins show their faces to the front.
- Mirroring by hand: multiply only the offsets by the side, never an anchor point that is off the midline.
- A signature part that IS the head (a dome of a skull, a face shield) must not sit on top of a head that is whole without it: the head fills it from behind and the face runs up into it, or it reads as a helmet or a hat in the game. A biped reptile is not a person: thighs fused into a leaning body, short shins, the head carried forward.
- A flat tail or plate behind the body: run its plane back AND some 35 degrees out to the model's +X side. Straight back it is hidden behind the body from the front three-quarter and edge-on from behind.
- A plate triangulated from its outline alone buries whatever is laid on it once it is bent: split its faces (sub=3) first. A patch wider than the thin part it is meant for lands on whatever lies behind, with no warning.
- A long-legged skeleton is not a thin animal: the joints are high, but shoulder and thigh are broad masses fused over the upper half of each leg, the trunk is as deep as the leg showing under it is long, the lower leg tapers and is never a stick, the neck is short and thick. Measure head height / total height and trunk depth / leg showing on the reference and reach both before polishing.
- An evolution of a base form sculpted in this batch keeps that script's frame (its named constants and functions, the way its paws, eyes, nose and mouth are made) and changes only what the pictures change.
- Eyes that are always shut: the open state is the slit; write Fierce by hand (the slit tilted), since `eye_look`'s Fierce draws a lid across it.
- A loose piece built on a hand-made frame (a wing, a vane, a plate with its own axes) keeps that frame right-handed, the mirrored twin too (side = face x direction): wound inside out it looks right in Blender's turntable, which draws both faces, and thin, grey and see-through in the game, which does not.
- A flyer's level wings are edge-on from the game's cameras: raise each pair in a V of 25 to 35 degrees and sweep the pairs apart; legs hang tucked and short.
- The art's pose is not the rest pose: arms flung wide or raised in the picture are carried forward and a little down in the model (a model wider than it is tall is drawn small in the game and can be cut by its cell).
- Stripes round a limb or a body are crisp as loose rings (a `sheet` wound round the part, 0.004 off its surface, its seam behind); a band mask in paint is `inside(z - top) * inside(bottom - z)`.
- Blades, claws, spikes and crests are solid pieces (a flat-faced hull at least 0.03 thick at its root), never thin plates in close pairs: at the game's size (a model some 150 pixels tall) thin plates alias into stripes. A crest laid back, not standing up, keeps the body large in the game.
- The kit has the hard-edged shapes now (the kit session, 2026-11-14): use them and write none of your own. `hull(points)` is a flat-faced solid from its corners (rock, armour, a blade, a claw, a gem), shaded flat with nothing more said; `slab(outline, origin, ex, ez, thick)` a flat plate with a hard rim from any outline (a wing, a fin, a bolt); `ring_band(c, axis, radius, length)` a loose stripe round a limb or a body, its seam behind; `lathe(c, d, profile)` a solid of turning with its profile's edges (a barrel with its bore); `outward(shape)` turns a closed shape of your own outward. Each is wound outward under any frame and under `mirror=True`. A loose `leaf` is `outward(leaf(...))`: the kit's leaf is wound inside out and is kept so for the models already built.
- `KIT: !! N loose pieces wound wrongly` names loose pieces whose faces are inside out: the game draws them dull and without their outline. Pass the shape through `outward(...)`; do not hand the script in while the line prints.
- The turntable's sheet has six views: front, three-quarter, side, back, then the game's own two (as its boards take them: from the front three-quarter with the face turned to the viewer's left, and from behind). Judge by the last two what the game will hide. A face seen from its inner side is drawn in ink: a black piece is wound inside out.
- A form or an evolution built by another script's functions imports it: `from sculptkit import sibling; u = sibling("unown")` (that script must build under `if __name__ == "__main__":`).
- The sheet is six tiles wide, so each view is smaller at the same size: crop the two game views (the last two tiles, from x = 4 x the size) as you crop the head, and look at them large before you report.
- A wide, low model whose FOUR CORNERS are filled (feet or points at the corners of its footprint: a crab, a toad, a table) can be cut by the game's board: the board frames a square of 1.3 x max(height, 0.85 x max(width, length)) and looks at the model turned 31.5 degrees, so such corners show up to 0.853 x width + 0.522 x length across. For that kind of body check the sum against the square from the KIT line's height, width and length before you report (a width of at most 0.68 of the length fits). It is an upper bound: a round or oval footprint shows far less than the sum (Shaymin, 0.44 by 0.48 and 0.35 tall, fills its cell with room to spare), so do not squeeze a round body for it.
- A bowl, a collar or a cup made of a `sheet` shows its far wall through its own opening: close it with a floor of its inner colour just under the rim. A reference drawn from the three-quarter shows a symmetric pair (wings, fins, loops) as two unlike parts: count pairs, not shapes.
