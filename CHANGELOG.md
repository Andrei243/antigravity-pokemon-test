# Changelog

What changes from one release to the next, for players. Every session's close-out adds its lines under **Unreleased**, in plain words (what a player sees or can do now, not how it was built); a release turns Unreleased into its version's section, which `.github/workflows/release.yml` puts on the release's page. Versions are `v0.y.z` until the story is complete.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- Hau'oli City on Melemele has a Trainers' School at the east end of its first street: battle its four pupils, then their teacher, who hands over three Quick Balls.
- Sandy Cave is open on Melemele: ride across the water off the south-west end of Big Wave Beach to its mouth, where Zubat and Psyduck live round a still pool and a boulder a Machamp can shift hides a TM.
- Alola's beaches look out on open sea: below Hau'oli City, Route 1 and Big Wave Beach, beyond Kala'e Bay, and off Heahea City's pier and the beaches of Hano and Konikoni, the water now runs on where a band of woods had stood a few rows off the shore.
- In Alola's first chapters you now walk up to Hala, Hau, Mallow, Gladion and Lillie before they speak, rather than hearing them from off the screen, and Team Skull's grunts are announced by their own names.
- Locked hearts: after each region's Hall of Fame, agents of a syndicate called Lockwork turn up at its port (the Battle Zone and the Fight Area's quay, Vermilion City's harbour, Olivine's port, Lilycove City's harbour and Castelia City's avenue) with Pokémon whose hearts have been locked shut. Looker, in the Survival Area, gives you the Reclaimer: with it you can throw a ball at a trainer's locked Pokémon and take it back. A locked Pokémon wears a grey haze and a padlock, holds the experience it earns, has some of its moves sealed and may break loose in battle (choose CALM to settle it). Its heart opens in time as it battles and walks beside you, at the camp and at Sendoff Spring's water once a day, when it takes in everything it earned. Each cell's admin waits in a room behind a door at the port, and the head of Lockwork waits in a tower on Castelia's avenue.
- Nimbasa City's theatre has a show at last. Its owner hands you a trunk of Props, and the desk lets you dress a Pokémon of your team in hats, glasses, scarves, capes, fans and tail ribbons, worn on the Pokémon itself, then take the stage in Sunny Side Revue or Hour of Lanterns against three performers. Press on the beat when the spotlight finds you to win the crowd's eyes; Props that suit the show's mood draw them further. After a show, a fan in the lobby waits with a new Prop.
- Goldenrod's Game Corner is open: its tables play a card-flip game of guessing which cards hide numbers and which a cracked tile, its clerk sells coins, and its prize counter trades TMs, held items, Abra, Ekans and Dratini for them. HeartGold's Celadon Game Corner plays the same cards.
- The Pokégear's radio has a new station in Johto, the Lucky Number Show, which reads out a lucky number every week. Take it to the man of the show on the Radio Tower's ground floor: if it matches the ID number of one of your Pokémon, you win a prize, once a week.
- A film studio's director in Jubilife TV's lobby shoots films with you: The Empty Seat, a ghost story, and The Gilded Hour, a heist. Each take is a battle with a cast she lends you, where the co-star and the villain speak as it goes and you choose your own lines; how you play it decides the ending, and every take is scored in stars.
- Once you are a region's Champion, the Town Map (and the Pokégear's map card) has a second page on the item button: the day's two outbreaks marked on the map with how many of each you have met, and a scan, once a day with the National Pokédex, that calls a Pokémon of another region to one of the region's routes for an hour.
- Wild Pokémon you can see in the grass may be bright: the more of a species you have beaten or caught, the more often one of it shimmers, with perfect IVs and an Egg move. Catching the same species in a row from the grass builds a streak, shown in the corner, that brings better and shinier ones from eleven; hooking the same Pokémon in a row on a rod from one spot raises the shiny chance too.
- After a region's Hall of Fame, rifts open in the field for an hour at a time: within reach of one the grass and the water hold Pokémon from other regions, three finds lie in its ground, and the Town Map's report shows where it is.
- Feebas can be fished from six spots of Route 119's water in Hoenn, different each day.
- Hidden items within three steps now glint now and then (the options' HIDDEN ITEMS GLINT row, on under the modern rules).
- Galar's Wild Area (its southern half): the train from Wedgehurst now runs north and stops short of Motostoke, and you cross the Wild Area on foot. Eight parts, from the Meetup Spot by the station past the Rolling Fields, the Dappled Grove, the Watchtower Ruins and two lakes to the steps under Motostoke, each with its own weather of the day, and its Pokémon change with the weather. Patches of grass rustle now and then: walk into one to meet a Pokémon you won't see wandering. Some very strong Pokémon roam there too, and you can't catch them until you have enough badges. The Watt Trader, the Ball Guy (a ball a day) and the Digging Duo wait by the paths, and a Galarian Yamask that has taken enough hits becomes Runerigus at the Stone Arch.
- Max Raid dens in the Wild Area: step up to a den of red stone and, if its light is on today, take its Watts and raid the giant Dynamax Pokémon inside beside three other Trainers. Its barrier goes up past half its HP and must be broken hit by hit, the strongest ones attack twice in a turn, and if your side falls four times the den blows you out. Win, and you can throw a ball at it. What waits in each den changes every day, and stronger ones appear as you win badges.
- Max Raid dens now show their beam of red light across the Wild Area on the days they are lit, and the raid itself shows the boss's shields as pips under its HP box and all four raiders' boxes down the side. A Wishing Piece, from the Watt Trader for 3,000 W, lights a quiet den when you throw it in.
- Galar's gym challenge begins: climb the steps from the Wild Area into Motostoke, register at the stadium with Hop (and meet Bede), spend the night at the hotel, and watch the opening ceremony on the pitch. Then out through Motostoke's west gate onto Route 3, through the Galar Mine (where Bede is waiting) and along Route 4 to Turffield, where Milo's gym mission has you nudging Wooloo into their pens before he battles you for the Grass Badge.
- With the crafting kit in your bag, rocks, fallen logs, herb bushes, heaps of shells and snow drifts appear along the routes of every region you can travel to. Each gives you something for the kit once a day, and cutting a tree or smashing a rock sometimes leaves wood or stone too.
- Berries planted side by side can now cross into rare berries, as long as you carry the crafting kit. The kit's new CROSSES page shows every cross and which berries make it. In Goldenrod, the florist gives kit holders four Berry Pots to grow berries in wherever you go.
- Waiters in the Seven Stars Restaurant, Celadon City's restaurant and the Olivine Café, and stall keepers in Slateport's market and on Castelia's avenue, now serve meals for your whole team and sell cooking goods, once you have the crafting kit.
- The hub's halls are open. The Laurel Gallery keeps every region's Hall of Fame: a plinth for each region you've crowned, each showing that region's first team and every team since. The PC's Hall of Fame now has a tab for each region.
- The Laurel Circuit: at the Gallery's desk you can go back to every League you've won and fight its Elite Four and Champion again, all at one level, then face every Champion in a row in the Laurel Court. Each circuit you win makes the next one harder and earns a title and a medal.
- Looker has opened the Open Door Agency in the hub, half in disguise as a clerk. He takes a case in each region you've crowned (Sinnoh, Kanto, Johto, Hoenn, Unova and Kalos): find the clues, tell him what they mean, and face the culprit. The cases show on the requests page.
- After you win a Laurel Circuit, Looker sails you to Grayfall Keep, where the old leaders of the villain teams have gathered, each lured by a rumour. Beat their admins and leaders, and the one who gathered them puts on a show of her own. The leaders stay on for a battle a day.

- The Battle Tree at the head of Poni Gauntlet is now the Partners' Tree, kept by Ione Calder. In its Super rooms the famous Trainers of every region you have won (Champions, Elite Four, Gym Leaders and kahunas) come down to battle with three of their own Pokémon at every tenth battle, and a Champion waits at the fiftieth. Beat one and they offer to partner you in the Multi room, each with a line of their own as they arrive. With FRIEND AS PARTNER on, a friend plays the partner in the Battle Frontier's partner rooms too.
- The Battle Château on Kalos's Route 7 now trains a team at any level: its members bring stronger teams the higher your title, a little above your best Pokémon, the same all day and new the next. Once you have won another region, one of its Trainers visits each day (a Leader at first, an Elite Four member as Steward, a Champion as Master of the House). Your title shows on Kalos's page of the Trainer Card.

- The Trainer Tower on Seven Island is open to Kanto's Champions: climb eight floors of trainers against the clock in one of four challenges (single battles, double battles, three trainers in a row, or a mix), with every opponent as strong as your strongest Pokémon. The owner on the roof tells you your time, keeps your best for each challenge and gives a prize the first time you finish each one.
- A stadium has opened off the Battle Frontier annex's lobby. Its five cups set their own rules (levels, totals, size, or only Pokémon that have yet to evolve), eight battles in a row with your own Pokémon or three borrowed ones, and a prize the first time you win each. The Leaders' round pits you against a finished region's every Gym Leader, Elite Four member and Champion in a row at level 100. In the sparring room you pick a type and battle Trainers of that type for as long as your streak lasts.

- Nimbasa's Battle Institute is open to Unova's Champions: sit its test of five battles for a score out of 9,999 and a rank, with your best on the institute's board, or climb its ladder, a rating that rises and falls with every battle, with a new season (and Battle Points for the best band you reached) every month.

### Changed
- The Pokémon walking behind you now has moods and things to say: it sulks with its back to you after a bad day, bounces along when it's happy, remarks on lakes, caves, snow, rain and the night, perks up in places its type loves, and shows a bubble of its own after a battle, a level or a new place.
- Treats for your team: confectioners in Veilstone, Goldenrod, Celadon, Lilycove and Castelia sell five, one for each flavour, and the baker beside them makes finer cakes from three berries of a flavour, or a Rainbow Loaf from one of each. A treat in a flavour your Pokémon loves makes it twice as happy.
- Look after each Pokémon of your team close up at the camp: stroke it by hand (each has a spot it loves), give it berries, brush off dust, leaves and snow, wipe off mud, and treat its conditions, while it turns to your hand, leans in, chews and grows sleepy at night. A new TEAM CARE option puts CAMP in the start menu even with the follower off.
- The camp now remembers your team day to day: a Pokémon that fought on sand, in grass, mud, water or snow comes back with it on its coat, its mood rises and falls with wins, losses, levels, evolutions, rest and care, a pat counts for friendship once a day, and each Pokémon eats until it's full for the day, however many times you visit.
- A gift box: answer Madame Ottilie Fray's questionnaire in Jubilife TV's lobby and the Gift Runners in the Pokémon Centers of Sinnoh, Kanto, Hoenn and Unova (and Johto's Poké Marts) hand over gifts: an Egg for each region's Hall of Fame, presents for catching 100, 500 and 1,000 kinds of Pokémon, for 50 hours played and on the game's anniversary, gifts on New Year's Day, Easter and Christmas, and one for the right two words.
- Festivals on the calendar: Hearthome's Night of Lanterns, Snowpoint's Midwinter Lights, Floaroma's Blossom Days, Sunyshore's Summer Night with lights over the sea, Kanto's Harvest Moon, Johto's Bellfall, Hoenn's Tide Day and Unova's Bridgelight decorate their towns on their days, with a stall and a gift. Each region's festival town decorates for Christmas, Easter and the New Year too, and something small happens on St Valentine's Day.
- A trade board at the Global Terminal and at link desks in Celadon, Goldenrod, Lilycove and Castelia: three trade requests a day from trainers far away, and five blind trades a day.
- Voices are on by default (VOICES is ALL unless you change it): with a voice pack beside the game every line that has a take is spoken, and without one the game shows its text as it always did.
- Every training hall now has a Tera keeper: with a Tera Orb in your bag, fifty Tera Shards of a type change the type a Pokémon takes when it Terastallizes (fifty Stellar Tera Shards make it Stellar). The eighteen Tera Shards and the Stellar one are in the game: every fifth gold in the drills earns a shard of the trainee's own type while you hold the orb, and the club's Battle Points clerk trades them. A Pokémon's summary shows its Tera Type once you have the orb.
- Powers have come: once you are a Champion, the teacher at Sunyshore City's training hall (and in the club's corners of Celadon, Goldenrod, Lilycove and Shopping Mall Nine) teaches you powers and gives you the Power Watch. Each power lasts ten minutes of play: more EXP, more effort, more prize money, cheaper shops, easier catches, more or fewer wild Pokémon, or faster-hatching Eggs. Using one costs charge, which fills as you walk, and the watch offers one power free each day. Powers in force show as little timers at the left of the screen. A run can turn them off with NO POWERS.
- In Galar's open country, a wild Pokémon that notices you now shows a "!" over its head for a moment, and one that comes over to look or charges at you cries out as it does; a shy one just runs.
- A Battle Royal now looks like one: the three other Trainers and their Pokémon each stand on a platform of their own in a ring around the field, all three Trainers appear as it begins, and choosing a target shows the four as they stand in the ring. First place at the Battle Frontier annex's Royal corner earns two Battle Points.
- The Hub has a covered market, Pennant Hall: its manager, Quincy Rowan-Pike, lets you choose which stall stands at each pitch, from a goods stall paid in pennants to branches of the Lottery Corner, the Seal stall, the Poffin pot, the Move Relearner, Kurt's Apricorn counter and the Berry Blender, each opening once its region is won. Every pennant the hall earns raises its rank, opening more pitches and finer goods. Trainers from the regions you have won visit each day, give a few pennants when you say hello and battle you once a day at your journey's level.
- The Hub's notice board, the Week's Work, posts five missions a week in the regions you have won (catch, battle, hatch, explore, win a contest), the same all week and new each Monday. Two are timed: begin one at the board and its clock runs while you play in its region. Claim each reward at the board for pennants for Pennant Hall, and sometimes an item.
- The Pastoria Gym is easier to read: the strips you can't walk on (between the walk round the room and the platforms, and between platforms) are now drawn as water instead of looking like more floor, and the bottom of the pool shows as dry floor only when the water is drained and you can walk on it.
