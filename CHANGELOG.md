# Changelog

What changes from one release to the next, for players. Every session's close-out adds its lines under **Unreleased**, in plain words (what a player sees or can do now, not how it was built); a release turns Unreleased into its version's section, which `.github/workflows/release.yml` puts on the release's page. Versions are `v0.y.z` until the story is complete.

## [Unreleased]

### Fixed
- The legendary Pokémon's meetings show the Pokémon: Uxie, Azelf, Mesprit, Heatran, Cresselia, Articuno and Zapdos are no longer hidden behind your own head while the camera looks at them, Rotom's television is no longer behind you, and Articuno is no longer under the black bar at the top of the picture. Palkia's rift lights up on its own pillar rather than an empty spot of floor, the beasts' and Cresselia's shadows now race away from them, and Suicune at Cianwood bounds off over the sea instead of over the town.
- Inside buildings, the low inner walls and the blocks of wall beside staircases no longer have dark navy tops that looked like slabs of furniture: their tops are now in the room's own colours, with a pale rail along the edges you can see.
- Sabrina in HeartGold's Saffron Gym looks like herself, in the Gym and on her battle's band, where she stood in the plain trainer's clothes. New Mauville is a power plant of steel walls and tiled floors, no longer a wooden house. The packs screen no longer hides a pack that couldn't be read while a change waits (it says both), keeps a long credit from running over the description, and shortens a folder too long for its panel.
- The Solaceon Ruins' rooms (and other small one-room caves) are now in the middle of the screen with darkness round their walls, instead of tucked into a corner beside a wide field of rock that looked like floor, so the way through each room is easy to see. And walking under the footbridge on Route 205 on the way to Eterna Forest, you now pass under its planks instead of your head poking up through them.

### Added
- Three options for seeing, all off unless chosen and also on the first start's page: COLOUR VISION (Protan, Deutan, Tritan) shifts the interface's colours for eyes that tell fewer apart, puts each type's own sign on every type pill and move card, and hatches an HP bar at half or less (crossed once it is in the red); TEXT SIZE Large writes the text box's and the battle's lines bigger, on up to four rows in the field and three in battle; HIGH CONTRAST draws both boxes white on solid black.
- Celebi and Jirachi can be met. Once you are Johto's Champion, Kurt hands you a strange ball engraved with GS that he can't open: lay it before the shrine in Ilex Forest and Celebi flickers into its glow. Once you are Hoenn's Champion, have beaten Steven in his cave at Meteor Falls and given Prof. Cozmo back his meteorite, Cozmo tells of a stone there that hums at night: talk to Steven after dark and Jirachi wakes before it. Both are met through a short scene before a battle at their region's post-game levels, and both show on the Pokédex's area page.
- Sinnoh's event Pokémon can be met after the Hall of Fame. Once Sailor Eldritch's son is well he gives you his father's Member Card, and a night at Canalave's Harbor Inn takes you to Newmoon Island, where Darkrai waits in the wood (level 50). Professor Oak, in Eterna City, gives you a letter asking you to meet him at the white rock at the end of Route 224: write two words of thanks on it and a path opens across the sea to Flower Paradise, where Shaymin waits (level 30), and Floaroma's flower shop then has a Gracidea for you. Catch Dialga, Palkia and Giratina, and Celestic Town's elder gives you the Azure Flute: play it at the top of Spear Pillar's steps and a stair of light leads to the Hall of Origin and Arceus (level 80). Read Mr. Backlot's book on Manaphy, and a Pokémon Ranger on Route 230's shore gives you a Manaphy Egg; bred with a Ditto, Manaphy gives Phione. Each is met through a short scene of its own.
- The start menu has REQUESTS once someone asks something of you (the first is the Pokétch campaign in Jubilife City): a page of the errands under way and done, each with who asked, its steps ticked off as you take them and what it brings. Pin one and its next step stands at the top left of the field, and the person it is taken to shows a note as you come into their town. With WHAT IS LEFT HERE on, the page has a third tab, THIS AREA, with the counts of where you stand. A new options row, REQUESTS, turns it all off.
- Three of Unova's mythical Pokémon can be met once you are Unova's Champion, each through a short scene before its battle at level 65: a sailor at Castelia's Liberty Pier gives you the Liberty Pass and sails you to the Liberty Garden, an island in the bay where Victini bursts out before the lighthouse; once you have faced Cobalion, Virizion and Terrakion, Keldeo waits on Route 13 where Cobalion stood; and at night Meloetta sings in the Musical Theater's lobby in Nimbasa City.
- Unova's legendary Pokémon after the League (Cobalion, Virizion, Terrakion, Azelf, Mesprit, Kyurem at rest in the Giant Chasm, Heatran in Reversal Mountain and the three Regis in their chambers) are now met through a short scene: black bars, the camera going to the Pokémon, a light, a shimmer or a shake by what it is, and its cry, before the battle. In the Giant Chasm, Zekrom now comes down as a shadow and two strokes of lightning, and Kyurem takes it in a flash of white you can watch, where the screen used to go dark.
- In Kanto, once you are Champion and hold the National Pokédex, a legendary beast races across Pallet Town and roams Kanto's routes, as in FireRed: Entei if you chose Bulbasaur, Suicune if you chose Charmander, Raikou otherwise, at level 50.
- Hoenn's legendary Pokémon are met through a short scene before their battles: Rayquaza's shadow sweeping down Sky Pillar's top, Kyogre and Groudon stirring in their pool and magma, the Regis' eyes lighting as the statues grind into life, Latias or Latios landing by Southern Island's stone, Deoxys appearing where the stone shatters, Mew flickering in the grass, Lugia lifting its head from the water and Ho-Oh sweeping down onto Navel Rock's peak. Each can be skipped by holding the menu button.
- Johto's legends are met in short scenes too: after the Kimono Girls' dance Ho-Oh's shadow sweeps over the Bell Tower's roof and it lands in a burst of rainbow fire (no more fade to black), and spoken to, its fire bursts and the roof shakes; Lugia's eyes open in sparkles as the Whirl Islands' cave shakes twice; Moltres blazes up in Mt. Silver. In the Burned Tower the camera goes to each of the three beasts in turn as it wakes, Raikou in two flashes of lightning, Entei in a burst of fire, Suicune in sparkles, and follows each one's shadow as it leaps away; at Cianwood the camera watches Suicune bound off across the sea. Each can be skipped by holding the menu button; the battles are unchanged.
- Meeting Dialga or Palkia through their rifts at Spear Pillar, and Giratina at the end of Turnback Cave, is now a short scene: the music drops, black bars slide in, the camera goes to the Pokémon, the rift tears open in slow motion (or Giratina's shadow sweeps across the cave) and the picture wavers as it cries, before the battle begins. Hold the menu button to skip it, as with any scene.
- More of Sinnoh's legendary Pokémon are met through a short scene of their own before the battle: Uxie, Azelf and Mesprit in their lake caverns (sparkles and a shimmer, and Mesprit flickering away), Heatran rising out of the glow of Stark Mountain's heart, Regigigas's eyes lighting up as the statue shakes, Giratina in the Distortion World (the camera rises to it as it comes down, and again before it attacks), Cresselia's shadow flying off over Fullmoon Island's trees, and Rotom sparking out of the Old Chateau's television.
- Kanto's legendary birds and Mewtwo get the same kind of short scene before their battles: frost glitters round Articuno in the Seafoam Islands, lightning strikes twice round Zapdos in the Power Plant, a burst of fire and shimmering heat rise round Moltres on Mt. Ember, and the camera pushes in on Mewtwo as it flickers in and out of sight in Cerulean Cave.
- Johto's old buildings look like themselves inside: Sprout Tower's floors are polished boards railed in timber over the dark, round a great wooden pillar that slowly sways; the Violet Gym's walkways stand high over the dark on timber with a sky painted on its walls; the Burned Tower is charred boards and ash; and the Ruins of Alph's chambers and hall are dressed sandstone, their carvings stone steles, with nothing past their walls. The building over the stairs down to the Ruins' hall no longer wears the research centre's LAB sign.
- Photo mode in battle: press F9 (Select on a pad) at any moment of a battle and it stops where it is, the HP boxes and menus disappear, and you can swing the camera round the field, bring it closer, borrow another time of day, nudge the Pokémon's pose, and add a filter or a frame before taking the picture with A. B goes back and the battle carries on exactly as it would have.
- What is left here: turn on the new WHAT IS LEFT HERE option and a place's sign, as you arrive, counts the items, trainers and Pokémon you can still find there ("Items 1 · Trainers 3 · Pokémon 1"), and the start menu shows the same under its other panels. It counts only what you can reach now from where you stand (the water's Pokémon once you can surf, a rod's once you have it, what lies past a tree once you can cut it), and never says which ones.
- New Game+: once a save has finished Sinnoh's story, NEW GAME on the title offers a third answer, NEW GAME+. It starts a new journey in a free slot with that save's Pokédex, its boxes (with your team and the Day Care's two put into them), half its money and the same Trainer Card number, on a harder road: one step up in difficulty and trainers that rise to your team. The story, the bag and the key items start over, the old save is kept as it was, and the Trainer Card's Run page counts how many times you have gone round.
- Lance now gives you HM10, Whirlpool, once the Team Rocket Hideout's generator is stopped. Teach it to a Water Pokémon (most of them can learn it), and with the Glacier Badge it carries you across the whirlpools on the way to the Whirl Islands. If you had already cleared the hideout, you will find the HM in your bag the next time you come to Mahogany Town.
- Auto-battle: turn on the new AUTO-BATTLE option and a battle's commands get an AUTO button (left of FIGHT). Choose it and the battle plays itself, each of your Pokémon using its strongest move and the lines read on by themselves, until one of your Pokémon faints, a move is to be learned or the battle ends; Esc stops it at any time. It never uses the bag, so it never throws a ball.
- With the box tools on, the PC keeps teams by name: SAVE THIS TEAM under TEAMS on a box's menu remembers who is on your team (up to eight teams), and SWAP IN brings a kept team out of the boxes in one step, healed, with your current team put where they were. A member you have since released is simply left out. JUDGE on a Pokémon's menu shows how good each of its stats is born, in six words from NOTHING to FLAWLESS.
- Dynamax touch-ups: a Champion's giant ace no longer has its head hidden under the boss's HP bar (the bar now stops short of it), in a double battle the camera watching your own Pokémon grow or launch a Max Move stands on the outer side so your partner no longer fills half the picture, G-Max Steelsurge's steel splinters now fly out blue-white with a trail so you can see them thrown, G-Max Gold Rush's coins pile up in a heap before the target, and G-Max Befuddle's wings have a butterfly's two lobes a side.
- In double battles a Dynamaxed Pokémon grows a little less than in a single, so it no longer swallows the partner standing beside it.
- Johto's seven week siblings are all out now, each on their own day of the week (Monica on Route 40, Tuscany on Route 29, Wesley at the Lake of Rage, Santos in Blackthorn City and Sunny on Route 37 join Arthur and Frieda), each with a gift, and once you have met all seven the sibling of the day puts that day's ribbon on your Pokémon. In the Pokéathlon Dome you can climb to the stands and go down to its halls of history, which open one by one as your teams win courses. And Clair, once you have her number, will take a photograph with you in the Dragon's Den.
- The Pokéathlon has three more events (Relay Run, Circle Push and Goal Roll) and its five courses (Speed, Power, Skill, Stamina and Jump). A course's score wins a gold, silver or bronze medal, the desk beside the course desk shows each course's best score and medal, and the stall on the dome's square sells vitamins, evolution stones and more for Athlete Points.
- The PC's boxes can be searched (by species, type, move, ability, nature, level, held item, gender, shiny and mark), put in order one box or all at once (by number, level, name, type or how recently met), and several Pokémon can be picked up together and put down in one box, or released together (behind the HOLD TO CONFIRM hold when that option is on). A new BOX TOOLS option turns it on or off; under Platinum's rules the PC stays as it was.
- Photo mode in the field: press F9 (Select on a pad) where you could save, and the world holds still while you move the camera around, zoom in or out, light the scene at another time of day, borrow rain, snow or fog, add a filter (vivid, black and white, sepia, soft) or a frame, and press A to save the picture. The real clock and the weather stay as they were.
- Two new options for players who can't hear the game or want fewer slips. CAPTIONS names every sound and cry on screen as it plays ("A door opens", "Starly cries"), in small pills at the left. HOLD TO CONFIRM makes deleting a save, resetting the options and releasing a Pokémon wait until the button is held for a moment, with a ring filling on the button.
- The strong moves Pokémon learn past level 100 are now staged like the big signature attacks: the camera punches in on the blows, rides the lances and pulses, circles the songs and storms, and pushes in on the capstone blasts, with a pause on the heavy hits, a light over the stage and what each leaves behind (cracks, embers, frost, shards, petals); the setup moves glow round their user in their type's colour.
- Five Gigantamax moves have scenes and sounds of their own: G-Max Wildfire's ring of flame walls, G-Max Volt Crash's bolt from the sky and lightning running along the ground, G-Max Terror's shadow hands and great eye, G-Max Resonance's aurora, and G-Max Replenish's shower of berries. Every Gigantamax now grows in clouds of its own colours instead of the plain Dynamax red.
- Five more Gigantamax moves have scenes and sounds of their own: G-Max Chi Strike's four golden fists, G-Max Gold Rush's shower of spinning coins, G-Max Cuddle's ring of hearts hugging its target, G-Max Befuddle's great wings and glittering scales, and G-Max Depletion's tower of panels going dark one by one. G-Max Resonance's aurora now hangs in soft upright curtains instead of streaks across the trees.
- In Johto's Kanto, Routes 12 to 15 lead south from Lavender Town to Fuchsia City, with Route 19 out to sea beyond it. In Fuchsia's Gym, Janine's trainers dress up as her: talk to each to unmask them, then find the real Janine for Kanto's Soul Badge. With seven of Kanto's Badges, Blue leaves Cinnabar Island for home, and his Viridian Gym opens for the Earth Badge.
- In Johto's Kanto, Route 11 leads east from Vermilion City to the gate before Route 12's sleeping Snorlax, Diglett's Cave tunnels from Route 11 to Route 2 by Pewter City, and the Cycling Road runs from Celadon City down to Fuchsia City: its gate keepers send back anyone without a Bicycle, and its bikers wait all the way down. Gym halls and other rooms that span several map pieces (the Viridian and Celadon Gyms, the Pokéathlon Dome, the S.S. Aqua and more) are now framed on the room itself instead of half a screen of dark wall.
- In Johto's Kanto, Route 9 leads east from Cerulean City to Route 10, where a Pokémon Center stands by Rock Tunnel and the Power Plant hums beside the river; Rock Tunnel's two floors of ladders and cracked rocks come out on the road down to Lavender Town.
- In Johto's Kanto, Celadon City's Department Store opens floor by floor up to its roof, with medicine, Poké Balls, TMs, gifts, vitamins and drinks to buy and a lift attendant to ride with; the Condominiums' stairs and two lifts lead up to a games office and the roof, and the Game Corner and Prize Corner open their doors, though their tables are still switched off.
- In Johto's Kanto, the Underground Path opens beneath Saffron City between Routes 5 and 6, with two items hidden along it, and its little gate houses now stand on the routes where there had been an empty lawn. Silph Co.'s lobby in Saffron City welcomes visitors, and its building now carries its own name instead of the Pokétch Company's.
- In Johto's Kanto, the Seafoam Islands' ladders lead down through four icy floors, with boulders to push, blocks of ice that slide across the ice, a pair of snowboarders and a skier to battle and items to find, to the cold water at the bottom where Articuno waits. The cave's western mouth now opens onto Route 20 too.
- Many more of the big moves are staged in battle: Sky Attack and Focus Blast pushed in on, Shadow Ball and Flash Cannon followed across, Brave Bird and Wave Crash lunging in, Dig and Dive bursting up under their target, Bounce coming down on it, Surf, Blizzard and Heat Wave circled by the camera, the three Pledges' pillars, Blood Moon's red light, Spiky Shield's thorns, and a glowing aura round a Pokémon using Swords Dance, Nasty Plot, Calm Mind or Dragon Dance. Earthquake's and Zygarde's cracks now spread round the target where you can see them, and Sacred Sword leaves bright steel blades.
- The Champions' Mega Max fights open on a band of their own, the Dynamax red rising through it behind the Mega Max mark; once their ace Mega Dynamaxes its health box widens across the top of the screen into a boss bar with the three turns as three lights, each Champion has words for the moment their ace grows, and the battle's lines now wait for a Dynamax to finish growing instead of running on into the next turn.
- The legendary and mythical Pokémon of Unova to Paldea have their own moves staged in battle: V-create and Bolt Strike charging in, the Swords of Justice's blades, Zygarde's arrows pressing its target down, Diamond Storm's glinting crystals, Necrozma's beams rocking it back, Thunder Cage's crackling sparks, Behemoth Blade and Collision Course lunging in, Glacial Lance's frost, Terapagos's starstorm and dozens more, each with its own camera, light and what it leaves behind. Outrage no longer deepens its red stage further.
- Medals: 120 of them in six categories (journey, battles, catching, growth, the Pokédex and the League), won as you play and kept on a new page of the Trainer Card with how far you are toward each. Turn on MEDAL NOTICES in the options to have each one drop in at the top of the field with a short fanfare of its own.
- A run timer for speedruns: turn on RUN TIMER in the options and a corner of the field and of battles shows the real time and the in-game time, with the last split. Choose what splits count (the Hall of Fame alone, every Badge, or every Badge and each Team Galactic commander too); each split is also written to splits.json beside your saves for a spreadsheet or an autosplitter, and the Hall of Fame keeps your final time beside the team's day.
- The pseudo-legendaries' big moves and the signature moves of the starters of Unova to Paldea are staged in battle: Outrage circled by the camera, Stone Edge tossing its target among falling shards, Dragon Rush and Glaive Rush lunging in, Meteor Mash's stars, Clanging Scales' rings of sound, Pyro Ball and Snipe Shot followed across the field, Flower Trick's petals and more, each with a brief hold on the heaviest blows (off under REDUCE MOTION).
- The Trainer Card gains a statistics page once you have played a little: steps (on foot, surfing, cycling, diving), battles won, lost and run from, blackouts, Pokémon met and caught, levels gained and evolutions, in four tabs (up and down change the tab).
- Johto's Champion can go on through the League gate's west door into Viridian City, up Route 2 and through Viridian Forest to Pewter City, where Brock gives Kanto's Boulder Badge, and east over Route 3 into Mt. Moon, its square and its gift shop. The red-haired boy now waits in Mt. Moon after the League, and beating him there is what starts the Dragon's Den's post-game.
- From Viridian City, Johto's Champion can now walk Route 1 south to Pallet Town (Red's house, Blue's house and Professor Oak's lab), surf Route 21 to what is left of Cinnabar Island, where Blue looks out over the ruins, and surf Route 20 east to the Seafoam Islands, where Blaine gives Kanto's Volcano Badge in his Gym in the rock.
- Max Moves land like finishers: the camera looks up at the giant, whips across to its target, punches in and holds the blow a moment, then pulls back for the rest; the target is thrown back, pieces of the move's type burst off it, the screen shakes, and its HP box jolts with the lost share flashing white before it drains (REDUCE MOTION keeps the ordinary camera and no jolt).
- A Max Move's weather, terrain or G-Max hazard now comes from its blow: the sun or rain rings out across the field from the Pokémon it hit, a terrain floods out from its feet over both platforms, and G-Max Steelsurge's splinters are thrown from the impact to where they hover. A Mega Dynamax shows both its halves: its clouds, wreath and glow are in the Mega's violet, pink, orange and cyan with the Mega mark flaring on its body as it grows, and the camera stays low on it from the Mega Evolution's light straight into its growth.
- Max Guard rings when a blow meets it: the attack stops at the dome and the hexagons facing it light up. A giant's three turns can be counted at a glance (its clouds thin and its glow quickens each turn, the turn counter ticks down, and a heartbeat sounds on its last turn), and its end is a fall: it shudders, bleeds red light and collapses back to size; a giant that faints sinks into its clouds first.
- Unova after the Hall of Fame: Clay Tunnel opens from Driftveil City, with miners to help dig through and mining carts between its floors; under it the Underground Ruins, whose door leads somewhere different by day and by night and, once its old switch is stepped on, to the chambers of Regirock, Registeel and Regice; and Mistralton Cave's upper floor and the Guidance Chamber.
- A new way to play at NEW GAME, "No later mechanics": nobody, you or the trainers you meet, can Mega Evolve, use a Z-Move, Dynamax or Terastallize in that run.
- Trainers use Mega Evolution, Z-Moves, Dynamax and Terastallization with more sense: a Z-Move only when it will hit well or its bonus helps, Dynamax kept for their last Pokémon or their ace, and Terastallization when it turns a weakness to your last move around or on their last Pokémon.
- Release builds for Windows, Linux and macOS (Intel and Apple silicon): one executable each with everything it needs inside, and the models the first screens show already made, so the first start takes seconds (plan 16 · T2).
- The title's bottom right corner, and the options' HELP line, say which version of the game this is; a save remembers the version that wrote it.
- The game's own icon.
- A 1280×800 window size, the Steam Deck's screen, which the first start picks on a screen of that size.
- `docs/playing.md`: running a release without installing anything, macOS's and Windows' first-start questions, the Steam Deck, the controls, where saves are kept and `overrides/`.
- `--verify` now has something to check: every release carries the list of its files (`manifest.sha256`).
- The Pokéathlon has four more events: Pennant Capture (race the other teams to the pennants on a field), Snow Throw (throw snowballs at the other teams, and keep the button down to duck theirs), Disc Catch (run and jump for the discs falling into your lane) and Lamp Jump (press just before your Pokémon lands to bounce higher and light the lamps). They join the courses once those are built.
- Johto: once the Director has given you the Rainbow Wing, the five Kimono Girls of Ecruteak's Dance Theater test you in five battles in a row with no rest between; pass and the gate opens onto Bellchime Trail and the Bell Tower's ten floors, where their dance on the roof brings Ho-Oh down to battle (beaten, it comes back after your next Hall of Fame). Afterwards they give you the Silver Wing, and Lugia waits for it in the deepest cave of the Whirl Islands. Johto's Hall of Fame ceremony and its credits have songs of their own, the Safari Zone has its own trek, your friend next door waits at home to see you off to the League after the eighth Badge, and Chuck and Jasmine offer their numbers to Johto's Champion.
- In Johto, putting a picture in the Ruins of Alph back together now drops you through the floor into a great hall under the Ruins, the only place Unown live; the hall's door on the grounds opens from then on.
