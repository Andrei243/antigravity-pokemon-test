# Changelog

What changes from one release to the next, for players. Every session's close-out adds its lines under **Unreleased**, in plain words (what a player sees or can do now, not how it was built); a release turns Unreleased into its version's section, which `.github/workflows/release.yml` puts on the release's page. Versions are `v0.y.z` until the story is complete.

## [Unreleased]

### Added
- The Pokéathlon has three more events (Relay Run, Circle Push and Goal Roll) and its five courses (Speed, Power, Skill, Stamina and Jump). A course's score wins a gold, silver or bronze medal, the desk beside the course desk shows each course's best score and medal, and the stall on the dome's square sells vitamins, evolution stones and more for Athlete Points.
- The PC's boxes can be searched (by species, type, move, ability, nature, level, held item, gender, shiny and mark), put in order one box or all at once (by number, level, name, type or how recently met), and several Pokémon can be picked up together and put down in one box, or released together (behind the HOLD TO CONFIRM hold when that option is on). A new BOX TOOLS option turns it on or off; under Platinum's rules the PC stays as it was.
- Photo mode in the field: press F9 (Select on a pad) where you could save, and the world holds still while you move the camera around, zoom in or out, light the scene at another time of day, borrow rain, snow or fog, add a filter (vivid, black and white, sepia, soft) or a frame, and press A to save the picture. The real clock and the weather stay as they were.
- Two new options for players who can't hear the game or want fewer slips. CAPTIONS names every sound and cry on screen as it plays ("A door opens", "Starly cries"), in small pills at the left. HOLD TO CONFIRM makes deleting a save, resetting the options and releasing a Pokémon wait until the button is held for a moment, with a ring filling on the button.
- The strong moves Pokémon learn past level 100 are now staged like the big signature attacks: the camera punches in on the blows, rides the lances and pulses, circles the songs and storms, and pushes in on the capstone blasts, with a pause on the heavy hits, a light over the stage and what each leaves behind (cracks, embers, frost, shards, petals); the setup moves glow round their user in their type's colour.
- Five Gigantamax moves have scenes and sounds of their own: G-Max Wildfire's ring of flame walls, G-Max Volt Crash's bolt from the sky and lightning running along the ground, G-Max Terror's shadow hands and great eye, G-Max Resonance's aurora, and G-Max Replenish's shower of berries. Every Gigantamax now grows in clouds of its own colours instead of the plain Dynamax red.
- Five more Gigantamax moves have scenes and sounds of their own: G-Max Chi Strike's four golden fists, G-Max Gold Rush's shower of spinning coins, G-Max Cuddle's ring of hearts hugging its target, G-Max Befuddle's great wings and glittering scales, and G-Max Depletion's tower of panels going dark one by one. G-Max Resonance's aurora now hangs in soft upright curtains instead of streaks across the trees.
- In Johto's Kanto, Routes 12 to 15 lead south from Lavender Town to Fuchsia City, with Route 19 out to sea beyond it. In Fuchsia's Gym, Janine's trainers dress up as her: talk to each to unmask them, then find the real Janine for Kanto's Soul Badge. With seven of Kanto's Badges, Blue leaves Cinnabar Island for home, and his Viridian Gym opens for the Earth Badge.
- In Johto's Kanto, Route 11 leads east from Vermilion City to the gate before Route 12's sleeping Snorlax, Diglett's Cave tunnels from Route 11 to Route 2 by Pewter City, and the Cycling Road runs from Celadon City down to Fuchsia City: its gate keepers send back anyone without a Bicycle, and its bikers wait all the way down. Gym halls and other rooms that span several map pieces (the Viridian and Celadon Gyms, the Pokéathlon Dome, the S.S. Aqua and more) are now framed on the room itself instead of half a screen of dark wall.
- Many more of the big moves are staged in battle: Sky Attack and Focus Blast pushed in on, Shadow Ball and Flash Cannon followed across, Brave Bird and Wave Crash lunging in, Dig and Dive bursting up under their target, Bounce coming down on it, Surf, Blizzard and Heat Wave circled by the camera, the three Pledges' pillars, Blood Moon's red light, Spiky Shield's thorns, and a glowing aura round a Pokémon using Swords Dance, Nasty Plot, Calm Mind or Dragon Dance. Earthquake's and Zygarde's cracks now spread round the target where you can see them, and Sacred Sword leaves bright steel blades.
- The Champions' Mega Max fights open on a band of their own, the Dynamax red rising through it behind the Mega Max mark; once their ace Mega Dynamaxes its health box widens across the top of the screen into a boss bar with the three turns as three lights, each Champion has words for the moment their ace grows, and the battle's lines now wait for a Dynamax to finish growing instead of running on into the next turn.
- The legendary and mythical Pokémon of Unova to Paldea have their own moves staged in battle: V-create and Bolt Strike charging in, the Swords of Justice's blades, Zygarde's arrows pressing its target down, Diamond Storm's glinting crystals, Necrozma's beams rocking it back, Thunder Cage's crackling sparks, Behemoth Blade and Collision Course lunging in, Glacial Lance's frost, Terapagos's starstorm and dozens more, each with its own camera, light and what it leaves behind. Outrage no longer deepens its red stage further.
- Medals: 120 of them in six categories (journey, battles, catching, growth, the Pokédex and the League), won as you play and kept on a new page of the Trainer Card with how far you are toward each. Turn on MEDAL NOTICES in the options to have each one drop in at the top of the field with a short fanfare of its own.
- A run timer for speedruns: turn on RUN TIMER in the options and a corner of the field and of battles shows the real time and the in-game time, with the last split. Choose what splits count (the Hall of Fame alone, every Badge, or every Badge and each Team Galactic commander too); each split is also written to splits.json beside your saves for a spreadsheet or an autosplitter, and the Hall of Fame keeps your final time beside the team's day.
- The pseudo-legendaries' big moves and the signature moves of the starters of Unova to Paldea are staged in battle: Outrage circled by the camera, Stone Edge tossing its target among falling shards, Dragon Rush and Glaive Rush lunging in, Meteor Mash's stars, Clanging Scales' rings of sound, Pyro Ball and Snipe Shot followed across the field, Flower Trick's petals and more, each with a brief hold on the heaviest blows (off under REDUCE MOTION).
- The Trainer Card gains a statistics page once you have played a little: steps (on foot, surfing, cycling, diving), battles won, lost and run from, blackouts, Pokémon met and caught, levels gained and evolutions, in four tabs (up and down change the tab).
- Johto's Champion can go on through the League gate's west door into Viridian City, up Route 2 and through Viridian Forest to Pewter City, where Brock gives Kanto's Boulder Badge, and east over Route 3 into Mt. Moon, its square and its gift shop. The red-haired boy now waits in Mt. Moon after the League, and beating him there is what starts the Dragon's Den's post-game.
- From Viridian City, Johto's Champion can now walk Route 1 south to Pallet Town (Red's house, Blue's house and Professor Oak's lab), surf Route 21 to what is left of Cinnabar Island, where Blue looks out over the ruins, and surf Route 20 east to the Seafoam Islands, where Blaine gives Kanto's Volcano Badge in his Gym in the rock.
- Max Moves land like finishers: the camera looks up at the giant, whips across to its target, punches in and holds the blow a moment, then pulls back for the rest; the target is thrown back, pieces of the move's type burst off it, the screen shakes, and its HP box jolts with the lost share flashing white before it drains (REDUCE MOTION keeps the ordinary camera and no jolt).
- A Max Move's weather, terrain or G-Max hazard now comes from its blow: the sun or rain rings out across the field from the Pokémon it hit, a terrain floods out from its feet over both platforms, and G-Max Steelsurge's splinters are thrown from the impact to where they hover. A Mega Dynamax shows both its halves: its clouds, wreath and glow are in the Mega's violet, pink, orange and cyan with the Mega mark flaring on its body as it grows, and the camera stays low on it from the Mega Evolution's light straight into its growth.
- Max Guard rings when a blow meets it: the attack stops at the dome and the hexagons facing it light up. A giant's three turns can be counted at a glance (its clouds thin and its glow quickens each turn, the turn counter ticks down, and a heartbeat sounds on its last turn), and its end is a fall: it shudders, bleeds red light and collapses back to size; a giant that faints sinks into its clouds first.
- Unova after the Hall of Fame: Clay Tunnel opens from Driftveil City, with miners to help dig through and mining carts between its floors; under it the Underground Ruins, whose door leads somewhere different by day and by night and, once its old switch is stepped on, to the chambers of Regirock, Registeel and Regice; and Mistralton Cave's upper floor and the Guidance Chamber.
- A new way to play at NEW GAME, "No later mechanics": nobody, you or the trainers you meet, can Mega Evolve, use a Z-Move, Dynamax or Terastallize in that run.
- Trainers use Mega Evolution, Z-Moves, Dynamax and Terastallization with more sense: a Z-Move only when it will hit well or its bonus helps, Dynamax kept for their last Pokémon or their ace, and Terastallization when it turns a weakness to your last move around or on their last Pokémon.
- Release builds for Windows, Linux and macOS (Intel and Apple silicon): one executable each with everything it needs inside, and the models the first screens show already made, so the first start takes seconds (plan 16 · T2).
- The title's bottom right corner, and the options' HELP line, say which version of the game this is; a save remembers the version that wrote it.
- The game's own icon.
- A 1280×800 window size, the Steam Deck's screen, which the first start picks on a screen of that size.
- `docs/playing.md`: running a release without installing anything, macOS's and Windows' first-start questions, the Steam Deck, the controls, where saves are kept and `overrides/`.
- `--verify` now has something to check: every release carries the list of its files (`manifest.sha256`).
- The Pokéathlon has four more events: Pennant Capture (race the other teams to the pennants on a field), Snow Throw (throw snowballs at the other teams, and keep the button down to duck theirs), Disc Catch (run and jump for the discs falling into your lane) and Lamp Jump (press just before your Pokémon lands to bounce higher and light the lamps). They join the courses once those are built.
